```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ghada built an order form page for a bakery. Her browser shows these parts of the first view: JavaScript 140 KB, images 60 KB, fonts 20 KB, style rules 10 KB, page text 10 KB. Her starting limit is about 170 KB, and the bakery's customers use phones on weak connections. What should Ghada do first?"
options:
  - "Leave the page: it is within the limit"
  - "Cut the JavaScript first: the page is 70 KB over"
  - "Trim the page text first: the page is 70 KB over"
  - "Swap one image: the page is 40 KB over"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The parts add up to 240 KB, not to a number under 170. The images are one part of the page, so the page is over the limit."
  - "Right. 140 plus 60 plus 20 plus 10 plus 10 is 240, which is 70 over 170. Only the JavaScript is bigger than 70 KB, so it is the part that can close the gap."
  - "The gap is 70 KB, which is right, but page text is only 10 KB. Removing all of it would save 10 KB, far short of the gap."
  - "240 minus 170 is 70, not 40. The images are 60 KB in total, so one image cannot save 70."
```

question: "Murad's sample cost report shows: compute $11.50, storage $4.20, data transfer $1.30, support $0. His budget alert is set at $20 for the month, and the forecast for the month is $26. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Leave it and trust the budget to stop spending at $20"
  - "Wait for the alert before looking at the costs"
  - "Review the compute item and delete unused resources"
  - "Upgrade the plan and treat $17 as over the budget"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A budget alert tells him. It does not stop spending, so costs can pass $20 with no pause."
  - "Alerts can lag behind charges, so the cost may pass the limit before the alert arrives. Waiting is not a plan."
  - "Right. The costs so far are 11.50 plus 4.20 plus 1.30 plus 0, which is $17. The forecast of $26 is over the $20 alert. Compute is the largest item, so he starts there."
  - "$17 is below the $20 alert, not above it. Upgrading would not lower the forecast either."
