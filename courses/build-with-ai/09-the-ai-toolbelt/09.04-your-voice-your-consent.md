## Whose voice, and what for?

<InteractiveCanvas slug="voice-use-check" />

Sort eight uses of a voice. Your own voice, or a stock voice, with a label is fine. Another person's voice needs their clear yes first. Some uses, such as a trick to get money, are never fine. A wrong try tells you why.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the rule behind each bucket">

ElevenLabs is one example of a voice tool. Its Prohibited Use Policy, updated on 9 October 2026, forbids cloning or imitating the voice of any living person without their explicit and informed consent or other valid authorization. It also forbids fraud and scams, and it forbids imitating political candidates or elected officials. This course goes further than that rule. Clone only your own voice. The rule is a minimum, and the course's rule is the one you follow.

If a real project needs another person's voice, you need that person's yes, in writing. It should say what the voice will say, where it will be shown and for how long. The tool's rules and the platform's rules may still say no. A public video is not consent. A joke is not consent. A friend who does not mind is not consent.

In February 2024, the US Federal Communications Commission said that calls made with AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under a US telephone law. Such calls need the called person's prior consent unless an exemption applies. That is a rule about phone calls in the United States. It is not a ban on cloning a voice, and you should not tell anyone that cloning is illegal.

</GoDeeper>

## Cloning your own voice is optional and paid

<MermaidDiagram title="What each plan allows, at the time of writing" chart={`flowchart LR
  F["Free plan: no cloning"] --> I["Instant clone: a paid plan"] --> P["Professional clone: a higher plan"]
`} legend="Checked 2026-10-25. Plans and prices change, so read the tool's pricing page." />

At the time of writing (checked 2026-10-25), a free ElevenLabs account cannot clone a voice. An instant clone needs a paid plan. A professional clone needs a higher plan, and it checks that the voice is yours. The tool is for ages 18 and over. This step is optional. The free path is your own recording.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the tool, an alternative and access">

An instant clone works from a short sample, under two minutes, and it cannot be shared publicly. A professional clone uses longer training audio, needs a higher plan, and uses a check, which ElevenLabs calls voice-captcha, to confirm that the clone comes from your own voice samples. The terms ask users to be 18 or older. ElevenLabs has no public list of supported countries. It lists the places it restricts, and Palestine is not one of them. So check whether the site opens and works for you, and if it does not, record your own voice.

OpenVoice is an open-source alternative with an MIT licence. It makes an instant clone, but it needs Python on a laptop, and its page gives no usage warning and has no consent step. So the rule has to come from you: own voice only, with a label. A third free choice is a stock voice from any tool, with the words "AI voice" on your video. No real person's voice is copied, so there is nobody to ask.

</GoDeeper>

## How a copied voice is used against people

<MermaidDiagram title="If a voice asks for money" chart={`flowchart TD
  C["A call in a relative's voice asks for money"] --> H["Hang up"]
  H --> B["Call back on a number you already know"]
  B --> D["Decide with the real person"]
`} legend="The check does not depend on how real the voice sounds." />

Scammers can copy a relative's voice from a short clip posted online. The US Federal Trade Commission warns about this and advises: hang up, then call the person back on a number you already know. Be careful with urgent requests for a transfer, cryptocurrency or gift cards.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: voice ID and banks">

In February 2023, a reporter at Vice used a clone made from about five minutes of his own speech to get through the phone voice ID of Lloyds Bank. It took more than one try and some adjusting of the audio. Lloyds said that voice ID is optional and that it had not seen this fraud against its customers. On 22 July 2025, the chief executive of OpenAI spoke at a Federal Reserve conference. He said that some financial institutions still accept a voiceprint as authentication, and he called that a poor thing to keep doing.

Do not say that voice cloning always fools banks. Two cases show that some voice ID systems can be fooled in tests, and many banks use other checks. What you can do is protect your own accounts. Do not rely on your voice as the only proof of who you are, and keep a second check that does not depend on how you sound. If a call asks for money, use the rule from the picture: hang up and call back on a number you know.

</GoDeeper>

## Say it is AI

<MermaidDiagram title="When to say it" chart={`flowchart TD
  Q["Did AI make the voice?"] -->|"no, you recorded it"| N["Nothing to label"]
  Q -->|"yes"| S["Say so in the first lines"]
`} legend="This is the course's rule. It asks for more than some platforms do." />

If you publish audio that sounds like a real person speaking and AI made it, say so in the first lines. Some platforms and some laws now require it. YouTube's own list says cloning your own voice for voice-overs needs no disclosure. This course still asks you to say it, because it costs one sentence and keeps trust.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: platforms and the EU rule">

YouTube asks creators to disclose realistic altered or synthetic content. Its list of things that need no disclosure includes help with a script, an outline or a thumbnail, and cloning your own voice for voice-overs or dubs. Repeated failure to disclose can bring a label added by YouTube, removal, or loss of access to the Partner Programme. TikTok's newsroom said in 2023 that it requires people to label AI-generated content with realistic images, audio or video. Meta may require an "AI info" label for photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio. LinkedIn forbids sharing synthetic or manipulated media that shows a person saying something they did not say, without clear disclosure. The details differ, so read the rules of the place where you publish.

In the European Union, Article 50 of the AI Act has applied since 2 August 2026. People who make or change audio, images or video that looks like real people or events, and could seem authentic, must say it was artificially made. For clearly artistic, creative, satirical or fictional work, the duty is lighter. The European Commission says personal, non-professional use is outside the Act. But a person who publishes for a client or regular income may count as a professional. This is not legal advice. It does not mean every post needs a label, and a mentor can help you check a real case.

</GoDeeper>
