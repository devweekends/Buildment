```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Samir, a student, posts write-ups of his labs on LinkedIn. Four facts: 1) His last post explains a real bug and links the commit, and 11 people read it. 2) A group chat of eight fellows proposes that everyone likes each other's posts within an hour. 3) LinkedIn's community policy tells members not to agree in advance to like or re-share each other's content. 4) A tool offers to comment on 50 posts a day in his name, and LinkedIn's User Agreement bans automated methods. What should he do next?"
options:
  - "Join the group's agreement to like each other's posts in an hour"
  - "Use the comment tool that leaves 50 comments a day in his name"
  - "Wait until a post has 100 readers before he writes the next one"
  - "Write up the next real bug fix and ask two readers for honest feedback"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Fact 3 names agreeing in advance to like each other's posts. Real people can still give likes that were arranged, and those likes tell a reader nothing about the post."
  - "Fact 4 says the tool is automated, and the User Agreement bans automated methods. A comment under his name that he did not write is not his."
  - "Fact 1 says 11 real people read a useful post. Reach is partly luck, so waiting for a number only stops him from sharing true work."
  - "Right. A real bug and a linked commit are useful and can be checked (fact 1), and two readers who answer honestly are not an agreement to like. Neither rule in facts 3 and 4 is broken."
```

What should he do with it?"

  - "Use it for his own habit from the next change onward"

  - "Set it aside until a mentor approves the advice"

explanations:
  - "Right. The advice is ordinary, it matches what he just read, and a wrong commit message costs him nothing he cannot fix. Using it as a draft of his habit is enough."
  - "Checking a primary page is a good habit for a fact that matters. Here the advice already matches the chapter, and the habit is easy to change if it does not suit him."
  - "A second assistant can repeat the same advice, so it adds little. The match with the chapter is already a check."
  - "Waiting for approval slows him down on a low-stakes habit. He can start now and adjust after his mentor's feedback."

  - "The detector score of 12% AI"
  - "The AI-use line, shown on its own"
  - "The dated notes and two drafts"
  - "The final post, shown on its own"

explanations:
  - "The score is a guess. Detectors have flagged real writers and have passed AI text, so a low score does not show authorship either."
  - "The line is true, but it is her statement. It says what the assistant did and does not show where her words came from."
  - "Right. Dated notes and drafts are files a mentor can open and compare, and they show the post growing from her own notes."
  - "A good post is not evidence of who wrote it. She has real evidence in her notes and drafts, and leaving it out wastes it."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A reviewer has five minutes with two repositories for small web pages. 1) Samir: one commit named 'final', 520 lines changed, made on Sunday night. 2) Wafa: seven commits over six days, each under 90 lines, with messages such as 'Fix: show today's soup, not yesterday's'. 3) Both pages run when the reviewer opens them. 4) The reviewer wants to know how each fellow handles a bug. Which conclusion do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Samir's repository is stronger, with one commit and less to open"
  - "Wafa's history shows a bug fix, with Samir's showing none"
  - "The two repositories are equal, with both pages running well"
  - "Wafa's repository is weaker, with seven commits to go through"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Fact 1 gives no timing, and a single commit named 'final' shows no step. Less to open is not more evidence about bugs."
  - "Right. Fact 2 shows a message that names a bug and its fix, and fact 4 is about bugs. A single commit named 'final' shows no step."
  - "Fact 4 asks about bug handling, not about whether a page runs. Both pages running (fact 3) cannot tell the reviewer that."
  - "Fact 2 says each of her commits is under 90 lines, so each is quick to go through. A reviewer can stop at the one about the bug."
```

  - "Right. Facts 1 and 4 point to readable steps and tests, which a mentor can follow, while fact 3 shows why the graph alone is weak."
