This is the gate for module 10. It asks for the post you drafted in chapter 10.7 in its finished form, plus the evidence behind it, so plan about 45 minutes. Tick each box only when it is true. Your answers save as you type.

## Module 10 checklist

- [ ] I can say what makes a post useful to one reader, and why bought, traded, automated or invented activity is a bad trade
- [ ] I keep dated notes and drafts as I write, and I write my AI-use line from a list of what the AI did
- [ ] I replace generic lines with a real fact, and I do not aim at a detector score
- [ ] I commit when one small thing works, with a message that says what changed, and I never fake activity, stars or numbers
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 10.2 to 10.7, and finished the quiz in 10.8
- [ ] I submitted my post pack, or my review of the provided pack, below

## Learning log

1. **Your reader:** Think of the last thing you shared, or one thing you wish you had. Could one reader use it and check it? What would you add so that they could? If you have shared nothing yet, use Alaa's three posts from chapter 10.4.
2. **Your trail:** If someone said your last post looked AI-made, which three dated files could you show them? What is missing from your folder?
3. **Your history:** Look at your last ten commits. Which message says nothing about what changed? Write the message you would write for it today.
4. **Your temptation:** Which number, headline or count are you most tempted to make bigger than it was? What will you do when that moment comes?

## Your post pack

This task finishes the work of chapter 10.7. A post by itself is easy to doubt. A post with its evidence is easy to check. So you hand in a pack of five parts. First, your finished post, about 250 to 400 words, improved after your mentor's reply to chapter 10.7 if you had one. Second, its source: three or more commits and notes, with their dates, that your claims come from. Third, your AI-use line, with the tool, what it did and what you checked. Fourth, a history check: look at your last ten commits, name one whose message says nothing, and write the message you would write now. Do not rewrite old history now. Say instead what you will do from today. Fifth, one sentence that starts "I will never fake", with a real example from your own work.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional fellow. Samir built a room booking page for his university library. His post told one story: on Wednesday a student said room 4 looked free at 7 pm, though the library closes at 5. The page showed every time two hours early, because the server and the library use different time zones. His fix was a commit named "Fix: show times in the library's time zone". On Thursday he added a test that checks the 9:00 slot shows as 9:00.

His pack matched every claim to a file. "Room 4 looked free at 7 pm" was in his Wednesday note. "Two hours early" was in the same note and in the test. His source list had four dated items. They were the Wednesday note, draft 1 saved before any AI help, the file with the assistant's three questions and his answers, and the Thursday commit. His AI-use line read: "A chat assistant asked me three questions about my first draft and fixed grammar in my second. I wrote the code and the post, and I checked every number against my commits and notes." For his history check, he found a Sunday commit named "final" with 520 lines in it. He wrote the message he would write today, which was three messages, because the work had three parts: the booking list, the time fix and the README. From now on, he said, he commits each time one thing works.

His last sentence was: "I will never fake a count. When I first wrote the draft, I wanted to say 300 students use the page. Six did, so the post says six." That sentence shows the habit working on a real temptation.

A weak pack looks different. The post says "my page handles 500 students", with no test and no note. The AI-use line says "all my own work" although the assistant rewrote two paragraphs. The history is one commit called "update". The pack says nothing about where the numbers came from. None of these is a bad person's choice. Each is what happens when the post is written first and the evidence is looked for afterwards. The order in this module is the reverse: notes first, drafts as you go, claims checked at the end.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer with one of the two tables below, depending on the route you chose. Your own project can be about anything. What counts is whether the habits are there.

**Route 1: your own post pack.**

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Post true to its source | Every number and event in the post is matched to a dated commit or note | All but one matched | Two or more unmatched, or any invented number |
| Source and trail | Three or more dated items that show the post growing, and draft 1 comes before any AI help | Two items, or the order is unclear | Fewer than two items |
| AI-use line | Tool, what it did and what you checked, all specific and true | Two of the three parts | One or none, or "all my own work" when AI helped |
| History check | One vague message named, a better message written, and a clear plan from today | The message named, but no plan or no better message | No vague message named |
| Nothing faked | The "I will never fake" sentence names a real temptation from your own work, and nothing in the pack is faked or private | The sentence is general | Any faked number, bought activity or private data in the pack |

**Pass rule for route 1:** your pack passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met", one of them "Post true to its source". Your reply to the mentor's follow-up question must also answer it with evidence.

**Route 2: Alaa's provided pack.**

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problems found | All four: the 500 volunteers, the AI-use line, the history, the agreed likes | Three of the four | Two or fewer |
| Habit named | The right habit named for every problem you found | Right for all but one | Right for fewer |
| Fix works | Every fix would work in practice | Three of four fixes work | Two or fewer work |
| True rewrite | A claim that matches her note (12 names typed by hand) and an AI-use line that says the assistant rewrote two paragraphs | One of the two is true | Neither is true |

**Pass rule for route 2:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met". The claim must not repeat 500 volunteers, and the AI-use line must not say "all my own work".

After you submit, your mentor replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which of your three source items would you show first, and why?" or "What would you write instead of 500?" A good reply points to something you can show, such as a dated file or a line in your notes. You can answer in writing or in a voice note.
