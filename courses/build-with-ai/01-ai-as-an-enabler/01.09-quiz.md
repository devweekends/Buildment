```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Someone in Dalia's study group chat shares a post by the founder of a company that sells an AI coding tool. The post says: 'Within a year, AI will write most of the code at every company.' The post links no data. How should Dalia read it?"
options:
  - "As a measurement of how companies use the tool today"
  - "As a prediction by a seller, to set against measurements"
  - "As an independent finding from an experienced person"
  - "As a reason for the study group to stop learning to code"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The post is about a year ahead, so it cannot be a measurement of today."
  - "Right. It looks ahead, the speaker sells the tool, and no data is offered. Measurements of current use are the next thing to look for."
  - "The founder sells the tool. Experience does not make a seller independent."
  - "The post offers no data, and a date does not make a prediction true. It is not a reason to stop learning."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A post says: 'One in four workers worldwide will lose their jobs to AI.' Khalil finds the source. The International Labour Organization estimated that about one in four workers are in jobs with some exposure to generative AI, and 3.3% are in the highest exposure group. It judged that transformation of jobs is more likely than replacement. How does the post compare with its source?"
options:
  - "Correct; the ILO counted the exposed jobs that will end"
  - "Nearly correct; the top exposure group is close to a quarter"
  - "Wrong; the ILO found that AI touches none of those jobs"
  - "Overstated; it mixes up exposure and job loss"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The ILO counted exposure, not endings, and judged that change is more likely than replacement."
  - "The highest exposure group is 3.3% of workers, far from a quarter. The two numbers measure different things."
  - "The ILO said these jobs are exposed and likely to change. It did not say AI touches none of them."
  - "Right. The source gives an estimate of exposure, and the ILO judged transformation more likely than replacement. The post changed what the number means."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Khalil asks an assistant whether AI makes developers faster. It answers: 'Yes. A 2023 study of 95 freelance programmers proved that AI tools make every developer 55.8% faster on any job.' Khalil looks up the study. The 95 freelancers each built the same small web server with a 2022 version of the tool. What is the mistake in the assistant's answer?"
options:
  - "It stretches one task and an older tool to developers in general"
  - "It cites freelancers, whose results do not carry over to employees"
  - "It reports a percentage, where a study should give a plain yes or no"
  - "It makes no mistake; a controlled trial is strong evidence"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The study covered one small task, 95 people and a 2022 tool. 'Every developer' and 'any job' go far beyond that."
  - "Nothing in the facts says freelancers differ from employees. The problem is the jump from one task to every job."
  - "A study can report a size. The problem is how far the claim reaches, not that it gives a number."
  - "A controlled trial can be strong evidence for what it tested. This one tested a single task with a 2022 tool."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A fictional school's help desk handled 600 requests a week with 6 people. After it added an assistant that drafts replies, 4 people handle the same 600 requests a week. Wrong drafts that had to be sent again rose from 10 a week to 40 a week. What do the numbers support?"
options:
  - "Output per person doubled, and two people were not needed"
  - "The assistant made the desk worse; wrong drafts rose from 10 to 40"
  - "Output per person rose by half; checking drafts is the work to protect"
  - "No change; the desk still handles the same 600 requests a week"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "600 divided by 6 is 100 requests per person, and 600 divided by 4 is 150. That is half more, not double."
  - "The desk handles the same work with two fewer people, so output per person rose. More wrong drafts is a cost to manage, not proof that the desk got worse overall."
  - "Right. 100 per person became 150, which is half more. Wrong drafts rose from 10 to 40, so checking is what keeps the gain."
  - "The total is the same, but the work now rests on 4 people instead of 6. That is the change."
```

How should Dalia treat the post?"

```quiz
  - "As false; the newsletter says about 30 engineers built the product"

explanations:
  - "The newsletter disputes the 900 figure and the claim that the AI was fake, so the post is not simply true."
  - "Right. Former staff report human work, and the newsletter disputes the number and the 'all fake' story. The CEO's denial is one side's statement. The honest label is disputed."
  - "Former staff describe people doing most of the work, so the story is not simply false."
  - "A denial is a statement by one party. It does not settle what the newsletter and the investigation report."
```
