```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Someone in Dalia's study group chat shares a post. The founder of a company that sells an AI coding tool wrote: 'Within a year, AI will write most of the code at every company.' 1) The post links no data. 2) The founder sells the tool. 3) The statement is about a year ahead. How should Dalia read it?"
options:
  - "As a measurement of today, because the founder sees how the tool is used"
  - "As a prediction from someone who gains if it is believed, so she looks for measurements"
  - "As an independent finding, because the founder has worked in the industry for years"
  - "As proof that the group should stop learning to code, because the claim has a date"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Fact 3 says the statement is about a year ahead. A statement about the future cannot be a measurement of today."
  - "Right. Fact 3 makes it a prediction, fact 2 shows who gains, and fact 1 shows nothing measured is offered. Measurements of current use would be the next thing to look for."
  - "Fact 2 says the founder sells the tool. Experience does not make a seller independent."
  - "A date does not make a prediction true. Until the year has passed, and with no data in the post (fact 1), it proves nothing."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A post says: 'One in four workers worldwide will lose their jobs to AI.' Khalil finds the source. 1) The International Labour Organization estimated that about one in four workers are in jobs with some exposure to generative AI. 2) Only 3.3% were in the highest exposure group. 3) The ILO judged that transformation of jobs is more likely than replacement. Which conclusion do the facts support?"
options:
  - "The post is right, because every job that has some exposure to AI will end sooner or later"
  - "The post is nearly right, because 3.3% in the top group is close to a quarter of workers"
  - "The post is wrong, because the ILO found that AI has no effect on any job it looked at"
  - "The post turns an exposure estimate into job loss, which the ILO's judgement does not back"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Fact 3 works against this. The ILO judged transformation more likely than replacement, and exposure is not job loss."
  - "Fact 2 gives 3.3% for the highest group, which is far from a quarter. The two numbers measure different things."
  - "Facts 1 and 3 say jobs are exposed and likely to change. The ILO did not say AI has no effect."
  - "Right. Fact 1 is exposure, not loss, and fact 3 says change is more likely than replacement. The post changed what the number means."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Khalil asks an assistant whether AI makes developers faster. It answers: 'Yes. A 2023 study of 95 freelance programmers proved that AI tools make every developer 55.8% faster on any job.' Khalil looks up the study. 1) The 95 freelancers each built the same small web server. 2) The tool was a 2022 version. 3) The study reported a range of results, not one fixed number. What is the mistake in the assistant's answer?"
options:
  - "It treats one task, 95 people and an older tool as proof for every developer on any job"
  - "It cites a small trial, but only a survey of thousands of workers could count as evidence"
  - "It relies on freelancers, whose results can never apply to people who have a job"
  - "It is correct, because a controlled trial with a large result settles the question"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Facts 1 and 2 show how narrow the study was. 'Every developer' and 'any job' go far beyond one task and one older tool."
  - "A controlled trial can be good evidence when it is read for what it tested. The mistake is how far the claim reaches (facts 1 and 2), not the kind of study."
  - "Freelancers are people who write code, and nothing in the facts says their results can never apply to others. The mistake is the leap from one task to every job."
  - "A controlled trial can be sound and still cover only what it tested. Facts 1 and 2 show that this one tested a single task with a 2022 tool."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A fictional school's help desk handled 600 requests a week with 6 people. After it added an assistant that drafts replies, 4 people handle the same 600 requests a week. Wrong drafts that had to be sent again rose from 10 a week to 40 a week. Which conclusion do the numbers support?"
options:
  - "Output per person doubled, so the two people who left were not needed by the desk at all"
  - "The assistant made the desk worse overall, because wrong drafts rose from 10 to 40 a week"
  - "Output per person rose by half, and checking the drafts is now the work to protect"
  - "Nothing changed at all, because the desk still handles 600 requests a week as before"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "600 divided by 6 is 100 requests per person, and 600 divided by 4 is 150. That is half more, not double."
  - "The desk handles the same work with two fewer people, so output per person rose. The rise in wrong drafts is a cost to manage, not proof that things got worse overall."
  - "Right. 100 per person became 150, which is half more. Wrong drafts rose from 10 to 40, so checking is what keeps the gain."
  - "The total is the same, but the work now rests on 4 people instead of 6. That is the change."
```

explanations:
  - "Fact 1 works against this. The newsletter disputes the 700 figure and the claim that the AI was fake."
  - "Right. Fact 2 reports human work, and fact 1 disputes the number and the 'all fake' story. Fact 3 is one side's denial. The honest label is disputed."
  - "Fact 2 works against this. Former staff describe people doing most of the work, so the story is not simply false."
  - "A denial in fact 3 is a statement by one party. It does not settle facts 1 and 2."
