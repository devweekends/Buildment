Patients phoned the clinic, and the clinic's manager, Rima, phoned Jawad. Nobody phoned the assistant, because it cannot answer, fix the texts or explain what happened. This module asks where a person must lead, and who answers when AI was part of the work.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the full story">

Jawad is the only developer at a fictional booking service. Nine small clinics use it to book visits and to send reminder texts. Until last month, a person on his team wrote the reminders by hand every evening. Jawad wanted to save that hour, so he asked an AI assistant to write the code that builds a reminder from a booking.

The assistant answered in a minute with about thirty lines. Jawad read them and understood them. He ran the code on his laptop with one booking at 13:00, and the text said 13:00. On Sunday afternoon he put it on the server. That was the whole test.

What neither of them knew was that the server's clock runs three hours behind the clinics' clock. The code asked the server for the time, so the texts said 10:00 for a visit at 13:00. The assistant could not have known this, because Jawad had not told it where the code would run. You met this idea in chapter 1.4: the assistant answers with the facts it is given. Jawad could have found the problem, if he had tested the code where it runs.

On Sunday evening, 230 texts went out. On Monday at 8:40, Rima phoned him. Patients were arriving three hours early, the waiting room was full, and two staff members were answering phones instead of seeing patients. In this example, 61 patients arrived early. Rima did not ask which tool had written the code. She asked what Jawad was going to do about it. A second batch of 190 texts was due at 18:00.

Jawad's first thought was to say that the assistant wrote the function. He stopped, because he knew how it would sound. The assistant cannot take a phone call. The service sent the texts in its own name, and Jawad had shipped the code. If he blamed the tool, Rima would hear that nobody was looking after her patients.

The rest of the week was ordinary work. He paused the next batch, told the clinics, fixed the clock problem, and wrote a short note. By Friday, two clinics had asked him how the reminders were tested. He now had an honest answer and a new test to show them.

This module is about that Monday. First you decide who should lead each kind of call when AI is in the room. Then you look at real cases where people and companies answered for an AI-made mistake, and you practise owning one. Then you learn when it is better not to use AI at all. After a mixed review, the capstone brings the whole course together.
