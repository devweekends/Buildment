Bayan and Sami use the same chat assistant for the same kind of task: a database query that joins two tables. Bayan is studying for a databases exam. Sami is writing a report for his manager. Should they use the assistant in the same way?

## Four questions before you open the tool

<MermaidDiagram title="Four questions before you use AI" chart={`flowchart TD
  A{"High stakes and you cannot check it?"} -->|"yes"| S["Do it yourself, or ask a person"]
  A -->|"no"| B{"Private data in it?"}
  B -->|"yes"| N["Anonymise first, or do not paste"]
  B -->|"no"| C{"Is it your lesson?"}
  C -->|"yes"| T["Tutor only: hints and quizzes"]
  C -->|"no"| U["Use AI, then check"]
`} legend="Ask in this order. The first yes decides. Stakes means what you or other people could lose if the result is wrong." />

Ask four questions before you open the tool. What would a mistake cost, and can you check the result? Is private data in it? Is this a skill you are learning? The order matters. A costly mistake you cannot catch comes first, then privacy, then your own learning. If none apply, use the tool and check.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why the questions come in this order">

First, a costly mistake you cannot catch. If a wrong answer would cost a lot and you have no way to check it, an AI answer is a guess you cannot afford. Chapter 11.3 showed that the EU AI Act asks for people who can decide not to use a high-risk system, and the same idea fits you. You can often change the task so that you can check it, for example by testing on a copy, and then the answer changes.

Second, private data. Chapter 2.2 gave the test: would I be fine if a stranger read this? If not, remove the names, numbers and keys, or do not paste. This question comes before your learning goal, because a privacy mistake cannot be taken back.

Third, your own lesson. Chapter 1.6 showed that answers can weaken practice, and that hints which make you think can help. If the task is how you learn the skill, use the tool as a tutor.

Last, everything else. If the stakes are low, you can check the result, it is not your lesson and nothing is private, use the tool and check. If you cannot check, treat the answer as a guess. Stakes are about what you or other people could lose. They are not about how hard the task is.

</GoDeeper>

## Change one fact, watch the answer move

<InteractiveCanvas slug="use-ai-or-not" />

Tap a pill to change one fact. Start with Bayan's exam: switch "Is it your lesson?" to "Learning it" and the answer becomes tutor only. Then switch "Private data?" to "Yes" and anonymise comes first. The task did not change, only the facts around it.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the rules behind the picture">

| Order | Facts | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Costs a lot and you cannot check it | Do it yourself, or ask a person who can check |
| 2 | Private data is in it | Anonymise first, or do not paste |
| 3 | It is your lesson | Tutor only |
| 4 | Costs little and you cannot check it | Use AI and treat the answer as a guess |
| 5 | Anything else | Use AI, then check |

The rules run in this order, and the first match wins. With four yes-or-no facts there are 16 possible contexts: 4 give "do it yourself", 6 give "anonymise", 3 give "tutor only" and 3 give "use AI".

There is another way out of the first row. If the answer is "do it yourself" because you cannot check, ask whether you can change the task so that you can. Testing a script on a copy of the data turns an unchecked task into a checked one, and then the answer moves to row 5. A reviewer does the same job. The rules are the course's own judgment. A workplace or a school may have a rule that wins, and then you follow it.

</GoDeeper>

## When you cannot check the result

<InteractiveCanvas slug="use-ai-or-not-login-code" />

Ehab asks an AI assistant for the login code of his study group's website. It runs, but he cannot tell whether passwords are stored safely. A mistake could expose 60 members, and he could not catch it. So he does not ship it alone: a mentor reviews it first.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: running is not checking">

Running is the weakest check for security code, because unsafe code runs as well as safe code. A security company's 2025 report on its tests of more than 100 AI models found that 45% of the code samples failed its security tests. It is vendor research on 2025 models. The page does not describe the tasks in full, so take it as a warning that AI code needs a real check.

Trust is also low among people who use these tools. In Stack Overflow's 2025 survey, 46% of developers distrusted the accuracy of AI tools and 33% trusted it. DORA's 2025 report, from a survey of about 5,000 technology professionals, found that 30% had little or no trust in AI-generated code. You have three options. Ask a person who can review it. Choose a simpler design that you can check. Or use a well-known sign-in service instead of writing your own. The mentor in the picture lowers the risk but does not remove it.

</GoDeeper>

## When the data is not yours to paste

<MermaidDiagram title="Can a fake copy stand in for the real list?" chart={`flowchart TD
  R["Task with a real list of names and numbers"] --> Q{"Can a fake copy stand in?"}
  Q -->|"yes"| F["Send the fake copy, then apply the fix to the real list yourself"]
  Q -->|"no"| H["Do the task by hand, or on your own device"]
`} legend="The real data never leaves your device. Chapter 2.2 gave the test: would I be fine if a stranger read this?" />

Ehab helps a tutoring centre tidy a list of 30 students and their parents' phone numbers. He does not paste the list. He makes a short copy with fake names and numbers, asks for the layout fix, and applies the fix to the real list himself. If a task needs the real data, he does it by hand.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what counts as private, and what anonymising misses">

Chapter 2.2 listed what never goes in: passwords, keys, a client's data without permission, other people's personal data and private documents. To anonymise is to replace details so that a reader cannot tell who they belong to. Fake names are not enough if the rest of the row still points to someone. A rare job title, a small village and a date of birth together can identify one person.

If you cannot make the copy safe, do not paste. Do the task by hand, or with a tool that runs on your own device. Before you paste anything of your own, find the tool's training setting, as chapter 2.2 taught.

</GoDeeper>

## Sometimes the answer is yes

<InteractiveCanvas slug="use-ai-or-not-quick-yes" />

Sami turns 12 made-up sales rows into a table. A mistake costs little, no private data is in it, and this is not a skill he is learning. He checks 3 rows by hand and all 3 match. Not using AI is a choice for some tasks, not a rule for all of them.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: Khaled, Rana and the four questions">

In chapter 1.1, Khaled gave everything to AI and Rana refused it. The four questions replace both habits with one question for each task. What do I lose if I use the tool here, and what do I lose if I do not? Tools change, as chapter 2.4 showed, and these questions do not. Three rows by hand is enough for a table of 12 made-up rows. For a real payroll, the check should be larger.

</GoDeeper>
