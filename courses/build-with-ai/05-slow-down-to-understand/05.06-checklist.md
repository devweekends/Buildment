This is the gate for module 5. It asks for your decision record, so plan about 45 minutes. Tick each box only when it is true. Your answers save as you type.

## Module 5 checklist

- [ ] I read the primary page and wrote my own facts before I asked an assistant
- [ ] I saved a first answer, with the tool and the date, so I can see what the research changed
- [ ] I asked two assistants the same question, each in a fresh chat
- [ ] I looked first where they differed, and I checked at least one fact where they agreed
- [ ] I asked what was left out, in neutral words, and checked what I was told
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 5.2 and 5.3, finished the practice in 5.4, and finished the quiz in 5.5
- [ ] I submitted my decision record, or my review of the provided record, below

## Learning log

1. **Your last fast answer:** Think of the last time you used an AI answer without checking it. If you cannot think of one, use Hadi in chapter 5.1. What would reading the primary page have shown?
2. **Your page:** Which primary page will you read first next time, and for which kind of question: a rule, a limit, a licence or a command?
3. **Your two tools:** Which two assistants will you use, and what will you do if only one of them opens for you?
4. **Your left-out question:** Which of the three questions will you ask, and how will you word it so that it does not invite a yes?

## Your decision record

This task turns the practice in chapter 5.4 into evidence. You have two routes. On route 1, you hand in your own decision record from chapter 5.4, improved with anything you learned since, and you add a short paragraph called "what changed". On route 2, you review the provided record below, which has problems in it, and you rewrite its decision. Either route takes about 45 minutes, and you can dictate your answer with your phone's microphone.

The paragraph "what changed" is the main part of route 1. Put your first answer next to your researched answer, and write what is different: the choice, a number, a rule, a condition. Then write which step found each difference: the primary page, the comparison, or the question about what was left out. If nothing changed, say so, and say what the checks confirmed. That is a real result, because now you know the first answer was right for a reason.

Suhaib's record shows what a strong one holds. Suhaib builds small web pages for local businesses, and a café asked him to choose a free tool for table bookings. The café gets about 120 bookings a month, and 6 staff need to see the booking list. His question was: "Which free booking tool fits the café?" His first answer, from an assistant on 2026-10-25, was "Use Tablenest: it is free, with unlimited bookings and unlimited staff". Assistants A and B, given his facts, both chose Tablenest. They differed on staff: A said unlimited, and B said 10 accounts. He looked there first and read Tablenest's plans page. It said the free plan is for up to 60 bookings a month and 3 staff accounts. Both assistants had agreed on unlimited bookings, and the page showed that was wrong too.

When Suhaib asked what was left out, the assistant named a second tool, Seatly. He read its page and wrote down the sentence he relied on: up to 200 bookings a month and 10 staff accounts. A café with 120 bookings and 6 staff fits both limits. His decision was Seatly. What would change it: more than 200 bookings, more than 10 staff, or a change to the page, which he would re-read each term. In his "what changed" paragraph he listed three differences: the tool, the bookings limit and the staff limit. Each one was found on a page, and one of them was a fact on which the two assistants had agreed.

Read his record again for four things. The first answer is saved, so the change can be seen. Each claim has a result from a page, with the date. An agreement was checked and found wrong. The decision comes with a condition. A weak record leaves these out. It copies both assistants' answers in full and says "they agree, so it is right". It names no page, or a page without a date. It has no first answer, so nobody can tell whether the research changed anything. The provided record below is weak in these ways, and in more.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your answer with one of the two tables below, depending on your route. Your decision can be about anything. What counts is whether the habits are visible in what you wrote.

**Route 1: your own decision record.**

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Record complete | All nine parts, including a first answer with the tool and the date | Seven or eight parts, or a first answer with no tool or date | Fewer than seven parts |
| What changed | At least two differences, each tied to the step that found it, or a clear account of what the checks confirmed | One difference, or differences without the step that found them | Nothing said about what changed |
| Agreement checked | At least one fact where the assistants agreed, checked on a page and marked right or wrong | An agreement noted but not checked on a page | Agreement taken as proof |
| Decision and limits | A decision with two reasons and a condition that would change it, and no private data in the record | A decision with reasons but no condition | No decision, or private data in the record |

**Pass rule for route 1:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met", one of them "Agreement checked". Your reply to the mentor's follow-up question must also answer it with evidence.

**Route 2: Aya's provided record.**

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problems found | At least four of the seven problems found | Three found | Two or fewer found |
| Habit named | The habit named for every problem you found | The habit named for all but one | The habit named for fewer |
| Numbers | Both monthly costs at $200, and the amount where they are equal | One of the two parts correct | Neither correct |
| Decision rewritten | A decision with a condition that would change it | A decision without a condition | Not rewritten |

**Pass rule for route 2:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met". Your reply to the mentor's follow-up question must also answer it with evidence. The problem of asking assistant B in the same chat is a must: an answer that misses it cannot pass.

Your mentor will write back with a question or two, such as "Which step found the biggest difference?" or "Where did the two assistants agree and the page disagree?" Answer with something you can show: a row in your record, a sentence on a page, a number. Writing or a voice note both work.
