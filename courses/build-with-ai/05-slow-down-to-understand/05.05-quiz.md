```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Razan helps a sports club of 40 members pick a free scheduling tool. She asks two assistants in separate new chats. Both say: 'The free plan has no member limit.' She notes four facts. 1) The two answers are almost word for word the same. 2) The tool's own pricing page, read today, says the free plan is for up to 25 members. 3) The club has 40 members. 4) The club can pay nothing this year. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Use the free plan, because two separate assistants gave the same answer"
  - "The free plan is too small for 40 members, so read other free tools' pages"
  - "Ask a third assistant, because the first two answers are so alike"
  - "Buy the paid plan, because the pricing page shows the free plan is too small"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The pricing page says the free plan is for up to 25 members, and the club has 40. Two matching answers do not change what the page says."
  - "Right. The pricing page is the primary page, and 40 members is more than 25. The club can pay nothing, so Razan looks at other free tools' pages next."
  - "Fact 1 says the answers are almost the same, so a third assistant may repeat the same mistake. The pricing page already answers the question."
  - "Fact 4 says the club can pay nothing this year, so buying a plan is not an option for Razan to choose."
```

options:
  - "Open the library's own page and read its last release date and its licence file"
  - "Ask the same assistant 'Are you sure?' and keep the answer if it says yes again"
  - "Accept the reply, because it gives two details and a confident yes to her question"
  - "Ask a second assistant the same question and keep any answer that matches the first"

explanations:
  - "Right. 'Actively maintained' and 'MIT licence' are facts that the library's own page can confirm. Her question also invited a yes, which is the agreeing habit from chapter 2.2."
  - "The same tool, asked again, usually repeats itself. A second yes from the same chat is not evidence."
  - "Details and confidence are not evidence. The question 'isn't it?' told the assistant which answer she wanted."
  - "A second assistant given the same leading question can agree too, and matching answers can share a mistake. The library's page is what settles it."

She notes four facts. 1) The two numbers differ. 2) The shop gets about 300 orders a month. 3) The plan page, dated this month, says the free plan allows up to 250 orders a month. 4) The shop cannot pay for a plan this year. What do the facts support?"

  - "Use B's answer, since 1,000 orders is above the 300 the shop gets"
  - "Plan on 550 orders, the average of the two answers, and use the free plan"

  - "Buy the paid plan now, since the free plan will not cover the 300 orders a month"

explanations:
  - "Fact 3 says the page allows 250 orders, so B's 1,000 is wrong. A number that fits the shop is not the same as a number that is true."
  - "An average of two wrong numbers is still not the number on the page. Fact 3 gives 250, which is below 300."
  - "Right. The differing answers told Aya where to look. The page says 250, and the shop needs about 300, so the free plan is too small."
  - "Fact 4 says the shop cannot pay for a plan this year, so buying one is not a choice she can make."

question: "Suhaib has 5 hours a week for 12 weeks. He reads the rules page of a fictional program, the Nile Open Lab. It says projects are scoped at about 40, 80 or 160 hours, and the program is for people who are 18 or older and new to open source. An assistant told him: 'Any project size fits a part-time contributor.' Suhaib is 19 and new to open source. Which statement do these facts support?"
options:
  - "All three sizes fit, because the assistant said any size fits a part-time person"
  - "The 80-hour project fits, because 5 hours a week for 12 weeks is about 80"
  - "He cannot join, because the program asks for people who are not new to open source"
  - "Only the 40-hour project fits, because he has 60 hours in total"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "He has 5 times 12, which is 60 hours. The 80-hour and 160-hour projects need more than that, so 'any size' is wrong for him."
  - "5 hours a week for 12 weeks is 60 hours, not about 80. Eighty hours is a third more than he has."
  - "The page says the program is for people who are new to open source, and he is new. He meets the rule."
  - "Right. 5 times 12 is 60 hours. The 40-hour project fits, and the 80-hour and 160-hour projects do not."

A country that is not in the table cannot be paid.' The assistant writes: 'The program pays every participant the same stipend.' What is the mistake?"
options:
  - "Nothing, because an assistant cannot differ from a text that was pasted in"
  - "It says everyone gets the same pay, but the page says pay depends on country and size"
  - "It is too short, because a summary has to repeat every sentence of the page"
  - "It should add an example amount from the assistant's own knowledge of similar programs"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Assistants can change, drop or add things when they summarise, even from pasted text. The summary has to be checked against the page."
  - "Right. The page says the stipend depends on country and project size, and that some countries cannot be paid. The summary turns that into one stipend for everyone."
  - "A summary is meant to be shorter than the page. The mistake is not its length but what it says."
  - "Adding an amount from the assistant's own knowledge brings in a fact that is not on the page and may be out of date. The page is the source."
