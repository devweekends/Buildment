```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Razan helps a sports club of 40 members pick a free scheduling tool. She asks two assistants in separate new chats. Both say: 'The free plan has no member limit.' She notes four facts. 1) The two answers are almost word for word the same. 2) The tool's own pricing page, read today, says the free plan is for up to 25 members. 3) The club has 40 members. 4) The club can pay nothing this year. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Use the free plan; two separate assistants gave the same answer"
  - "Look for another free tool; this plan is for up to 25 members"
  - "Ask a third assistant; the first two answers are almost the same"
  - "Buy the paid plan; the free plan is too small for 40 members"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The pricing page says the free plan is for up to 25 members, and the club has 40. Two matching answers do not change what the page says."
  - "Right. The pricing page is the primary page, and 40 members is more than 25. The club can pay nothing, so Razan looks at other free tools' pages next."
  - "Fact 1 says the answers are almost the same, so a third assistant may repeat the same mistake. The pricing page already answers the question."
  - "Fact 4 says the club can pay nothing this year, so buying a plan is not an option for Razan to choose."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Leen asks an assistant: 'Quillpad is the right library for my notes app, isn't it?' It replies: 'Yes, you are right. Quillpad is the right choice. It is actively maintained and has an MIT licence.' Quillpad is a fictional library. What is the right next step?"
options:
  - "Open the library's own page and look at its release date and licence"
  - "Ask the same assistant to check its own answer once more"
  - "Accept the reply and build the notes app on Quillpad"
  - "Ask a second assistant the same question, then compare the two replies"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. 'Actively maintained' and 'MIT licence' are facts that the library's own page can confirm. Her question also invited a yes, which is the agreeing habit from chapter 2.2."
  - "The same tool, asked again, usually repeats itself. A second yes from the same chat is not evidence."
  - "The reply gives two details and a confident yes, but it is the answer to a leading question. The details are unchecked."
  - "A second assistant given the same leading question can agree too, and matching answers can share a mistake. The library's page is what settles it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Suhaib manages the order form for a print and copy shop that gets about 300 orders a month. He asks two assistants about a form tool's free plan. A says it allows 100 orders a month. B says 1,000. He notes four facts. 1) The two numbers differ. 2) The shop gets about 300 orders a month. 3) The plan page, dated this month, says the free plan allows up to 250 orders a month. 4) The shop cannot pay for a plan this year. What do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Use B's number of 1,000 orders; it is above the shop's 300 orders"
  - "Plan on 550 orders; it is the average of the two answers"
  - "Call both answers wrong; the free plan is too small for 300 orders"
  - "Buy the paid plan today; the free plan will not cover the 300 orders"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Fact 3 says the page allows 250 orders, so B's 1,000 is wrong. A number that fits the shop is not the same as a number that is true."
  - "An average of two wrong numbers is still not the number on the page. Fact 3 gives 250, which is below 300."
  - "Right. The differing answers told Suhaib where to look. The page says 250, and the shop needs about 300, so the free plan is too small."
  - "Fact 4 says the shop cannot pay for a plan this year, so buying one is not a choice he can make."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Aya has 5 hours a week for 12 weeks. The rules page of the Nile Open Lab, a fictional program, says projects are scoped at about 40, 80 or 160 hours, and the program is for people who are 18 or older and new to open source. Aya is 19 and new to open source. An assistant tells her: 'Only the 40-hour project fits your 60 hours, and you meet the age and experience rules.' What should she do?"
options:
  - "Ask a second assistant to confirm; one answer may be wrong"
  - "Pick the 80-hour project; extra hours can be found in some weeks"
  - "Skip the program; she may not meet the age or experience rules"
  - "Use the answer; it matches the page and the sum of 60 hours"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Every claim in the answer can be matched against the page and her own numbers, and they match. A second assistant adds nothing the page does not already give."
  - "Her 5 hours a week is a fixed fact. The 80-hour project is a third more than her 60 hours, and the answer is right to rule it out."
  - "The page says the program is for people who are 18 or older and new to open source, and Aya is 19 and new. She meets the rules."
  - "Right. 5 times 12 is 60 hours. The 40-hour project fits, the 80-hour and 160-hour projects do not, and Aya meets the age and experience rules. This answer is correct, so no further check is needed."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Fares asks two assistants to write a short, friendly welcome message for his bootcamp study group chat. Both write almost the same three lines. Nothing in them is a rule, a date or a number. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Check each line on a primary page before he posts it"
  - "Edit the draft into his own voice, then post it"
  - "Ask a third assistant to settle which version is right"
  - "Hold the message until a mentor approves the wording"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "There is no primary page for a welcome message, and no fact in it to check. Checking pages is for rules, limits, dates, prices and licences."
  - "Right. The message is wording, so it can be kept as a draft. Fares reads it, changes it into his own voice and posts it, and he answers for every sentence."
  - "Wording has no right version to settle. A third assistant would give a third draft."
  - "A mentor's approval is not needed for a friendly message to a study group. Waiting costs time and checks nothing."
```
