```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Faisal, a developer at a delivery startup, wants to adopt a queue library called Safha (made up). He asks an assistant: 'Does Safha keep messages when the server restarts?' It answers: 'Yes. Safha writes every message to disk, so a restart loses nothing. You can adopt it now.' His docs page for version 4.1 is dated this month. It says messages stay in memory unless the persist setting is on, and that setting is off by default. What is the right next step?"
options:
  - "Adopt it now, since the assistant gave a clear yes and a clear reason for it"
  - "Ask the assistant the same question twice more, and adopt it if two answers agree"
  - "Trust the dated docs, then test persist in a tiny lab with a deliberate restart"
  - "Adopt it with persist left off, because the docs and the assistant are both partly right"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A clear yes is not a checked yes. The docs, which are the tool's own and dated this month, say messages stay in memory by default."
  - "Repeated answers from one assistant can repeat the same mistake. The docs page is the source to check against, not another answer."
  - "Right. The docs are versioned, dated and the tool's own, and they conflict with the assistant. A tiny lab with a restart on purpose shows what persist really does before it goes into the project."
  - "With persist off, a restart loses the messages. So the assistant's yes is wrong for the default, and leaving it off keeps exactly the risk the docs describe."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Yazan's study group has room to show one project at a demo day. Four candidates. Project A: no users, a README that says only 'built with AI', no log. Project B: 3 classmates use it, the README lists 2 trade-offs and 1 failure, and there are 6 weekly log entries. Project C: no users, a README copied from the tutorial, 40 commits made in one hour. Project D: 12 club members use it, no README, 2 log entries. Which project gives the group the most to check about how its builder thinks?"
options:
  - "Project B, because it has users, written trade-offs and a log a reader can follow"
  - "Project A, because a finished app made with AI shows the most skill to anyone who reads it"
  - "Project C, because 40 commits are the most work any of the four projects shows"
  - "Project D, because 12 members is the most real use any of the four projects has"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It has the three things a reader can check: real users, choices written with their costs, and a log that includes a failure. Nothing here promises a job. It gives a reader evidence."
  - "Its README says only 'built with AI', and it has no log and no users. A reader sees that a tool ran, not what the builder chose or can explain."
  - "40 commits in one hour with a copied README show a burst of activity, not a decision. The count of commits is the weakest evidence on the list."
  - "Twelve users is real and worth keeping. But with no README and 2 log entries, a reader cannot see why the builder decided anything."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hanan has 12 hours a week, needs no stipend and is new to open source. She wants to read about one team project first. Four facts: 1) A paid program asks for 30 hours a week for 13 weeks. 2) A paid program's project is 175 hours over the usual 12 weeks. 3) A community project she found has a licence, a commit last week, nine contributors and answered issues. 4) Anyone may start there with a small change, and there is no application. Which should she read about first?"
options:
  - "The 30-hour paid program, because working full time shows the most to a reader"
  - "The 175-hour paid project, because its 12 weeks work out to about 15 hours a week"
  - "The community project, but only after a paid program has first approved her plan"
  - "The community project, since it has no application and she sets her own pace"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Fact 1 says 30 hours a week. She has 12, so it does not fit, and she does not need the stipend it pays."
  - "175 divided by 12 is about 14.6 hours a week, which is more than the 12 she has. It also pays a stipend she does not need."
  - "Fact 4 says there is no application. A community project needs no one's approval, so she does not have to wait for a program."
  - "Right. She sets her own pace there, which fits her 12 hours, and she needs no stipend. Fact 3 shows the project is healthy, and fact 4 says she can start with a small change. Its contributing page is its rules page."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Yazan asks an assistant to summarise GitLab's handbook page on communication. It writes: 'GitLab proves that written, asynchronous updates make every team faster, so your team should stop all meetings.' The page says GitLab uses asynchronous communication as a starting point, works through public issues and merge requests, and writes down what offline conversations decide. What is the mistake in the summary?"
options:
  - "It is wrong, because GitLab does not use written updates at all"
  - "It turns one company's stated practice into proof that the practice works everywhere"
  - "It is right, because a large company would not keep a practice that failed"
  - "Only 'every' is wrong, because a company page proves the practice works for most teams"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The page says the opposite: GitLab does work through written issues and merge requests. The summary gets that part right."
  - "Right. The page describes how one company works. It does not test whether that way is faster, and it says nothing about stopping all meetings. A practice is something to try and measure, not proof."
  - "A page that describes a practice does not evaluate it. Many practices continue for reasons other than being the fastest."
  - "Changing one word does not fix the problem. A description of one company's practice proves nothing about most teams either."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Faisal wants to plan his exam fees around a program's stipend. The program's 2026 stipend table lists his place. Its page also says a country missing from the table cannot be paid, and that proof of residency and tax forms are needed. He asks an assistant: 'Will I receive the stipend?' It answers: 'Yes, your place is in the table, so the money will arrive.' Which next step is right?"
options:
  - "Plan the fees now, since being in the table means the program will pay him"
  - "Skip the program, because the forms and residency proof make payment impossible"
  - "Ask the program in writing how a participant in his place is paid, and then decide"
  - "Ask the assistant to check the payment rules for his place, then plan the fees"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The table shows amounts. It does not say how the money reaches a person in a given place, and the forms and proof are still needed."
  - "Forms and proof are requirements to meet, not a statement that payment is impossible. The page does not say that."
  - "Right. The page does not answer how payment reaches him, and an assistant cannot know it. A written answer from the program, with its date, is what he can plan around."
  - "The assistant just gave a confident yes without a source. Asking it again does not give the program's own answer."
```
