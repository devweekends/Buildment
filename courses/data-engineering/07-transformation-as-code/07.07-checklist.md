## Learning log

## Your task: one test that protects you, and one problem it misses

A test is a promise that one rule holds. This task asks you to look at one of your own tests and say two things about it. The first is what it protects you from. The second is what it cannot see. Both halves matter. A person who knows only the first half trusts a green build too much, and a person who knows only the second half stops writing tests.

Choose one test from your Mizan dbt project, for example `unique` on `report_id`, `relationships` from the fact table to `dim_market`, the positive price test or the unit test. Write four parts. First, name the test and the rule it holds, in one sentence. Second, describe a real problem it protects against, and say what you would see if the problem happened, such as the count in a failure message. Third, describe one problem that this test would miss, with an example from your own data, and say why no written rule would flag it. Fourth, say what check you would add, and where it would live: a contract, a data test, a unit test or a pytest test.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional student. Nabil studies data engineering in the evenings and works in a pharmacy during the day. He picks the `relationships` test from `fct_price_report` to `dim_market`. The rule is that every fact points at a market version that exists. It protects against a report whose market is missing, which would otherwise fall out of every weekly basket without any warning. If it happened, the build would show a failing test with a row count.

For the missed problem, Nabil uses chapter 7.4. If market M03 changes twice between two snapshot runs, the reports of the lost day join the earlier version. Every one of those facts has a valid market key, so the test passes, yet the reports are filed under the wrong area. He says that no rule he has written describes "the right version", only "a version exists". He would add a daily snapshot schedule. He would also add a pytest test. For each market with a recent `updated_at`, it checks that the snapshot has a row starting at that time.

Look at what makes Nabil's answer strong. The rule is stated in one sentence. The protected problem comes with something observable. The missed problem is concrete, comes from his own project, and he explains why the test is blind to it. The added check has a type and a place.

Compare it with a weak answer: "I use unique tests because they are important and they stop bugs. A thing it would miss is a bug in the data." This answer names no rule. It gives no example and no number. It says "a bug in the data" without saying which bug, so the mentor cannot tell whether the writer ran anything. It also adds no check at all.

If you do not have a finished project, answer for Layla's case. Layla is a freelancer who builds an order report for an online shop that sells handmade soap. The model `fct_orders` has these tests: `order_id` is unique and not null, `customer_key` is not null and exists in `dim_customer`, and `status` is one of `paid`, `refunded` or `cancelled`. In March the shop had 412 orders. Layla's report says revenue was 9,830 dollars, and the shop owner reads 9,310 dollars on the payment screen. All tests pass. Choose one of her tests, say what it protects, and explain the gap of 520 dollars.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer with the table below. There is no single right test. Any test from your project can pass, as long as you can say what it holds and what it misses.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The test and its rule | Names a real test and states its rule in one sentence | Names a test but the rule is vague, such as "it checks quality" | No test is named, or the test is not part of the project |
| The problem it protects against | Describes a real problem and what you would see when it happens | Describes a problem but nothing observable | No problem, or only "bugs" |
| The problem it would miss | A concrete example from the data, and a reason why no written rule flags it | An example without the reason, or a reason without an example | No example, or an example that the test does catch |
| The check you would add | A named kind of check and where it lives | A kind of check without a place, or the other way round | No new check |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three of the four parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "What would the failing output of that test look like?" or "Would your added check run before or after the model is built?" Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra. Your reply to the mentor is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
