## Your task: the incident note for a late batch

An incident note is a short written record of something that went wrong with a number that people rely on. It is not a confession and it does not look for someone to blame. Its job is to let a reader who was not there understand what happened, what it cost, why it happened and what changed. Data engineers write these notes after a late batch, a failed run or a wrong report, and a clear note is a strong thing to show in an interview.

Write the note in five parts. First, what happened, in a few sentences with dates and times. Second, the impact, with numbers: what the readers saw, and how far it was from the truth. Third, the cause, explained as a chain, using the words event time, processing time and window where they fit. Fourth, the fix and how you checked it. Give the exact command or change, the date range and the reason for its start date, and say which total you compared before and after. Fifth, what changes so that it does not repeat: a setting, a check or a message to the readers, with the cost of that choice.

You may write about the M05 outage as it would have happened with a lookback of 0, using the numbers from your own run of the pipeline. Or you may write about Fares's clinic bookings, which is the provided case below. Anyone without a finished pipeline should use the provided case.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional student. Layla writes about the M05 outage. She says that on 12 March, 35 reports dated 9 to 11 March reached the lake, and that the fact table did not take them in. The weekly report for M05 showed one full day of data instead of three. Her cause is a chain. The reports have an event time of 9 to 11 March and a processing time of 12 March. The run read a window of one day, so 9 to 11 March fell outside.

For the fix, Layla runs `python scripts/backfill.py --start 2026-03-12 --end 2026-03-12`, and she explains that dbt then starts three days earlier, on 9 March. She compares the fact count before and after, and says it rose by the number of good M05 reports. For prevention she sets the lookback to 3, which matches the longest delay in her data, and she names the cost: each run reads four days and not one. She also adds a message to Omar, saying that numbers for the last three days can still change.

Look at what makes her note strong. It has dates, counts and a command. The cause is a chain and not a label. The fix explains why the range starts early, and the prevention names its cost. Nothing in it blames the volunteer or the phone.

Compare it with a weak note: "There was a problem with the data and it was late. We fixed it. It should not happen again." There is no date and no number. The cause is just the word late. The fix is not described, so nobody can repeat it, and "it should not happen again" is a hope and not a change.

The provided case is this. Fares manages a clinic. The front desk types each visit into the booking system. On Monday and Tuesday the desk computer was offline, so the staff wrote visits on paper, 46 on Monday and 41 on Tuesday, and typed them on Wednesday. Fares's pipeline runs every night and reads the last 1 day of visit dates. On Thursday the weekly report shows no visits at all for Monday, and Fares asks why.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your note with the table below. There is no single right incident. What matters is that a reader who was not there can follow it.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| What happened and the impact | Dates and at least two numbers, such as how many records and how far the report was wrong | Dates or numbers, but not both | A general statement with no dates or numbers |
| The cause | A chain that uses event time, processing time and the window, and says why the data was missed | One of the three terms, or a label such as "it was late" | No cause, or a cause that blames a person |
| The fix and its check | The exact command or change, why the range starts where it does, and which total was compared | A command without the reason for the range, or no check | No fix, or a fix that cannot be repeated |
| Prevention | One specific change with its cost, and a message to the readers | A change without its cost, or only a message | "It should not happen again" |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three of the four parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Why does your backfill start before the upload date?" or "What would your prevention cost if the data were 100 times bigger?" Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra. Your reply to the mentor is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
