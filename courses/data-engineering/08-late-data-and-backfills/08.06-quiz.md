```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ola's gym loads check-ins every night. Of 700 check-ins, 640 arrive on the day, 40 arrive 1 day late, 15 arrive 2 days late and 5 arrive 3 days late. Each run re-reads the last N days. The gym wants a lookback that catches at least 95 percent of the 60 late check-ins and reads as few days as it can. Which N fits?"
options:
  - "1 day, which catches 40 of the 60 late check-ins"
  - "3 days, which catches all 60 late check-ins"
  - "2 days, which catches 55 of the 60, about 92 percent"
  - "0 days, which catches the 640 on-time check-ins"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A lookback of 1 day catches only the 40 check-ins that are 1 day late. That is 67 percent of the 60, which is below the 95 percent target."
  - "Right. At 2 days the share is 55 of 60, about 92 percent, which is below 95. At 3 days it is 60 of 60. The smallest N that reaches 95 percent is 3."
  - "At 2 days the lookback catches 40 plus 15, which is 55 of the 60. That is about 92 percent, just under the 95 percent target."
  - "A lookback of 0 reads only the newest day. The 640 on-time check-ins are not late, and none of the 60 late ones is caught."
```

```quiz
  - "The whole row, because two identical lines are duplicates"

  - "H4 had two real deliveries that day, w-1 and w-2. This key keeps one line for H4 and the total falls to 300 litres, so a real delivery is lost."
  - "All four lines have different sent times, so none is removed and the total is 700 litres. A sent time describes the delivery of a line and not the delivery of water."
  - "Right. The phone keeps delivery_id on a resend, so w-1 is kept once. The second delivery to H4 has its own id, w-2. The total is 200 plus 200 plus 100, which is 500 litres."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mustafa appends each day's file to his fact table. He fixes a bug in the source and wants to reload 3, 4 and 5 March, which are already in the table. An AI tool says: 'Go ahead and rerun those days. Appending is fine here, because each day is loaded only once.' What is wrong with the answer?"
options:
  - "Nothing is wrong, because a rerun loads each day one time"
  - "Appending fails when a day already exists, so the rerun stops"
  - "The three days are added again, so their totals are doubled"
  - "Appending is only a problem when the files get a new field"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A rerun loads each of those days a second time. They were loaded before, so 'only once' is not true for these days."
  - "An append does not check for existing rows, so it does not fail. It adds the rows again and nothing reports the problem."
  - "Right. An append adds rows and never replaces them. The rerun puts a second copy of each of the three days into the table, and every total for those days is doubled."
  - "The problem here is the rerun and not the fields. Appending the same days again doubles them, with or without a new field."
```

```quiz
  - "A lands; B lands, the bad row quarantined; C is stopped"

  - "All three land, and the row with the empty date is dropped"
  - "A lands, and B is stopped because one bad row spoils it"

  - "Right. Extra fields are allowed, so A lands. In B, only the row that breaks the rule goes to quarantine, with its reason. C lacks a required field, so the whole batch is stopped and nothing is written."
  - "The contract allows extra fields, so A is not stopped. A row that breaks a rule goes to quarantine and does not stop its batch. Only C, which lacks a required field, is stopped."
  - "C has no booking_id, a required field, so it cannot land. A bad row is not dropped silently either: it goes to quarantine with its reason."
  - "A single bad row does not stop a batch. Only a missing required field does that. The good rows of B land, and the bad row is kept in quarantine."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Layla's fact table reads a lookback of 2 days. A bug in a model is fixed, and she must rebuild the facts for the upload dates 10 to 12 March. A report uploaded on 10 March can be dated up to 2 days earlier. From which report date should dbt start its range?"
options:
  - "10 March, because that is the first upload date"
  - "8 March, because a report from 10 March can be 2 days older"
  - "12 March, because the range must end on the last upload date"
  - "1 March, because a backfill always starts at the beginning"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Reports uploaded on 10 March may be dated 8 or 9 March. A range that starts on 10 March would not rebuild them."
  - "Right. The range of report dates starts at the first upload date minus the lookback, which is 8 March, and it ends on the last upload date, 12 March."
  - "12 March is where the range ends. Starting there would rebuild only one day and skip the older reports that arrived on 10, 11 and 12 March."
  - "A backfill rebuilds only the range that was wrong. Starting at the beginning does more work, and it is not what the fix needs."
```
