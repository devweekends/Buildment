## Your data protection note

A data protection note is a short page that answers questions before anyone has to ask them. It says what personal data a pipeline holds, why it holds it, where it lives, who can read it, how it is protected and how long it is kept. Writing it helps you more than it helps a reader, because it forces you to find the column, the export or the backup that you had forgotten. It is also something you can show to a client, and it proves that you think about the people in the rows.

Write the note for your own Mizan pipeline, in your own words, in about 250 to 400 words. Cover five parts. First, the purpose and the personal data: which columns, and why the report needs them or does not. Second, where the data lives and who may read each place, using your zones. Third, the protection: how the phone number becomes a code, where the secret key is kept, and what the code still does not hide. Fourth, retention: how long each kind of data is kept, how it is deleted, and which copies the job does not reach. Fifth, the mistake: what you do if a file with personal data reaches the wrong folder. Your note describes what your pipeline does. It does not claim to meet any law.

If you prefer not to use your own pipeline, or it is not finished, use Lubna's case. Lubna coordinates a small fictional relief kitchen network. Families register for weekly food parcels on a phone form. Each form holds five things: the name of the head of the household, a phone number, the district, the number of adults and children, and a yes or no for a special diet. The diet question is asked for a medical reason. In the first month, about 600 families registered. Lubna wants two outputs: a weekly count of parcels per district for donors, and a list for the drivers. Write the note as Lubna's data engineer. Health information needs particular care, so say who may see that field and why, and say that a real project would ask a data protection adviser.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional student called Mais who writes the note for Mizan. She names two personal columns, the volunteer's name and phone, and says the weekly report needs neither. She says the best fix is to ask Karam to stop sending them, and until then they stay in the restricted zone. She lists the zones and the readers: only Hala reads restricted and quarantine, and the shared zone and warehouse hold codes. She explains that the code is a keyed hash. The key lives in an environment variable and never in the project. The shared data is still about people. She gives the periods: 30 days for restricted and quarantine. She says the job does not reach the raw upload files, a spreadsheet export or a backup, so she lists those with an owner each. Her last paragraph says: remove the file, find who opened it, tell Yasmin the same day.

Here is a weak answer, for a fictional student called Yahya: "Mizan takes privacy seriously. All phone numbers are hashed, so they are anonymous. We keep data only as long as needed, and only authorised people can access it." Nothing in it can be checked. It names no column, no zone and no period. It repeats the mistake the module taught against, because a hash of a phone number is not anonymous. And it says nothing about the day something goes wrong.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your note with the table below. There is no single right design. A note that keeps data for 14 days can pass as easily as one that keeps it for 60, if it explains the period. What matters is that each part names something real that a reader could check.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Purpose and columns | Names each personal column and says why it is needed or why it could go | Names the columns but gives no reason | No columns named, or "personal data" only |
| Zones and readers | Names each place the data lives and who may read it | Names the places but not who | One place only, or "authorised people" |
| Protection | Says how the code is made, where the key is kept, and that the code is still personal data | Says "hashed" or "pseudonymised" with no key, or no limit | Says the data is anonymous because of a hash |
| Retention and copies | Gives a period per kind of data and names at least one copy the job does not reach | Gives a period but forgets copies | No period |
| When it goes wrong | Gives the first three steps after a file reaches the wrong place | Gives one step | Nothing |

**Pass rule:** your note passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three of the five parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Where is the key stored, and who can read that place?" or "What happens to the spreadsheet you exported last week?" Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra. Your reply to the mentor is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
