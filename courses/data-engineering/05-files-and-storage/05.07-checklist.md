## Your partitioning note

A partitioning choice is easy to state and hard to defend. Anyone can say "partition by day". The useful note says which queries the choice serves. It gives the number of files and the rows in each. It says what the choice gives up, and how a night's job writes without doubling the data. This task asks you to write that note with numbers, in plain English.

You can use your own Mizan lake or the provided case. For your own lake, use the experiments from chapter 5.3. Use the number of files and the average rows per file for the layouts you tried. Add the `Scanning Files` line for the week from 2 to 8 March. For the provided case, use Wael's data below. Either way, write about one layout you recommend and one you reject.

Plan about forty minutes for the note, and write it in two passes. In the first pass, put down the numbers only: the files, the rows per file and the files opened for two layouts. In the second pass, add the sentences that explain them. If the power goes while you write, the numbers are already saved, and the sentences are the easier part to finish later. A short note with real numbers passes more easily than a long note without any.

Here is the provided case. Wael runs a small shop that sells solar panels and batteries. He logs a reading from each of 6 customer sites every 15 minutes: the site, the time, the battery level and the panel output. That is 96 readings a day for one site, so 576 rows a day for all six, and 210,240 rows in a year. Two queries matter. A customer asks for the last 7 days of one site. Wael adds up each month for all six sites. A nightly job adds the day's readings, and a power cut sometimes makes it run twice.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional freelancer. Duaa keeps the orders of an online shop: about 300 orders a day and 109,500 rows in a year. Her queries are a monthly report by product category and now and then one day's orders. She compares three layouts. A folder per day gives 365 files of 300 rows. A folder per month gives 12 files of about 9,000 rows. No partitions gives one file. She recommends a folder per month. The monthly report opens one file of 12, and a lookup of one day reads that month's 9,125 rows or so, which is nothing. She rejects a folder per day because 300-row files are small and the report would open about 30 of them. She says what the choice gives up. The nightly job rewrites the month's file every night. That is fine at this size, and it would not be at a thousand times more data. She says the job writes a temporary file and swaps it in, so a second run leaves the same rows. Last, she checks the claim with the plan, which says `Scanning Files: 1/12`.

Look at what makes her answer strong. She names the queries, so the layout has a reason. She gives three numbers for each layout: files, rows per file and files opened. She says what she gives up and when the choice would stop working. She says how a rerun stays safe. And she checked one claim with a command, so the note is not a guess.

A weak answer is "I would partition by day, because detailed is better." It names no query, so nothing can be right or wrong about it. It has no number, so nobody can compare it with another layout. It does not say that more detail also means more, smaller files, and it says nothing about a rerun. A mentor cannot tell whether the writer understood the module or only picked a popular answer.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your note with the table below. There is no single right layout. A folder per month, a folder per day and a different choice can all pass if the numbers and the reasons hold together. A choice with no numbers cannot pass.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The queries | Names at least two queries and ties the layout to them | Names one query, or names them without tying them to the layout | No query named |
| The numbers | Files, average rows per file and files opened, for the recommended layout and one other | Some of the three numbers, or numbers for one layout only | No numbers, or numbers that do not add up |
| What it gives up | States a cost of the choice and when it would stop working | States a cost with no limit, or only a benefit | Only benefits |
| A safe rerun | Says how the nightly job avoids doubling a day | Mentions reruns without saying how | Not mentioned |
| A check | Reports a plan line, a count or a file listing that supports one claim | Says how it could be checked | No check |

**Pass rule:** your note passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least four of the five parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example, the mentor may ask: "What happens to your layout if Wael adds 60 more sites?" Or: "Which of your numbers would change if the readings came every minute?" Write your answer in plain English. Short sentences are fine. You may add a voice note link as an extra. Your reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
