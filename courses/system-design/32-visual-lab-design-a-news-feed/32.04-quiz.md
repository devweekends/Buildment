```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A design has a cache at a 95% hit rate and a database with no read-only copy (read replica). At 3,200 feed opens a second the board shows it passing every check in the peak scene and failing both checks in the cache scene. What explains the difference?"
options:
  - "The cache is too small and drops data when it gets busy"
  - "The load balancer is the busiest part in the cache scene"
  - "Without a cache, the database sees all 3,200"
  - "The cache scene removes the app servers as well"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The cache's size is not the problem here. In the peak scene it works and the database is almost idle. The failure appears only when the cache is gone."
  - "The load balancer is not behind the cache. Its load is 3,200 requests a second in both scenes, so it cannot be what changed."
  - "Right. At a 95% hit rate the database sees about 160 requests a second. When the cache dies it sees all 3,200, which is more than one database without read-only copies can serve."
  - "The scene only takes the cache down. The app servers carry the same load as before, so they are not what fails."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "About 1 feed open in 50 is really a post, and 3,200 requests a second arrive. Each worker handles 2,400 jobs a second. How much work reaches the queue, and how busy is one worker?"
options:
  - "All 3,200 a second, so one worker is 133% busy and falls behind"
  - "About 64 a second, so at least three workers are needed"
  - "About 64 a second, so one worker is only 3% busy"
  - "About 160 a second, the same as the cache misses"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Only the posts leave a job on the queue, not every feed open. Chapter 21.1 has 10,000,000 posts against 500,000,000 feed opens a day, which is 1 in 50."
  - "64 jobs a second is a small fraction of what one worker handles. Even after a 2 minute outage, the 7,680 waiting posts are delivered in about 4 seconds, so a second worker only adds cost."
  - "Right. 3,200 divided by 50 is 64 jobs a second, and 64 out of 2,400 is about 3%. That is why one small worker is enough on the board, and why a post from an account with millions of followers, which is far more work than one job, is what the board cannot show."
  - "The 160 a second is the cache misses that reach the database (5% of 3,200). Those are reads, and they do not go through the queue."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A team removes the queue and the worker. Each feed is now built from the database when it is opened, and nothing is written into feeds when a post is saved. Which checks does the board fail for this change?"
options:
  - "None, because feeds still open and posts are still saved"
  - "Saving a post and opening a feed, because they need a queue"
  - "Delivery to followers' feeds and the worker check"
  - "Only cost, because the queue was the cheapest part"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Saving and opening still work, because the app server can read the database directly. But two checks need the queue and the worker, and they fail."
  - "Saving needs an app server and a database, and opening needs the app server to read posts. Neither needs a queue, so those two checks still pass."
  - "Right. The delivery check needs the chain app server, queue, worker, cache, and the worker check needs a queue to hold posts while the worker is away. Both fail. This is fan-out on read, and it works until the reads add up."
  - "Removing the queue makes the design cheaper, not dearer. The failures are about what the design can no longer do."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "The board gives every post the same weight. Which real problem of a home feed does that hide?"
options:
  - "One post from a huge account is millions of writes"
  - "A post from a new account is too small to be saved"
  - "The cache cannot hold more than one post for each follower"
  - "Posts from different accounts cannot share one queue"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. One post from an account with 20 million followers is 20 million feed writes, which holds the workers for minutes while everyone else's posts wait. The board has no per-account fan-out, so it cannot show this. Chapter 21.3 handles it by pulling the few huge accounts."
  - "Every post is saved the same way, whatever the account. Size of the account is not the problem."
  - "A feed holds the ids of many posts. The cache's room is a matter of its size, which the board does model."
  - "Posts from every account can share one queue. The trouble is how much work one post can be, not where it waits."
```
