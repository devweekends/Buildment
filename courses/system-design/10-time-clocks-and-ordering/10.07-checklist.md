## Clock uncertainty checklist

- [ ] I can draw a lower and an upper bound around a clock reading.
- [ ] I can explain why overlapping ranges do not prove event order.
- [ ] I can show when a server may safely acknowledge a commit after choosing a timestamp.
- [ ] I can describe the latency cost and a case that does not need this guarantee.

## Learning log

1. In Alice's privacy and photo example, what visible mistake could happen if the photo receives an earlier timestamp?
2. If a clock reading is 20 ms with 4 ms uncertainty, what range does it report, and what must be true before a server can safely say its chosen timestamp is in the past?
3. Which part of the guarantee comes from waiting, and which part comes from the two actions starting in a real observed order?

## Your timeline

Draw two transactions on separate servers. Mark the moment the first transaction chooses its timestamp, the end of its commit wait, the reply to Alice, and the start of the second transaction. Give each server a clock range. Explain why the second timestamp must follow the first after the reply. Then name one cost of the wait and one application that may accept a weaker ordering promise.
