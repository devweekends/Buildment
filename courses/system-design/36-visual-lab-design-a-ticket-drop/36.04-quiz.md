```quiz
type: mcq
question: "At peak 8,000 requests a second reach a ticket service. Its waiting room has a limit of 3,500, and its load balancer, app servers and workers are sized for about that. A teammate raises the limit to 8,000 to be fair to everyone. What happens?"
options:
  - "Nothing changes, because the waiting room still protects them"
  - "The waiting room turns away more people than before"
  - "The parts behind it see all 8,000 and fill up"
  - "Only the database is affected, because it holds the seats"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The protection is the limit itself. A limit equal to the crowd is the same as having no waiting room, and every part behind it is overloaded."
  - "A higher limit turns away fewer people, not more. That is why the parts behind it fill up."
  - "Right. A waiting room only turns away what is above its limit. At 8,000 it turns away nothing, so the load balancer is 167% busy and p99 passes 1,400 ms."
  - "Every part on the path sees the extra load. The load balancer and the app servers fill first, long before the database does."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A payment queue has 210,000 payments waiting after a 60 second outage. Payments arrive at 3,500 a second and each worker handles 2,400 a second. With two workers, how long do the waiting payments take to clear?"
options:
  - "About 57 seconds, which is well inside the 120 allowed"
  - "About 100 seconds, which is just inside the 120 allowed"
  - "They never clear, because 3,500 is more than 2,400"
  - "About 162 seconds, too slow for the 120 allowed"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "That is the answer for three workers, which have 3,700 a second to spare. With two, only 1,300 a second are spare."
  - "That mixes in the numbers of another design. Divide the backlog by the spare capacity: 210,000 divided by 1,300."
  - "One worker would never catch up, since 2,400 is below 3,500. Two workers together handle 4,800, so they do catch up, slowly."
  - "Right. Two workers handle 4,800 a second. After 3,500 for the new payments, 1,300 a second are spare, and 210,000 divided by 1,300 is about 162 seconds."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A seat-map cache answers 90% of 3,500 views a second. The database has one read-only copy (read replica) and passes every check at peak. In the cache scene it fails. What explains the difference?"
options:
  - "The cache scene also takes away the app servers"
  - "The database now sees all 3,500 views"
  - "The waiting room lets in more people in that scene"
  - "The load balancer is the busiest part in that scene"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The scene only takes the cache down. The app servers carry the same load as before, so they are not what changed."
  - "Right. At a 90% hit rate the database sees about 350 views a second. When the cache dies it sees all 3,500, and one read-only copy is not enough: the database is 118% busy."
  - "The waiting room's limit is fixed. It lets in the same 3,500 in every scene, so it cannot explain the failure."
  - "The load balancer is not behind the cache, and its load is the same in both scenes. The database is the part that changed."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A design passes all eleven checks on the board, with room to spare on cost. A reviewer says it may still sell the same seat to two fans. Is the reviewer right?"
options:
  - "Yes, the board never looks at which seat is asked"
  - "No, because a design that passes every check is correct"
  - "Yes, but only if the cache has a low hit rate"
  - "No, because the database always allows one winner"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The board counts requests and capacity. Two fans asking for seat 7 look like two requests. Preventing a double sale needs a conditional update on the seat row (chapter 25.2)."
  - "The checks measure capacity, speed, survival and cost. None of them tests whether two buyers can win the same seat, so a green board is necessary and not enough."
  - "The hit rate decides how many views reach the database. It has nothing to do with who wins a seat, which is decided when the hold is written."
  - "A database lets two writers through if the check and the write are two separate steps. It allows one winner only when the check is part of the write."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tazkara could size every part for the full 8,000 requests a second and have no waiting room. On the board this passes ten of eleven checks. Which check does it fail?"
options:
  - "Capacity, because 8,000 is more than the board allows"
  - "Availability, because bigger parts fail more often"
  - "Cost, about $5.75 an hour against the $2.50 budget"
  - "The payment path, because there is no queue"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Capacity is fine: the design serves well over 3,000 a second. What fails is the price of getting there."
  - "Size does not lower availability. The board computes it from how many parts run and in how many zones."
  - "Right. Parts sized for 8,000 pass the speed and survival checks and cost about $5.75 an hour. The queue alone costs about $1.16 an hour at 8,000 messages a second."
  - "The design still has its queue and workers, so the payment path is intact. The failing check is cost."
```
