At the start of this module you made a guess about the wait when the cache is switched off. Since then you ran a real system, stopped its parts one at a time and compared your numbers with measured ones. This chapter closes the loop. You do not need new facts. You need to say, in your own words, what happened and why.

## Look back at your guess

<MermaidDiagram title="Guess, measure, explain" chart={`flowchart LR
  G["Your guess<br>in chapter 31.1"] --> M["Your number<br>from your run"]
  M --> E["Your reason<br>in your words"]`} />

Go back to chapter 31.1 and read your guess. Then open your own result for the cache task. Was your guess close? If it was far off, that is the most useful thing you found.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why being wrong is useful">

A guess that was wrong tells you which picture in your head does not match the real system. Maybe you thought a database read was nearly as fast as a cache hit, or that losing a part always shows as an error. Name the picture you had, and the one you have now. That change is what you take to your next design, and it is also what an interviewer is looking for when they ask what you learned from a failure.

</GoDeeper>

## Say it in your own words

<MermaidDiagram title="One number, one reason" chart={`flowchart LR
  N["One number<br>from your run"] --> R["The reason<br>behind it"]`} />

Write about 100 words. Use plain words, one number from your own run, and the reason behind it. Avoid copying the author's sentences. If you cannot explain it without them, go back to the task and run it again.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: a good answer, and a weak one">

A weak answer repeats the result: "the cache made it faster." A good answer says by how much, on what, and why: "with the cache off, my typical redirect went from 4 ms to 9 ms, because every request had to parse a query and read a row in the database instead of looking up a key in memory. The slowest 1 in 100 went up even more, because the database queued requests when many arrived at once." It names the number, the cause and the effect on the slowest requests. It would let a teammate who had not run the lab understand what happened.

</GoDeeper>
