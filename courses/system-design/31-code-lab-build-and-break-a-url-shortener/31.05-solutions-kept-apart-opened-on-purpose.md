You have tried the tasks, and you have compared your numbers with the real ones. Some of you are stuck on a task, and some want to check your reasoning. This short chapter says where the worked solutions are, and how to use them without losing what the tasks are for.

## Where they live

<MermaidDiagram title="Tasks here, solutions in the repository" chart={`flowchart LR
  C["This course<br>directions, no answers"] --> R["Repository<br>solutions/ folder"]`} />

The solutions are not in this course, on purpose. They live only in the repository, in the `solutions/` folder next to the tasks, in github.com/ZeeshanAdilButt/system-design-labs under `labs/lab-1-url-shortener`.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why they are kept apart">

If the answer is on the same page as the question, most people read it first and tell themselves they would have found it. The effort of being wrong, then finding out why, is where the learning is. So the tasks give directions only: what to change, what to predict, what to read. The solutions sit one click away in the repository, where opening one is a decision you make. They also stay next to the code they explain, so when the code changes, the solution changes with it, and the course does not go out of date.

</GoDeeper>

## How to use one

<MermaidDiagram title="Try, compare, then read" chart={`flowchart LR
  T["Try the task"] --> G["Write your result and your reason"]
  G --> S["Open the solution"]
  S --> D["Note what differs and why"]`} />

Try the task first and write down your result and your reason in a sentence. Then open the solution and compare. Do not ask "did I get it right?" Ask "where does my reason differ from theirs, and which one fits the numbers?"

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: if you are stuck for a long time">

Being stuck for ten minutes is normal. Being stuck for an hour means the task needs a hint, not the answer. Open the solution, read only the first lines (the direction it takes), close it and try again. If you needed the whole solution, that is fine. Do the task again from a reset, in your own words, without looking, and then write one sentence on what you were missing. That sentence is what you will use in the reflect chapter.

</GoDeeper>
