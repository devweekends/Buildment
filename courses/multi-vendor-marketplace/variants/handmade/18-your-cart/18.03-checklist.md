# Cart page gate

A cart page can survive a click-through on a fast laptop and still fail the buyer it was built for. The failures this gate looks for are quiet: a subtotal that includes a piece that just sold, a badge that says two while three rows are on screen, a remove button that fires twice on a slow connection, and a page that shows an empty cart when the server is simply unreachable. None of them appear when you tap through the happy path once.

Build the fixture before you open the page. One buyer, Lina. Two workshops, with Bayt al Zayt holding a stitched cushion cover at 2400 minor units and a glazed bowl at 1800, and the second workshop holding a carved olive wood tray at 3100. Bayt al Zayt ships to Lina for 500 with handling of 2 to 3 days and transit of 7 to 14 days. The second workshop serves her country too, at 700. Write those numbers down with the cart ID, the three piece IDs and both workshop IDs. Every check below compares the screen with that fixture and with the raw `GET /cart` response.

## The page at 360 pixels

Open the cart at 360 by 740. Two workshop headings, three rows, no horizontal scroll, and a remove control reachable with a thumb. Read each group subtotal aloud and compare it with the API: 4200 for Bayt al Zayt before shipping, 3100 for the second workshop. The page total should be 4200 plus 3100 plus 500 plus 700, which is 8500, and the same figure must appear in the API response.

Then use the keyboard alone. Reach the first remove control, open the confirmation, read the piece title inside it, and cancel. No request may leave the browser on cancel. Reach the last row the same way and check that the focus ring is visible in both themes. A control that only responds to hover has already failed on a phone.

Check the header count against the rows. Lina carries three pieces, so the badge says three. Remove one and the badge must say two without a full page reload. Reload once and confirm the number came from the server rather than from a number kept in memory.

## Remove, and remove only once

Throttle the network to 3G. Tap the remove control on the tray twice in quick succession. The API log must show one `DELETE /cart/lines/:lineId`, the button must be disabled while the request is in flight, and the page total must drop to 4200 plus 500. Open the network panel and read the response: the server returns the cart after removal, and the page repaints from it.

Then remove a line in a second tab and press remove on the same line in the first tab. Whatever your route returns for an already removed line, the page must settle on the current cart and show a short sentence that the cart changed. It must not show a stack trace, a bare status code, or a total that includes the line that is gone.

## A piece that sells while the page is open

Leave Lina's cart open with all three pieces. In a second browser, sign in as Omar and complete checkout for the tray. Return to Lina's tab and trigger a read.

The tray row must stay on the page. It should carry the sold sentence, keep its photo and title, and drop out of both the subtotal and the total. The page total becomes 4200 plus 500 for the remaining workshop if that group can still ship. Nothing may disappear without a word, and nothing may keep a sold piece inside a number.

```quiz
  - Remove the line and lower the total without a word

  - Show the tray as ready and let checkout refuse it
correct: 1
explanations:
  - A silent removal looks like a bug and hides why the amount changed.
  - The row stays visible with its state, so she can remove it and keep shopping.
  - One sold piece does not invalidate pieces from another workshop.
  - The page would show a purchase path the server has already refused.
```

## A workshop that pauses shipping

Pause shipping for the second workshop through its shipping profile. Reload the cart. That group must stay on the page with a sentence saying the workshop has paused shipping, and its pieces must stay visible. The page total becomes unavailable, because a total that quietly drops a group tells Lina a number that checkout will reject.

Then set Lina's country to one the second workshop does not serve and repeat the read. The sentence changes to name the destination, the group stays visible, and the total stays unavailable. In both cases the path to checkout must be blocked for that group, with a link back to the collection.

## The two states that get confused

Empty the cart, then reload. The page says the cart is empty and offers a way back to the collection. Now stop the API, reload the page, and read it again. The page must say it could not reach the server and offer a retry. An empty cart and an unreachable cart look identical if both render zero rows, and one of those mistakes makes a buyer rebuild a cart that already exists.

## Gate checklist

- [ ] Two workshop groups render at 360 by 740 with no horizontal scroll and no truncated piece title.
- [ ] Each group subtotal matches the API value, and the page total equals the group subtotals plus both shipping amounts.
- [ ] The header badge counts pieces, matches the row count, and updates after a removal without a reload.
- [ ] No quantity input or increase and decrease control exists anywhere in the cart.
- [ ] A double tap on remove at 3G sends exactly one `DELETE`, and the button is disabled while it is in flight.
- [ ] Cancelling the removal dialog sends no request, and keyboard focus stays visible on both themes.
- [ ] A piece that sells in another browser keeps its row, shows the sold sentence, and leaves the subtotal.
- [ ] A group whose workshop paused shipping, or does not serve the buyer's country, stays visible with a sentence and an unavailable total.
- [ ] An empty cart and an unreachable server show two different, named states.
- [ ] `docs/cart-ui-decisions.md` holds the fixture IDs, the four row states, the shipping sentences, the badge rule and the two totals.

## Learning log

1. Which four row states did you implement, and what sentence does each one print? Include the state that a draft piece falls back to.
2. What changed in the `GET /cart` response between Module 17 and this module, and why does the page need a state per line rather than inferring it from a missing price?
3. In the double-tap test, which mechanism kept the second tap from sending a request, and what did the button do while the first was in flight?
4. When the tray sold under the open page, what did the total become, and which field in the response made the page repaint without a reload?
5. What does the page show when the API is down, and how does that differ from the empty cart state in words the buyer can act on?

## What this gate does not prove

The cart still holds nothing. Two buyers can carry the same available piece in two carts, and neither cart has a claim on it. This gate says nothing about a race between two checkouts, about a hold that expires, or about a payment that fails after a claim. Module 19 owns all of that, and it will read the same cart response as its starting point.

Leave the fixture in place. The next module reuses Lina's two workshops, the three piece IDs and both shipping amounts, and it asks a harder question of the same data: which of these pieces can still be claimed, and what does the loser of a claim see. If a check above fails, fix the state that caused it rather than the sentence on the screen. A page that reports change accurately is worth more than a page that never admits anything changed.
