# Track orders gate

This gate asks one question in several ways: does the page show what was recorded, or does it show something the software guessed. A pretty order card proves nothing on its own. Two accounts, one edited piece and one refused transition are what turn a page into evidence.

Prepare the fixture from Module 19 and Module 20. Lina has one paid checkout that became two orders: Bayt al Zayt with the stitched cushion cover at 2400 plus 500 shipping, total 2900, and the second workshop with the carved tray at 3100 plus 700 shipping, total 3800. Amina owns Bayt al Zayt and Kareem owns the second workshop. Record the two order IDs, both piece IDs and the checkout ID before you touch the screen.

## Both sides see the same sale

Sign in as Lina. Her order list shows two orders, each with its own workshop, total, handling and transit range, and one line per piece. Open the cover order and read the title, the craft, the maker credit and the price. All of them come from the snapshot, and the price is 2400 with 500 shipping, not a recomputed number.

Sign in as Amina. Her list shows the cover order and nothing from the second workshop. The second workshop's owner sees the reverse. Sign in as Kareem and open Amina's order by ID: the response must be the course's forbidden or not-found answer and must not include Lina's destination, her note or the cover's price. Then sign in as Lina and call the maker route: a buyer is not a maker.

Now change the world under the pages. Edit the cover's title to something unmistakable, change its price to 9900 and archive it. Reload both Lina's order and Amina's order. Neither page may move. The title, the price and the state sentence stay as they were at payment. Archive the workshop's public page as well and confirm the order still renders, because a purchase record outlives a listing.

## Packed, then shipped

As Amina, press Mark packed on the cover order. The response carries `packedAt` and status `packed`. Reload and read the same. Then press Ship with an empty reference. The server must refuse with a field message and leave the order at `packed`.

Fill the carrier as a local road courier with a name you invented, and the reference as a forty character string with a hyphen in the middle. The order should accept both exactly as typed, show them on the buyer page and on the maker page, and offer a copy control. Nothing in the app may claim the reference resolves anywhere, and nothing may reformat it.

```quiz
type: mcq
id: handmade-track-orders-gate-q1
question: A maker ships a parcel with a reference that no carrier site recognises. What should Ghurza do with it?
options:
  - Reject the reference until some carrier resolves it
  - Store the text and show it back exactly as typed
  - Generate its own reference and hide the typed one
  - Mark the order received when the buyer does not check
correct: 1
explanations:
  - The app has no carrier integration, so it cannot judge a reference.
  - The maker typed a real fact from a paper slip, and the buyer needs it.
  - A generated reference would describe a parcel nobody can trace.
  - Only the buyer confirms arrival, and the app cannot infer it.
```

## The buyer closes the order

As Lina, press Received on the packed order before Amina ships it. The server must answer 409 and the order must stay `packed`. Then have Amina ship it, reload Lina's page and press Received again. The status becomes `received`, the maker list shows no next action, and a second press changes nothing.

Run the two-tab test on the maker side. Open the maker list twice, press Pack in both, and record both responses. One wins with 200, the other returns 409 and repaints from the stored order. Repeat with two different tracking references on Ship. One reference is stored, and the losing tab shows the stored one rather than the text it typed.

## Gate checklist

- [ ] Lina's list shows both orders with their own workshop, total and transit range, and no pooled total across workshops.
- [ ] The cover order reads title, craft, maker credit and price from the snapshot after the piece was edited, repriced and archived.
- [ ] Amina's maker list shows only Bayt al Zayt orders, and Kareem gets a denial on Amina's order ID.
- [ ] A buyer token on the maker route is refused, and a credited artisan gets no order access.
- [ ] Ship with an empty reference returns a field error and leaves the order at `packed`.
- [ ] A carrier and reference survive a reload unchanged, with a working copy control at 360 pixels.
- [ ] Received before shipped returns 409, and Received after shipped moves the order once.
- [ ] Two tabs pressing Pack give one 200 and one 409, and the losing tab shows the stored status.
- [ ] The destination and buyer note appear on the maker page only from `paid` onwards.
- [ ] `docs/order-status.md` lists the statuses, who moves each one, the two shipment fields and the guard predicate.

## Learning log

1. Which fields on both order pages come from the snapshot, and which single field, if any, is read live from another record?
2. What exact predicate did the packed transition use, and what did the second tab receive when it lost the race?
3. What validation did you apply to the carrier and the tracking reference, and what did you deliberately refuse to validate?
4. Why can the buyer, and only the buyer, mark an order received? What would change if the maker could?
5. Which query scope protects the maker list, and which one protects the buyer detail route? Include the denial you tested for each.

## Week 3 demo

This gate is the Week 3 demo. Record one continuous screen capture with two browser profiles. Browse the collection, add a cover from Bayt al Zayt and a tray from the second workshop, open the cart and read both subtotals aloud, pay with a Stripe test card, and show the two orders that appear. Then sign in as Amina, pack the cover, ship it with a carrier and a reference, and return to Lina to mark it received. ## What this gate does not prove

Nothing here follows a parcel. There is no carrier integration, no live position and no delivery estimate that the app can verify. A tracking reference is text a person typed, and the course makes no claim about which routes or carriers are available from any place. The order statuses also say nothing about a commission, where a piece is made over weeks after a deposit and before a balance. Module 24 reuses the shipped transition for that path, and it will need the guards you proved here.

If a check fails, fix the state that caused it. A page that renders a paid order from live pieces is not fixed by adding a note to the screen, and a maker route without a workshop predicate is not fixed by hiding a button. Keep the fixture and the two order IDs for Module 25, which will send notifications on these same transitions and must not send the same one twice.
