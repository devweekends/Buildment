# Cancellation and rescheduling gate

Plans change, and this gate is where the platform has to change with them without losing money or time. You will cancel one booking from a two-provider payment and show the partial refund in test mode, move another booking and show the claims and the reminder version travel with it, then force a swap to lose its start and prove that nothing moved. The last check runs a cancellation and a reschedule against the same booking at the same moment and looks for exactly one winner.

Start from one confirmed two-provider payment, made in test mode, with the Stripe CLI forwarding events to your local API. Confirm the amounts on the two Orders first, because a refund evidence page without the original total cannot show that a partial refund was partial. Record the Booking id, the Order id, the payment intent, the start instants and the claim block lists in `docs/change-evidence.md` before you change anything.

## Cancel inside the late window

Take the first booking and cancel it inside the late window, far enough from the free boundary that the arithmetic is unambiguous. The response carries the quote: the window name, the percentage, the amount paid, the amount refunded and the amount retained. Check the arithmetic by hand before you look at Stripe, because a quote that is wrong in your own database will be wrong in the dashboard too.

Open the Stripe test dashboard and find the refund against the original payment intent. It must be a partial amount matching the quote, not the full charge. Then show the other provider's Order and Booking unchanged, with the same amounts and the same confirmed state. That comparison is the point of the check: one payment covered two obligations, and only one of them changed.

Reload the cancelled Booking and show four things. The status is cancelled. The claims for its blocks are gone from the SlotClaim collection and those blocks appear again as offers on the provider's calendar. The history entry names the policy values used, so a reviewer can recompute the refund from the record alone. And the Order's refunded total equals the refund records, not an increment that drifted.

Then send the same cancellation again. Expect 409, a single refund in the dashboard and a single refund row locally. If a second refund appears, your idempotency key is not derived from the booking and the action, and every dropped connection in production becomes a double payment back to the client.

## Cancel as the provider

Take the second booking and cancel it from the provider account ten minutes before the start. The refund must be the full amount paid, with no late window applied, even though a client cancellation at that moment would have returned the late percentage. Show the history reason, because the record has to explain why the same timing gave two different outcomes.

This is also the check that stops a refactor from merging the two paths. If your provider cancellation calls the client quote with a role flag, and someone later inverts the flag, the platform silently keeps money from clients whose provider cancelled. Two endpoints are easier to defend than one flag, and this gate asks you to say which one you built.

## Move a booking and watch everything follow

Create a third confirmed booking so you have something to move. Note its current block list, its `reminderVersion` and the scheduled reminder job if one exists. Reschedule it to a start that is close to the old one, ideally fifteen minutes away, so the swap has to keep some blocks and add others rather than replacing the whole set.

After the move, show the block list on the server, not on the screen. Some blocks are shared with the old time, some are new, and the count in the response matches the difference you computed. Show `reminderVersion` incremented by exactly one and a `rescheduleCount` of one. If a reminder job exists, show that a job carrying the old version is not sent, or that the queue holds a move instruction with the new version.

Then move it twice more if the snapshot allows it, and once past the limit. The refusal must name the limit and must not change anything. This is the cheapest way to prove that the policy snapshot governs the move count rather than a number read from today's service.

## Force a swap to lose its start

Pick a start that is currently offered for the booking's service and claim its blocks with a second account, using the checkout route. Then ask to reschedule into it. The response is 409 with a code naming the lost start.

Now prove the negative, because the status code alone proves nothing. The Booking still has its original instants. Its original claims are all present. `reminderVersion` and `rescheduleCount` are unchanged. The history has no new entry. Compare a dump of those four things before and after the request and put both in the evidence page.

Run the same request twenty times in parallel against one contested start, from ten different bookings. Exactly one must succeed and the rest must read as conflicts, with one claim set existing afterwards. After that, revert the winner so the rest of the gate can run on a clean seeding, and say in the evidence that you reverted it.

## Race a cancellation against a reschedule

This is the check the module is named for. Prepare one confirmed booking, then send a client cancellation and a client reschedule at the same moment with two parallel requests. Exactly one may succeed. The loser must read a conflict that names the state, not a server error, and the database must agree with the winner: a cancelled Booking with no claims and a refund, or a moved Booking with new claims and no refund.

Repeat the race in the other direction by sending them in the opposite order, and once with the provider cancelling instead of the client. The winner may differ each time and that is fine. What may not differ is the count: one state change, one refund at most, and no case where a Booking is cancelled while still holding claims or moved while a refund exists.

## What this gate does not prove

There is no worker yet in most builds, so the reminder check proves the version and the instruction, not a delayed job firing on a real queue. Say so plainly rather than implying the message was sent. Module 24 proves the send path and the move.

The gate does not cover refunds that fail after they are created, disputes, or payouts. A card refund can settle later than the API call that created it, so the status you observe in test mode may be pending when you write your notes. Record the status you saw and the event that changed it, rather than describing a settled refund that your evidence does not show.

## Gate checklist

- [ ] A client cancellation inside the late window returns a quote whose refund equals the floored percentage of the amount paid for that booking.
- [ ] The Stripe test dashboard shows a partial refund on the original payment intent, matching the quote to the minor unit.
- [ ] The other provider's Order and Booking are unchanged after the cancellation, with the same amounts and state.
- [ ] The cancelled Booking has no remaining SlotClaims, its blocks appear again as offered starts, and its history records the policy values used.
- [ ] A second cancellation of the same booking returns 409 and creates no second refund locally or in test mode.
- [ ] A provider cancellation refunds the full amount paid even minutes before the start, and the history reason says the provider ended it.
- [ ] A fifteen minute reschedule keeps the shared blocks, adds only the new ones, releases only the unneeded ones, and increments `reminderVersion` once.
- [ ] A reschedule past `rescheduleCutoffHours` or past `maxReschedules` is refused with a named code and changes nothing.
- [ ] A reschedule into a start claimed by another client returns 409, and the booking, claims, version, count and history are identical to a dump taken before the attempt.
- [ ] Twenty parallel reschedules onto one start leave exactly one winner and one claim set.
- [ ] A cancellation and a reschedule sent at the same moment leave exactly one state change, with no cancelled booking holding claims and no moved booking holding a refund.
- [ ] The quote before the confirm step shows the same amounts the server applied, and a quote that goes stale between preview and confirm is refused.
- [ ] The refund body, the two Order totals, the before and after dumps and the race output are saved in `docs/change-evidence.md`.

## Learning log

1. Which document decides the refund for a booking, and what did you have to add to the policy snapshot so the reschedule limit agreed with it?
2. Show the arithmetic for one refund from your own data: the amount paid, the percentage, the floored result and the retained amount.
3. Why is a reschedule claimed before it is released, and what does the database look like after a swap that loses its start?
4. What happens to a reminder that was scheduled before a reschedule, and which field makes the stale reminder harmless?
5. In your cancel and reschedule race, which request won, and what in the losing handler caused a conflict rather than a partial write?

Answer with field names, amounts in minor units and the claim counts you observed. If you cannot explain why a cancelled booking holds no claims while a moved one holds a new set, revisit the swap transaction before starting the async work, because the reminder worker will inherit exactly that state.
