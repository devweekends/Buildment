# Cart UI gate

This gate is passed in a browser with a narrow window, a keyboard and a buyer who owns one thing. The API was proved with curl in Module 16. What is left is whether a person can tell what they are buying, from which creator, under which license, and what Midad will refuse.

Prepare three accounts and three products. Amina buys from Nour and from Salma, so her cart shows two creators. Give Amina an active License for one product under the personal license and leave the other two products unowned. Keep the browser's device mode at 360 by 640 for the whole run, including the screenshots, because the wide layout will pass checks the narrow one fails.

Before you tap anything, look at the network panel and confirm the page makes one request for the cart. A page that asks for the cart once per row is a page that will be slow on the connection your buyer actually has, and it is worth catching now rather than in Week 4.

## Two creators on one screen

Add Nour's font and Salma's template to the cart, then open the cart page. Two group headings appear, each with the creator's name, and each group holds its own lines. The cart total at the bottom must equal the sum of the two group subtotals in minor units.

Now check the ordering. Reload the page twice. The groups must appear in the same order both times, sorted by creator name, and that order must not change when you switch one line's license and the subtotal moves. A group that jumps position mid-tap is a defect the buyer experiences as a mis-tap, so record the order before and after the switch.

Then remove every line from one group using the row's remove action. The empty group must disappear, the remaining group must stay, and the cart total must equal the remaining subtotal. If an empty heading stays on the page, the grouping function is keeping a key it should have dropped.

## Badge and price on every row

Each row shows the product title, the license badge as a written sentence, and its own price. The badge must say what the license allows, not only its name: "Personal license, 1 seat" and "Commercial license, up to 3 seats" are the two strings to look for. A single tap target smaller than 44 pixels is a finding, and so is a price that only appears in the group subtotal.

Change Amina's cart so that one line is personal and the other is commercial. Both badges must be visible at once, in the same group if you can arrange it by adding two products from Salma. Two different badges in one group is the case a group level badge cannot represent, and it is the case this gate exists to protect.

## The owned item

The row for the product Amina owns under the personal license must offer the library, not a switch. The wording names the license type and the destination, in the shape "You already own this under the personal license". Tapping the library link must land on the library page with that product visible.

Then try to buy it anyway. There is no control that lets you, which is the point, but the API still refuses the request if a stale tab sends one. Repeat that with two tabs: open the cart twice, remove the license in the database, add the product in one tab, and confirm the other tab refuses on the next write and resyncs rather than showing a price it cannot honour.

For the product Amina owns under personal only, open the commercial option. The switch stays available and the note says the commercial license is a separate purchase whose price is not reduced. If that sentence is missing, a buyer will read the full commercial price as a double charge, and the support thread that follows will be about your pricing rather than their mistake.

## The switch, from the buyer's side

Select Commercial on a row that offers both options. While the request is in flight, the row shows a pending state and the old badge stays. When the response arrives, the badge becomes the commercial sentence, the row price becomes the commercial price, the group subtotal and the cart total both change once, and the row count stays the same.

Do it again on the same row. Nothing changes except the same cart coming back, and no second row appears. Then reload the page and confirm the stored cart matches what you saw, because a page that shows the right thing from local state and the wrong thing from the server is a page that fails the next time it loads.

Force the refusal. Switch a row to a license type that the buyer owns in the database, and confirm the radio returns to the previous option, the owned message appears, and the totals do not move. Force a network failure by stopping the API, tap a switch, and confirm the page shows the last confirmed cart with a retry that works after the API returns.

## 360 pixels, keyboard and both themes

The screenshots for this gate are taken at 360 by 640 in the light theme, and then repeated in the dark theme. Nothing may scroll sideways, no text may be cut off, and the longest product title in the seed must wrap rather than truncate. Check the same page with the browser text size one step larger; the layout may grow taller and must not overlap.

Then put the mouse away. Tab through the page from the top: the order must follow the reading order, every radio must be reachable, the selected radio must be announced with its label and price, and the library link and remove button must both be reachable before the next group. Press space on a radio and confirm the switch fires exactly once. A keyboard user who cannot change a license cannot buy the commercial option at all.

## What this gate does not prove

The cart shows a total, and this gate does not prove that total is what will be charged. Module 18 re-reads the Product, freezes a snapshot on each OrderLine and returns a 409 when the frozen sum disagrees with what the page showed. Do not describe a passing cart gate as a checkout guarantee, and do not add a "Pay" button to this page that skips that step.

The gate also does not prove the owned check for a buyer whose License was revoked by a refund. That path belongs to Module 26, and the cart simply inherits it: a revoked License does not appear in the owned flags, so the row offers the switch again. If you can, seed a revoked License and confirm the flag is absent, because it is cheap to check here and expensive to discover at the capstone.

## Gate checklist

- [ ] The cart page loads with one request for the cart, not one request per row.
- [ ] Two creators from one cart appear as two groups, each with the creator's name and its own lines.
- [ ] The cart total equals the sum of the group subtotals, checked in minor units.
- [ ] Group order is stable across reloads and does not change when a license switch changes a subtotal.
- [ ] Removing every line in one group removes that group and leaves the other group intact.
- [ ] Every row shows a written license badge that names the seat allowance, and its own price.
- [ ] One group can show a personal badge on one line and a commercial badge on another at the same time.
- [ ] A product owned under the selected license offers a library link instead of a switch, and the wording names the license type.
- [ ] A product owned under the other license type keeps the switch and states that the second license is a separate purchase at full price.
- [ ] A switch changes the badge, the row price and the totals once, keeps one row, and survives a reload.
- [ ] A refused switch restores the previous radio, shows the owned message, and leaves the totals unchanged.
- [ ] A stopped API leaves the last confirmed cart on screen with a retry that works after the API returns.
- [ ] The page at 360 by 640 in both themes has no horizontal scroll, no truncated title and no tap target under 44 pixels.
- [ ] Keyboard order follows reading order, and every radio, link and remove button can be reached and used.

## Learning log

1. Explain why the group key is `studioId` and the label is `studioName`. Describe the two failures that appear if the two are swapped.
2. The owned flag comes from the cart response rather than from the client's license list. Give one concrete sequence where the client copy shows a wrong badge, and say how the wrong badge reaches the buyer.
3. Your switch reads the response before it changes the badge. State what the pending state costs on a slow link and why that cost is worth paying here.
4. Write the two owned sentences from your own page. Explain what a buyer does after reading each one, and which of the two prevents a support thread.
5. At 360 pixels, which element did you have to move to its own line, and what did the wider layout hide about that decision?

Commit the grouping function and its unit tests, the row and badge components, the switch with its three outcomes, and the screenshots for both themes at 360 pixels. Save the network panel entries for the page load and the switch. A mentor should be able to rerun every refusal without editing the database by hand.
