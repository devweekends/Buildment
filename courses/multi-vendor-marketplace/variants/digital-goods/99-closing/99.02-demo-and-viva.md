# Five-minute demo and capstone defense

The demo is the product seen from outside. The defense is the product seen from inside. A reviewer who watches only the recording learns that Midad works; a reviewer who asks the questions learns whether you built it or followed steps. This gate needs both, and it needs the second one without notes.

Record against the deployed app, not localhost. Use two windows, or two devices if you have them, and keep the creator on one side and the buyer on the other so the recording shows two people rather than two tabs of one session. Put the recording link in the README beside the live URL, and set the video to unlisted rather than private so a mentor can open it without an account.

## What the recording must contain

Seven beats, in this order, about forty five seconds each. Keep the whole recording between four and six minutes. A longer demo usually means you are narrating code, which belongs in the defense.

The first beat is the boundary. Open a published product in the creator dashboard and show its two license options and its private file version. Then open the public catalogue response for the same product and point at what is absent: no storage identifier, no checksum, no license key. Say why in one sentence, because this beat proves the model rather than the screen.

The second beat is the split purchase. Add a font from one studio and a template from another, pay once with a Stripe test card, and show one payment producing two orders, each with its own frozen price, license type, seat limit and terms version. Say that a creator who edits a price tomorrow cannot change a receipt from today.

The third beat is the library filling. Show the success page reading "Preparing your files", then the webhook delivery in the Stripe CLI or dashboard, then the library appearing with both products and two license keys. Do not cut the gap; if the first poll shows an empty library, that is honest and it is the state the page exists for.

The fourth beat is the link. Download one file, copy the URL, wait past its five minute expiry, and show it failing. Ask for a new link from the library, show it working, and show the remaining count dropping by one. This is the beat that separates a download button from an access control.

The fifth beat is the race. Run the parallel download script against a license with three downloads remaining, and show exactly three links issued and the rest refused with a clean error. Read the summary line out loud rather than the script.

The sixth beat is the refund. Creator A refunds their order. Show the verify endpoint answering `revoked`, show creator A's license refusing a new link, and show creator B's license still downloading. One payment, two studios, one removed.

The seventh beat is the replay. Resend the original payment event and show that no new license appears and no count changes. A demo that ends on idempotency is a demo that ends on the hardest property in the system.

Do not cut the recording to hide a failure, and do not rerecord a beat that went wrong without saying what you changed. A visible slow first job is more convincing than a polished sequence that never shows the system under load. If a beat fails completely, stop the take and start again, and keep the failed take out of the repository.

## Answer without notes

The defense is nine questions answered in thirty to sixty seconds each. No notes, no slide, no reading from the README. The shape of a strong answer is always the same: what the buyer or creator experiences, then what the server does, then the record that proves it. If you cannot answer, say what you know and name the file or the query you would open. That is an engineering answer; a confident wrong one is not.

Have a mentor ask the questions in a live call, or record the answers in one unbroken take and note which format you used. The gate accepts either, and it does not accept written answers, because writing gives you time that a viva does not.

## The nine questions

**Why does the webhook, and not the success page, grant access?** Because the browser can close, lose power or navigate to the success URL without paying, and because a page is under the user's control. A strong answer names the verified signature, the stored checkout that supplies the amount and the lines, and the license rows as the record that proves access. It also says the success page reads state and never writes it.

**Stripe sends the same event twice at the same moment. Walk through what your code does.** Two handlers load the same checkout and iterate the same paid lines. Both try to insert a license for each line, and the unique index on `orderLineId` lets exactly one insert win. The loser catches the duplicate key, reads the winning row and finishes any line that is still missing. A strong answer adds that the event log is written after the effects exist, so a crash before the effects can still be repaired by the next delivery.

**A buyer shares a download link in a group chat. What can the people in the chat do, and for how long?** They can fetch the file until the link expires, which is five minutes by default, and they can save whatever they fetched. A strong answer states that the link is a bearer capability, that the count records issued links rather than finished downloads, and that the honest mitigation is a short lifetime plus a check before each issue rather than a promise of revocation.

**Why can a refund not take back a link you already issued? What did you choose to limit the damage?** Because the link is enforced by the storage provider at fetch time and the server is no longer in the path. A strong answer names the two step policy: new issues are blocked as soon as the refund is requested, and licenses move to revoked when the webhook confirms, with the five minute lifetime bounding the window. It also says a copy already saved is outside every access control.

**Why is the download endpoint a POST?** Because it spends something. Each request decreases `downloadsRemaining` and writes a `DownloadGrant`, so it is not a safe read that a browser or a proxy may retry freely. A strong answer mentions that a GET that changes a counter is a bug, and that the grant record survives a refresh.

**Your download count counts issued links, not finished downloads. Defend that, or explain what you would change.** Counting finished downloads means streaming bytes through the API to know when a transfer completes, which costs bandwidth and makes the API a file server. Issue counting is cheap, auditable and cannot be gamed by closing a connection. A strong answer names the change it would consider: a storage side log imported by the worker, with the grant id as the join.

**Why does embedding seats in the license make seat assignment safe without a transaction?** Because one conditional update can compare the array size with the seat limit, check for a duplicate email and push the new name in a single document write. A separate seats collection would need a count, a uniqueness check and an insert to agree, which needs a transaction or a compensating design. A strong answer says what the filter contains, and adds that the parallel test is the evidence.

**One payment covered two creators. How did you refund only one?** The refund names an order, the order names one studio, and only the licenses belonging to that order's lines are revoked. The sibling order keeps its own state and its own licenses. A strong answer mentions that the refund amount is derived on the server from that order's snapshot lines, and that the sum of per studio orders still reconciles with the checkout total.

**What happens if your server is down when the payment succeeds?** The provider keeps retrying the delivery, and the reconciliation job finds succeeded payments whose checkout is still unfulfilled and calls the same fulfilment function. A strong answer says the buyer sees "Preparing your files" rather than an error, and that nothing is lost because the payment record lives with the provider rather than only in the browser.

## Two more about the catalogue

Expect a question about the model from module 3, because the ERD is the first file a reviewer opens. Which record answers who may download right now, and why is it not the product? The answer names the license, its status and its allowance, and says that a product describes what is for sale while a license describes what one buyer owns.

The second is about the file. Why is the deliverable not a field on the product? Because of the 16 MB document limit, the backup cost of storing bytes, and the fact that the database would then sit on the only path to a file that must leave through an expiring link. The model keeps facts about the file and storage keeps the file.

## Gate checklist

- [ ] Recording link is in the README, unlisted, between four and six minutes, with audible narration.
- [ ] The recording uses the deployed app, with no localhost request visible in the network tab.
- [ ] Beat 1: a published product with two options and a private file, then the catalogue response with no storage identifier, checksum or key.
- [ ] Beat 2: one payment, two studio orders, with frozen prices, license types, limits and terms versions.
- [ ] Beat 3: the success page in its preparing state, the webhook delivery, then the library with both products.
- [ ] Beat 4: a link that fails after expiry, a fresh link that works, and the remaining count dropping by one.
- [ ] Beat 5: the parallel run issues exactly three links for an allowance of three.
- [ ] Beat 6: a refund revokes one studio's license, the verify endpoint answers `revoked`, and the other studio still downloads.
- [ ] Beat 7: a replayed payment event creates no second license.
- [ ] `docs/DEMO.md` matches the recording beat for beat, including timings.
- [ ] `docs/download-security.md` names the threats, the controls and the residual risk, and lists what is out of scope.
- [ ] All nine defense questions answered live or in one unbroken take, without notes.
- [ ] At least one answer names a limit or a trade off honestly rather than claiming a guarantee.
- [ ] `docs/EVIDENCE.md` has no row marked proven without a link, a command or a file behind it.
- [ ] README, `.env.example`, `docs/erd.md`, the fulfilment state diagram and the evidence index are committed and linked.
- [ ] The live URL still works on a phone at the time of the gate, with the worker running.

## What this gate does not prove

It does not prove that Midad is ready for real money. Payments are in test mode, notifications are logged, there is no creator payout, no tax handling and no chargeback process, and one API instance serves everyone. Saying that in the viva is part of the defense, not a weakness in it.

It also does not close the variant. Other modules in this path are authored and reviewed separately, and this gate speaks only for the work in front of you: the shipped API, the worker, the client, the documents and the recorded demo. A learner who can record these seven beats and answer these questions has met the bar for this lesson, and the course creator decides when the whole track is ready for new learners.
