# Cart page gate

The gate starts from a cart with three copies from three shops and one spare copy you will sell in the middle of it. Write the expected numbers before you open the browser: three groups, three subtotals, one total, a count of three in the badge, and one attention line after the sale. A cart page that looks right and reports a wrong total is the single most expensive defect in this module, because the number is what the student believes she will pay.

Use a phone sized window at 360 CSS pixels for the whole pass, and put the mouse away after the first section. The page has one destructive action and it must be usable without a pointer, on the device most students actually use.

## Prove the cart survives a reload

Add the three copies from their edition pages rather than through curl. Each add must leave you on the edition page with a short confirmation, and the header badge must go from nothing to one, two and three without a page load. Then reload the cart page and confirm that the three rows are the same three copies, with the same grades and the same prices, and that the badge still reads three.

Then close the tab, open the site again, and open the cart. The rows are still there, which is the whole point of a server cart: nothing in this design depends on the browser remembering anything. Clear the browser storage for the site and reload once more, and the cart must be unchanged. If it disappears, some part of the page is reading a local copy instead of the response.

## Prove the grouping and the numbers

Read the page against the database. For each shop group, add up the stored prices of its ready copies and compare with the printed subtotal, then compare the total with the sum of the printed subtotals. They must match to the minor unit, and the count of groups must match the number of distinct shops holding a ready copy.

Then check the arithmetic is not happening twice. Remove one copy, confirm the whole view is replaced by the response rather than adjusted in the browser, and check the new subtotals against the database again. A page that sums its own rows usually passes the first check and fails this one, because the row it just removed is still in its own array.

Look for the number that is easy to get wrong: a shop whose only copy is not ready. Withdraw one copy in the database, reload, and that shop must leave the ready groups entirely rather than appear with a subtotal of zero. A group with a zero subtotal is a shop Hana thinks she can pay.

## Prove a sale behind her back

Keep the cart page open, then mark one of Hana's ready copies sold in the database as if another student had paid. Reload the page. The sold line must now appear in the attention group, in words that say it is no longer available, keeping its title, grade and price for context, and it must be absent from every subtotal and from the copy count. The attention count beside the heading must say one.

Repeat for a held copy and a withdrawn copy if your fixtures allow it, and confirm each state gets its own sentence. Then remove the sold line with the remove control and confirm the attention count returns to zero and the total returns to the earlier number.

This section is the one that catches the subtotal bug in production, because it is the only part of the gate where the state of a copy changes after the page first rendered.

## Prove removal by keyboard

Tab to the first remove control. The focus ring must be visible against the row, and the accessible name must identify the copy: grade, title and shop. Press enter, and while the request is in flight the row must stay visible and the button must be disabled with the word "Removing". Then the view updates, the row disappears and the focus lands on the next remove control in the same group.

Remove the last row of a group and confirm the focus falls back to that group's heading, and the group itself disappears when it has no rows left. Then empty the cart completely and confirm the page says the cart is empty, in a sentence a person can read, with a link back to browsing editions. The empty state is where a student who removed everything by accident needs one obvious way back.

Listen to the announcement if you can test with a screen reader, and read the markup if you cannot. A polite live region must contain the sentence naming the removed copy and the number of copies left. A removal that is silent is a removal the student has to verify by counting.

## Prove the add path and the badge

From an edition page, add a copy and watch the button: idle, then "Adding" with the control disabled, then "Added", and the badge increments once. Tap twice quickly and confirm one request, not two, and that the second tap does not produce a conflict message the student has to interpret.

Add a copy that is already in the cart and confirm the refusal is calm. Then try a sold copy and a withdrawn copy and confirm each response lands beside the button with its own sentence and a way forward, which for a sold copy means other copies of the same edition.

## Prove there is no count control

Search the client for a route or a form field that could change a number of copies. There must be no stepper, no number input, no plus or minus control and no `PATCH` request carrying a quantity anywhere in the cart code. The correct evidence is an empty search, not an opinion, so run it and paste the result in your notes.

Then check the other direction: adding two copies of the same edition must produce two rows with two grades and two prices, not one row with a two beside it. That pair of rows is the design working as intended, and it is also the fastest way to show a reviewer why the model has no number in it.

## Gate checklist

- [ ] The expected groups, subtotals, total, badge count and attention count were written before the first request.
- [ ] Three copies added from edition pages survive a reload, a new tab and cleared browser storage.
- [ ] The header badge counts ready copies, matches the page, and shows nothing at zero.
- [ ] Every printed subtotal matches the sum of stored prices for the ready copies in that shop, to the minor unit.
- [ ] A shop whose only copy is not ready leaves the groups entirely, and no group shows a zero subtotal.
- [ ] A copy sold after the page loaded appears in the attention group, outside every subtotal, and the attention count says one.
- [ ] Held and withdrawn copies each get their own sentence, and a removed attention line clears the count.
- [ ] The remove control names the grade, the title and the shop, and is disabled with "Removing" while in flight.
- [ ] After a removal, focus lands on the next remove control, then on the group heading, then on the empty state.
- [ ] A polite live region announces the removed copy and the remaining count.
- [ ] A failed removal keeps the row, clears the pending state and shows a message.
- [ ] The add button shows idle, adding and added states, and a double tap sends one request.
- [ ] The four refusal codes render as four different messages beside the button, and none is a browser alert.
- [ ] The client contains no stepper, no number input and no request that carries a quantity.
- [ ] Two copies of one edition render as two rows with two grades and two prices.
- [ ] The page completes at 360 pixels with no horizontal scrolling and every control at least 44 pixels tall.

## Learning log

1. Which copy sold while the page was open, and what exactly did the page show for it? Quote the sentence and the two numbers it stayed out of.
2. What did the printed subtotal say for each shop before and after a copy was removed, and how did you check those numbers against the database?
3. Where did focus land after each of your three removals: the next row, the group heading and the empty state? Name the element that received it.
4. What did the add button do on a double tap, and which control in your code prevents the second request?
5. What did your search for a count control return, and what would a reviewer see if the cart had one?
6. What does the badge read with three ready copies and one sold copy, and why is that number different from the number of rows on the page?

## What a passing gate leaves behind

Four artefacts. Screenshots at 360 pixels of the grouped cart, the attention group and the empty state. The before and after numbers for each subtotal, beside the database values they came from. The empty result of the search for a count control. And the learning log with the six answers, including the copy you sold mid-test.

Two honest limits belong in the write-up. The gate proves the page describes what the API returned, not that the API's prices are right, which is module 27's subject. And the keyboard pass is a developer with a keyboard, not a student with a phone and a screen reader, so the announcement and the focus rules are worth one more run on a real device before the week two demo.

Do not build the reading list page against a cart page that has not passed this gate. Module 18 adds many rows at once, and a layout that already fails at three rows will not survive twenty.
