# Listing gate

The gate is one shop, one edition and three physical copies. North Street Books has three returned copies of the College Algebra print edition, ISBN-13 `9781938168383`. The copy in the worst condition is Acceptable, with water damage through the first forty pages. The next is Good, with highlighting on about twenty pages. The best is Very Good, with a creased cover and a clean interior. Your job is to put all three on sale through the back office you built, and to show the guidance each price was set against.

Write the expected numbers before you touch the browser. The catalogue already has two other shops on this edition: Campus Shelf has one available Very Good copy at 3,400, and Corner Books has one available Good copy at 2,600. Before North Street publishes anything, guidance should show a Good row at 2,600 with a count of one and a Very Good row at 3,400 with a count of one, and no Acceptable row at all. If your first run shows a different set, stop and read the aggregation before you continue.

## Start from a state you can describe

Begin with a development database whose seed you can re-create. Note the counts for Edition, Listing and Shop before you start, and note the IDs of the three shops. The gate is evidence, and evidence that cannot be re-created after a power cut is not evidence. A one-line note with the database name, the seed command and the counts is enough.

Confirm the two other shops' copies are `available` and belong to the edition above. A guidance panel is only meaningful if the market it reads is the market you think it is reading. One `find` on the Listing collection with the edition id, grouped by status, tells you in a minute which rows will appear and which will not.

## Prove the ISBN step before the grade step

Type the ISBN-10 form, `1-93816-838-0`, with the hyphens and the language group as a person might copy it from the back cover. The server should normalise it to `9781938168383`, find the existing edition, and show the preview without creating a second Edition. Check the Edition count after the request. It must be unchanged.

Then type a number with the last digit changed, so the check digit fails. The response must be a 400 with a message under the ISBN field, and the string you typed must still be in the input. Nothing may be written to the Edition or Listing collection. This pair of runs proves both halves of the first step: valid input converges on one shared record, invalid input never reaches the catalogue.

Finally, open a second browser profile as Corner Books and type the same valid ISBN. Corner Books must get the same edition id and create its own listing, separate from North Street's copy. Two shops, one edition, two owners of two physical copies. If either shop can change the shared title, the edition lock is not working and the gate stops here.

## Put three copies in three grades

Create three drafts from North Street Books. Each one is a separate submission with its own grade, its own defect flags and its own price. Acceptable at 1,600 with `water_damage` and a note about the first forty pages. Good at 2,300 with `highlighting`. Very Good at 3,100 with a note about the creased cover. Read the guidance before each save and write down what it said, because the point of the gate is that the price was a decision and not a guess.

After the three copies are `available`, guidance for this edition should read Acceptable 1,600 with a count of one, Good 2,300 with a count of two, and Very Good 3,100 with a count of two. Every one of those numbers is checkable against the fixtures. If the Good row still says 2,600, the aggregation is probably reading only other shops or the publish step did not commit. If the counts are higher than expected, you have duplicate listings from an earlier run.

Now break it on purpose. Send an Acceptable grade code with `gradeRank: 5` through the raw API. The response must be a 400 naming the grade field, and the stored document for that copy must still carry rank 1. Then mark the Acceptable copy `held` through your status route and reload guidance. The Acceptable row must disappear, not fall back to a stale number. A guidance panel that keeps a held or sold copy in the market is the failure this module is built to prevent.

## Prove the boundary

Sign in as Corner Books and send a PATCH to North Street's Good listing with a lower price. The response must be 403, the message must be readable, and the stored price must be unchanged when you re-read the document. Do this with the raw request, not through the table, because a hidden button is not a test.

Then try the shared record. As Corner Books, PATCH the Edition title. The response must be 403 with the edition-locked reason, and the title must be unchanged for both shops. The two refusals are different problems for the owner: one is "this is not your copy", the other is "this is not yours to correct". The UI should not collapse them into the same sentence.

## Answer this before you write the log

The question below is the one a mentor will ask at the demo. Answer it before you look at your code, then check the answer against the responses you saved.

```quiz
options:
  - A 409 telling the owner to correct the shared edition
  - A new edition so the typed title is kept exactly
  - The existing edition id, so the copy links to it
  - A 404 because the owner did not create that edition
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A repeated ISBN is the normal case at a counter, not a conflict."
  - "A second edition for one ISBN splits copies away from the shared record."
  - "The route reuses the winning edition and creates the shop's own listing."
  - "An existing edition is a success, not a missing resource."
```

## Gate checklist

- [ ] The Edition count is unchanged after listing three copies, and the ISBN-10 and ISBN-13 forms resolve to the same edition id.
- [ ] A bad check digit returns 400 with the typed string still in the input and no new Edition or Listing row.
- [ ] Three listings exist for `9781938168383` from one shop, each with its own grade code, derived rank, defect flags and price in minor units.
- [ ] Stored ranks are 1, 2 and 3 for Acceptable, Good and Very Good, and a request that sends its own rank is refused with 400.
- [ ] Guidance before publishing showed the other shops' prices, and guidance after publishing shows 1,600 at Acceptable, 2,300 at Good with a count of two, and 3,100 at Very Good with a count of two.
- [ ] A held or sold copy leaves the guidance instead of lowering the market price.
- [ ] Each price was written down beside the guidance value it was compared with, and the note explains any deliberate difference.
- [ ] A PATCH from the second shop returns 403 and the stored price is unchanged.
- [ ] A PATCH on the shared edition title returns 403 for a shop owner, and the title is unchanged for both shops.
- [ ] The listings table shows the three copies with their status, grade and price, and a draft is visibly different from an available copy.
- [ ] The 400 and 403 responses you saved are raw JSON with the `error.code` field present.
- [ ] The Mongo documents are small: no embedded edition metadata and no photo bytes on a listing.
- [ ] The seed can be re-run to the same counts, so a mentor can repeat the gate without your laptop.

## Learning log

1. Which value did the price guidance read for the Good grade before North Street published its own copy, and which value did it read afterwards? Name the records that changed the answer.
2. The ISBN-10 `1-93816-838-0` and the ISBN-13 `978-1-93816-838-3` describe one edition. Which function returned the canonical key, and where is the only place it is called?
3. Explain why the server derives `gradeRank` instead of accepting it from the browser. Give the exact wrong outcome that a mismatched pair would cause for a student filtering with a Good floor.
4. What does the 403 on a cross-shop PATCH prove that a disabled Edit button cannot prove? Quote the response code and the unchanged field you read.
5. A mentor asks why one shop cannot simply correct a shared title. Answer in three sentences, and name the record that carries the shop's own report of the problem.

## What a passing gate leaves behind

Four artefacts, not one screenshot. First, the three listing documents as Mongo shows them, with grade, rank, defects and price visible. Second, the guidance response from before and after publishing, with the numbers above. Third, the raw 400 and 403 responses with their `error.code` values. Fourth, the learning log with the five answers and the guidance note beside each price.

Keep the two shops' tokens out of the repository and out of any note you commit. A gate transcript with a live token in it becomes a security problem the moment it is pushed, and the mentor will not read past it. Use a throwaway account in a development database.

Two honest limits belong in the write-up. Nothing in this gate proves that a copy is worth what the shop asks; it proves that the marketplace records the shop's claim and shows the shop the market before the claim is made. And no photograph is required yet, so the two lower grades are accepted on the owner's word alone. Module 13 closes that gap, and it will refuse to publish an Acceptable or Good copy until at least one photo of the actual copy exists.
