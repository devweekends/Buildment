```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In these made-up cases, all the people are fictional. Omayma, a data analyst, drafts a claim card. Weekly report preparation fell from 90 to 30 minutes. The source is her timing sheet. Her share: she wrote the macro and her manager checked the output. What else changed: a new finance template arrived in the same period. The card has no dates, no second source, no percent figure and no comment from her manager. Which missing item matters most?"
options:
  - "The window: which weeks the 90 and 30 minutes cover"
  - "A second source, such as the finance team's records"
  - "A percent figure, so the size of the drop is clear"
  - "A line from her manager praising the new macro"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Without a window a reader cannot tell which weeks the 90 and the 30 minutes cover. So they cannot check the claim or see how close it sits to the new template. The other three would add polish, not checkability."
  - "A second source could add confidence, but one clear source is enough to check a claim, and the timing sheet is already named. What cannot be checked yet is which weeks it covers."
  - "A percent is only arithmetic on the two times, so a reader can work it out alone. It adds no evidence about where the times came from or when they were recorded."
  - "A comment is an opinion, not a measurement, and the card already says what her manager did. It could not replace the dates the claim is missing."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ramzi is a mobile developer. His resume bullet reads: 'Cut crash rate from 3.1 to 1.2 percent (store console, 30 days before and after) by upgrading a networking library.' His team also reviewed the change. The bullet does not name the library, give crash counts, give the app version or say who did the upgrade. Which missing item matters most?"
options:
  - "The name and version of the library he upgraded"
  - "The number of crashes behind each of the two rates"
  - "Which part he did himself and which part the team did"
  - "The app version that shipped with the change"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The library's name is a useful detail, but the rates, source and window already let a reader check the result. It does not tell the reader whose work it was."
  - "Counts would help a careful reader, but the rates, the source and the window are given, so the claim can already be checked. Counts do not show who did the work."
  - "Right. The bullet says what changed, but not whether Ramzi or his team upgraded the library. A reader cannot tell his part from the team's."
  - "The app version helps a reader place the change in time, but the 30-day window already does that. It does not separate Ramzi's work from his team's."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Salim is a full-stack developer. He links his project from his resume. The link opens a demo that asks every visitor for an email sign-up. If the demo is down, nothing else exists. What is missing from his showcase?"
options:
  - "A longer README with install steps for every visitor who wants to run the project"
  - "A base layer with no sign-up: a case study page with screenshots and a short recording"
  - "A paid plan, so that the demo never sleeps and the sign-up page always loads in time"
  - "More features in the demo, so that each visitor has more to try once signed in"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A longer README helps someone who already wants to run the project. It does not help a visitor who meets a sign-up wall, and it does nothing when the demo is down."
  - "Right. A case study page with screenshots and a short recording opens with no sign-up, and it still works when the demo is asleep."
  - "No step in this course needs a paid plan. A page that does not depend on the demo solves the problem for free."
  - "More features sit behind the same sign-up wall. A visitor who leaves at the wall never sees them."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Using what module 8 taught (checked 2026-10), consider Taleen, a frontend developer. She posts a full copy of her article on another platform. Nothing in the copy says that the original lives on her own site. What is missing?"
options:
  - "A different title, so that search sees the copy as a separate article"
  - "More hashtags, so that readers can find the copy on the platform"
  - "A request that the platform hide the copy from search results"
  - "A line saying where it was first published, with a link home"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A new title does not point anyone to the original. The copy needs to say where the original lives, not try to look like a new article."
  - "Hashtags help readers find the copy on that platform. They do not tell a reader, or a search, where the original lives."
  - "Hiding a copy does not send readers to the original, and a platform may not offer the choice. The fix is a line that points home."
  - "Right. A line such as 'First published at' her site, with a link, tells readers and search where the original lives."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wesam is a backend developer. His ledger row says the p95 of his orders search endpoint fell from 1.8 s to 0.6 s. The p95 is the time that 95 of every 100 requests finish within. The window is 14 days before and 14 days after, from the team dashboard. He added an index, and his lead reviewed it. An AI tool summarises his case study as 'Improved performance by 90 percent across the platform.' What is wrong with the summary?"
options:
  - "Only the percent is wrong, because the scope and the ownership both match the ledger row it came from"
  - "Nothing, because 90 percent is a fair rounding up of a drop of about two thirds in the time taken"
  - "It widens one endpoint to the platform, uses a figure the ledger lacks, and drops his lead's part"
  - "Only the word choice, because it should say we so that the whole team gets the credit it earned"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The percent is wrong, but so is the scope: the ledger row covers one endpoint, not the platform. The summary also drops his lead's part."
  - "The drop was about two thirds, from 1.8 s to 0.6 s. Rounding it up to 90 percent is not rounding, and the summary still has no window or source."
  - "Right. The ledger row covers one endpoint's p95 over 14 days from the team dashboard, and his lead reviewed the change. The summary drops all of that, and 90 percent is not in the ledger."
  - "Saying we would hide Wesam's part further. The fix is to say what he did and what his lead did."
```
