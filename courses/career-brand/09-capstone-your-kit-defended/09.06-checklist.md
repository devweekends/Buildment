## Your task: your baseline against your defence

This task closes the loop that module 1 opened. At gate 1.6 you recorded where your kit stood. Now you show what changed, using evidence from both sides. The mentor does not grade how impressive your kit is. The mentor reads whether you can point at one thing that was weak and is now stronger, and say what you will do next.

Find your baseline first. It is the flags from your first drill, or your written answers for three lines of your old resume, or your ten-second test result. Pick one flag or one answer from it. Then pick one flag or one answer from your capstone defence. Say what the difference shows. Then state the one thing you will build or measure next, and the date. Write about 150 words. You can speak the answer with your phone's microphone.

Here is a strong answer from a fictional fellow. Rimas is a data analyst. She wrote: "At gate 1.6 my line was 'Improved reporting'. Asked how it was measured, I had no answer, and I wrote that I did not know my own part. In my defence I picked 'Rebuilt the weekly report so it no longer needs a manual copy step; I have not timed it.' The mentor asked how it was measured. I said I had not measured it, that I wrote the macro, and that my manager chose the layout. The change is that I now say what I did and what I do not know, and my kit says the same thing as my answer. Next, I will time the weekly report on five Mondays and put the median in my ledger by the end of November."

Look at what makes it strong. It names one line from each side, so a mentor can see the change. It says what was missing before and what is there now. The next step is small, and it has a date. It does not claim a result that was not measured.

Here is a weak answer. "My kit is much better than at the start and I defended it well. Next I will keep improving it." It names no line from the baseline and none from the defence. A mentor cannot see what changed, so the answer cannot be checked. The next step has no action and no date. If a mentor asked "how was that measured?" of this answer, there would be nothing to open.

If you prefer to use Areej's case, here it is. At gate 1.6 her old resume drew two flags: vague ownership on "worked on backend performance", and unsupported number on "improved speed by 90%". She could not say of what, over which days, or how it was measured. In her capstone defence, she was asked about the new report line. Every number came from her ledger, from the team dashboard, with its window. Her part and her lead's were separate. The interviewer drew no unsupported number flag. It drew one vague ownership flag, on "we deployed it", which she fixed in the line and in the ledger to "my lead deployed it after my migration passed review". Her next step came from her honest gap: she never measured whether customers received invoices sooner, so she would ask the shop owner for two weeks of order and invoice timestamps and compare the median gap with the spreadsheet history. Use these facts to write the comparison and to choose a date.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your kit and your answer part by part. Each part is Met, Partly or Not yet. A kit is reviewed in under 30 minutes: the index, three sampled lines from your number map, five public links on a phone, and your defence.

**Part 1: Evidence.**

- **Met:** Every number in your resume and profile maps to a ledger row or an honest gap. No numbers are left unmapped.
- **Partly:** One or two numbers are not mapped.
- **Not yet:** Three or more are not mapped, or you cannot say where a number came from when asked.

**Part 2: Ownership.**

- **Met:** In three lines your mentor samples (from your resume, your case study and your defence), your part is stated apart from the team's.
- **Partly:** One of the sampled lines is blurred.
- **Not yet:** Two or more say only "we", or you claim the whole change.

**Part 3: Clarity.**

- **Met:** In your first five resume bullets, your headline, the first paragraph of your About section and the first paragraph of your post, there is no "responsible for", no big claim without a number or a source, and no unnamed experts.
- **Partly:** There are one to three empty phrases across those places.
- **Not yet:** There are four or more, or the text is mostly adjectives and duties.

**Part 4: Access and safety.**

- **Met:** At least five public links open on a phone with no setup, your demo plan lists the six controls or names each gap, and nothing private is public.
- **Partly:** One link is broken, or one gap in the demo plan is not named.
- **Not yet:** A key, a secret, customer data, an employer name without permission or your contact details are public.

**Part 5: Defence.**

- **Met:** For three lines your mentor picks, you say how each was measured, over what window, from what source and what your part was. One fix is recorded with a before and an after, and your next step has a date.
- **Partly:** Two of the three lines are answered, or no fix is recorded.
- **Not yet:** One line or none is answered.

**Part 6: Comparison and next step.**

- **Met:** You name one change since the baseline, with evidence from each side, and one next thing to build or measure with a date.
- **Partly:** The change has evidence from only one side, or the next step has no date.
- **Not yet:** There is no comparison.

**Pass rule:** all eight parts of the kit exist. Evidence (Part 1) is Met. The stranger test passed on a phone at gate 5.8. Parts 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 are at least Partly. Your mentor's follow-up questions are answered. A flag from the AI interviewer is feedback only. It is never a pass or a fail. A deployed demo, a published post and a live website are noted but not required.

Your mentor then replies with one to three questions, chosen from the part that needs the most work. For example, if Evidence is weak: "Show me the ledger row for the line about X." If Ownership is weak: "For that bullet, what was your part and what was your lead's?" You answer in writing or in a voice note, and you can resubmit. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
