<GoDeeper title="The capstone brief: what you hand in">

The capstone has three steps: assemble the kit (chapter 9.3), defend it (chapter 9.4) and compare it with where you started (chapter 9.6). The kit has eight parts. Each one is something you built in an earlier module.

1. Your career arc document (module 2). Private.
2. Your proof-of-work inventory and claim ledger (module 3). Private, because it can hold employer details.
3. Your project plan (module 4). Private. It may become the project README later.
4. Your showcase plan and one case study (module 5). The case study is public, with names removed and permission recorded.
5. Your resume, and one copy tailored to a posting (module 6). Shared with applications. Never posted publicly, because it holds contact details.
6. Your LinkedIn profile (module 7). Public.
7. Your post and your plan to share it in about ten places (module 8). The post is public once you publish it.
8. Your website plan (module 8). The plan is private. The site itself is optional.

Some things are good to have but not required: a project that is started and deployed with a demo account, a post that is published on at least one channel, and a website that is live. Your mentor will note them. They will not fail you.

Three kit requirements apply to everything. Every number in your resume and profile maps to a ledger row, or to an honest "not measured" line. Nothing private, no client names and no secrets appear in anything public. Every public link opens on a phone with no setup. Nothing in the course needs a paid plan, a paid domain or LinkedIn Premium.

If you used Areej's material for a part, such as her case study or her post, say so in your kit index. The arc, project plan, resume, profile and website plan must be your own.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="The defence: what your mentor will ask">

Your mentor, or the AI interviewer, opens your kit, picks a line and asks. Over a whole defence, expect these six questions, in any order.

1. How was that measured? Give the method, the window with dates and the source.
2. What was your part, and the team's? Name one thing you did and one thing someone else did.
3. What else changed in that window? Name at least one other change, or say honestly that you cannot separate them.
4. Why this project, and who is the first real user? Answer in a sentence, with a person or a small group you can reach.
5. If the demo is asleep, what does a reader see? Name the page, screenshot or recording that opens with no setup.
6. What would you build or measure next? Give one action with a date.

"I did not measure that" is a full answer when it is true, as long as you add how you would measure it. Nobody is asked to guess or to invent a source.

There are three ways to defend, and any one of them completes chapter 9.4. The first is a full interview with the AI interviewer, which needs your own key. The second is a recorded walkthrough of about five minutes. Audio only is fine. The third is a written defence plus a short call of about 15 minutes. Your mentor decides between the second and the third, so ask early. None of the three needs you to share personal hardship. If the interviewer's request is refused or fails, use the second or third way. Do not use a VPN or change your location.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="How your mentor reads the kit">

Your mentor reviews a kit in under 30 minutes. The review has five parts. Each is marked Met, Partly or Not yet.

**Evidence.** Every number in your resume and profile maps to a ledger row or an honest gap. This is the part everything else rests on.

**Ownership.** In your resume, your case study and your defence, your part is stated apart from the team's.

**Clarity.** Plain and specific writing. No "responsible for", no big claim without a number or a source, and no empty phrases.

**Access and safety.** The public links open on a phone with no setup. Your demo plan is safe for a stranger. Nothing private is public.

**Defence.** For any line the mentor picks, you say how it was measured, over what window, from what source, and what your part was. You make one fix after the feedback, and your next step has a date.

The pass rule is short. All eight parts exist. Evidence is Met. The stranger test passed on a phone at the end of module 5. The other four parts are at least Partly. Your mentor's one to three follow-up questions are answered. The AI interviewer's flags are feedback only. They are never a pass or a fail.

</GoDeeper>
