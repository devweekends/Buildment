Diala's site had a template and nothing of hers. This chapter is about a small site that holds your case study and your blog posts, and that a visitor believes. Areej is a fictional fellow, and her plan is the worked example. Planning the site is required. Putting it live is strongly encouraged, and optional.

## A few pages are enough

<MermaidDiagram title="A five-page personal site" chart={`flowchart TD
  H["Home: who you are and your best proof"] --> W["Work: the case study"]
  H --> R["Writing: the posts, newest first"]
  H --> A["About: a little about you"]
  H --> C["Contact: one way to reach you"]
`} legend="Five pages. Work holds your case study. Writing works as a blog." />

A personal site is a few pages. Home says who you are and shows your best proof. Work holds your case study. Writing holds your posts, newest first, so it works as a blog. About says a little about you. Contact gives one way to reach you. A free static host is enough for all of it.

<GoDeeper title="What each page holds, from Areej's plan">

A static host is a service that stores your page files and gives them to anyone who opens the address. It does not run your code for each visitor. That makes it simple, fast and free for small sites.

**Home.** Her one-line promise: "A full-stack developer who measures before and after, and writes it down." Then two proofs, each with a link. One is the report that went from 4.6 s to 0.41 s at p95. The other is the cache that went from 820 ms to 95 ms at p95.

**Work.** The public version of her case study, "From 4.6 seconds to 0.41: fixing a slow report with one index", with the company name removed. Also a link to the Campus Board repository and its load test.

**Writing.** Her post, "Same script, same data: what a 30-second cache did to our p95", with its date, and any later posts above it.

**About.** A few lines on the coding club she helps run and the football notebook she has kept since she was 12. This is where a personal detail belongs, once.

**Contact.** Email only. No phone number and no home address.

Why not more pages? Every page needs you to keep it true and dated. Five pages that are current beat fifteen that are stale. Most visitors will read the home page, open one case study and read one post. Check that it reads well on a phone.

</GoDeeper>

## Specifics make it yours

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m8-specifics-or-template" />

A visitor meets a template page: a stock photo and "loves problems". They think it could be anyone's. Then they meet a page with a real number and a real mistake, and they believe it. The second page was not better written. It held things only its author could have put there.

<GoDeeper title="Why specifics, and why not detectors">

Google (checked 2026-10) does not ban AI-made content. It warns against pages that add no value, asks you to fact-check AI-made text, and suggests saying how content was made. Its page on helpful content asks for original information, first-hand experience and honest authorship.

Detectors are a poor target. One study found that several popular detectors often labelled non-native English writing as AI-made. So a fellow's own plain English can be flagged for no good reason. And a "humaniser" tool changes style, not facts. It adds no information and does not make a site yours.

What makes a site yours is a short list. A number with its source. A screenshot you took, with the date. The name of a real tool you used. A mistake you made and what it taught you. A date on every post. A photo of your real working place, only if it is safe and you want to share it.

If an AI tool helped you draft, check every fact and say so, as you learned in chapter 8.2.

</GoDeeper>

## Swap generic for specific

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m8-swap-generic" />

Build Areej's home page from five parts: a real screenshot, her own line, dates, a number with its test and an honest mistake. A stock photo, a slogan, missing dates and a humaniser are refused, each with a reason. Tap a chip, then the part it fills. The counter shows how many parts a visitor could check.

<GoDeeper title="Do the same swap on your own site">

Put a specific in every place a template puts a generic.

A stock photo becomes a screenshot of your own dashboard, log or test result, with names and customer data removed. Ask permission before you show anything from a team or client, as Areej asked her lead.

A slogan like "I am passionate" becomes your own line from your career arc document, in your own words.

"No dates, so it never looks old" becomes a date on every post and every number.

"I love coding" becomes a number with its source and window, from your ledger.

"I am a perfectionist" becomes an honest mistake. Areej's is that she tested only on seeded data, not on a copy of production data.

Keep one personal detail for the About page and use it once. Keep contact to an email address.

</GoDeeper>

## A free static host, checked

<MermaidDiagram title="Free static hosts, checked 2026-10" chart={`flowchart TD
  H["A free static host, checked 2026-10"] --> G["GitHub Pages: up to 1 GB, not for shops or software services"]
  H --> C["Cloudflare Pages free: 500 builds a month"]
  H --> V["Vercel Hobby: personal, non-commercial only"]
`} legend="Three free hosts and the limit that matters most for each." />

A static host stores your files and serves them to anyone. Free is enough for a personal site. The limits matter, so read them and write the date you read them. GitHub Pages: a site up to 1 GB, not for shops or software services. Cloudflare Pages free: 500 builds a month. Vercel Hobby: personal, non-commercial use only. All checked 2026-10.

<GoDeeper title="Limits and what they mean for you">

**GitHub Pages** takes HTML, CSS and JavaScript from a repository. The published site can be up to 1 GB, with a soft limit of 100 GB of traffic a month and 10 builds an hour. It is not meant for a site that mainly sells things or provides commercial software as a service. A personal portfolio is fine.

**Cloudflare Pages** on the free plan allows 500 builds a month, up to 20,000 files per site and 25 MiB per file.

**Vercel Hobby** is free for personal, non-commercial use only.

A free address that ends in `github.io` or `pages.dev` is enough. You do not need to buy a domain. If you want one later, you can add it.

GitHub's page on trade controls lists the places where it restricts service. It does not name Palestine. It also says that a user flagged by mistake can appeal. This does not promise access from your phone, so test it. If a host will not open, plan the site anyway and ask your mentor.

A site can be plain HTML files. If you want a site generator, a tool that builds pages from Markdown notes, pick one with a beginner guide and put a one-page test live before you build anything. This course does not recommend one, because we have not tested them on every host.

All limits checked 2026-10. If your screen differs, follow your screen.

</GoDeeper>

## Put one test page live first

<MermaidDiagram title="Five small steps to a live site" chart={`flowchart TD
  A["1. Make a repository for the site"] --> B["2. Add one page that says hello"]
  B --> C["3. Turn on the free host"]
  C --> D["4. Open the address on your phone"]
  D --> E["5. Add the case study and the post"]
`} legend="Do steps 1 to 4 before you write any more." />

Do the small thing first. Put one test page live and open its address on your phone. Only then add the case study and the post. If the test fails, you learn it in ten minutes, not after a weekend of work. Putting the site live is strongly encouraged. It is optional if your power, laptop or access does not allow it.

<GoDeeper title="If you cannot put it live">

A complete website plan counts in full at the gate. You can still write every page's content as text files on your phone or laptop, and publish later.

Each host has its own guide for turning the host on, and the screens change. Follow the host's guide on your screen (checked 2026-10). If power is short, do steps 1 and 2 in one sitting so you do not stop halfway.

Keep addresses stable. Once you share a link, do not rename the page. Your resume and your copies in other places will point to these addresses.

</GoDeeper>

## Small, dated and alive

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m8-small-and-alive" />

The site is the home of your originals and the base for your resume links. Dated posts show it is current. One small real update a month is enough: a new post, a new number or a fixed mistake. Do not post only to look busy.

<GoDeeper title="A monthly update, and Diala's line rewritten">

Once a month, spend 20 minutes. Open every link on your phone. Add one dated item. Fix anything that is out of date. Update the "last updated" date. This is a suggestion from this course, not research.

Here is Diala's template line, rewritten with her own result: "I cut my team's CI pipeline from 22 minutes to 9. Here is the post, with how I measured it." She adds the window and source from her ledger, so the number can be checked.

</GoDeeper>
