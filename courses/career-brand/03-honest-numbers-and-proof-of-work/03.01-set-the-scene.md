## Seventy percent of what?

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m3-seventy-of-what" />

Tawfiq is a data engineer at an e-commerce analytics company. His resume says he made the nightly pipeline 70 percent faster. A hiring manager asks three questions: 70 percent of what, over which days, and was it you? The line answers none of them. The number may be true, but nothing behind it can be reached.

<GoDeeper title="Why a reader asks these three questions">

A percentage is a ratio, and a ratio hides its two ends. "70 percent faster" could be 10 minutes down to 3, or 10 hours down to 3. It could be one lucky night or a month of nights. It could be the work of one person or of a whole team. A careful reader cannot tell which, so the careful reader asks.

The three questions are not a trap. A hiring manager asks them of every number, including honest ones. If you can answer in one calm sentence each, the number becomes more believable than before. If you cannot, the reader does not decide that you lied. The reader decides that the number is not usable, and moves on. Both outcomes cost you the line.

This is why the course builds the proof before it builds the prose. In course 14, "The Founder Interview", you practise defending a number aloud. Here you make sure that, when you are asked, there is something real to point at.

</GoDeeper>

## The same week, bigger machines

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m3-same-week" />

The whole run fell from 5 hours to 1.5 hours on one night, the week the platform team moved it to larger machines. That is 70 percent. Tawfiq's own change was one join step, from 50 to 20 minutes. His job logs show that step. They do not show the machines.

<GoDeeper title="The arithmetic, step by step">

Change the hours to minutes so that both numbers use one unit. 5 hours is 300 minutes. 1.5 hours is 90 minutes. The whole run lost 300 minus 90, which is 210 minutes. And 210 divided by 300 is 0.7, so the cut is 70 percent. The percentage is correct.

Tawfiq's join step went from 50 minutes to 20 minutes. That is 30 minutes, and 30 divided by 50 is 0.6, so his step became 60 percent shorter. If both measurements come from the same night, his 30 minutes are one seventh of the 210 the run lost, because 30 times 7 is 210. So the 70 percent is a true number about the whole run, and only a small part of it is his.

There is one more careful point. The larger machines may have helped his join step as well. The logs show that the step got shorter. They do not show how much of that came from his rewrite and how much from the bigger machines. That is why an honest card for Tawfiq names the machines even next to his own step. You will fill that card in chapter 3.2.

</GoDeeper>
