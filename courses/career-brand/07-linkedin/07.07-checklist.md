## Your task: your second ten-second test

In module 1 you ran the ten-second test on your old resume, and you saved what the viewer remembered. This task is the same test on your new profile. It shows whether the rebuild worked. The ten seconds are a practice limit from this course, not a research finding. The result is not a grade. A mentor wants to see that you ran a fair test, compared it with the first one, and chose a next step.

Run the test with a different viewer than the first time if you can, so the result is not just memory. Open your profile on your phone and hand it over. Start a timer for ten seconds. Then take it back and ask what the viewer remembers. Write the answer in the viewer's words. If you have no partner, use a timer and look at the first screen yourself, close it, and write what you remember. A day after you last edited it is best.

Then write about 150 words. Say what was seen first in module 1, and what was seen now. Say what you will change next, one thing only. Paste your profile link, or the text if you have no account yet, and your new headline. Name the ledger row for each number on the profile. Say whom your three outreach drafts go to, by kind of tie, and how many notes you plan for the month. End with one sentence that confirms you use no automation, send no promotional invitations to strangers and need no Premium. You may use Areej's profile instead of your own. Say that you did.

Never paste your full name, phone number, email, address, ID numbers, passwords, keys, client or employer names, private company data or other people's details. A profile link is your public choice. If you prefer, paste the text with roles in place of names, such as "a parcel delivery company" or "my team lead".

My drafts go to a former lead, a classmate's brother two connections away, and a founder after a comment. I plan three notes this month. I use no automation, no promotional invitations and no Premium."

Look at the shape of his answer. It names both results and their difference. It chooses one change that follows from the test. Every number has a row, the drafts are described by kind of tie, and the note count is stated. The last sentence answers the rules.

Here is a weak answer. "My profile is much better now. People said it looks professional. I will keep improving it. I use LinkedIn the right way." It names nothing that was seen, no ledger row, no tie type and no change. The first test is not mentioned. A mentor cannot tell whether the profile changed, whether a rule is respected, or what to help with next. It also does not answer the question a founder would ask about any number on it: how was that measured?

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer part by part. Each part is Met, Partly or Not yet.

**Part 1: Profile rebuilt.**

- **Met:** Headline, About, Featured and Experience are rebuilt, and the mentor can open the link or read the text. Every number names a ledger row. Featured holds the case study link, or a dated plan to add it.
- **Partly:** One field is missing, or one number has no row.
- **Not yet:** The profile is not rebuilt, or the numbers have no source.

**Part 2: Test compared.**

- **Met:** Both results are named, what was seen first in module 1 and what was seen now, and the difference is explained in a sentence.
- **Partly:** Only one result is given.
- **Not yet:** There is no test.

**Part 3: Drafts.**

- **Met:** Three outreach drafts, each with one specific question and a note that fits the counter on your screen. Five comment drafts, each saying what it adds.
- **Partly:** There are fewer drafts, or the drafts are generic, such as "great post".
- **Not yet:** There are no drafts.

**Part 4: Rules respected.**

- **Met:** You use no automation, you send no promotional invitations to strangers, and nothing you did needs Premium. All of it is stated and agrees with your drafts.
- **Partly:** One point is unclear.
- **Not yet:** A rule is broken.

**Pass rule:** every part is at least Partly, and "Rules respected" must be Met.

Your mentor then replies with one question about your outreach drafts. It may be one of these. "Which of the three would you send first, and what is its one question?" Or: "What will you do if the person says no or does not reply?" Or: "Which of your notes could be read as promotional to a stranger, and how would you change it?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
