```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In these made-up cases, all the people and numbers are invented. Noura is a backend developer who is writing her LinkedIn headline. A founder will read only the first words in a search result. Which headline tells the founder what she does in the first words?"
options:
  - "Passionate engineer | Lifelong learner | Open to opportunities | Ready for my next big challenge now"
  - "Seeking my next challenge in a dynamic team where I can grow, learn and make a real difference"
  - "Software Engineer | Problem Solver | Team Player | Detail Oriented | Always eager to learn and grow"
  - "Backend developer (Go, PostgreSQL). I make slow reports fast, and I show the logs behind each fix."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "These are moods and a hope. 'Open to opportunities' is what the Open to Work setting is for, and the first words say nothing about what Noura does."
  - "A wish for a team and a challenge. The founder learns nothing about her role, her tools or her work."
  - "The role comes first, which is good, but 'Problem Solver' and 'Team Player' are labels. Nothing says what she does or how a reader could check it."
  - "Right. The role and the tools come first, then one sentence about what she does and how she shows it. It holds no number, because the numbers belong in About, where there is room for their window and source."
```

```quiz
question: "Saja, a DevOps engineer, is invited to a group whose members agree to like and re-share each other's posts every day. A friend says it is fine. Which statement of LinkedIn's rule is accurate, as taught in chapter 7.3 (checked 2026-10)?"
options:
  - "It is allowed while the group is small, because the rule only applies to large groups that trade likes for money or sell reach."
  - "It is allowed when nobody in the group uses a bot, because the rule only covers automated tools or scripts"
  - "The policies bar members from arranging ahead of time to like or re-share one another's posts, however small the group"
  - "It is allowed when every member has Premium, because paid members may arrange their activity as a group"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "No size limit appears in the policy. The group is a rule problem however many people join, and trading likes for money is not the test."
  - "A separate rule bans bots. This one is about the agreement itself: members must not agree ahead of time to like or re-share each other's content."
  - "Right. The policy names the agreement in advance, with no exception for a small group. LinkedIn does not use the word pod, but this is what a pod does."
  - "Premium does not change the policies. Amjad in chapter 7.1 paid for a month and still broke the rule on invitations."
```
