```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In these made-up cases, all the people are fictional. Ghaith is a web developer with four project ideas. Which one passes all four filter questions? The questions are: useful on day one to one known person or group, a result he can measure, depth he can explain in writing, and no need for two sides at once."
options:
  - "A food delivery marketplace for his town, with restaurants on one side and customers on the other, because demand for food is large"
  - "A booking sheet for his aunt's clinic that tracks missed appointments, with a written note on how it handles reminders and double bookings"
  - "A clone of a popular chat app, built to show that he can match the look and the features of a product people already use"
  - "A 'learn everything' dashboard that gathers courses, notes and goals in one place for any learner who wants to track progress"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A marketplace needs restaurants and customers at the same time, and neither side has a reason to join first. It fails the question about two sides, however large the demand is."
  - "Right. His aunt is a user on day one, and missed appointments are a number to count. Reminders and double bookings give him depth to write about, and only one side is needed."
  - "A clone can show skill, but it has no real user and nothing to measure, so it fails the first two questions. People already have the original, so a reader learns little new."
  - "This has no first user he can name and no number to move. 'Any learner' is not a small known group, so it fails the first two questions."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rafif, a mobile developer, wants to build an app that matches private tutors with students in her city. Which reshape makes it useful to one person or a small known group on day one?"
options:
  - "Build both sign-up flows first, then run an advertising push to fill both sides before anyone has seen the search and booking screens"
  - "Launch with made-up tutor profiles, so the first students see a good choice of tutors even though no real tutor has agreed to join yet"
  - "Build a booking and reminder tool for one tutor she already knows, and for her current students, and count lessons that are missed"
  - "Add more features, such as ratings, payments and a chat, so that both the tutors and the students want to join once the app is ready"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Both sides would still need to arrive at once, and that is the hard part. Advertising gives a tutor no reason to join before students are there, or the other way round."
  - "Made-up profiles are dishonest, and a student who books a tutor who does not exist loses trust. A real small group is the honest way to start."
  - "Right. One tutor and her current students can use it on the first day. Nobody waits for a second group, and missed lessons give her a number to count."
  - "More features do not solve the wait. Tutors still need students and students still need tutors, and each new feature adds work before anyone has used the app."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Munir, a data engineer, has 6 weeks at 4 hours a week for a stock tool. His guesses are: core flow with its low-stock alert 16 hours, CSV export 4 hours, second user role 9 hours, mobile view 10 hours. Which scope fits his time box and still lets one flow work from start to end?"
options:
  - "The core flow with its alert, and the CSV export"
  - "The core flow, a second user role and a mobile view"
  - "The core flow with its alert, the export and a second user role"
  - "The CSV export, a second user role and a mobile view"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The core flow and the export take 16 + 4 = 20 hours. The box is 6 x 4 = 24 hours, so this fits and leaves 4 hours of room."
  - "The core flow, the second role and the mobile view take 16 + 9 + 10 = 35 hours. That is 11 hours over the 24-hour box."
  - "The core flow, the export and the second role take 16 + 4 + 9 = 29 hours. That is 5 hours over the 24-hour box, and guesses are often too low."
  - "The export, the second role and the mobile view take 4 + 9 + 10 = 23 hours, which fits the box. But they leave out the core flow, so nothing works from start to end."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Eman, a junior developer, used an AI tool to draft a payment-reminder service. She will show the project to an interviewer. Which evidence best shows that she understands the code?"
options:
  - "A screenshot of her AI chat that shows each prompt she typed while the tool drafted the service for her over three evenings"
  - "A list of the extra features she shipped quickly because the tool made each one fast to add, with a screenshot of each feature"
  - "A line in the README that says she reviewed all of the generated code before she committed it, and that it works as expected"
  - "A short written explanation of the hardest part in her own words, plus one bug she found by testing and then fixed"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A chat of prompts shows what she asked, not what she understood. Anyone can type prompts, and the chat cannot tell a reader how any part of the service works."
  - "A feature list shows how much was built, and AI makes that quick. It does not show that she can explain or fix any of it."
  - "A line saying she reviewed everything is a claim, and nothing in it can be checked. A reader would still ask how the code works."
  - "Right. An explanation of the hardest part, in her own words, shows she knows how it works. A bug she found and fixed shows that she tested it and could repair it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rim, a student, asks an AI tool whether she can finish her project in a week. The tool replies: 'Yes. AI makes developers 55 percent faster, so a week is enough.' What is wrong with this answer?"
options:
  - "Nothing is wrong, because 55 percent is a proven speed-up that holds for any developer on any project of any size"
  - "Only the time is wrong. The 55 percent figure is reliable, so she should plan two weeks and not one, and then start building"
  - "The studies all agree that AI tools slow developers down, so the claim is backwards and she should avoid using AI tools"
  - "That figure came from one small task in one study, and other studies found different results, so it cannot promise a week"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The 55 percent figure came from one small task, writing a simple web server, in one study. It is not a promise for any project, and other studies found different results."
  - "The figure is not reliable for a new project. It came from one small task in one study, so scaling a plan from it is still guessing."
  - "The studies do not all agree. Some found faster work or more tasks done, and one found experienced developers slower. 'Always slower' is as unsupported as 'always faster'."
  - "Right. The number comes from one small task in one study, so it cannot promise a week for her project. Other studies found different results. The honest plan is a time box in hours and a log of what she checks."
```
