## Your task: the filter, the number and your first user

This task shows that your project is a plan a reader can check, and not a list of features. In module 5 you decide what to show, and the person you name here is the first one who will see it. Write about 150 words in four parts. First, answer the four filter questions in order, each with a reason that names a person, a number or a topic to write about. Second, name your first real user and say how or when you will reach them. Third, give the number you will move, where its baseline comes from, and how you will measure it after launch. Fourth, state your appetite as weeks times hours a week, your core flow, at least two items that are out of scope, and what your listed scope adds up to in hours. Use your own plan from chapter 4.5.

Here is a strong answer from a fictional fellow. Khalid is a web developer who volunteers at a small neighbourhood library. He wrote: "I will build a loan tracker for the library where my aunt volunteers. One, useful on day one: my aunt is the first user, and she writes every loan in an exercise book. Two, measurable: books more than 30 days overdue. The baseline is 12 in the last two months, counted from her exercise book with her on a Saturday. The tool will mark each overdue book, and I will compare the two months after launch and note school holidays. Three, depth: how to stop one book being lent to two people at once. Four, one side only: the volunteers use it, and readers do not log in. My first user is my aunt. I will sit with her this Saturday, and she will enter one week of loans by the end of week 3. Appetite: 5 weeks times 4 hours is 20 hours. Core flow: record a loan, the book shows as out, a list of overdue books appears. I guess 10 hours for the loan record and 6 for the overdue list, which is 16 hours. Out of scope: reader accounts, text reminders, a mobile app."

Look at the shape of his answer. Each question has a reason that a reader can check. The baseline has a source, and he says who counted it and how. His first user is a person he can reach, with a day. He does not promise a result. The listed scope adds up to 16 hours inside a box of 20.

Here is a weak answer. "I will build a social app for students to share notes. It passes all four questions because students need it. My first users will be students everywhere. It will make their lives much better and cut wasted time by 50 percent. I will build it in my spare time, with chat, groups, ratings and a mobile app." Asked "how was that measured?", the writer has nothing to say. "Because students need it" is not a reason. "Students everywhere" is not a person you can reach. The 50 percent has no baseline and no source, and it is a promise written as a fact. There is no appetite, so the features cannot be added up. Sharing notes also needs people who write notes and people who read them, which means two sides.

If you prefer Areej's case, use her plan from chapter 4.5: Raqim Stock for the owner of a print shop. Her four answers, her baseline of 5 stockouts in August and September 2025 from the owner's notebook, her 6 weeks at about 5 hours a week, her core flow, her three cuts and her week 3 milestone are all in that chapter. In her case the listed scope is the one core flow inside about 30 hours, so there is nothing more to add up. Say that you used her case.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer part by part. Each part is Met, Partly or Not yet. The parts are the same for your plan and for Areej's.

**Part 1: Filter applied.**

- **Met:** All four questions are answered, each with a reason that points at something specific, such as a person, a number or a topic to write about.
- **Partly:** Two or three questions are answered with reasons, or all four are answered with "yes" only.
- **Not yet:** Zero or one question is answered.

**Part 2: First real user.**

- **Met:** A specific person by role or relationship, or a known group with a size, whom you can reach, with a step or a date for reaching them.
- **Partly:** A group with no size or no way to reach it, such as "small businesses".
- **Not yet:** There is no user.

**Part 3: Number and measurement.**

- **Met:** A baseline with a source (or a plan to get one first), and how the number will be measured after launch.
- **Partly:** A number with no source and no plan.
- **Not yet:** There is no number.

**Part 4: Scope fits the appetite.**

- **Met:** The appetite is stated as weeks times hours, there is a core flow and at least two out-of-scope items, and the listed scope adds up to no more than the hours.
- **Partly:** The appetite is stated, but the scope is over by less than a quarter, or the out-of-scope items are missing.
- **Not yet:** There is no appetite.

**Pass rule:** every part is at least Partly, and "Filter applied" and "First real user" are Met.

Your mentor then replies with one question about your first user, for example: "How will you reach your first user this week?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit. There is no AI drill in this gate. The written task completes it.
