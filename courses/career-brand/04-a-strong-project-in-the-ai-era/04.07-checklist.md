## Your task: the filter, the number and your first user

This task shows that your project is a plan a reader can check, and not a list of features. In module 5 you decide what to show, and the person you name here is the first one who will see it. Write about 150 words in four parts. First, answer the four filter questions in order. Give each a reason that names a person, a number or a topic to write about. Second, name your first real user and say how or when you will reach them. Third, give the number you will move and where its baseline comes from. Say how you will measure it after launch. Fourth, state your appetite as weeks times hours a week, your core flow, and at least two items that are out of scope. Add what your listed scope comes to in hours. Use your own plan from chapter 4.5.

If you chose the open source plan in chapter 4.5, you still write the same four parts, about your contribution. For the four questions: it is useful on day one when a real reader of that page or a maintainer can use the fix, the result is a merge or a maintainer reply, the depth is what you learned about the project, and it needs no second side because the maintainer decides. Your first real user is the maintainer, or the people who read the page you fix, and you say how you will reach them, for example by commenting on the issue by a date. Your number is a merge or a maintainer reply, counted from the date you open the issue, and you say how you will check it. Your core flow is the one small change, and you list at least two things you will not do, such as a new feature or a large rewrite. Your hours for the change, the tests or docs and the discussion add up to no more than your box.

A fictional fellow shows what a strong answer sounds like. Khalid is a web developer who volunteers at a small neighbourhood library. He wrote: "I will build a loan tracker for the library where my aunt volunteers. One, useful on day one: my aunt is the first user, and she writes every loan in an exercise book. Two, measurable: books more than 30 days overdue. The baseline is 12 in the last two months, counted from her exercise book with her on a Saturday. The tool will mark each overdue book, and I will compare the two months after launch and note school holidays. Three, depth: how to stop one book being lent to two people at once. Four, one side only: the volunteers use it, and readers do not log in. My first user is my aunt. I will sit with her this Saturday, and she will enter one week of loans by the end of week 3. Appetite: 5 weeks times 4 hours is 20 hours. Core flow: record a loan, the book shows as out, a list of overdue books appears. I guess 10 hours for the loan record, 6 for the overdue list and 2 for tests and a README, which is 18 hours. Out of scope: reader accounts, text reminders, a mobile app."

Notice what makes it work. Each question has a reason that a reader can check. The baseline has a source, and he says who counted it and how. His first user is a person he can reach, with a day. He does not promise a result. The listed scope, with tests and a README, adds up to 18 hours inside a box of 20.

Now compare a weak answer. "I will build a social app for students to share notes. It passes all four questions because students need it. My first users will be students everywhere. It will make their lives much better and cut wasted time by 50 percent. I will build it in my spare time, with chat, groups, ratings and a mobile app." Asked "how was that measured?", the writer has nothing to say. "Because students need it" is not a reason. "Students everywhere" is not a person you can reach. The 50 percent has no baseline and no source, and it is a promise written as a fact. There is no appetite, so the features cannot be added up. Sharing notes also needs people who write notes and people who read them, which means two sides.

If you prefer Areej's case, use her plan from chapter 4.5: Raqim Stock for the owner of a print shop. Her four answers are in that chapter. So are her baseline of 5 stockouts in August and September 2025 from the owner's notebook and her 6 weeks at about 5 hours a week. Her core flow, her three cuts and her week 3 milestone are there too. Her in-scope guesses are 18 hours for the core flow, with the low-stock warning inside it, and 5 hours for tests, a README and demo data. That is 23 hours inside about 30. Say that you used her case.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer part by part. Each part is Met, Partly or Not yet. The parts are the same for your plan, for Areej's and for an open source plan. On the open source route, "user" means the maintainer or the readers of the page you fix, "number" means a merge or a maintainer reply counted from the date you open the issue, and "core flow" means the one small change.

**Part 1: Filter applied.**

- **Met:** All four questions are answered, each with a reason that points at something specific, such as a person, a number or a topic to write about.
- **Partly:** Two or three questions are answered with reasons, or all four are answered with "yes" only.
- **Not yet:** Zero or one question is answered.

**Part 2: First real user.**

- **Met:** A specific person by role or relationship, or a known group with a size, whom you can reach, with a step or a date for reaching them.
- **Partly:** A group with no size or no way to reach it, such as "small businesses".
- **Not yet:** There is no user.

**Part 3: Number and measurement.**

- **Met:** A baseline with a source (or a plan to get one first), and how the number will be measured after launch. On the open source route: the start date, and how you will check for a merge or a reply.
- **Partly:** A number with no source and no plan.
- **Not yet:** There is no number.

**Part 4: Scope fits the appetite.**

- **Met:** The appetite is stated as weeks times hours. There is a core flow (on the open source route, the one small change) and at least two out-of-scope items. The listed scope adds up to no more than the hours.
- **Partly:** The appetite is stated, but the scope is over by less than a quarter, or the out-of-scope items are missing.
- **Not yet:** There is no appetite.

**Pass rule:** every part is at least Partly, and "Filter applied" and "First real user" are Met.

Your mentor then replies with one to three questions, usually starting with your first user. For example: "How will you reach your first user this week?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. The gate is only finished after you answer that reply, so check back for it. There is no AI drill in this gate. The written task completes it.
