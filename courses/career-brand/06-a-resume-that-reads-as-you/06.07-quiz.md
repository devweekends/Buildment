```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In this made-up case, Sadeel, a final-year student, has one strong internship result and one study project, and wants a one-page resume. Which section order puts his proof first?"
options:
  - "Education first, then a skills list, then the internship and the study project, because that is the usual habit"
  - "A skills block of every tool he has touched, then the internship, then the study project, then his education"
  - "Name and links, a two-line summary, the internship result, the project, skills in one line, education"
  - "Everything in date order with the oldest first: school, then the study project, then the internship, then skills"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is the traditional habit. It puts education and a skills list ahead of the one result that proves something, so his proof comes late."
  - "A block of every tool is a list of names. It fills the first screen and pushes the internship result below it."
  - "Right. His strongest proof, the internship result, comes right after the summary. The skills shrink to one line, and education is a plain fact near the end."
  - "Date order puts the oldest and weakest item first. The reader meets school before any work that proves something."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tuqa, a support engineer, wrote a script that sorts incoming tickets. Her ledger row says open tickets fell from 120 to 45, counted weekly for 8 weeks before and 8 weeks after, and her manager set the priorities. Which bullet shows what she owned, the change with before and after, the window and her share?"
options:
  - "Responsible for reducing the ticket backlog through process improvements, better use of the team's tools and regular weekly reviews"
  - "Cut open tickets from 120 to 45 (weekly counts, 8 weeks before and after) with a triage script; my manager set the priorities"
  - "Cut the backlog by over half through innovative scripting that made the whole support process much faster for everyone"
  - "We cut open tickets from 120 to 45 as a team, using a triage script that sorted all of the incoming tickets each day"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This is a duty. It says what Tuqa was supposed to do and gives no change, no number and no window."
  - "Right. It names what she owned, the before and the after, the window, how, and her manager's part."
  - "'Over half' is close to the facts, but it gives no starting count, no window and no source. 'Innovative' and 'faster for everyone' cannot be checked."
  - "'We' with no window hides what Tuqa did. A reader cannot tell her part from the team's, or how long it was measured."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Samer, a junior developer, hears a friend say: '75 percent of resumes never reach a human, so stuff in the posting's keywords.' Which reply is honest and useful?"
options:
  - "That figure has no source. People set the rules and decide, so reorder real evidence for the reader and add no word you cannot back with proof."
  - "You are right, so hide the posting's keywords in white text on the page, where only the software can see them and no person ever will."
  - "You are right, so paste the keywords, but only into the skills block, where a person is less likely to look at them closely at all."
  - "You are wrong, because employers never use sorting software, so no change to a resume could ever matter to how it is read."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. One consultant who traced the 75 percent story reports that it came from a sales pitch, not a study. Some employers do use filters, but people set them and decide, so the useful move is to reorder true evidence."
  - "Hidden white text is dishonest, and nothing shows it works. It also accepts a figure that has no source."
  - "Pasting words into the skills block adds no proof. The figure also has no study behind it."
  - "Some employers do sort and filter with software. The honest reply is not that it never happens, but that people set the rules and decide."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Dunya, a backend developer, applies to a logistics startup. The posting asks for PostgreSQL performance work. She has a ledger row for a query she sped up. Which tailored version is honest?"
options:
  - "Paste the posting's list of requirements into a new skills block at the top, so that the match is easy to see"
  - "Rewrite every bullet in the posting's own phrases, so that each line sounds like it was written for this job"
  - "Add the posting's tools to her skills line, including two she has not used yet, so that her skills match"
  - "Move the query bullet first, start the summary with her backend focus, and back every line with a row"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Pasting the requirements adds the posting's words and no proof. The reader learns nothing new about her."
  - "Rewriting in the posting's phrases copies their words. Some lines may stop matching her ledger rows."
  - "Adding tools she has not used is dishonest. If she cannot back a line with proof, the line should come out."
  - "Right. Moving true lines first and changing the summary is real tailoring. Each line still has a row behind it, and no new claim is added."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Fayez, a data engineer, has a ledger row: a nightly job fell from 50 to 20 minutes (job logs, 14 nights before and after). He rewrote one join, and his lead reviewed it. An AI tool rewrote his bullet as 'Spearheaded a transformative initiative that boosted pipeline efficiency 10x across the platform.' What is wrong with it?"
options:
  - "Nothing is wrong, because the wording is more professional than his own and the number from the ledger is kept"
  - "Only the word 'Spearheaded' is a problem, and the rest is a fair summary of what his ledger row says about the nightly job"
  - "Empty phrases, a 10x figure that is really 2.5 times, a scope wider than one job, and a lost window, source and share"
  - "It should add a bigger number to look stronger, because a larger figure draws more attention from readers who skim a large pile"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The number is not kept. The row says 50 to 20 minutes, which is 2.5 times, not 10x. Smoother wording does not make a figure true."
  - "'Spearheaded' is empty, but it is not the only problem. The false figure, the wider scope and the missing window, source and share are worse."
  - "Right. The tool added empty phrases, a figure that is not in the row, a scope of the whole platform, and it dropped the window, the source and his share."
  - "A bigger number that is not in the ledger is an invented number. It would fail the first question, 'how was that measured?'."
```
