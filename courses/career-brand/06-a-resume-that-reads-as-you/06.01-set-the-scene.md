## Lines that could be anyone's

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m6-lines-anyones" />

Waed is an operations coordinator on the logistics team of an NGO, a non-profit organisation. Her resume says "Responsible for coordinating shipments". An AI tool wrote her summary. Three other applicants send resumes with almost the same words. The reader opens all four and cannot tell them apart. Nothing on them is false. Nothing on them is hers alone.

<GoDeeper title="Why these lines are everywhere">

A line that names a duty was common long before AI tools. A duty is what the job asked for. Every person in the same job can write it, so it tells the reader very little about one person.

AI tools make this worse in one way: they make a smooth duty line cost almost nothing. Module 1 explained the result. When anyone can produce a smooth paragraph in a minute, a smooth paragraph proves less. What still proves something is a line that a reader can check, and that only one person could write.

This is not a reason to blame Waed, or the tool. She wrote down what her job was. She has not yet written down what changed because she did it.

</GoDeeper>

## The work that is missing

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m6-missing-tracker" />

Waed also built a shared tracker for her team. Missed delivery confirmations fell from 90 in the three months before to 18 in the three months after, counted from the team's tracker. The team also began a daily check in those months. None of this is on her resume. The result is real but missing.

<GoDeeper title="How Waed counted">

Waed did not estimate her result from memory. She counted the missed delivery confirmations from the team's own tracker.

For the three months before, the tracker showed 90 missed confirmations, which is 30 a month. For the three months after, it showed 18, which is 6 a month. That is a fall of 72 in three months. Because the counts come from the team's tracker, a reader can ask to see them.

Two other facts keep the result honest. The team also began a short daily check of open shipments in the same months. Her part was to build the tracker and do the counting. The team's part was to fill it in every day and run the daily check. So the count shows what changed, and it does not give the tracker all the credit.

A good resume line keeps these parts: what changed, the numbers before and after, how long she counted, where the numbers live, and who else changed things. This module shows you how, one part at a time.

</GoDeeper>
