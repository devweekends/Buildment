```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In this made-up case, Taha is a DevOps engineer who keeps a release log with a count of rollbacks for each quarter. His numbers here are illustrative. He drafts four pillar statements for his career arc. Which one is backed by a proof and a number?"
options:
  - "Releases that hold: rollbacks fell from 6 to 2 a quarter over two quarters (release log; my pre-release checklist)"
  - "Reliable, calm and a strong team player who stays steady when a release goes wrong for the people around him"
  - "Expert in cloud tooling and automation, with deep working knowledge of pipelines, containers and scripts"
  - "Certified on three cloud platforms, which shows how deep his knowledge of release work and of running systems goes now"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It names a plain strength and gives a number, a window and a source. It also says what he did, so a stranger knows where to look."
  - "Reliable, calm and a team player are adjectives. Nothing in the statement can be looked up or tested, so it has no proof and no number."
  - "A tool pillar says what he has touched, not what he did or what changed. 'Expert' is also a claim about him that a reader cannot check."
  - "A certificate shows that he finished some training. It names no project and no result, so a reader has nothing to check."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In this made-up case, Ranya is a backend developer. Her role card says 'we built the checkout service'. In her notes, she wrote the order validation and its tests, her lead designed the service, and the team deployed it. Which rewrite is honest about who did what?"
options:
  - "I built the checkout service, with its order validation, its tests and the way it was put live for customers"
  - "We built the checkout service as one team, and strong teamwork made the whole project a success for us all"
  - "I contributed to the checkout service, working closely with my lead and the team through the whole project"
  - "I wrote the order validation and its tests; my lead designed the whole service and the team deployed it for customers"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This claims the whole service. Her lead designed it and the team deployed it, so the sentence takes their part and gives it to her."
  - "It is still 'we'. A reader cannot tell what Ranya did, and 'strong teamwork' is an opinion with nothing to check."
  - "'Contributed' is a vague share. It hides what she did, so a reader has nothing to ask about and cannot tell her part from anyone else's."
  - "Right. It names her two pieces of work and then names her lead's part and the team's part. A reader knows who did what and can ask about each."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In this made-up case, Qais is a security analyst who runs a weekly chess club for school children and wants to use that true detail. Where is the best single place for it?"
options:
  - "In the resume bullet about his best project, so a reader meets the chess club beside his main result"
  - "One line in his About text, the place where a reader looks for the person and can ask about it"
  - "In the case study of his best project, to make the story warmer and keep the reader reading on to the end"
  - "Nowhere, because personal details never help and a kit should hold only items that can be checked"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A bullet is for a result. The chess club would take room from the number and hide it, so the detail distracts there."
  - "Right. An About text is where a reader looks for the person. One true line there gives the reader something to remember and ask about."
  - "A reader who opens a case study came for the problem and the numbers. A hobby in the middle delays both."
  - "This goes too far. One true, small detail in the right place helps a reader see a person. It only hurts where the reader looks for proof."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In this made-up case, Najla is a data analyst who asked three people for a time she did well. Reply 1: she rewrote a handover note so a new hire ran the job on day two. Reply 2: she turned a messy export into a template that others reused. Reply 3: she explained a filter mistake kindly and showed the fix, and once ran the Friday review when the lead was out. Which theme repeats across all three replies?"
options:
  - "She works late to hit every deadline, which each of the three replies describes in different words"
  - "She knows a wide range of tools, and each of the three replies lists the ones she uses in her work"
  - "She makes her work easy for other people to pick up, use and reuse without needing her to be there at all"
  - "She leads the team, as the third reply shows most clearly, and the others hint at it in their stories"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "None of the three replies mentions late work or deadlines. A theme has to appear in what the people said, not in what sounds good."
  - "No reply names a tool. They describe what she did for the people who came after her, so tools are not the theme."
  - "Right. A handover note, a reusable template and a kind explanation with a fix all make her work easy for others to use. It is in all three stories."
  - "Only the third reply mentions running a review. Leadership appears in one reply, so it is a detail, not the theme that repeats."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "In this made-up case, Jood is a backend developer who pastes her own notes into an AI tool to build her career arc. The tool adds 'led a team of five' and 'expert in Kubernetes'. Neither is in her notes. What is wrong?"
options:
  - "It added a role and a skill not in her notes; AI may restructure her material but never add to it"
  - "Nothing is wrong, because those phrases are normal for her job title and a founder expects to see them"
  - "Only the length is wrong, because the tool made the arc longer than it needs to be while the content is fine"
  - "The mistake is using an AI tool on her own notes at all, since the rule does not allow any tool in the arc"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. A tool may sort and shorten her notes and ask questions. It may not add a role, a skill, a number or a result."
  - "A phrase can be normal for a title and still be false for her. A founder will ask about the team of five, and she will have no answer."
  - "The content is the problem. Two facts appear that her notes do not support, and cutting the length would not remove them."
  - "The rule allows a tool to restructure her own notes. The mistake is not the tool, it is the two lines that the tool added."
```
