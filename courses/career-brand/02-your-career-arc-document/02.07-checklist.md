## Your task: one pillar, two proofs, and what stays private

This task checks the centre of your career arc. Can you name a strength and show it twice? And do you know which parts of your arc must stay private? A pillar with two proofs is the part that every later module uses. In module 3 you turn each proof into a claim with a source. In module 6 the claims become resume lines. If this step is honest now, those steps are easier.

Write about 120 words, in plain sentences. You may type your answer or speak it. State one pillar of your career arc in one plain line. It names a strength and the kind of work, with no word like best, expert or world-class, and no personality label. Then give its two proofs. For each proof, say where it lives, such as a dashboard, a timing sheet, a plan, a pull request or a message count. Also say what part was yours and what part belonged to others. At least one proof should carry a number with its window, or an honest line such as "I did not measure that, and here is how I would". Last, list what in your arc stays private and why, in a few words each.

Here is a strong answer from a fictional fellow. Hanadi is a support engineer, and her numbers are illustrative. She wrote: "My pillar is that I turn repeated support questions into short guides, and I count what changes. Proof 1 is a password reset guide. Password tickets averaged 42 a month over the two months before it and 15 a month over the two months after. The numbers live in the ticket system's monthly report. I wrote the guide, and my lead reviewed it and put it on the help page. Proof 2 is a checklist for new accounts. Setup mistakes fell from 8 in the month before to 3 in the month after. I counted them in my own tracking sheet, and I wrote the checklist myself. Private: customer names in tickets, because they belong to customers. My employer's name, because I have not asked. The monthly ticket total is ask first, because my lead owns it."

Look at the shape of her answer. The pillar is a plain line with a habit and a method. Each proof says what happened, where the number lives and whose part it was. The first proof names her lead's part, so a reader trusts her part. The second proof is honest about its weakness: one month and her own sheet. The private list gives a reason for every item. Nothing in it is invented, and every claim can be followed to a place.

Here is a weak answer. Sulaf wrote: "My pillar is that I am a hard worker who is great with people. Proof 1: I worked on many projects and we improved things a lot. Proof 2: my manager said I did well. It lives in my work. Private: everything, because it is personal." The pillar is two adjectives. The proofs have no number and no place, so "how was that measured?" has no answer. The word "we" hides what Sulaf did, so "what was your part?" has no answer either. A manager's praise is a reply, not a proof. And "everything is private" gives a mentor nothing to check, because no reason is given for any item. Each of these is easy to fix with the cards from chapter 2.3.

If you prefer to use Areej's case, take her first pillar, "I make slow things fast, and I measure it". Her proofs are the orders report from her internship and the events cache from Campus Board. Chapters 2.2 and 2.3 tell you where each number lives and who did which steps. Then list what in her arc stays private and why. Use roles in place of names if you want to: "a parcel delivery company" and "my lead".

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor reads your answer part by part. Each part is Met, Partly or Not yet. Use this list to check your own answer before you submit it.

**Part 1: Pillar stated.**

- **Met:** One plain line names a strength and the kind of work, with no superlative and no personality label.
- **Partly:** The strength is buried in adjectives or a list of tools.
- **Not yet:** There is no pillar.

**Part 2: Two proofs.**

- **Met:** Two different pieces of work, each with where it lives, and at least one number or an honest "not measured" line.
- **Partly:** One proof, or two proofs with no place where they live.
- **Not yet:** No proof.

**Part 3: Ownership.**

- **Met:** Each proof says what you did apart from what others did, or says "alone".
- **Partly:** One proof blurs your part into "we".
- **Not yet:** Both proofs say only "we".

**Part 4: Privacy.**

- **Met:** At least two items are named as private, each with a reason of a few words.
- **Partly:** One item, or items with no reason.
- **Not yet:** None.

**Pass rule:** every part is at least Partly, and "Two proofs" is Met.

Your mentor then replies with one question, usually about your weaker proof. For example: "Where exactly does proof 2 live, and could you show it to me?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit. "I did not measure that" is a full and honest answer when it is true. Never invent a place or a number to look prepared. A mentor can tell, and an after-the-fact reason is the quickest way to lose trust.
