Ruqaya is a fictional fellow. Her story is made up, but her question is real: what are you best at, and how would anyone know? Before you write a resume, you need an answer that is more than a list of tools.

<LearningObjectives objectives={["Say why a list of tools does not answer 'what are you best at?'", "Choose two or three strengths and back each one with a proof and a number a stranger could check", "Write one private document, your career arc, that every later piece of your kit is drawn from"]} />

## "What are you best at?"

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m2-best-at-what" />

Ruqaya is a product designer with three years of work, first at an agency and then as a freelancer. On a first call, a founder asks what she is best at and how anyone would know. She names her tools. Each tool is real. None of them answers the question, and the founder is still unsure.

<GoDeeper title="Why a list of tools is a weak answer">

A tool name says what Ruqaya has used. It does not say what she did for anyone, or what changed because of it. The founder cannot test it. The only choice is to believe it or not.

You met this idea in module 1. A line a reader can look up or try is a line they can check. A skill word with nothing behind it is only a claim. "Skilled in design tools" is a claim. "Turned forty pages of interview notes into one page the team used in planning" is a line a person can ask about.

None of this says tools are unimportant. A posting may list them, and your kit should show them. The point is the order. First you gather what you did. Then the tools appear inside those stories, where they have a job to do.

</GoDeeper>

## Three people, one story

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m2-three-replies" />

After the call, Ruqaya asks three colleagues for a time she did something well. A designer, a researcher and a manager reply. None names a tool. All three describe the same thing: messy research turned into one clear page. What repeats is her first pillar: a strength you can back with proof.
