## Start with the result

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m5-start-with-result" />

Watch the first frame. A viewer opens a demo, and it shows the payoff: what the product does for the person who uses it. A logo intro would show nothing yet. In the first seconds, the viewer should be able to say what the product is for. Keep the logo for the last frame, or leave it out.

<GoDeeper title="Why the result comes first">

This is the course's method, not a research finding. A viewer decides early whether to keep watching, and a logo or a title gives them nothing to decide with. A frame that shows the result gives them a reason to stay.

Try a quick test on your own plan. Cover everything except the first frame. Could a stranger say what the product does for someone? If the answer is "it has a name and a colour", change the first frame.

The 60 to 120 second range is also a course rule. It is long enough to show the core of a small project and short enough that a reader can give it a moment of their day.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="If your project has no screen">

Many fellows build an API, a script or a data job, so there is no screen with a warning on it. The result is still something a person can see.

Show the output a user would receive. It can be a clear response to one request, a generated file opened on screen, or a chart of the before and after. For a speed fix, the first frame can be the two numbers side by side, with their source written under them. For a script, it can be the folder of finished files. Choose the picture of the result, then show how you got it.

</GoDeeper>

## Cut the filler, keep the core

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m5-demo-storyboard" />

Six frames compete for 90 seconds. Tap a frame, then move it up or down. The logo, the sign-up and the settings tour take 55 seconds and show little. Move the warning, the sale and the invoice up, and all three fit. The three filler frames are pushed past the 90-second line, so they are cut. No frame got shorter.

<GoDeeper title="Why 60 to 120 seconds, and what the research says">

The range is a course rule. One study is sometimes used to support a number: it looked at 6.9 million video-watching sessions on one online course platform. It found that engagement was close to complete for videos shorter than 6 minutes, and fell as videos got longer. The authors advised shorter segments.

That study is about lecture videos, and we could read only a summary of it. It supports the general idea that shorter is better. It does not say how long a demo should be. So do not repeat the advice as "keep your demo under 6 minutes because engagement drops". The honest sentence is: keep the demo to one or two minutes, and start with the result.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="Areej's 90-second storyboard">

Areej's storyboard for Raqim Stock is a plan, because she has not built the tool yet. It has five parts, in this order. The low-stock warning takes 15 seconds. Recording a sale, with the paper count going down, takes 20. The hard part, two sales at once, takes 25. The invoice export takes 15. A closing card takes 15 and shows the link to the case study and what is not finished.

The sum is 15 + 20 + 25 + 15 + 15 = 90 seconds. The picture shows only the three core frames, which cost 15, 20 and 15 seconds. The hard part and the closing card come from her full plan, and the next section explains the hard part.

What she cut is as important as what she kept. The logo intro is gone. The sign-up is gone, because the demo account opens with one tap. The settings tour is gone, because a tour of menus shows little.

</GoDeeper>

## Show the hard part, not a tour

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m5-show-the-hard-part" />

Watch both sales reach the count. Two sales arrive at the same moment and reach one stock count. The count stays right because the update runs in one transaction, a group of database steps that run as one piece of work. Look at this part closely. A tour of menus proves little. The core flow and one hard detail make a reader ask a question, and a question is interest.

<GoDeeper title="Areej's hard part">

In Areej's plan, the hard part of Raqim Stock is two sales arriving at the same moment. Her AI-use log template has an example entry for it. The entry reads:

> AI suggested a stock update without a transaction; I tested two sales at once and saw the count go wrong; I wrapped it in a transaction.

That entry is what she expects to log. She has not built the tool, so it has not happened yet.

So the plan for that part of the demo is to show two sales arriving together, with the count staying correct once the update is in a transaction. It will show that she understood the problem, and that she tests an AI tool's suggestion instead of trusting it.

Find the hard part of your own project. It is often the bug that took you longest, the decision you changed your mind about, or the case that is easy to get wrong. If an AI tool wrote part of the code, show the part you checked and corrected.

</GoDeeper>

## Captions, because many watch muted

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m5-captions" />

Watch how captions keep the viewer with the demo. Some viewers will watch with the sound off, on a phone, in a public place or on a shared connection. Captions put the words on the screen, so the demo still makes sense. They also help viewers who cannot hear. Keep each caption short, and say what is happening now.

<GoDeeper title="How to write captions that help">

Write the script first, then use it as your captions. One idea in each caption, on one or two short lines. Leave each one on screen long enough to read it slowly. Say what is happening, such as "Two sales arrive at the same moment." Do not announce what you are about to do.

Check them the way a viewer will. Watch your own demo on your phone with the sound off. If you lose the story anywhere, add or change a caption there.

Below the video, add a short text version of the same steps. A reader who cannot play the video, or who is on weak data, can still follow it. That text is also a plain way to make the page easier for everyone.

</GoDeeper>

## Screenshot first on weak data

<InteractiveCanvas slug="career-m5-screenshot-first" />

Watch the reader choose. A reader on weak data opens a light page. It shows a screenshot first, and a link that says "watch the 90-second demo" with the file size beside it. Nothing plays until the reader taps. The reader chooses to spend the data. Nothing is spent for them.

<GoDeeper title="A light demo page">

Many readers, and many fellows, use weak or costly data. A page that starts a video on its own can use a lot of it before the reader has decided to watch. So follow four rules. Do not autoplay. Put a screenshot of the core first. State the real size of the file, in megabytes, next to the link. Give a text path, which is the captions or the short list of steps.

This is a rule for this course, not a research finding. It also protects you. If the video is blocked or slow, the reader still sees the screenshot and the steps.

Put the recording where it opens without a sign-in, and test the link on your phone before you share it. Chapter 5.6 asks you to include the screenshot and the recording in your showcase plan as the base layer.

</GoDeeper>
