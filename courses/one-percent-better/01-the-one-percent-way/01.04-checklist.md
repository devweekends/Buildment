## Module 1 checklist

- [ ] I can explain why the most consistent deeds, even if small, are the most beloved to Allah (Sahih al-Bukhari 6465)
- [ ] I know that the five prayers are obligatory in full from today, and that the course grows how I pray them
- [ ] I can choose a first habit with the three questions: what I nearly do, what it attaches to, and its smallest version
- [ ] I can tell showing off from being seen, and I know the cure is a renewed intention, not a dropped deed
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 1.2 and 1.3
- [ ] I submitted my starting plan below

## Learning log

1. **The big start:** Think of a time you tried to change many things at once, in worship or anything else. What happened on the first hard day? If you prefer, answer about Hamza's nine days in chapter 1.1.
2. **Your easiest win:** Which habit on your list do you nearly do already? What makes it easier than the others?
3. **Intention:** Where do you most feel the pull to be seen when you do something good? What line will you say to yourself in that moment?
4. **Sharing:** Who in your life could benefit from the one-habit-a-week idea, and how would you share it without sharing your own score?

## Your starting plan

This task turns module 1 into a plan you can start tomorrow. You do not need to know every habit yet, and you do not need to plan all thirteen weeks. You need a list, a first habit that is set up to succeed, and a clear rule that keeps the whole effort for Allah.

You may write about your own habits, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded, and your mentor will never ask to see your tracker.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Ali is a support engineer who works night shifts three times a week. His list has nine of the eleven habits in this course and one of his own: sending salawat on the Prophet on the drive home. His first habit is Dhuhr with the first takbir at the mosque near his flat, because on non-shift days he already prays Dhuhr there most of the time. Its anchor is the adhan on the mosque speaker, which he can hear from his desk at home. His smallest version, for days he wakes late after a shift, is to pray Dhuhr at the start of its time at home, then go to the mosque the next day. His intention line is: "I want to pray as the Prophet prayed, for Allah." His privacy rule: the tracker is a paper card in his drawer, he shares nothing about it online, and if his brother asks, he will explain the method only.

Look at the shape of Ali's answer. The first habit is one he nearly does already. It has a real anchor that happens at a known time, and a smallest version for his hardest days, which are tied to his real life. His intention is one sentence he can say. His privacy rule names where the tracker lives and what he will share.

A weak answer would say: "I will start all the habits and try my best, inshallah, and I will keep my intention pure." It is sincere, but it has no first habit, no anchor, no smallest version and no rule. It is Hamza's September list.

One more thing before you write. Your first habit does not need to be the most important one on your list. It needs to be the one most likely to go well this week. A good first week matters more than an impressive first choice, because it is the week that teaches you the plan works. The harder habits, such as Fajr for many people, come later, when their own preparation is ready and the easier habits are already carrying you.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own habits, use this one. Maryam is a 31-year-old teacher and the mother of a two-year-old. She prays all five prayers at home. Fajr and Isha are usually on time; Dhuhr and Asr are often late because of school. She reads the Quran only in Ramadan. She knows Ayat al-Kursi and a few adhkar by heart. Her husband leaves for work at 7:30, and her daughter sleeps at 20:00. Maryam wants to build daily Quran, the morning adhkar, the adhkar after prayers and Surah al-Mulk. Last year she posted daily "Quran journey" photos online, then felt uneasy about it and stopped reading altogether. Write Maryam's starting plan.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your plan with the table below. The rubric grades your plan only, never your faith or your personal circumstances.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| The list | Habits listed, each with something it could attach to | A list, but no anchors | No list |
| First habit | One habit you nearly do already, with a named anchor | A first habit, but not one you nearly do, or no anchor | No first habit, or all habits at once |
| Smallest version | A smallest version for the hardest day, with its size | A smallest version that is vague ("try a bit") | None |
| Intention | One sentence you can say when the pull to be seen comes | A general wish for a pure intention | No intention named |
| Privacy rule | Where the tracker lives, who sees it, and what you will share | One of the three | None, or a plan to post the score |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example, they may ask what you will do if your anchor moves, or what your smallest version is on a travel day. You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.
