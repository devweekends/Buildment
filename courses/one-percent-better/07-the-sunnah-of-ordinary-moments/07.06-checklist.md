## Module 7 checklist

- [ ] I know the dua on waking (Sahih al-Bukhari 6312) and use a miswak or brush after waking
- [ ] I know the duas for entering and leaving the washroom (Sahih al-Bukhari 142; Jami' at-Tirmidhi 7)
- [ ] I know the dua for getting dressed (Sunan Abi Dawud 4023) and start with the right
- [ ] I know the dua for leaving home (Sunan Abi Dawud 5095) and for entering and leaving the mosque (Sahih Muslim 713)
- [ ] I say Bismillah and give salam when I enter my home (Sahih Muslim 2018; Quran 24:61)
- [ ] I eat with Bismillah, the right hand, from in front of me, and say Alhamdulillah at the end
- [ ] I drink in breaths, outside the cup, sitting when I can
- [ ] I spread the salam, reply to a sneeze in three lines, and visit or call the sick with the Prophet's dua
- [ ] I keep durood and istighfar at a cue I chose
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 7.2 to 7.4, and finished the quiz in 7.5
- [ ] I submitted my moments plan below

## Learning log

1. **Your forty minutes:** Walk through your own morning from waking to leaving. How many Sunnah moments did you find? Which do you already keep?
2. **The weak and the sound:** Did any dua or saying you knew turn out to be weak? How did you feel, and what will you use instead?
3. **At the table:** Who eats with you most often? Which manner could you start together?
4. **Between people:** Who could you greet with salam, or visit, this week, that you usually would not?

## Your plan for ordinary moments

This task turns module 7 into a plan for weeks, not a list for one day. There are more than fifteen habits in this module. Nobody should add them all at once. Your plan chooses an order, one or two a week, attached to the moments of your own day, with a card or reminder at each cue until it is learned.

You may write about your own day, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Saba is a university student who lives in a hostel. Her order: week one, "Ghufranak" on leaving the washroom, because it is one word and happens several times a day; week two, the washroom entering dua; week three, Bismillah and Alhamdulillah at hostel meals; week four, the leaving-home dua at the hostel gate; week five, the waking dua; week six, the dua for dressing; week seven, the sneeze exchange with her roommates; week eight, istighfar at the lift. Each habit has its source next to it. Her cues: a sticker on the inside of the washroom door, a note on her water bottle, the hostel gate itself. Her minimum for exam weeks: keep the habits she has, add nothing new. She also notes that one popular dua on her old list, for entering the home, is weak, and she uses Bismillah and salam instead.

Look at the shape of Saba's answer. It is ordered by how often each moment happens and how short the words are. Every habit has a cue in her real day and a sound source. It has a rule for hard weeks, and it shows she checked one item on her old list.

A weak answer would say: "I will learn all the daily duas and say them every day." It is sincere, but it has no order, no cues and no sources. In a week, most of them would be forgotten.

A family can make one shared plan. If you live with others, consider choosing the meal habits together, since a whole table can keep each other on track. Children often enjoy being the one who reminds everyone.

Before you write, walk through your own day once, from waking to sleeping, and write down every moment from this module that happens to you. Most people find between twelve and twenty. Then mark the three that happen most often. Those three are usually the best start, because they give you the most practice in the first weeks.

If any habit in your plan comes from a list, an app or a family tradition rather than from this module, check its source first. Write the collection and number next to it. If you cannot find a sound source, keep the good meaning in your own words as a dua, but do not present it to others as the Prophet's words.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own day, use this one. Kashif is 34, a delivery driver who leaves home at 7:00 and returns at 19:00. He lives with his wife and two young children. He says Bismillah before meals but nothing else from this module. He eats lunch alone in his van. He visits his elderly father every Sunday. He wants to start "all the Sunnah duas". Write Kashif's plan for the next eight weeks.

Remember that the habits of ordinary moments are easy to lose without noticing, because nobody sees whether you said a dua at the washroom door. Your tracker from module 15 can hold them as a single line, "moment duas", with the number you said today, so that you can see whether the habit is growing or fading.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order | One or two habits a week, ordered with a reason | An order with no reason | All at once, or no order |
| Cues | Each habit tied to a moment in the real day, with a card or reminder | Some habits with cues | No cues |
| Sources | Each habit with its source | Some sources | None, or a weak one presented as sound |
| Hard weeks | A rule for busy or hard weeks | A vague rule | None |
| People | At least one habit between people (salam, sneeze, the sick) | Mentioned without a plan | None |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
