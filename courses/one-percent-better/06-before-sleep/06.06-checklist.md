## Learning log

1. **Your nights:** What usually happens in the last half hour before you sleep? What is the real competitor of the night recitations for you?
2. **The duas of 2:286:** Which of the believers' duas at the end of al-Baqarah do you most need at the end of a day?
3. **Checking:** Think of a saying about a good deed that you have heard often. Did you know its source before this module?
4. **Memorising:** If you plan to learn al-Mulk by heart, when will you learn new verses, and when will you review?

## Your bedtime routine

This task turns module 6 into a routine you can keep. A bedtime routine is a short chain. It works when its first step removes the thing that usually breaks it, when it has a minimum for tired nights, and when it ends early enough to protect the next morning. The recitations are the heart of it, but the steps around them decide whether they happen.

You may write about your own nights, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Saqib is a university lecturer who often marks papers at night. His first step: when he finishes marking, his laptop closes and his phone goes on the charger in the kitchen. His minimum: the last two verses of al-Baqarah on his bed, about two minutes (Sahih al-Bukhari 5009). His full version: the two verses and then Surah al-Mulk from the mushaf by his bed, about twelve minutes (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2891). His tired-night rule: if he finishes marking after 23:00, he does the minimum only. His lights-out time: 23:00 on most nights, which gives him six and a half hours before Fajr, and he moves marking to the afternoon on two days a week to make that possible. His memorising plan: five verses of al-Mulk a week, learned after Fajr and recited at night, with a review week when needed.

Look at the shape of Saqib's answer. The first step targets his real competitor, the laptop and the phone. The minimum and the full version both have a size and a source. The tired-night rule names exactly when he uses the minimum. And his lights-out time is linked to Fajr, with one change to his day that makes it possible.

A weak answer would say: "I will read al-Mulk and the last verses of al-Baqarah every night before sleeping, inshallah." It names the deeds, but no first step, no minimum and no time. On the first late night with the phone in bed, it would not happen.

Be honest about your competitor. For most people it is the phone, but for some it is a laptop, a television, a late meal or a long conversation. Your first step should remove or move that one thing. If you share a room, think about a place where you can recite quietly without disturbing anyone, or recite under your breath.

Memorising al-Mulk is welcome in your plan but not required by this gate. If you include it, give it the same shape as the rest: how many verses a week, when you learn them, when you review them, and what you do in a week the old verses slip. If you do not, the full version from the mushaf is complete on its own. Many people memorise later, once the routine itself has been steady for a month and the phone no longer pulls at them each night.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own nights, use this one. Rubina is a 29-year-old graphic designer and mother of two young children. Her children sleep at 21:00. After that she usually watches videos on her phone in bed until about 0:30. She prays Isha at about 21:15. She knows the last two verses of al-Baqarah by heart, but rarely recites them. She wants to recite them and al-Mulk every night, and she also wants to pray Fajr at the start of its time, which she misses about four mornings a week. Write Rubina's bedtime routine.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your plan with the table below. The rubric grades your plan only, never your faith.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| First step | Removes or moves the real competitor, with where it goes | Names the competitor, but no step | No first step |
| Minimum | A minimum with its size and source | A minimum with no size or no source | No minimum |
| Full version | The full recitation with its size and source | The full version, no source | None |
| Tired-night rule | When exactly you use the minimum | "When I am tired", with no rule | None |
| Lights out and Fajr | A lights-out time, linked to Fajr | A time, no link to Fajr | No time |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
