## Learning log

1. **Listening:** When did a parent last stop talking because you were not listening? What will you do next time?
2. **Your share:** Which household job could you own without being asked?
3. **Kinship:** Which relative have you not spoken to for the longest time?
4. **Next door:** What do you know about your nearest neighbour? What would you like to know?

## Your family plan and golden rules

This task has two parts. The first is a short plan of family habits from this module: one for parents, one for relatives, one for your home, and one for neighbours or guests. The second is your home's golden rules: five to ten short sentences that sum up how you want your family to live, each one tied to a source from this course.

You may write about your own family, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded. If your family situation is difficult, you may write the plan for the habits that are possible for you, or use the provided case.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Ayesha writes: "Parents: phone face down whenever my mother-in-law or my parents speak to me; the dua of Quran 17:24 after Isha. Relatives: a Sunday call to one relative from our list. Home: Hamza owns the dishes, I own the laundry, Musa owns the table when he stays. Neighbours: food to our elderly neighbour every Friday. Golden rules: Truth is always better than a lie (Sahih al-Bukhari 6094). We please Allah before people (Sahih al-Bukhari 50). We learn something every day (Sahih Muslim 2699). Nobody is mocked in this house (Quran 49:11). Anger does not make decisions (Sahih al-Bukhari 6114). Haya is who we are (Sahih Muslim 35). Each day, a little better than yesterday (Sahih al-Bukhari 6465). This world is a road; the akhirah is home (Sahih al-Bukhari 6416)."

Look at the shape of her answer. Each habit names who does it and when. The household jobs are owned, not shared vaguely. And each golden rule is short enough to remember, with a source beside it, so that it can be explained to a child who asks "why?".

A weak answer would say: "We will be a good Islamic family and respect each other." It is sincere, but it names no habit and gives children nothing to remember.

Write your golden rules with your family if you can. Rules a family writes together are kept far better than rules one person hangs on the fridge. Children especially like to suggest a rule, and a rule a child suggested is one they will remind everyone of. Keep the list short. Ten rules is the most most families can remember; five is often better.

Then put the rules where everyone will see them, and attach them to a cue: read one aloud at dinner on Fridays, for example, and talk about it for two minutes. A rule that is never mentioned fades like the old chart of duas on Hamza's fridge.

Choose the first habit carefully. In many homes, the habit with the biggest effect is the simplest one: listening fully to a parent or grandparent, with the phone down. It costs nothing but attention, and it changes the feeling of a home within a week. If you are unsure where to start, start there.

For household jobs, agree them openly. A job that one person assumes is theirs, and another assumes is shared, causes more friction than no plan at all. Say it aloud at dinner: who owns what, starting when.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own family, use this one. The Siddiqui family lives in a flat: two parents, a 15-year-old son, a 10-year-old daughter and a grandmother. The children rarely help at home. The son answers his grandmother with "hmm" while looking at his phone. The father has not visited his brother in another city for a year. The family does not know the names of their neighbours. Write the Siddiqui family's plan and five golden rules.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parents | A daily habit for listening or help, and a dua | One of the two | None |
| Relatives | A regular call or visit with a cue | A wish to stay in touch | None |
| Home | Owned jobs and a rule for promises or mistakes | Jobs listed but not owned | None |
| Neighbours and guests | A specific kindness or welcome | A general wish | None |
| Golden rules | Five to ten short rules, each with a source, and a cue for reading them | Rules without sources or without a cue | No rules |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".
