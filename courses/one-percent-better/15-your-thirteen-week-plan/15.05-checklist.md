## Course checklist

- [ ] I add one habit a week, attached to one that is already steady, and I know the five prayers are obligatory in full from day one
- [ ] I keep my intention for Allah, my tracker private, and I share the idea, not the score
- [ ] I write each prayer's plan as an if-then line with its real obstacle, and I catch the first takbir by leaving at the adhan and walking with calm
- [ ] I have a daily Quran portion with a minimum, a make-up time and a restart rule
- [ ] I have a sourced starter set of morning and evening adhkar at two anchors, and the adhkar after the prayer stacked on the salam
- [ ] I have a bedtime routine that starts with the phone away and ends early enough for Fajr
- [ ] I check a saying for a collection, a number and a grading before I pass it on
- [ ] I keep the duas of ordinary moments, the Sunnah of eating, wudu through the day and a weekly hygiene day
- [ ] I guard my tongue, eyes, ears and phone with planned responses
- [ ] I have habits for the heart, for character with people, and for family, neighbours and guests
- [ ] I plan my time, learn daily, earn honestly and give a small, fixed sadaqah
- [ ] I have a Friday chain and a Ramadan habit to keep after Eid
- [ ] I count days done, review once a week, change one thing, and restart at the next cue with no debt
- [ ] I submitted my capstone plan in chapter 15.4
- [ ] I submitted the final reflection below

## One change in how you live

This last task looks back and forward. In module 1, you wrote a list and chose a first habit, before you knew most of the tools in this course. Looking at that list again is a clear way to see how your thinking has changed, and it usually shows you what to do next.

Then look forward. Thirteen weeks is the length of Hamza's Notion page, but it is not the length of a habit. The habits in this course are meant for the rest of your life. Your plan for the next thirteen weeks may add new habits, such as the sunnah prayers before or after the obligatory ones, a weekly fast, or regular charity. Or it may add nothing new and simply steady what you have. Both are good plans. The question is only whether it is a plan you can keep.

You may write about your own plan, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded, and your mentor will never ask to see your tracker.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Faraz started the course with a list of eleven habits and chose Fajr first, "because it is the most important". It lasted four days. Looking back, he would choose Isha first, which he already prayed in the mosque most nights, and let Fajr come in week three, after two weeks of earlier nights. For the next thirteen weeks he will add no new habits for four weeks, because his evening adhkar and al-Mulk are still at four of seven. In week five he will add two rak'ahs of sunnah after Maghrib, attached to the adhkar after Maghrib, with a source he checked. His one sentence: "The plan is to keep, not to impress."

Look at the shape of Faraz's answer. It is honest about the first choice and what it taught him. Its next plan is based on his real counts, and it waits where his adding rule says to wait. The one new habit has an anchor and a source.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own plan, use this one. Naila finished the course with nine habits running. Seven are at six or seven of seven. Two are lower: the morning adhkar at three of seven, because she leaves for work in a rush, and al-Mulk at four of seven, because she often falls asleep putting her children to bed. In module 1 she chose daily Quran first, and it is still her steadiest habit. She wants to add a weekly charity habit and the four rak'ahs before Dhuhr. Write Naila's look back and her plan for the next thirteen weeks.

## Passing the idea on

This course began with one person who read a chapter of a book, made a private page and quietly asked a friend to pass the idea on. He did not want to share his page, because it was his worship and he wanted to keep it for Allah. He wanted the idea to reach others, so that they could build their own. That is the whole spirit of this course in one decision.

You can do the same. Tell a friend, a sibling, a study circle or a colleague: "Add one Islamic habit a week, attached to something you already do, with a small minimum for hard days." Offer to help them choose their first habit. Lend them this course if they would like it. You do not need to tell them how many weeks you have kept, or which habits you have. Whoever guides to good has a reward like the one who does it (Sahih Muslim 1893), and the good you guide them to will be theirs, kept for Allah, on a page only they can see.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your reflection with the table below. The rubric grades your reasoning and plan only.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Look back | Your module 1 first habit, how it went, and what you would choose now, with a reason | A look back with no reason | None |
| Your counts | The habits that are steady and the ones that are not, with numbers | A general feeling ("most are fine") | None |
| The next 13 weeks | A plan that waits or adds according to your adding rule, with reasons | A plan that ignores your counts | No plan, or everything at once |
| New habit | Any new habit with an anchor, a minimum and a checked source, or a reason for adding none | A new habit missing one of these | A new habit with an unchecked or weak source |

**Pass rule:** your reflection passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".
