## Module 3 checklist

- [ ] I know that the reader who stumbles has two rewards (Sahih Muslim 798)
- [ ] I know how to make up a portion missed through sleep (Sahih Muslim 747), and that illness and travel do not erase my habit (Sahih al-Bukhari 2996)

- [ ] I submitted my Quran habit plan below

## Learning log

1. **Your past attempts:** Think of a time you tried to read the Quran regularly. What size did you start with, and what ended it? If you prefer, answer about Hamza's juz in September.
2. **Shame:** Has slow or imperfect reading ever kept you from the Quran? How does Sahih Muslim 798 change that for you?
3. **The missed day:** What thought usually comes after you miss a day of a good habit? What will you say back to it now?
4. **Learning:** Who could you read to once a week, even for ten minutes?

## Your Quran habit

This task turns module 3 into a habit you can keep. A good plan does not promise a lot. It makes the habit easy to start, gives it a minimum for hard days, a rule for growing, and a way back after a miss. It answers the question Hamza asked in chapter 3.2: how much can I read every single day, including the worst day of the month?

You may write about your own habit, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Your mentor will not ask how much you read, and nothing personal is graded.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Hamid is a delivery rider who works long shifts. His cue is his return home from Fajr. His place is the chair by the window, with the mushaf on the sill and a ribbon at today's page. He timed himself: one page takes him six minutes. His minimum is half a page, about three minutes, for days he starts work early. His growing rule: when one page has felt easy on most days for two weeks, he will add half a page. His missed-day line: "If I miss my page after Fajr, I read it on my phone at my lunch break, before Dhuhr if I can." His restart rule: "After any miss, the next morning I read my usual page, no catching up." And he reads to the imam for ten minutes after Jumu'ah every week.

Look at the shape of Hamid's answer. Every part has a size, a time or a place. The minimum fits his worst day, and he measured it. The growing rule says when, not just "eventually". The missed-day line has a time, and the restart rule protects him from a growing debt.

A weak answer would say: "I will read the Quran every day after Fajr, inshallah, and more when I can." It is sincere, but it has no size, no minimum, no rule for growing and no plan for a miss. The first late morning would break it.

Think about your hardest day before you write. For many people it is a travel day, a day of guests, a day of illness in the family or the day before an exam. Write your minimum for that day. If your minimum feels almost too small to bother with, it is probably the right size.

The learning part may look like an extra, but it is what makes the daily habit easier over the months. Ten minutes a week with someone who reads well corrects a few words at a time. Each correction makes tomorrow's page a little smoother, which is the 1% idea applied to the reading itself. Choose a person you can reach easily: an imam, a parent, a sibling, a friend or a teacher on a call.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own habit, use this one. Shahid is a 40-year-old shopkeeper. He opens his shop at 9:00 and closes at 21:00, with a break for Asr. He can read the Quran fairly well, but he has only read it in Ramadan for years. He wants to read every day. He tried a juz a day after Fajr last month, managed four days, and then stopped. He has a mushaf at home and a Quran app on his phone. His shop is quiet between 14:00 and 16:00. Write Shahid's Quran habit plan.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your plan with the table below. The rubric grades your plan only, never your faith or how much you read.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cue, place and time | A named cue, a fixed place and the mushaf or app ready there | Two of the three | None |
| Portion and minimum | A daily portion and a smaller minimum, both with a size | A portion without a minimum, or no size | No portion |
| Growing rule | When you will grow, and by how much | "More later", with no rule | None |
| Missed-day plan | A make-up time, and a restart rule with no growing debt | One of the two | None, or a plan to repay all missed pages at once |
| Learning | A person or a time to read to a teacher | A wish to learn, with no person or time | None |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example, they may ask what your minimum is on a travel day, or how you will know a portion "feels easy". You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
