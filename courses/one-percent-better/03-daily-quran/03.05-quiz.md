```quiz
question: "Hassan starts a daily Quran habit. On a very good first morning he reads ten pages and decides that ten pages is now his daily portion. What does module 3 suggest?"
options:
  - "Set his portion by what he can read on his hardest day"
  - "Keep ten pages, since he has shown he can do it on a good day"
  - "Read ten pages only on days he feels as inspired as today"
  - "Double it to twenty, to keep the feeling of the first day going"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Recite what is easy (Quran 73:20). The minimum is set by the hardest day, and good days can add more."
  - "A good first morning is not a guide to a tired one. The portion should fit the hardest day."
  - "A habit that waits for inspiration is not a daily habit. A small fixed portion keeps going without it."
  - "That is the big start that broke Hamza's September plan."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sumaira reads slowly and makes mistakes. Her brother, a hafiz, tells her it might be better to listen until she reads well. She feels ashamed. Which reply fits Sahih Muslim 798?"
options:
  - "Her brother is right, so she should only listen for now"
  - "She should read in secret, so nobody hears her mistakes"
  - "Her stumbling has two rewards; keep reading and learn"
  - "She should only read the surahs she already knows well"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Listening is good, but it need not replace reading. The one who reads and stumbles has two rewards (Sahih Muslim 798)."
  - "Private reading is fine, but shame is not the reason to hide. Reading to a teacher is how she will improve."
  - "Right. The one who reads the Quran stumbling and finding it hard has two rewards (Sahih Muslim 798), and learning with a teacher helps (Sahih al-Bukhari 5027)."
  - "Avoiding hard words keeps her from learning. The difficulty is what the hadith rewards."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Imran has read two pages after Fajr every day for three weeks. Most mornings it feels easy now. When does module 3 suggest he grow his portion?"
options:
  - "Only in Ramadan, when everyone reads more"
  - "Now, because it has felt easy on most days for a while"
  - "Never, because growing a habit risks breaking it"
  - "On the next morning he wakes up feeling especially inspired"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "He can grow at any time the current amount is easy. There is no need to wait."
  - "Right. Grow when the current amount is easy on most days. Three weeks of easy mornings is a good sign."
  - "Growing a little, when the current amount is easy, keeps it within what he can manage. Staying the same is also fine, but not required."
  - "Inspiration is a poor guide. The test is how the portion feels on most days."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Laiba usually reads her portion after Fajr. She slept through that time today after a late night with her baby. It is now 10:00. What does Sahih Muslim 747 suggest?"
options:
  - "Read it now, before Dhuhr; there is a way to make it up"
  - "Today's portion is lost, so she should start again tomorrow"
  - "Read double tomorrow morning, to make up for today"
  - "Stop the habit until her baby sleeps through the night"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Reading it before Dhuhr is the make-up the Sunnah teaches (Sahih Muslim 747)."
  - "There is a way back the same day. Whoever sleeps through his portion and reads it between Fajr and Dhuhr, it is written as if read at its time (Sahih Muslim 747)."
  - "Doubling makes tomorrow heavier. Today she can still make it up."
  - "A hard season calls for a smaller portion or a make-up plan, not a stop."
```

```quiz

  - "He should push himself to read his full portion in hospital"
  - "His usual deed is written for him while illness stops him"
  - "He should repay the week's pages as soon as he is home"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The hadith says the opposite: what he used to do when healthy is written for him (Sahih al-Bukhari 2996)."
  - "He can do what he is able to, but the hadith does not ask a sick man to force himself."
  - "Right. When a servant falls ill, there is written for him the like of what he used to do when healthy (Sahih al-Bukhari 2996)."
  - "There is no debt to repay. On his first healthy morning he reads his usual portion."
```
