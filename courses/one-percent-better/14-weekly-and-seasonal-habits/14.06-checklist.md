## Learning log

1. **Your Friday:** What does your Friday look like now, from Thursday night to the prayer? Which link in the chain is weakest?
2. **Sadaqah:** When did you last give without telling anyone? What made it possible?
3. **Last Ramadan:** What rose in Ramadan, and what was left a month after Eid?
4. **One habit:** Which one habit would you most like Ramadan to plant in you permanently?

## Your weekly and seasonal plan

This task gives the weekly and yearly rhythms of this module a place in your calendar. Friday comes every week, so its plan can start this week. Sadaqah can start today. Ramadan may be months away, so its plan is written now and reviewed a month before it begins.

You may write about your own life, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded, and you do not need to share any amounts.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Imran, the senior engineer from module 4, writes: "Friday: Thursday night, nails and clothes ready; Friday, ghusl before work; a recurring 12:10 block in my calendar; at the mosque by 12:30, phone off, durood until the adhan; durood again after Asr (Sahih al-Bukhari 883, 881, 934; Sunan Abi Dawud 1047). Sadaqah: a standing order on payday for a fixed small part of my salary to a trusted charity, plus the family box every Friday after Maghrib; a bag in the cupboard for outgrown clothes (Sahih Muslim 2588; Sahih al-Bukhari 1423). Zakat: calculated each year in Sha'ban with the imam. Ramadan: suhoor with my wife and children, iftar promptly with its words, taraweeh at the mosque; Quran from one page to a juz a day with a page minimum; my chosen habit to keep after Eid is two rak'ahs of night prayer before Witr, which I will start in the first week of Ramadan (Sahih al-Bukhari 1923, 1957, 37; Sunan Abi Dawud 2357)."

Look at the shape of Imran's answer. The Friday plan starts on Thursday, because preparation is most of the chain. The sadaqah habit has a fixed day, a fixed amount, a quiet channel, and a separate line for zakat. The Ramadan plan grows existing habits with minimums, and names one habit to keep after Eid, with when it will start.

A weak answer would say: "I will make Friday special, give more charity, and have a good Ramadan." It is sincere, but there is no day, no amount and no habit to keep.

If you do not attend Jumu'ah, write your Friday plan around the habits that are yours: ghusl and cleanliness, much durood, the family's weekly habits, and the sadaqah box. If you cannot fast, write your Ramadan plan around the Quran, the night prayer, sadaqah and guarding the tongue, and the habit you will keep.

Put a reminder in your calendar one month before Ramadan to read your plan again. Plans written months ahead are easy to forget, and a month is enough time to prepare the cue for your chosen habit.

Choose your one Ramadan habit carefully. It should be something you already do a little, or something Ramadan naturally strengthens, so that the month gives it a running start: the night prayer, a larger Quran portion, daily sadaqah, a habit of the tongue. Choose something small enough to keep on an ordinary Tuesday in the middle of the year. A habit that only fits Ramadan will end with it.

The weekly habits in this module also work as anchors for habits from earlier modules. If your fitrah day, your reading circle or your family's golden-rules talk has no fixed day yet, Friday is a natural home for them.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own life, use this one. Bilal is a 32-year-old bank officer. He attends Jumu'ah every week, arriving during the khutbah, and checks his phone during it. He gives sadaqah only in Ramadan, a large amount at once. Last Ramadan he read the whole Quran, and he has not opened it since. He wants this year to be different. Write Bilal's weekly and seasonal plan.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friday | A chain from Thursday to the prayer, with phone off and durood | Some links | None |
| Sadaqah | A fixed day, a fixed small amount, quietly, and zakat named separately | Two of these | None |
| Ramadan Sunnahs | Suhoor, prompt iftar with its words, taraweeh or night prayer, tongue guarded | Two or three of these | None |
| After Eid | One named habit to keep, with its cue and start date | A wish to keep "something" | None |
| Sources | Sources for each part | Some sources | None |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
