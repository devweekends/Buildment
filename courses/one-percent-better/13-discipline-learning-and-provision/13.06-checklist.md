## Learning log

1. **Your last free day:** Where did it go? What would you have liked it to hold?
2. **The hard task:** Which important task do you delay most often? What would doing it first look like?
3. **Learning:** What was the last book you finished? What will the next be?
4. **Money:** Is there any deal, invoice or sale where you have hidden a fault or given short measure? What will you do about it?

## Your plan for time, learning and money

This task turns module 13 into three habits, one for each question in Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2417 that this module covered: life and time, knowledge, and wealth. Each habit should be small, attached to a cue, and written so that someone else could see whether you did it.

You may write about your own life, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded, and you do not need to share any amounts of money.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Uncle Tariq, the shopkeeper, writes: "Time: on the bus home I write tomorrow's three tasks on the back of a receipt. The hardest, usually the accounts, I do first, at 9:00, before customers come. Friday afternoon is family time, closed. Learning: ten pages after Isha, alternating between a book on the Prophet's life and a book on electronics repair. One question a week to the imam, from a notebook. Money: every repair has a written note of what was done, and faults are shown to the customer before I start. I keep a spending record for the shop and for home. A fixed small part of each day's takings goes into a sadaqah box. I have one debt to my brother; I pay a fixed amount on the first of each month." Each habit has its source beside it.

Look at the shape of his answer. Each habit has a cue: the bus, 9:00, Isha, the first of the month. Each is small enough to do on a bad day. The money habit covers both earning and spending. And the debt is named, with a plan, not hidden.

A weak answer would say: "I will manage my time better, read more and be honest with money." It is sincere, but there is no cue, no size and nothing anyone could check.

Start with the area that is weakest for you. For many people it is time, because time habits support the other two: without a plan for the evening, the reading does not happen and the spending record is not kept. If you are unsure where to start, start with three tasks written the night before.

For the money part, think about your real situation: salary, freelance work, a shop, pocket money or a household budget. The habits are the same at every size: honest in, moderate out, a record, and a small fixed part for others.

If the honest look at your money in the learning log showed something you are not proud of, such as a padded invoice or a fault you hid, include in your plan what you will do about it. Tawbah for wrongs against people includes putting them right where you can: returning what was taken, or asking the person's pardon (Sahih al-Bukhari 2449). You do not need to describe it here in detail; one line, such as "I will settle one past invoice this month", is enough.

Keep the three habits light enough to survive a hard week. A plan with three small habits that you keep is worth far more than a detailed system that collapses on the first busy Monday.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own life, use this one. Fatima is a 24-year-old graphic designer who works freelance from home. She often starts work at 13:00 after a morning on her phone, and works until 2:00. She has not read a book since university. She sometimes bills clients for "revisions" she did not do, to make up for low rates. She spends most of her income on online shopping and is often short at the end of the month. Write Fatima's plan.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time | A plan the night before, the hardest task first, and chosen rest | One or two of these | None |
| Learning | A daily amount at a fixed cue, and a way to act on it | A wish to read more | None |
| Earning | Honest billing or dealing, with faults shown and full measure | A general wish to be honest | None, or a dishonest practice kept |
| Spending | A record, needs before wants, and a fixed part for sadaqah | One or two of these | None |
| Sources | A source for each habit | Some sources | None |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
