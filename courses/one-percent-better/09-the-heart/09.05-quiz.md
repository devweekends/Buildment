```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Imran's friend opens a successful shop, and Imran catches himself hoping it fails. What does Sahih al-Bukhari 13 ask of him?"
options:
  - "To hide the feeling and say nothing about it to anyone"
  - "To work harder until his own shop is bigger"
  - "To avoid his friend until the feeling passes"
  - "To love for his friend what he loves for himself"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Hiding it is not enough; the hadith asks for an action toward the friend."
  - "Ambition for himself is fine, but wishing failure on his friend is envy."
  - "Turning your back on one another is forbidden (Sahih Muslim 2563)."
  - "Right. None of you believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself (Sahih al-Bukhari 13). A dua for the shop is a good start."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Naveed slips into a private sin he has been fighting. He decides to stop praying at night until he 'deserves' it. What does Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1987 teach?"
options:
  - "Follow the bad deed with a good one, and keep his worship"
  - "Stop the night prayer, since a sinner's worship is not accepted"
  - "Ignore the sin, since it was private"
  - "Wait a month before returning to worship"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Be mindful of Allah wherever you are, and follow a bad deed with a good one to wipe it out (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1987)."
  - "That thought keeps him away from the very worship that helps him."
  - "Private deeds matter (Sunan Ibn Majah 4245); tawbah is the response."
  - "Tawbah is the same night, not after a month."
```
