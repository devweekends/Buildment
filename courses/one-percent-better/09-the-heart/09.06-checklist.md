## Module 9 checklist

- [ ] I can explain ihsan: worshipping Allah as if I see Him, knowing He sees me (Sahih al-Bukhari 50)
- [ ] I have a reminder where I am most often alone with a choice
- [ ] I ask "would Allah be pleased with this?" before private decisions
- [ ] I have a plan for a slip: tawbah, a good deed and one changed means (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1987)
- [ ] I meet envy with a dua for the other person and refuse to belittle anyone (Sahih al-Bukhari 13; Sahih Muslim 2563)
- [ ] I can name the two signs of pride and accept fair correction (Sahih Muslim 91)
- [ ] I say specific Alhamdulillahs, make dua first, and put wrongs right the same day
- [ ] I remember the akhirah with hope, and ask for Paradise three times (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2572)
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 9.2 to 9.4, and finished the quiz in 9.5
- [ ] I submitted my plan below

## Learning log

1. **Alone:** Where are you most often alone with a choice? What reminder would you see there?
2. **The heart:** Whose blessing do you find hardest to see? What dua could you make for them? You do not need to name them here.
3. **Pride:** When was the last time you rejected a correction because of who gave it?
4. **Gratitude:** Name three specific blessings from today.

## Your plan for the habits nobody sees

This is the most private plan in the course. Your mentor will never ask what your private struggles are, and you should not write them here. Describe the shape of your plan, not its content. "My hardest private moment is late at night with my phone" is enough detail. "A colleague I find hard to be glad for" is enough detail.

The plan has four parts, one for each habit area in this module: a reminder for private moments, a plan for a slip, an action for the heart, and the daily turnings to Allah.

You may write about your own life, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Saad is a university student. His reminder: "Allah dekh raha hai" as his laptop wallpaper, because his hardest private moments are late at night at his desk. His question: before closing the laptop lid each night, "would Allah be pleased with what I did on this today?". His practical means: the laptop stays on the desk, never in bed, after 23:00, and he has turned on a filter and given the password to his older brother. His slip plan: istighfar the same night, two rak'ahs the next morning before university, and one means changed. His heart action: a classmate whose results he envies; a short dua for him after Fajr each day, and a rule of never commenting on his marks to others. His daily turnings: three Alhamdulillahs at meals, a dua before each study session, an apology before the next prayer when he snaps at his younger brother, and asking for Paradise three times before sleep. Each line has its source.

Look at the shape of Saad's answer. It reveals almost nothing private, and yet it is a complete plan. The reminder is where his weak moment is. The means are physical. The slip plan is decided in advance. The heart action is an action, not a wish to feel differently. The turnings are attached to meals, study and the prayer.

A weak answer would say: "I will try to have taqwa and a clean heart, and remember Allah more." It is sincere, but it gives no reminder, no means, no slip plan and no action.

Be gentle with yourself as you write. This module touches the parts of life where people most often feel shame. The aim is a plan that helps you get up quickly when you fall, not a record of failures. Allah is Most Merciful, and tawbah is always open.

Choose one part to start with. Most people find the reminder and the practical means are the quickest to set up, because they are physical: a wallpaper, a charger in the hall, a filter. The heart action and the daily turnings take longer to feel natural. That is fine. Set up the physical parts this week, and let the others grow over the next month, one week at a time, using the order in each chapter's "Building it the 1% way" section.

If you track these habits, track the actions, not the feelings: "dua for X made", "no belittling today", "three Alhamdulillahs". Feelings are not a fair thing to score, and they change slowly. Actions are in your hands every day.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own life, use this one. Erum is a 29-year-old accountant. She is honest at work, but she often feels resentment toward her sister-in-law, who seems to have an easier life, and she speaks about her unkindly to friends. Late at night she spends an hour on social media comparing herself with others and feels worse afterwards. She rarely makes dua except in the prayer. Write Erum's plan.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reminder and question | A reminder placed where private choices happen, and the question before deciding | One of the two | Neither |
| Means and slip plan | A practical means, and a slip plan decided in advance | One of the two | Neither |
| Heart action | A specific action against envy, pride or hatred, with a source | A wish to feel differently | None |
| Daily turnings | Gratitude, dua, tawbah and the akhirah, each with a cue | Two or three of them | None |
| Privacy | The plan describes shapes, not private details | Some unnecessary detail | Private details shared |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions about the plan's shape, never its private content. You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
