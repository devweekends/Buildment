## Learning log

1. **A hard moment:** Think of the last time you were angry with someone. Which step of the sequence was missing?
2. **Rights:** Is there anything you have borrowed and not returned, or anyone you owe an apology?
3. **Humour:** Which jokes in your circle are at someone's expense? What will you do next time?
4. **Courage:** When did you last see someone wronged? What would you do now?

## Your character plan

This task turns module 11 into four habits: one sequence for hard moments, one check for rights, one rule for humour, and one plan for mercy and courage. Character is built in small, repeated actions, so each part of your plan should name a real situation from your week and the action you will take in it.

You may write about your own life, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Bilal, the new team lead from module 9, writes: "Hard moments: when a developer misses a deadline, my habit is to snap in the team chat. New sequence: don't reply for ten minutes; then a private message with the facts, no sting; name my part if my planning was unrealistic; thank them for what they did finish. Rights: I take credit in meetings for work my team did. Rule: name the person who did it, every time. Humour: our team jokes about one developer's accent. I will stop laughing, and say 'come on' once. Mercy and courage: I will help the junior developer who is struggling with one hour a week; and if I see anyone in the team spoken to harshly by a client, I will step in on the call." Each line has its source beside it.

Look at the shape of Bilal's answer. Every part names a real situation, not a general virtue. The hard-moment sequence follows the order of chapter 11.2. The rights part catches a subtle wrong, taking credit, that many people never notice. The humour rule is one sentence. And the courage plan names when and how, in his actual role.

A weak answer would say: "I will have good character, control my anger and be kind to everyone." It is sincere, but it names no situation and no action.

Choose real situations, even small ones. Character habits grow from the moments that happen to you most often: the same argument at home, the same group chat, the same colleague. A plan for those moments is worth more than a plan for rare dramatic ones.

If you are a parent, you may write the plan with your child, as Rida did with Musa, choosing one action from each part that fits their age. Children often take these habits more seriously when they helped write them.

Start with the part that would make the biggest difference to the people closest to you. For most people that is the hard-moment sequence at home, because that is where anger and harsh words do most damage and are least noticed. For others it is a right they have left unsettled for a long time, such as a loan or an apology. Settling one long-standing right often brings a relief that makes the other habits easier.

Keep the sources beside each part of your plan. When a hard moment comes, remembering "the strong one controls himself" or "seek pardon today" in the Prophet's words gives the habit more weight than remembering your own plan. It reminds you whose way you are following, and why.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own life, use this one. Rashid is 42 and runs a small electronics repair shop with two employees. He often shouts at his younger employee when a repair goes wrong. He has borrowed money from his brother-in-law and has not mentioned it for a year. He jokes with customers about one employee's village accent. Stray cats come to the back of the shop, and he chases them away with water. Write Rashid's character plan.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard moments | A real situation with the full sequence: control, truth, own part, forgiveness, thanks | A partial sequence | None |
| Rights | A specific right to return or settle, with when | A general wish to be fair | None |
| Humour | A rule for a real joke situation | A general wish to be kind | None |
| Mercy and courage | One act of mercy and one plan for a wrong, both specific | One of the two | Neither |
| Sources | A source for each part | Some sources | None |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".
