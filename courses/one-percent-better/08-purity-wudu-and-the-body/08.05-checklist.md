## Module 8 checklist

- [ ] I can explain why purity is half of faith (Sahih Muslim 223)
- [ ] I make wudu with care and with little water (Sahih Muslim 244; Sahih al-Bukhari 201)
- [ ] I check my wudu before leaving home and renew it early, not in the last minute
- [ ] I make wudu before sleep (Sahih al-Bukhari 247)
- [ ] When angry, I sit down and seek refuge in Allah (Sunan Abi Dawud 4782; Sahih al-Bukhari 3282)
- [ ] I have a weekly hygiene day for a bath, nails and clothes, inside forty nights (Sahih al-Bukhari 898; Sahih Muslim 258)
- [ ] I use a miswak or brush daily, start with the right, and keep my shoes in their place
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 8.2 and 8.3, and finished the quiz in 8.4
- [ ] I submitted my purity plan below

## Learning log

1. **Your wudu:** When do you usually make wudu, early or at the last minute? What would change if you made it earlier?
2. **The weak saying:** Did you know the "wudu when angry" saying? How will you remember the sound advice instead?
3. **Your rhythm:** Which hygiene habit slips most for you, and what day could give it a cue?
4. **For others:** How does your cleanliness affect the people around you at home, at work or in the mosque?

## Your purity plan

This task turns module 8 into two small habits: one for the day and one for the week. The day habit is about wudu: when you make it, how you keep it, and how you meet anger. The week habit is about the body: a fixed day for the things nothing else reminds you of.

You may write about your own life, or about the provided case below. Both count exactly the same. Nothing personal is graded, and your mentor will not ask about your body or your habits in detail. Describe them at the level you are comfortable with.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Rizwan is a teacher. His day habit: wudu after Fajr at home with the tap low; a check at the front door; if it breaks at school, renew it at the first break, not at the Dhuhr bell; wudu before bed after brushing his teeth. His anger plan: in class, if he feels anger rising while standing, he sits on the edge of his desk and says "a'udhu billahi minash-shaytan" under his breath before speaking. His week habit: Thursday evening, because Friday mornings are busy: nails, a proper bath, and his work shirts in the wash. A miswak in his jacket pocket for before Dhuhr. His sources are written next to each line. He also notes that he used to believe "wudu when angry" was the Sunnah, and has replaced it.

Look at the shape of Rizwan's answer. The day habit has an early cue, the front door, so the last minute is never the plan. The anger plan is specific to where anger actually happens for him, in class, and uses the sound sources. The week habit has a fixed day that suits his life, not a day chosen because someone else uses it. Each line has a source.

A weak answer would say: "I will keep clean and stay in wudu as much as I can." It is sincere, but it has no cue, no day and no plan for anger.

Choose your weekly day honestly. Friday is common and has its own Sunnah of ghusl (module 14), but if Friday mornings are rushed for you, a Thursday evening or a Saturday morning works too. The day matters less than having one.

If you care for children, you can include one line for them in your plan, such as nails on the same day as yours, done together. Habits shared in a family are kept more easily than habits each person tracks alone.

Before you write, notice which of the two habits is weaker for you. Most people find one is already fairly steady and the other is not. Put most of your effort into the weaker one for the first few weeks, and keep the stronger one as it is. That is the 1% way inside a single module: one change at a time, aimed where it will help most.

For the anger plan, think about where anger actually happens in your week: at home, at work, in traffic, online. A plan for the real place works better than a general wish to be calm.

**The provided case.** If you prefer not to use your own life, use this one. Nadia is a nurse who works three twelve-hour shifts a week. She makes wudu at the hospital just before each prayer, often rushed, and sometimes misses the prayer's start because of the queue at the staff washroom. She gets angry with a colleague who often leaves tasks for her. She bathes daily but forgets her nails until they become a problem with her gloves. Write Nadia's purity plan.

## How your mentor will read it

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wudu cue | An early cue for wudu, before the last minute, with renewal | A wish to make wudu earlier, no cue | None |
| Care and water | Wudu with care and little water, with a practical reminder | One of the two | Neither |
| Anger | Sitting or changing position and seeking refuge, tied to where anger happens | A general wish to be calm | None, or the weak narration presented as sound |
| Weekly day | A fixed day for bath, nails and clothes, inside forty nights | Habits listed with no day | None |
| Sources | Sources for each habit | Some sources | None |

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

Your mentor then replies with one or two short questions. You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate.
