## Learning log

1. **Your first move:** When someone gives you a problem, do you start with answers or with questions? Give one short example from your own work or study, or use Mais's ten ideas from chapter 4.1.
2. **Your line:** In your case, what result would have changed your mind, and did you set it before you imagined the result? Or use Hadeel's case and write the line you would set for her.
3. **The hardest part:** Which part was hardest for you: asking questions, comparing options, or deciding? What would make it easier next time?
4. **Your main assumption:** Which assumption in your answer matters most, and how would you check it?

## Your task: work through one case

This task brings the module together on one case question. You will do what Mais learned to do: say the question back, ask one to three questions, say your assumptions, give two or three options and compare them, choose one in a sentence, and name the number you would watch with its value now and the line that would change your mind. The point is not to find the one right answer, because there is none. The point is to show how you think, in an order that a founder can follow and argue with.

You can write about a case from a team you would like to join. Describe it in a few lines without names: "a team that runs a booking app for small gyms, where sign-ups fell last month". Keep it to a problem that has a number in it. You can also use the provided case below. Either way, write as if you are speaking: short sentences, one idea in each.

Here is what a strong answer looks like, told about a fictional person. Anas is asked about a small online shop. It got about 500 orders a month, and last month it got about 400. In the same month, the shop changed its delivery promise from 3 days to 5 days. The shop has one developer for two weeks. Anas asks two questions. Did the fall start when the promise changed? Is it in all products or in a few? The answers are yes, the same week, and in all products. He says his assumption: the promise is one cause, not the only one, and he would check the order list for a sale in the month before. His three options are to go back to 3 days for the best-selling products, to show the 5 days earlier with a reason, and to ask ten visitors who left the cart. He says that time and what he learns matter most, so he picks the first one for four weeks. He will watch orders per month: 400 now, and the line is 440. In the six months before the fall, orders stayed between 480 and 520, so the normal swing is about 20. He would keep the change at 460 or more, change course below 420, and keep watching in between.

Now see a weak answer to the same case: "I would improve the website, run some ads and offer discounts, and see what happens." The founder asks which one first, and what number Anas would watch. There are no answers, because there is no choice and no number. The strong answer is not longer. It is just ordered, and every part has a reason.

If you prefer not to use your own case, use this one. Hadeel is applying to small remote teams. A founder asks her: "Our online tutoring service gets about 300 new sign-ups a month. Last month it got 200. Last month we also started asking for a phone number on the sign-up form. About 80 percent of visitors are on phones. What would you do?" That is a fall of 100 sign-ups, which is one third. Write the answer Hadeel could give. You do not know the number of visitors, so say what you would ask for, and do not invent it.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your answer with the table below. Two careful people can give different good answers to the same case. If your decision is different from the one your mentor would have made, that is fine, as long as your reasons are clear and you did not invent facts.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarify and assume | One to three questions that each would change the plan, and at least one assumption said as an assumption | Questions but no assumption, or more than three questions | No questions and no assumptions |
| Options and trade-offs | Two or three options written as actions, compared on cost, time, risk and what you learn, with what matters most named | Options listed but not compared, or no weight named | Many ideas with no comparison, or one option only |
| Decide and measure | One recommendation, one number with its value now, a line set in advance, and a check for the main assumption | A number but no line, or no value now | No decision |
| Honest gaps | Says what is not known and how to find out, and invents no numbers | One number with no source | Invented numbers stated as facts |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions, often a "why". For example: "Why that option and not the other?" or "What would you do if the number came back between your two lines?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

## Practice with the AI interviewer

The AI interviewer gives you a problem that a small team has now. It asks you how you would work out what to do, and follows your answer with "why" and "how do you know" questions. Then it moves to a second topic, and at the end it gives you strengths and weaknesses, and a fix for each weakness. It is a text chat, so you have time to think, which a live call does not give you.

Answer as you would in a real interview. Asking a question first is a good start. "I do not know yet, and here is what I would check" is a good answer, and so is "I did not measure that". Its feedback is practice, not a hiring decision, and it can be wrong. Check it against your own words in the transcript, and ask your mentor if you disagree.

Privacy: do not paste your full name, phone, email, address, ID numbers, employer or client names, passwords, keys or private company data. If you describe a case from a team you want to join, leave out names and use roles such as "a booking app for gyms". If you do not have a key, the written task below completes this gate.
