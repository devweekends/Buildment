## Learning log

## Your task: work through one case

This task brings the module together on one case question. You will do what Mais learned to do: say the question back, ask one to three questions, say your assumptions, give two or three options and compare them, choose one in a sentence, and name the number you would watch with its value now, your target and the results that tell you to keep the change or change course. The point is not to find the one right answer, because there is none. The point is to show how you think, in an order that a founder can follow and argue with.

Use your own work. You can write about a case from a team you would like to join. Describe it in a few lines without names: "a team that runs a booking app for small gyms, where sign-ups fell last month". Keep it to a problem that has a number in it. If you have no case of your own for this, use the provided case below and say so. Either way, write as if you are speaking: short sentences, one idea in each.

Here is a short contrast. A weak answer to a shop's falling orders is: "I would improve the website, run some ads and offer discounts, and see what happens." The founder asks which one first, and what number the candidate would watch, and there are no answers. A strong answer is: "I would go back to a 3-day delivery promise for the best-selling products for four weeks. I would watch orders a month: 400 now, target 440, normal swing about 20. I keep it at 460 or more and change course at 420 or less." It is not longer. It is ordered, and every part has a reason.

If you prefer not to use your own case, use this one. Hadeel is applying to small remote teams. A founder asks her: "Our online tutoring service gets about 300 new sign-ups a month. Last month it got 200. Last month we also started asking for a phone number on the sign-up form. About 80 percent of visitors are on phones. What would you do?" That is a fall of 100 sign-ups, which is one third. Write the answer Hadeel could give. You do not know the number of visitors, so say what you would ask for, and do not invent it.

When your written answer is ready, say it aloud once and time it. A good aim is under two minutes. Write the number of seconds, and one thing you changed after hearing yourself, such as a long sentence you cut in two. Text practice does not train your pace or your voice, so this step matters. A voice note link is optional, because data costs money.

## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your answer part by part. Two careful people can give different good answers to the same case. If your decision is different from the one your mentor would have made, that is fine, as long as your reasons are clear and you did not invent facts.

**Part 1: clarify and assume.**

- Met: one to three questions that each would change the plan, and at least one assumption said as an assumption.
- Partly: questions but no assumption, or more than three questions.
- Not yet: no questions and no assumptions.

**Part 2: options and trade-offs.**

- Met: two or three options written as actions, compared on cost, time, risk and what you learn, with what matters most named.
- Partly: options listed but not compared, or no weight named.
- Not yet: many ideas with no comparison, or one option only.

**Part 3: decide and measure.**

- Met: one recommendation, one number with its value now, a target, the edges that keep or change course, and a check for the main assumption.
- Partly: a number but no target or edges, or no value now.
- Not yet: no decision.

**Part 4: honest gaps and aloud.**

- Met: says what is not known and how to find out, invents no numbers, and gives the seconds and one change from saying it aloud.
- Partly: honest, but the aloud step is missing.
- Not yet: invented numbers stated as facts.

**Pass rule:** no part is "Not yet", and at most one part is "Partly".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions, often a "why". For example: "Why that option and not the other?" or "What would you do if the number came back between your two edges?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

## Practice with the AI interviewer

This drill gives you a problem that a small team has now, and asks how you would work out what to do. It is a text chat, so you have time to think, which a live call does not give you. There is nothing to paste, because the case is in the opening question. Answer as you would in a real interview, and do not type names, contact details, keys, passwords or private company data. If you do not have a key, the written task below completes this gate.

```aiinterview
title: "A problem the team has now"
opening: "Our small online tutoring service got 300 new sign-ups a month on average. Last month it got 200. You start next week. How would you work out what to do?"
focus: [case-study, impact]
context: none
maxTurns: 10
whyDepth: 3
required: false
rubric: "A strong interview shows the learner working a case in order: they say the question back, ask one to three clarifying questions that would change the plan, and state assumptions as assumptions. They give two or three real options as actions, compare cost, time, risk and learning, and say what matters most. They decide in one sentence, name one number with its value now, a target set in advance with the results that keep the change or change course, and the main assumption with a check. Probes: ask 'What would you ask first, and what would the answer change?', 'What number would you watch, and where is it now?', 'What result would make you change course?'. Stop rules: accept 'I do not know yet' and 'I did not measure that' as full answers, then ask what they would check. Never push for a guessed number or invented facts. Scoring: judge features, not length; an honest gap with a plan beats an unsourced number. Quote the learner's words. Use these flags by name when they apply: vague ownership, unsupported number, pseudo-story, invented reason risk, jargon without explanation. Tone: kind, about the answer, not the person. If the evidence is thin, suggest how to get it."
```
