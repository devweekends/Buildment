```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Iyad is asked: 'Our delivery drivers are late more often. You start next week.' He asks which days and which areas. The founder says: 'Mostly Fridays, in the east of the city.' What should Iyad do next?"
options:
  - "Ask about the company's history and its plans for the next few years."
  - "Say the problem looks like Fridays in the east, and look there first."
  - "Go back to his first plan and check each day in each area equally."
  - "Say he cannot start until the founder shares a year of delivery data."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "These questions would not change what Iyad does first. The founder has just given him a place to look."
  - "Right. It shows that he heard the answer, and it turns the answer into a first step. A clarifying question is worth asking only if the answer changes the plan."
  - "This ignores the answer. The founder said where the problem is, and checking each day equally throws that away."
  - "A case is meant to be worked with missing facts. He has enough to name a first step, and he can say what he would ask for next."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lama practises with an AI chat tool. The case: 'Our app's sign-ups fell by a third last month. What would you do?' The AI suggests this answer: 'I would improve onboarding, run tests, tighten the funnel, add game features and personalise emails, and I would measure engagement. Many tactics show breadth.' What is the mistake?"
options:
  - "A list gives no choice, no number to watch and no target to act on."
  - "It should list more tactics, so the founder sees the ideas she has."
  - "It uses too few technical words for a product role in a startup team."
  - "It is right, because many tactics show more thinking than one choice."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. A founder with one developer needs to hear what Lama would do first, how she would know it worked, and what result would make her stop. The list gives none of these."
  - "More tactics would make the problem worse. The answer already leaves the choosing to the founder."
  - "The answer has plenty of technical words already, and they do not help. A founder can follow a plain answer with a choice in it."
  - "Many tactics can look like thinking, but a small team can do only one or two things at a time. The founder asked what she would do."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mazen tests a free-shipping banner. Before the test, 12 percent of visitors bought. His target is 15 percent and the normal swing is about 1 point. After four weeks, 17 percent bought. What should he conclude?"
options:
  - "Change course: the rate did not reach 20 percent after four weeks."
  - "Keep watching: any rise in one month could be down to chance."
  - "Keep it: 17 is above the 16 needed, the target of 15 plus a swing of 1."
  - "Keep watching: 17 is only 5 points above the starting rate of 12."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "His target was 15, not 20. A target is set before the result, and it cannot grow afterwards."
  - "The result is 2 points above the target, and the normal swing is 1 point. That is too far above to put down to chance."
  - "Right. He keeps the change at the target plus one swing, which is 16, and 17 is above that."
  - "The rise from 12 to 17 is 5 points, but his test was whether the rate beat the target of 15 by more than the swing. It did."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hiba's resume says 'Cut the time to print a job by 30 percent.' Her notes: before the change she timed 5 jobs by hand, about 20 minutes each. After it, she timed 5 jobs, about 14 minutes each. The shop also bought a new printer the same week. Which statement about the line is right?"
options:
  - "The cut is 6 minutes out of 14, so it is about 43 percent."
  - "30 percent is right, and the new printer does not matter, because she timed the jobs herself."
  - "The cut is 70 percent, because the new time is 70 percent of the old time."
  - "30 percent matches her timings, but a new printer arrived that week, so part may not be hers."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The cut is measured against the old time, 20 minutes, not the new one. 6 out of 20 is 30 percent."
  - "Her timings are real, but the printer arrived the same week. Timing the jobs herself does not show which part of the change was hers."
  - "14 minutes is 70 percent of 20 minutes, so the time fell by 30 percent, not 70."
  - "Right. 6 minutes out of 20 is 30 percent, which matches her timings. A strong answer also names the printer, so the founder can trust the rest."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer is asked: 'Support emails about failed payments doubled this month. You start next week.' He finds that payments fail on Mondays and Thursdays, when one of the two payment servers is restarted for updates. Failed payments are about 9 a week now. Which answer to 'What would you do first?' is best?"
options:
  - "Hire a second payments company, because that is where most failures come from."
  - "Move the restarts to a quiet night; failed payments are 9 a week, target 5 or fewer."
  - "Study the failed payments for a month first, then pick a number to watch."
  - "Tell customers payments may fail, then watch support emails: 9 a week now."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Nothing in his facts points at the payments company. The failures match the days a server is restarted."
  - "Right. It starts from the days the facts point to, names the number with its value now, and gives a target. The founder can say yes or no and can check it later."
  - "A month of study is not a first step. The facts he has already point to two days of the week."
  - "This treats the emails and leaves the failed payments. The 9 a week is failed payments, not emails, and there is no target to change course."
```
