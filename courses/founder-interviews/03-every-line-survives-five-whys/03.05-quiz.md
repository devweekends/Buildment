```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lama keeps the timetable sheet for a school. The head teacher asks why 10 lessons started late last month. Her sheet notes show 7 where the room was still in use by the class before, and 3 where the teacher was walking between two buildings. Which answer stands up under the next why?"
options:
  - "Teachers are slow to move between rooms, and that explains the 10 late starts."
  - "7 of the 10 late starts followed a class still in the room, so a room clash is the cause."
  - "7 of the 10 late starts followed a class still in the room, and 3 followed a walk between buildings."
  - "3 of the 10 late starts followed a class still in the room, and 7 followed a walk between buildings."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This ends at blame and explains none of the counts in her sheet. The founder's next why would have nothing to point at."
  - "This is true for 7 of the 10 and it leaves out the 3 late starts from the walk between buildings. A problem with two causes needs both."
  - "Right. It gives both causes with their counts, from her sheet. When a problem has more than one cause, a strong chain names each one."
  - "The two counts are swapped. Her sheet shows 7 room clashes and 3 walks, so this answer would be caught by the first check of her own notes."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer practises with an AI chat tool. His notes: his manager asked him to build the new sign-up page from the designer's screens, and he chose to remove two fields. The founder asks, 'Why did you redesign the sign-up page?' The AI replies: 'Say you spotted friction in the sign-up funnel and led a data-driven redesign. Founders like a strategic reason.' What is wrong with this advice?"
options:
  - "It swaps his real reason, a manager's request, for one he did not have."
  - "It uses too few technical words, so the answer sounds junior for a developer role."
  - "It is right to use a strategic reason, but it should add a figure for the gain."
  - "It should tell him to say we throughout, so that he does not seem to take credit."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The AI asks him to give a reason he did not have at the time. His real reason, a request from his manager plus his own choice about the fields, stands up at every why."
  - "More technical words would not fix the problem. The reason is invented, and a founder who hears jargon will usually ask a simpler why."
  - "A strategic reason Tamer did not have needs a new invention at every why, and a figure for the gain would be one more thing he cannot source."
  - "Saying we throughout hides his own choice about the fields. The problem is the invented reason, not how much credit he takes."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Anas fixed a bug in a delivery app. His resume says 'Cut app crashes by 60 percent.' The crash report tool shows 25 crashes in the week before his fix and 10 in the week after. In the same week another developer fixed a login bug. What should Anas say if the founder asks about the number?"
options:
  - "Keep 60 percent: 15 fewer crashes out of 25 is 60 percent, and the tool is the source."
  - "The tool showed 25 crashes in the week before and 10 in the week after, and a login bug was also fixed that week."
  - "The tool showed 25 crashes in the week before and 10 over the next month, so about 60 percent."
  - "Replace the number with 'improved app stability', since another developer also changed the app that week."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The arithmetic is right: 15 out of 25 is 60 percent. But this gives all of the change to Anas, and another fix was made in the same week."
  - "Right. It uses the real counts and the real window, and it names the other change. The founder can trust a number that comes with its limits."
  - "The tool shows one week after, not a month. Changing the window changes the number, and a founder may ask for the month's counts."
  - "A vague phrase can be questioned too: 'improved how, by how much?' It also throws away a number he can source."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Iyad wrote a job that builds a nightly report for a shop owner. His lead chose a nightly run. Iyad chose to retry each failed row once, because most failures in the logs were short network errors. A founder asks, 'Why a nightly job?' Which answer is best?"
options:
  - "Nightly runs scale better than live ones for small teams. I chose that for this report, because it cost less."
  - "We chose a nightly run because it is the simplest way to build a report. I also added a retry for some rows."
  - "My lead chose a nightly run. I do not remember why I added a retry, so I cannot say more about the report."
  - "My lead chose a nightly run. I retried each failed row once, because most failures were short network errors."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Iyad did not choose the nightly run, and nothing in his facts is about scale. The next why would have nothing under it."
  - "'We' hides who decided, and the facts do not say simplicity was the reason. It is a tidy reason made up afterwards."
  - "He remembers a lot: who chose the nightly run and what he chose himself. Saying he remembers nothing hides his own decision."
  - "Right. It says what was not his decision and then names the choice that was. The logs back up the retry, so a further why has an answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ruba is the support person on a small app team. Her line: 'Cut slow replies in the support inbox with saved answers.' She wrote the saved answers herself. The founder asked her to after users complained about waiting. Replies now take about 2 hours, which she checked in the inbox this month. She has no reply times from before. She would record reply times before the next change. Which check does the line fail?"
options:
  - "Check 1: she did not write the saved answers herself, so 'cut' is too strong."
  - "Check 2: she has no reason, because nobody asked her to write saved answers."
  - "Check 4: she has no reply times from before, so she cannot show replies got faster."
  - "Check 5: she would change no step, because replies now take about 2 hours."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "She wrote the saved answers herself, so the verb is true and check 1 passes."
  - "The founder asked her to after users complained, which is a real reason. Check 2 passes."
  - "Right. She can say the result, but with no time from before she cannot show a change. That is the only check the line fails."
  - "She has an honest answer: she would record reply times before the next change. Check 5 passes."
```
