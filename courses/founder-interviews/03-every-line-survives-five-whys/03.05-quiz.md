```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Lama keeps the stock sheet for a charity shop. A founder asks why shelves ran empty last month. Her sheet shows 10 empty-shelf days: on 7 of them no delivery had arrived that week, and on 3 volunteers forgot to refill. Which answer holds up under the next why?"
options:
  - "Volunteers forget to refill the shelves, and that is the whole problem"
  - "Deliveries are late, so the empty shelves were out of our control"
  - "On 7 of the 10 days no delivery came, and on 3 refills were forgotten"
  - "I cannot say why they ran empty, because a sheet only shows counts"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This explains only 3 of the 10 days, and it ends at the volunteers. The founder's next why would be about the other 7."
  - "This explains 7 of the 10 days and ends at the suppliers. It leaves out the 3 refill days, which Lama's own sheet shows."
  - "Right. It gives both causes with their counts, from her sheet. When a problem has more than one cause, a strong chain names each one."
  - "Her sheet shows more than counts: it shows which days had no delivery. Saying she cannot tell would hide what she knows."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tamer practises with an AI chat tool. His notes: his manager asked him to build the new sign-up page from the designer's screens, and he chose to remove two fields. The founder asks, 'Why did you redesign the sign-up page?' The AI replies: 'Say you spotted friction in the sign-up funnel and led a data-driven redesign. Founders like a strategic reason.' What is wrong with this advice?"
options:
  - "It swaps his real reason for a strategic one he never had"
  - "It should add more technical words so the answer sounds more senior"
  - "It should tell him to say 'we' throughout so he does not seem boastful"
  - "It is right, because founders prefer a strategic reason to a request from a manager"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The AI asks him to give a reason he did not have at the time. His real reason, a request from his manager plus his own choice about the fields, holds up under every why."
  - "More technical words would not fix the problem. The reason is invented, and a founder who hears jargon will usually ask a simpler why."
  - "Saying 'we' throughout hides his own choice about the fields. The problem is the invented reason, not how boastful it sounds."
  - "A founder asks why again and again. A strategic reason Tamer did not have needs a new invention at every why, while the real one has facts behind it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Anas fixed a bug in a delivery app. His resume says 'Cut app crashes by 60 percent.' The crash report tool shows 25 crashes in the week before his fix and 10 in the week after. In that same week another developer fixed a login bug. What should Anas do so the line survives the whys?"
options:
  - "Keep the line as it is, because 25 to 10 is exactly 60 percent"
  - "Say 25 to 10 in one week each, and that a login bug was also fixed"
  - "Replace the number with 'improved app stability', which cannot be questioned"
  - "Change 60 percent to 'about half', since half sounds more careful"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The arithmetic is right: 15 out of 25 is 60 percent. But the line gives all of the change to Anas, and another fix was made in the same week."
  - "Right. It uses the real counts and the real window, and it names the other change. The founder can trust a number that comes with its limits."
  - "A vague phrase can be questioned too: 'improved how, by how much?' It also throws away a number he can source."
  - "'About half' is not what the tool shows, which is 60 percent. It does not deal with the second fix in the same week."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Iyad wrote a job that builds a nightly report for a small shop's owner. His lead chose a nightly run. Iyad chose to retry each failed row once, because most failures in the logs were short network errors. A founder asks, 'Why a nightly job?' Which answer is best?"
options:
  - "Nightly jobs are more scalable than real-time ones for small teams"
  - "We chose nightly because it is the simplest way to build reports"
  - "I do not remember, so I cannot say anything about the job at all"
  - "My lead chose nightly. I chose to retry each failed row once"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Iyad did not choose nightly, and nothing in his facts is about scale. The next why would have nothing under it."
  - "'We' hides who decided, and the facts do not say simplicity was the reason. It is a tidy reason made up afterwards."
  - "He remembers a lot: who chose nightly and what he chose himself. Saying he remembers nothing is not honest, and it hides his own decision."
  - "Right. It says what was not his decision and then names the choice that was. The logs back up the retry, so a further why has an answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ruba's resume line says: 'Designed the club's event poster, which brought 42 people to the talk.' She made the poster alone. The club president asked for it because the last talk had few visitors. Two other members also shared the talk in their own groups. Nobody was asked how they heard about it. Next time she would ask people how they heard. Which check does the line fail?"
options:
  - "Check 1: she did not make the poster alone, so 'designed' is too strong"
  - "Check 2: she has no reason, because nobody asked her to make the poster"
  - "Check 4: she cannot show that the poster, and not the sharing, did it"
  - "Check 5: she has nothing to change, since the talk went very well"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "She made the poster alone, so 'designed' is true. Check 1 passes."
  - "The club president asked for it because the last talk had few visitors. That is a real reason, so check 2 passes."
  - "Right. The line claims the poster brought the 42 people. Two members also shared the talk, and nobody was asked, so she cannot show where the people came from."
  - "She has an honest answer: she would ask people how they heard. Check 5 passes."
```
