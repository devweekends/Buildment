```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Manar is a product designer. She asks an AI chat tool for a good answer to 'What is your weakness?' The AI replies: 'Say: My biggest weakness is that I am a perfectionist. I care so much about quality that I sometimes polish things for too long. Interviewers like this answer because it shows you care.' Her real habit is that she waits until a research summary is complete before she sends it, so developers hear about problems late. What is the mistake in the AI's advice?"
options:
  - "The advice is right, since it shows a care for quality that a team wants to see."
  - "It is a humblebrag, and 'what is an example?' would break it."
  - "It is too long, so she should say only 'I am a perfectionist' and stop talking."
  - "It names a weakness that is too serious, so she should pick a weaker one for the team."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Care for quality is a strength, and that is the problem. A strength dressed as a weakness tells the team nothing about her habit, and people reading answers in one study preferred a real weakness."
  - "Right. Her real habit, sending summaries late, is a better answer with an example and a plan. The AI's line sounds reasonable, but it gives her nothing true to say at the next question."
  - "A shorter humblebrag is still a humblebrag. The next question, 'can you give an example?', would be harder with less to say."
  - "Her habit is not too serious. It is a habit that a plan can fix, such as sending a short note after each session. The mistake is hiding it, not choosing it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sana is an operations coordinator at a school office. The interviewer asks 'What are you good at?' She says 'I am good with spreadsheets.' He asks 'How do you know?' Her facts: last term the timetable sheet had 30 room clashes. She added a column that shows each teacher's part-time days, and the next term had 9 clashes. The head teacher's assistant now asks her to check other sheets. Which reply is best?"
options:
  - "I have used spreadsheets for 6 years, and my teachers praised my work in each class I took. Clashes fell from 30 to 9 in the sheet after that."
  - "The head teacher's assistant tells me I am the strongest coordinator the school has had, and clashes fell from 30 to 9 in one term at the school last year."
  - "I do not like to say what I am good at, since other people can judge it for me. I only know that clashes fell from 30 to 9 in a term of work."
  - "I find where a timetable goes wrong. Clashes fell from 30 to 9 after I added a part-time days column, and the assistant asks me to check more sheets."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Years of use and teachers' praise are not in her facts, and neither is a skill. '6 years' says how long, not what she can do."
  - "The assistant is not said to have called her the strongest. This makes the witness bigger than her facts, and the next question, 'did she say that?', would end it."
  - "Modesty is understandable, but it leaves the interviewer with nothing to hold. A true, specific statement about her work is not boasting."
  - "Right. It is a narrow skill with a before and an after, a source she can show, and a witness who acted: the assistant asks her for more."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Osama is a developer. The interviewer says: 'Tell me about your experience with testing.' Osama wrote 14 automatic tests for the address import of a delivery dashboard, and he checked 30 routes by hand. The interviewer's team writes only automatic tests, which Osama knows. What should he do first?"
options:
  - "Say he will take the question as automatic tests, and lead with the 14 tests for the import."
  - "Ask whether the interviewer means automatic tests or testing by hand, before he says anything."
  - "Start with the 30 routes checked by hand, since he has the most detail about those."
  - "Talk about both kinds for the next few minutes, so the interviewer hears the 14 tests and the 30 routes."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. He knows what the team wants, so he states his assumption in one sentence and leads with the 14 tests. If he is wrong, the interviewer will say so."
  - "He already knows the answer to this question. A check is for when the answer would change your plan and you do not know it."
  - "He knows the team writes only automatic tests, so leading with the 30 routes could waste the first minutes, which colour what follows."
  - "A long answer about both kinds has no assumption in it and gives the interviewer nothing to pick. He also knows which kind the team wants."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Osama built a nightly import of driver addresses for a delivery dashboard. The interviewer asks: 'Why did you build it as a nightly job?' His facts: his lead asked for a nightly job. Inside that, Osama chose to read the file row by row and to skip bad rows and list them. Which answer is best?"
options:
  - "I chose a nightly job to reduce the load on the system and to let the import scale in a future year."
  - "I do not remember why it was nightly. I only remember that I skipped the bad rows and listed them for the team."
  - "My lead asked for a nightly job, so that was not my choice. I chose to skip bad rows and list them."
  - "I chose a nightly job because the dashboard users need yesterday's addresses by 07:00 each morning."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "This is a tidy reason invented afterwards. It sounds strategic, but it was not his reason at the time, and the next why would not hold."
  - "He does remember: his lead asked for it. Saying he does not remember hides a fact he has."
  - "Right. It gives the reason he had at the time, says which part was not his decision, and names the choice that was his. Each part holds at the next why."
  - "The 07:00 reason is not in his facts. It is a reason invented to sound sensible, and it takes the choice away from his lead."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rana's interview is in 3 days. Her English is clear, but her sentences are often 45 words long, and she speaks quickly when she is nervous. She worries that her accent will count against her. What is the best use of the 3 days?"
options:
  - "Spend the 3 days changing her accent with videos, since that is what she thinks interviewers judge her on."
  - "Practise sentences of about 15 words, one idea each, and start each answer with a pause sentence."
  - "Practise speaking faster for 3 days, so the interviewer has less time to notice her accent."
  - "Tell the interviewer her English is poor, so that he is kind and asks 3 easier questions."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "An accent cannot be changed in 3 days, and the effort adds worry. Her real, fixable problem is the length of her sentences."
  - "Right. Short sentences and a planned pause fix what she controls: how easy her answers are to follow. Her English is already clear."
  - "Speed and pauses are what listeners use to judge how fluent a speaker sounds, so faster speech can make her harder to follow, not easier."
  - "Her English is clear, so this is not true. It also lowers the interviewer's first impression of her, and that colours the first answers."
```
