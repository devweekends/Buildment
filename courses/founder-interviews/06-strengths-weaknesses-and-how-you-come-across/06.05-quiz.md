```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Manar is a QA tester on a small software team. She asks an AI chat tool for a good answer to 'What is your weakness?' The AI replies: 'Say: My biggest weakness is that I am a perfectionist. I care so much about quality that I sometimes check things too many times. Interviewers like this answer because it shows you care.' Her real habit is that she waits until a test report is complete before sending it, so developers hear about bugs late. What is the mistake in the AI's advice?"
options:
  - "The advice is right, since it shows a care for quality that every team wants to see"
  - "It is a humblebrag that hides her real habit, and 'what is an example?' would break it"
  - "It is too long, so she should say only 'I am a perfectionist' and stop talking after that"
  - "It names a weakness that is too serious, so she should pick a weaker one for the team"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Quality care is a strength, and that is the problem. A strength dressed as a weakness tells the team nothing about her habit, and people reading answers in one study preferred a real weakness."
  - "Right. Her real habit, sending reports late, is a better answer with an example and a plan. The AI's line sounds reasonable, but it gives her nothing true to say at the next question."
  - "A shorter humblebrag is still a humblebrag. The next question, 'can you give an example?', would be worse with less to say."
  - "Her habit is not too serious. It is a habit that a plan can fix, such as sending a short report every day. The mistake is hiding it, not choosing it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Duaa is a data analyst for a clinic. The interviewer asks 'What are you good at?' She says 'I am good with spreadsheets and data.' He asks 'How do you know?' Her facts: she added checks to the clinic's monthly appointment report, and her time notes show it went from about 3 hours to about 45 minutes. The clinic manager now asks her to build other reports. Which reply is best?"
options:
  - "I have used spreadsheets for six years, and my teachers always praised my work very highly, in every class that I took at school and university."
  - "Clinic managers tell me that I am the best analyst they have ever worked with in their whole careers, in any clinic they have run."
  - "I do not like to say what I am good at, since other people are better placed to judge that for me, so I would rather not say."
  - "I cut the monthly report from 3 hours to 45 minutes, by my time notes. The manager now asks me for more."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Years of use and teachers' praise are not in her facts, and neither is a skill. 'Six years' says how long, not what she can do."
  - "No manager is said to have called her the best. This makes the witness bigger than her facts, and the next question, 'which manager?', would end it."
  - "Modesty is understandable, but it leaves the interviewer with nothing. A true, specific statement about her work is not boasting."
  - "Right. It is a narrow skill with a before and an after, a source she can show, and a witness who acted: the manager asks her for more."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Osama is a junior developer. The interviewer says: 'Tell me about your experience with testing.' Osama has done two kinds. He wrote automatic tests for a bakery's order form. He also tested a school timetable sheet by hand. The interviewer's team only writes automatic tests, which Osama knows. What should he do first?"
options:
  - "Say 'Let me think for a moment', then ask 'Do you mean automatic tests or testing by hand?'"
  - "Talk about both kinds for the next few minutes, so the interviewer hears everything he has done"
  - "Talk about testing by hand, since that is the kind where he has the most to say about"
  - "Ask for a different question, since this one is not clear enough for him to answer well"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The question has two readings, and the pause and the check cost a few seconds. He may then lead with the bakery form, which fits the team."
  - "A long answer about everything rushes past the check and gives the interviewer nothing to pick. He also knows which kind the team wants."
  - "He knows the team writes automatic tests only. Leading with the other kind could waste the first minutes, and first minutes colour what follows."
  - "The question is clear enough to check. Asking for a different one gives up a chance to show the story he has, and it can sound as if he has none."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Karim is a freelance designer. He says his weakness is that he answers client messages late when he is deep in work. Last month a client waited two days for an answer about a file. Now he checks messages at 12:00 and 17:00, and keeps a note of each check. In three weeks no client has asked about a delay. The interviewer asks: 'How do you know it works?' Which reply fits his facts?"
options:
  - "It works perfectly. I will never be late with a message again, and all of my clients are happy now."
  - "My whole team says that my replies are much faster now, and the clients have noticed the change."
  - "It seems to work: no client has asked about a delay in three weeks, and I note each check."
  - "I do not know if it works, because I have not looked at any of the messages or my notes."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "'Perfectly' and 'never' go past three weeks of notes. The next question, 'what if you are ill?', would break it."
  - "Karim is a freelancer, so there is no team, and nobody is said to have commented. This invents a witness."
  - "Right. It gives the real signal, three weeks with no delay questions, and says where the proof is: his notes. It claims only what three weeks can show."
  - "He does have evidence: his notes and the three quiet weeks. Saying he does not know hides facts he can show."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sana's interview is in three days. Her English is clear, but her sentences are often 45 words long, and she speaks quickly when she is nervous. She worries that her accent will count against her. What is the best use of the three days?"
options:
  - "Spend them changing her accent with videos, since that is what interviewers judge her on most"
  - "Practise sentences of about 15 words, one idea each, and start with a pause sentence"
  - "Practise speaking even faster, so the interviewer has less time to notice her accent at all"
  - "Tell the interviewer her English is poor, so that he is kind and asks her easier questions"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "An accent cannot be changed in three days, and the effort adds worry. Her real, fixable problem is the length of her sentences."
  - "Right. Short sentences and a planned pause fix what she controls: how easy her answers are to follow. Her English is already clear."
  - "Speed and pauses are what listeners use to judge how fluent a speaker sounds, so faster speech can make her harder to follow, not easier."
  - "Her English is clear, so this is not true. It also lowers the interviewer's first impression of her, which colours the first answers."
```
