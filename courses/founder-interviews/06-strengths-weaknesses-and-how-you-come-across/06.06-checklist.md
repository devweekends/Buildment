## Module 6 checklist

- [ ] My strength is narrow: one skill that someone could check
- [ ] It has one recent example with a fact or number, and what another person said or saw, or an honest gap
- [ ] My weakness is real: a habit or skill that matters for the work, not a strength in disguise
- [ ] My weakness has one example and a plan that a person could see me follow
- [ ] I have one pause sentence and one check question ready for my next video call
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 6.2 to 6.4, and finished the quiz in 6.5

## Learning log

1. **Your strength:** What was the broad version of your strength, and which questions made it narrow? (Or use Hadeel's: what was her broad version, and what did the questions change?)
2. **Your witness:** Who could say something about this strength, and what would you ask them to write? (Or use Hadeel's: who could say it for her?)
3. **Your weakness:** Which weakness did you almost choose that was really a strength? Why did you not choose it? (Or use Hadeel's: why is waiting too long to ask for help a real weakness for a team?)
4. **Your next call:** What is the one thing you will do before your next video call: a test of your camera, light and sound, a pause sentence, or a check question? (Or use Hadeel's: what would you tell her to test first?)

## Your task: one strength and one weakness

This task puts the module in two short answers. The first is a strength that someone can check. Name one skill, tell one recent time with a fact or a number, and say what another person said or saw. If nobody has said anything yet, say that honestly and say whom you will ask. The second is a real weakness. Name a habit or a skill gap that matters for the work, give one example with what it cost, and say what you do about it. The plan should be something a person could see.

Write each answer in about 60 to 90 words, in short sentences. Then add one pause sentence and one check question that you would use on a video call. You do not need to write about health, family or hardship, and you should not. Choose a skill or a habit from work or study.

Here is a strong answer from a fictional fellow. Jamal is an operations coordinator at a small online shop. His strength: "I find where an order goes wrong between the shop and the courier. Last month I looked at 30 late parcels and found that 9 had a phone number in the wrong format. I added a check on the order form. The shop manager now asks me to look at other order problems." His weakness: "I say yes to too much. In March I said yes to three requests in one week and finished two of them late. Now I write the requests in a list and tell the manager what will move before I say yes. I have only done this for a few weeks, so I cannot say yet how much it helps." His pause sentence: "Let me think for a moment." His check question: "Do you mean this month or this year?"

Look at what makes it strong. The strength is one skill with an input and an output. The example has numbers, 9 of 30, and the witness is an action: the manager asks for more. The weakness is a habit with a cost, and the plan is a rule with a clear step. The last sentence is an honest limit. Nothing in it is an adjective that cannot be checked.

A weak answer to the same task looks like this: "My strength is that I am a hard worker and a team player. My weakness is that I care too much about my work." It fails at the first follow-up. "How do you know you are a hard worker?" has no answer, and "can you give an example of caring too much?" has no time to point to. Neither sentence is false, and neither can be checked. The second one is also a humblebrag, which chapter 6.3 showed.

If you prefer not to use your own work, use Hadeel's. Hadeel is 24, a frontend developer applying to small remote teams. Her strength is finding why pages are slow on cheap phones. Her evidence is the booking page for a clinic booking app. It took about 6 seconds to load on the team's cheapest test phone on a slow connection, and she cut it to about 2 seconds. She measured 10 loads before and after and reports the middle value. Afterwards the founder asked her to check the clinic dashboard too. Her weakness is that she waits too long before asking for help. She once spent two days stuck on a time-zone bug in the booking form before she asked the backend developer, who found it in an hour. Her plan: after two hours stuck, she writes down what she tried and asks. She has done this on the bakery work.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your answers with the table below. Short answers can be Met. Length is not a criterion.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Narrow strength | One skill with an input and an output that someone could check | A kind of work, but not yet one skill | Adjectives only, such as hard-working |
| Evidence | A recent example with a fact or number, and a witness or an honest gap | An example, but no witness and no gap named | No example, or a quote or number you cannot source |
| Real weakness | A habit or skill that matters, with one example and what it cost | A real habit, but no example or no cost | A strength in disguise, or a weakness that is the whole job |
| Plan | A step that a person could see, with an honest limit | A plan, but only a wish such as "try harder" | No plan, or a claim that it is already fixed |

**Pass rule:** your answer passes when no part is "Not yet" and at least three parts are "Met".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions, and often one of them is a "why". For example: "Who saw that?" or "What did the late work cost?" You answer in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

## Practice with the AI interviewer

The drill below plays an interviewer. It asks one opening question about a strength. It follows your answer with "why?" and "how do you know?", then moves to a second topic. It ends with strengths and weaknesses, each with a fix. It is best to answer as you would in a real interview: one real time, in short sentences.

Use it like a real conversation. "I did not measure that" and "no one has said that yet" are good answers, and the interviewer is set up to accept them. Its feedback is practice, not a hiring decision, and it can be wrong. Check each point against your own words, and ask your mentor if you disagree. AI tools tend to agree with you and to like long answers.

Do not paste your full name, phone number, email, address, ID numbers, passwords, keys, client or employer names, private company data or other people's details. Use roles instead: "a client", "my team lead", "a clinic manager". If you do not have a key, the written task below completes this gate.

```aiinterview
title: "Strengths and weaknesses"
opening: "What is one real strength you bring to a small team? Tell me about one time it showed."
focus: [strengths-weaknesses, storytelling]
context: none
maxTurns: 10
whyDepth: 3
required: false
rubric: "A strong interview shows a narrow strength with one recent time, a fact or number, and something another person said or saw; and a real weakness that matters for the work, with one example and a plan someone could see. Probes: ask for one specific time when the answer is adjectives or a habit; ask 'What was your part?' when the answer says we; ask 'How do you know?' or 'Who saw that?' after a strength; ask 'What did it cost?' and 'What are you doing about it?' after a weakness. Treat a strength dressed as a weakness, such as perfectionism, as a pseudo-story and ask for a real example. Stop rules: accept 'no one has said that yet', 'I don't know' and 'that was not my decision' as full answers, reply briefly and ask what they would do. Never push the learner to guess. Score features, not length; an honest gap with a plan beats an unsourced number. Quote the learner's words. Flags by name: vague ownership, unsupported number, pseudo-story, invented reason risk, jargon without explanation. Be kind and direct, about the answer, not the person. Never ask about health, family or hardship. If evidence is thin, suggest how to get it, such as asking a past client for one sentence."
```
