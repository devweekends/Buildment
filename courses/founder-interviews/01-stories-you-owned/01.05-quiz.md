```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tareq builds the checkout for a small online shop. Interviewer: 'Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.' Tareq: 'I usually say what I think, politely.' Interviewer: 'Which time?' Tareq's facts: his manager wanted coupon codes to be case-sensitive. In the coupon log, 6 of 20 failed entries differed only in capital letters. He showed her, and she agreed to ignore case. What should he say next?"
options:
  - "My manager wanted case-sensitive codes. I showed her that 6 of 20 failed entries differed only in capitals, and she agreed to ignore case."
  - "I usually say what I think, in a polite way. In the 2 years I have worked here, my managers have liked that, and I plan to keep doing it here."
  - "Disagreeing with a manager is a skill that I learned early on. I try to bring 1 or 2 facts to each disagreement, and I stay calm."
  - "My manager and I once had a long talk about the codes. She agreed with me in the end, and the 20 entries went down that same week."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It answers 'Which time?' with one disagreement, the fact he brought and the result. Each part comes from his stated facts."
  - "This is a habit again. The interviewer asked for one time, and 'my managers have liked that' is an opinion, not a day."
  - "This is a skill statement, not an episode. It names no day, no manager and nothing Tareq did."
  - "It names a talk, but not the fact he showed or what changed. 'The 20 entries went down' is not in the facts: 20 was the number of failed entries before."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Fadi, a mobile developer in a team of four, worked on the order status screen of a delivery app. The designer drew the screen. A backend developer made the status data. Fadi wrote the screen code and fixed a bug that showed old statuses. His team lead decided to release on Friday. Which sentence about his part is accurate and clear?"
options:
  - "I owned the order status screen end to end, from the first design to the Friday release. I made sure it worked on 4 phone sizes."
  - "We built the order status screen in a team of four. I contributed to the success of the team on it in many different ways."
  - "The designer drew it and a teammate made the data. I wrote the screen code and fixed the bug that showed old statuses."
  - "I helped with the order status screen in a team of four. I was involved in the bug fixing and in the testing of it."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The designer drew the screen and the team lead chose the Friday release. 'End to end' claims places that were not Fadi's, and '4 phone sizes' is not in the facts."
  - "'We' and 'contributed in many different ways' leave the founder with the same question: what did Fadi do?"
  - "Right. It names the others by role and gives Fadi's two real actions, writing the screen code and fixing a named bug."
  - "'Helped with' and 'was involved in' are soft verbs that hide what he did. The facts give two clear actions, and this uses neither."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Asma is a support analyst at a small app company. First replies took about 20 hours. She had three options: a template for first replies, handwritten answers, or an automatic message only. A reply within one day mattered most. She chose a template with a personal line, and replies fell to about 6 hours. Two customers found it cold, so she rewrote the line. Which version is a decision story?"
options:
  - "I chose templates because they are fast, and reply times fell from 20 hours to 6. It was a clear success for the customers, and the team was pleased with it."
  - "Replies were slow, so we made a template for the first reply. After that, replies fell to about 6 hours, and we read the feedback from some customers."
  - "I think about the options before I decide. Then I pick what suits the customers and the team, and I check how it went after a few weeks."
  - "I weighed a template, handwritten replies and an automatic message. Speed mattered most, so I chose a template. Replies fell from 20 hours to 6, and I rewrote a cold line."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "It names one option and a perfect ending. The facts include two other options and a cold reaction from two customers, and both are missing."
  - "'We' hides Asma's part, and no options or reasons appear. The founder cannot tell if anyone decided anything."
  - "This describes how Asma thinks in general. It has no time, no options from the case and no result."
  - "Right. It names the three options, what mattered, the choice, the result and what she changed after the feedback. The founder has a decision to ask about."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adam is an operations assistant at a charity shop. His manager decided to count donations at the door. Adam designed the tally sheet and trained 4 volunteers to use it. An AI tool rewrites his line: 'I led a team that redesigned the charity shop's stock system, which shows my strong leadership.' What is the problem with this line?"
options:
  - "It claims too little. Adam designed the sheet and trained 4 volunteers, so the line should say that he ran the shop."
  - "'I led' claims a role Adam did not have, because the manager decided to count at the door. He designed the sheet."
  - "It leaves out the shop's real name and its manager, so the interviewer cannot check the 4 volunteers or the sheet."
  - "It says 'strong leadership', and the only fix is to add a percentage, so that the line sounds concrete to a reader."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The line already claims too much, not too little. The manager decided, and Adam did not run the shop."
  - "Right. The facts say the manager made the decision, so 'led' and 'redesigned the stock system' claim more than Adam did. He can say: 'My manager decided to count at the door. I designed the tally sheet and trained 4 volunteers.'"
  - "Names are private details. A practice answer should use roles such as 'my manager' and 'a charity shop', as the course teaches."
  - "A percentage with no source would be a second invented claim. The fix is a true account of his part."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nadia, a volunteer, built a check that finds two classes in one room at the same time for a school timetable. Last year's timetable had 6 clashes that the teachers found by hand. Nadia's check found those 6 and 4 more that nobody had seen. It ran in two seconds. The teachers have not yet used it on this year's timetable. The interviewer asks, 'How do you know the check works?' Which answer is best?"
options:
  - "I know it works because I wrote it carefully and tested it a lot. I am sure about my own code and my own work."
  - "The teachers like it, so it works on any timetable. They will use it again next year, and I expect more clashes."
  - "On last year's timetable it found the teachers' 6 clashes and 4 more. I have not run it on this year's yet."
  - "It ran in two seconds with no errors, so it finds the clashes in any timetable. I tested it on 1 timetable."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Care and confidence are not evidence. The next question, 'tested how?', has no answer in this reply."
  - "The teachers have not used it on this year's timetable, so 'works on every timetable' is more than the facts show."
  - "Right. It gives a source anyone can check (the teachers' own list), what the check added, and an honest limit about this year's timetable."
  - "Speed does not show correctness. Two seconds with no errors says nothing about clashes the check might miss, and one timetable is a small test."
```
