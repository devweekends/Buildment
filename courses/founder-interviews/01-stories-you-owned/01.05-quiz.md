```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Salma designs the event form for a university club. Interviewer: 'Tell me about a time a design of yours failed.' Salma: 'Designs fail all the time. I usually test with a few users and fix what they find.' Interviewer: 'Which time?' Salma's facts: last term she tested the form with 3 friends on small phones. Two could not find the Submit button. She moved it up and tested again, and all 3 sent the form. What should she say next?"
options:
  - "Last term I tested the club's form with 3 friends. Two could not find Submit, so I moved it up. All 3 then sent it."
  - "It happens in every project, and I always fix what users find, so in the end the club's form was fine and everybody could use it."
  - "Many designs fail at first. Testing with a few users is a habit that I believe every designer should follow in every single project."
  - "I am good at learning from failure. The club's form taught me a lot about users and about myself, and I still think about it often."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It answers 'Which time?' with one term, one test, one problem, her action and the result. Each part comes from her stated facts."
  - "'It happens in every project' and 'I always fix' are another habit. The interviewer asked for one time, and this still gives none."
  - "This is an opinion about designers in general. It names no time, no test and nothing Salma did."
  - "This describes Salma and her feelings. It does not use any of the facts, such as the 3 friends or the Submit button."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Fadi, a mobile developer in a team of four, worked on the order status screen of a delivery app. The designer drew the screen. A backend developer made the status data. Fadi wrote the screen code and fixed a bug that showed old statuses. His team lead decided to ship on Friday. Which sentence about his part is accurate and clear?"
options:
  - "I owned the order status screen end to end, from the first design to the Friday release, and I made sure it worked."
  - "We built the order status screen, and I contributed to the team's success on it in many different ways."
  - "The designer drew it and a teammate made the data. I wrote the screen code and fixed the old status bug."
  - "I helped with the order status screen, and I was involved in the bug fixing and in testing that it all worked."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The designer drew the screen and the team lead chose the Friday release. 'End to end' claims places that were not Fadi's."
  - "'We' and 'contributed in many ways' leave the founder with the same question: what did Fadi do?"
  - "Right. It names the others by role and gives Fadi's two real actions, writing the screen code and fixing a named bug."
  - "'Helped with' and 'was involved in' are soft verbs that hide what he did. The facts give two clear actions, and this uses neither."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Asma is a support analyst at a small app company. First replies took about 20 hours. She had three options: a template for first replies, handwritten answers, or only an automatic message. A reply within one day and warmth mattered most. She chose a template with one personal line. The inbox report showed about 6 hours over four weeks. Two customers found it cold, so she rewrote the line. Which version is a decision story?"
options:
  - "I chose templates because they are fast, and reply times fell a lot. It was a clear success for the team and for the customers, and everybody was happy with the result."
  - "Replies were slow, so we made a template for the first reply. After that replies were fast, and we also looked at some of the customer feedback that came in later."
  - "I always think about the options before I decide anything. Then I choose what is best for the customers and for the team, and I check that it works for everybody."
  - "I weighed a template, handwritten replies and an automatic message. Speed mattered most, so I chose a template, and I changed it after two customers found it cold."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "It names one option and a perfect ending. The facts include two other options and a cold reaction from two customers, and both are missing."
  - "'We' hides Asma's part, and no options or reasons appear. The founder cannot tell if anyone decided anything."
  - "This describes how Asma thinks in general. It has no time, no options from the case and no result."
  - "Right. It names the three options, what mattered, the choice, and what she changed after the feedback. The founder has a decision to ask about."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Reem is an operations coordinator at a translation agency, in a team of five. The operations lead chose a rule that every file gets a version name. Reem added the names and counted complaints on the agency's complaints sheet: six wrong-file complaints the month before and two the month after. An AI tool rewrites her answer: 'I led a process improvement that reduced wrong-file complaints by 67 percent, which shows my strong leadership.' What is the problem with this answer?"
options:
  - "The percentage is wrong, because going from six complaints to two is a drop of about 50 percent."
  - "It says 'I led', but the operations lead chose the rule. It gives Reem a role she did not have."
  - "It is too short, because a good answer should always be longer than a single sentence."
  - "It uses a number, and numbers should never be used in an answer to an interviewer."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The arithmetic is right: six to two is a drop of two thirds, about 67 percent. The problem is a different one."
  - "Right. The facts say the lead chose the rule, so 'I led' claims ownership Reem did not have. A founder's second question would break it. She can say: 'The lead chose the rule. I added the version names and counted the complaints.'"
  - "Length is not the issue. A short answer with a true account of her part is better than a long answer with a borrowed role."
  - "A number is fine when it has a source. Reem has one: the complaints sheet. The problem is the word 'led'."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nadia, a volunteer, built a check that finds two classes in one room at the same time for a school timetable. Last year's timetable had 6 clashes that the teachers found by hand. Nadia's check found those 6 and 4 more that nobody had seen. It ran in two seconds. The teachers have not yet used it on this year's timetable. The interviewer asks, 'How do you know the check works?' Which answer is best?"
options:
  - "I know it works because I wrote it carefully, I tested it a lot, and I am very sure about my own code and my own work."
  - "On last year's timetable it found the teachers' 6 clashes and 4 more. I have not run it on this year's yet."
  - "The teachers like it, so it works on every timetable, and they will use it again next year as well."
  - "It ran in two seconds with no errors, which shows that it finds every clash in any timetable at all."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Care and confidence are not evidence. The next question, 'tested how?', has no answer in this reply."
  - "Right. It gives a source anyone can check (the teachers' own list), what the check added, and an honest limit about this year's timetable."
  - "The teachers have not used it on this year's timetable, so 'works on every timetable' is more than the facts show."
  - "Speed does not show correctness. Two seconds with no errors says nothing about clashes the check might miss."
```
