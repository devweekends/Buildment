```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tareq's resume says: 'Cut support emails about coupons by 30 percent.' The interviewer asks, 'From what?' His notes say: the support inbox held 40 coupon emails in the 4 weeks before his check and 28 in the 4 weeks after. Which reply do his notes support?"
options:
  - "Coupon emails fell by 12, which is 43 percent of the 28 left. The count comes from the support inbox."
  - "Coupon emails fell from 40 to 28, which is 30 percent fewer. The count comes from my memory of those weeks."
  - "Coupon emails fell from 40 to 28 in the week with the fewest emails each month. That is 30 percent fewer, from the inbox tool."
  - "Coupon emails fell from 40 to 28 in four weeks each, which is 30 percent fewer. The count is from the inbox."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "This counts the change from the 28 left, not from the 40 before. Twelve out of 40 is 30 percent, so the baseline is wrong."
  - "The numbers are right but the source is not. His notes say the count came from the support inbox, not from memory."
  - "The window is wrong. His notes compare 4 weeks with 4 weeks, not the best week of each month."
  - "Right. It has the before (40), the after (28), the window (4 weeks each side), the arithmetic (12 out of 40 is 30 percent) and the source."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Reem's complaints sheet shows wrong-file complaints each week. Four weeks before her change: 3, 2, 3, 2. Four weeks after: 1, 2, 1, 0. She wants to put a result on her resume. Which line is honest and matches the sheet?"
options:
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell from 10 in four weeks to 4 in the next four weeks (the sheet)."
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell from 2.5 a week to 0 in the last week, a drop of 100 percent (the sheet)."
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell by about 90 percent, from 10 in four weeks to 1 in the last week (the sheet)."
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell from 3 a week to 1 a week, using the last two weeks of the 4 (the sheet)."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 is 10, and 1 + 2 + 1 + 0 is 4. Same window on each side, and a source she can name."
  - "This compares an average with the single best week. One week with 0 is a chance, not a result."
  - "This compares four weeks with one week, and 10 to 1 is not 90 percent of anything the sheet shows. The window is mixed."
  - "This picks the weeks that look best. Choosing the window after seeing the result makes the claim look better than it was."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Adam kept the month-end stock count at a charity shop. After his tally sheet, the count fell from 4 hours to 1 hour. A second charity shop, which did not use the sheet, fell from 4 hours to 3.5 hours in the same months, because a new volunteer joined. Which sentence is honest about his part?"
options:
  - "The count fell by 3 hours, and the 3 hours are mine, because the tally sheet was my idea."
  - "The count fell by 3 hours. The other shop fell by 0.5 hour, so only 0.5 hour is mine, as a hint."
  - "The count fell by 3 hours. The other shop fell by 0.5 hour without the sheet, so about 2.5 hours may be mine."
  - "I cannot say anything, because a second shop is too different to compare. The 3 hours are a rough guess."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The other shop saved 0.5 hour with no sheet, so the full 3 hours is not his. A new volunteer also helped there."
  - "This swaps the two numbers: 0.5 hour is what happened without the sheet, not his share. His share is 3 minus 0.5."
  - "Right. 4 minus 1 is 3, the other shop saved 0.5, and 3 minus 0.5 is 2.5. 'May be mine' keeps it as a hint, not proof."
  - "Both shops started at 4 hours, so they were alike at the start. The comparison is imperfect, but it is a useful hint."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Salma designed a university club's event form. The form tool's report shows 62 of 100 starters finished before and 81 of 100 after. The club also emailed the link to all members for the first time that term. She never counted attendance. An AI tool rewrites her line: 'Redesigned the club's event form. Completion rose from 62 to 81 out of 100 starters (form tool report), and attendance rose by 30 people.' What should she do with it?"
options:
  - "Keep both sentences. The AI used her figures, and 30 people is a fair estimate of the extra attendance at events."
  - "Keep the completion figures and source, and say the club also emailed the link. Cut the attendance claim, which she never counted."
  - "Cut the whole line, because an AI wrote it and none of its figures can be trusted on a resume that people will read."
  - "Keep the attendance figure and cut the completion figures, because the club also emailed the link to all members that term, which affects every figure."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The 30 people has no source: she never counted attendance. The AI added a number that sounds concrete."
  - "Right. The first sentence matches her report, and the email is an honest limit. The attendance claim is invented, so she cuts it."
  - "Most of the line is correct and sourced. Cutting it all loses true evidence, and the check should be line by line against her own facts."
  - "This keeps the one number that has no source and cuts the ones that do. The email affects how much is hers, but it does not make the report wrong."
```
