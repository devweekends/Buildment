```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tareq's resume says: 'Reduced page load time by 40 percent.' The interviewer asks, 'From what?' His notes say: on the shop's cheapest test phone, the middle of 10 loads was 2.5 seconds before and 1.5 seconds after. Which reply do his notes support?"
options:
  - "On the shop's fastest phone, load time fell by 40 percent, and that is how I measured the whole of my change."
  - "Load time fell by 1 second, which is 100 percent faster, because one second is the full size of the change."
  - "I do not remember the exact numbers, but the tool I used reported 40 percent, and I trust the tool."
  - "On the cheapest test phone, the middle of 10 loads fell from 2.5 to 1.5 seconds, which is 40 percent less."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "His notes say the cheapest test phone, not the fastest. The reply changes a stated fact."
  - "One second out of 2.5 seconds is 40 percent, not 100 percent. The reply counts from the wrong number."
  - "A tool's figure that Tareq cannot explain is not a source he can defend. His notes hold the real numbers, and this reply ignores them."
  - "Right. It gives the before, the after, the method (10 loads, the middle value, the cheapest phone) and the arithmetic: 1 out of 2.5 is 40 percent."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Reem's complaints sheet shows wrong-file complaints each week. Four weeks before her change: 3, 2, 3, 2. Four weeks after: 1, 2, 1, 0. She wants to put a result on her resume. Which line is honest and matches the sheet?"
options:
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell from 10 in four weeks to 4 (the sheet)."
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell from 2.5 a week to none in the last week, a drop of 100 percent."
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell by about 90 percent, which made the whole agency much more reliable."
  - "Wrong-file complaints fell from 3 a week to 1 a week, because the last two weeks were the best."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. 3 + 2 + 3 + 2 is 10, and 1 + 2 + 1 + 0 is 4. Same window on each side, and a source she can name."
  - "This compares an average with the single best week. One week with 0 is a chance, not a result."
  - "From 10 to 4 is 60 percent less, not 90. The line also adds 'much more reliable', which has no source."
  - "This picks the weeks that look best. Choosing the window after seeing the result makes the claim look better than it was."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Nadia moved the sign-up button on a school workshop page. Sign-ups went from 20 to 35 a month. The same month, the school announced the workshop in its newsletter. A similar workshop page that she did not change went from 20 to 29. Which sentence is honest about her part?"
options:
  - "Sign-ups rose from 20 to 35 because I moved the button, so the full rise of 15 is mine."
  - "Sign-ups rose by 15, but the newsletter did all of it, so my change did nothing at all."
  - "Sign-ups rose from 20 to 35. The page I did not change rose from 20 to 29, so 9 of the rise is mine."
  - "Sign-ups rose from 20 to 35. A similar page I left alone rose from 20 to 29, so about 6 may be mine."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The page she did not change rose by 9 without the button. Claiming all 15 ignores that."
  - "Her page rose by 6 more than the page she did not change. 'Did nothing' is more than the facts show."
  - "The page she did not change rose by 9, so 9 is what happened without her. This sentence swaps her share and the comparison."
  - "Right. 35 minus 20 is 15, the comparison rose by 9, and 15 minus 9 is 6. 'May be mine' keeps it as a hint, not proof."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Fadi fixed a bug that made an app close by itself, but he never counted crashes. He asks an AI tool to improve his resume line. It suggests: 'Reduced app crashes by about 40 percent, improving the user experience.' What is the mistake in this suggestion?"
options:
  - "It says 'about', and the number would sound more exact and more believable to an interviewer without that word."
  - "The 40 percent has no source, because Fadi never counted crashes. It was added only to sound concrete."
  - "The line has only one number, and a good resume line should always have at least two numbers in it."
  - "Nothing is wrong, because AI tools suggest numbers only when they know them to be true from the facts."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Removing 'about' would make an invented number look exact. The problem is where the number came from."
  - "Right. Fadi has no count of crashes, so the number is made up. It would fall apart at 'how do you know?'. He can write what he fixed and add 'I did not count crashes; I would use the crash report tool'."
  - "More numbers would be more claims without sources. The line needs a real number or an honest gap, not a second number."
  - "AI tools can add a number that sounds right. They do not know what Fadi measured, and they tend to agree with what the user wants."
```

The log lines also hold customer emails. He knows the shop's real name and his lead's full name. Which message is safe and still useful?"
options:
  - "Here are 20 log lines with the customers' emails, so that you can see real examples of the failures."
  - "At the shop I work for, which I name in full with its web address, I cut failed payments, and my lead, whom I also name in full, can confirm."
  - "At a small online shop I cut failed payments from 5 percent to 2 percent: 124 of 2,480 to 52 of 2,600, from the payment log."
  - "I paste the shop's name, my lead's name and the 20 worst failures, so that you can judge my claim."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Customer emails are private data. Counting needs totals only, and the AI interviewer must never get personal details."
  - "This puts the shop's real name, its address and a real full name into the chat. Use roles such as 'a small online shop' and 'my lead'."
  - "Right. It uses a role for the shop and only totals, and it holds the before, the after and the source. Nothing private is in it."
  - "The shop's name, the lead's name and failure records are all private details. None of them is needed for a practice answer."
