```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Duaa is a data analyst at a clinic. She has one minute to answer 'What is the best technical thing you have done?' She could tell the story of a dashboard in a new tool, which she built by following a tutorial step by step. Or she could tell the story of a monthly report she moved into a spreadsheet formula, not a script, so the nurse manager could edit it. She saved about 3 hours a month, from her own time notes. Which should she tell?"
options:
  - "The dashboard, since the interviewer will know the new tool and respect it"
  - "Neither, since a spreadsheet formula is too small to count as technical work"
  - "The report: she chose the formula, and the 3 hours come from her own notes"
  - "Both, so the interviewer can pick whichever one sounds better to them"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "She followed a tutorial, so she made no choices to explain, and the first 'why this way?' would have no answer. A known tool does not replace a decision."
  - "Technical here means a careful decision in your own craft. Choosing a formula over a script, for a reason, is exactly that, and it has a source for its number."
  - "Right. The choice is hers, the reason is clear and the number has a source. She can explain it five whys deep, which the tutorial dashboard cannot offer."
  - "Two stories in one minute give neither of them room for a decision. She has about a minute, and the interviewer will follow only one of them anyway."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sana is an operations coordinator at a charity shop. She says to a founder: 'I added conditional formatting rules that flag duplicate SKUs.' The founder asks: 'What was the problem?' Her facts: volunteers counted the same boxes twice, with 14 duplicate rows in one month. She chose to colour a repeated box red, not block the entry, because volunteers type fast. Two months later there were 3 duplicates. Which reply is best?"
options:
  - "Volunteers counted boxes twice: 14 duplicate rows in a month. I made repeats turn red, not blocked. Two months later, 3."
  - "It was a data quality problem. I added validation rules to the sheet, and the data quality is much better now than before."
  - "I cut duplicates by about 79 percent, using a custom formula on the SKU column, so the sheet is more accurate now overall."
  - "Volunteers counted some boxes twice. I added a rule that stops any repeated box, so there are fewer duplicates in the sheet now."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It names the problem in the volunteers' terms, says what she changed in one sentence, gives the choice she made against blocking, and gives a before and an after."
  - "'Data quality' and 'much better' leave the founder with nothing to picture or check. There is no count, no choice and no time."
  - "The percentage hides the 14 and the 3, and it gives no time window. 'Custom formula on the SKU column' is a term the founder has to ask about."
  - "She chose to colour a repeated box, not stop it, because volunteers type fast. This reply says she blocks entries, which is not what she built."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jamal is a backend developer who built the checkout for a small online shop. An engineer asks: 'What happens if the payment service sends the same paid message twice?' His facts: he saves each message's id and ignores a repeat. He tested by sending the same test message twice, and the order was marked paid once. He never tested messages that arrive in the wrong order. Which answer fits his facts?"
options:
  - "That cannot happen, because the payment service is reliable and is built to send each message only once, to each shop"
  - "I save each message's id, so every case is covered and the order is always right, even when messages arrive late"
  - "I do not know, because I never tested what happens when a message repeats, and I would need to check first"
  - "A repeat is ignored. I sent one test message twice and it was marked paid once. I did not test wrong order."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "He ran a test with a repeated message because repeats can happen. This answer says the opposite of what he did, and it gives no test."
  - "'Every case is covered' goes past his facts. He never tested messages in the wrong order, so the claim breaks at the next question."
  - "He did test repeats. Saying he did not know hides his own test and throws away the best part of his answer."
  - "Right. It says what happens, names the small test he ran, and says honestly what he did not test. The engineer can ask about the gap, and he has a true answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wael built a sign-up form for his university club's events. It saves each sign-up to a sheet, and 40 students used it in the first week. He never measured speed. He asks an AI chat tool to improve his answer to 'best technical thing'. The AI replies: 'Say: I architected a serverless, event-driven registration pipeline with sub-second latency. These terms show the founder you are senior.' What is the mistake in this advice?"
options:
  - "The advice is right, since senior engineers always use exact technical terms"
  - "It adds a speed he never measured, and its terms hide the problem he solved"
  - "It is too short, so he should add a longer list of tools to the answer"
  - "It is fine, but he should add a percentage so the answer sounds concrete"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The founder does not write code, and a sentence full of terms gives him nothing to follow. Plain words are what a founder asks for."
  - "Right. 'Sub-second' is a number he never measured, and the first 'from what?' would break it. The terms also bury the problem: 40 students signed up in a week."
  - "More tools would add more unexplained words. The answer needs the problem, one choice and a true result, and not a longer list."
  - "A percentage he cannot source would be a second invented number. AI tools can add plausible figures, so the facts should come from his own records."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rana built an online order form for a bakery. Once, two orders took the same pickup slot, and she learned of it when a customer complained. Now she checks the sheet by hand each morning. An engineer asks: 'What would you change?' Which answer is best?"
options:
  - "Nothing, since the form works and the customers are happy with it as it is"
  - "I would rewrite the form with a newer framework to make it better overall"
  - "I would stop two orders taking one slot. I check the sheet by hand now."
  - "I would add more features so the bakery can sell more things online too"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "She knows of a real fault: two orders took one slot. 'Nothing' hides it, and an engineer is unlikely to believe that any work has no weak point."
  - "A new framework has nothing to do with the fault she found. A change should come from a weak point she can name."
  - "Right. It names the real weak point, the change, and the hand check she does today. It is short, specific and true."
  - "More features do not fix the double booking. The answer ignores the one problem she actually met."
```
