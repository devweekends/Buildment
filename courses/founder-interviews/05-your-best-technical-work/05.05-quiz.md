question: "Sana is an operations coordinator at a school office. She says to a founder: 'I added conditional formatting rules that flag double-booked rooms.' The founder asks: 'What was the problem?' Her facts: teachers booked the same room for the same period, with 14 clashes in one term. She chose to colour a clash red, not block the booking, because teachers book by phone. The next term there were 3 clashes. Which reply is best?"
options:
  - "Our school had 14 clashes in a term. The head teacher's assistant asked for a rule that blocks a double booking, and I added it. The next term had 3."
  - "Teachers booked one room twice: 14 clashes in one term. I made a clash turn red, not blocked, because teachers book by phone. The next term, 3."
  - "I added conditional formatting rules to the booking sheet. They flag a clash in red, and the next term had 3 clashes against 14 before."
  - "I cut room clashes by about 79 percent with a custom rule on the room column, so the booking sheet is more accurate now."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "This gives the wrong owner and the wrong choice: the assistant did not ask for a block, and Sana chose to colour a clash, not block it. The numbers are right, but the story is not hers."
  - "Right. It names the problem in the teachers' terms, says what she changed, gives the choice she made against blocking, and gives a before and an after with their time window."
  - "The numbers are right, but it starts with a term, 'conditional formatting', and not with the problem. The founder has to ask what a clash is, and why red."
  - "The percentage hides the 14 and the 3, and it gives no time window. 'Custom rule on the room column' is a term the founder has to ask about."
