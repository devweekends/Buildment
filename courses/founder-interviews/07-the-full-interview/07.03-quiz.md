```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Majd is a backend developer in a team of four that builds a school timetable tool. He tells a founder: 'We moved timetable changes to a shared sheet with a script, and it made changes about three times faster.' The founder asks: 'Three times faster than what? And what was your part?' Majd's facts: he wrote the script himself, and the team chose the shared sheet. The school office logs the time of every change, but Majd has never looked at the log. Which answer should he give?"
options:
  - "I think changes took about 30 minutes before and take 10 now. We all worked on the sheet, so it was a team result."
  - "I wrote the script, and the team chose the sheet. I did not measure it. I would ask the office for its log and compare before and after."
  - "I wrote the script and the team chose the sheet. Changes took about 30 minutes before and take 10 now, so it is three times faster."
  - "The office liked the sheet, and nobody has complained since. That is the result I can point to from the last month of use."
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "'We all worked on the sheet' hides his own part, and 'I think' shows he has no source for the 30 and 10 minutes. He has never looked at the log."
  - "Right. He separates his part from the team's, says plainly that he did not measure the speed, and names the log he would ask for and how he would compare."
  - "His part and the team's are clear, but he has never looked at the office log, so 30 and 10 minutes would be numbers he made up. The next 'how do you know?' has no answer."
  - "It answers neither question. It gives no before, no after and no part of his own, and 'nobody has complained' is not a measure of speed."

  - "Teams answer faster when each message has its own button, so I chose that layout. It also matches how the team already works today."
  - "It was my lead's decision, so I cannot say why the inbox shows the owner's name or where the button sits. I only built it."
  - "I decided that double replies were the main problem for the team. So I put a button on each message, and my lead agreed with it."
  - "My lead asked for a button on each message after two customers got two replies. I chose to show the owner's name once assigned."

explanations:
  - "This is a tidy reason that sounds sure. She has no data on efficiency, so the next 'how do you know?' has no answer."
  - "It is honest about the lead's call, but it gives up the part she did decide: showing the owner's name after assigning."
  - "The reason appears only now and contradicts the facts. Her lead asked for the button, so saying she decided it is a reason invented afterwards."
  - "Right. It gives the reason she had at the time, says what was her lead's request, and names the part she chose herself. Every next why can be answered."

question: "Mustafa is a student who also tests software part time. He is applying to a small team that runs a phone repair booking site. The founder says: 'Bookings fell from 200 a month to 140. You start next week. What would you do?' Mustafa asks one question and learns that in the same month the site began asking for a $5 deposit, and the confirmation email began going to spam for some customers. One developer is free for two weeks. Turning the deposit off takes a day. The team's target for a fix is 170 bookings a month, and the normal swing is about 10. Which answer follows the case shape from module 4?"
options:
  - "I assume both changes matter. I turn the deposit off first, as it takes a day. I keep that at 180 or more, and check the emails at 160 or less."
  - "Turn the deposit off for customers now. It began in the same month that bookings fell, so it is the only cause of the 60 lost bookings."
  - "Fix the confirmation emails first, because spam is the bigger problem. That would use the free developer for two weeks and bring bookings back to 200."
  - "Wait one more month and gather data on the 200 earlier bookings, then choose from what I find. A decision now would only be a guess."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It states an assumption, picks the cheaper fix, and uses the target of 170 and the swing of 10 to say when to keep it and when to change course."
  - "It treats timing as proof. The emails changed in the same month, and the answer states no assumption and no number to watch."
  - "It starts with the costly fix and gives no reason that it is bigger. It never compares the day-long fix, and it names no number to watch."
  - "It avoids a decision. The founder asked what Mustafa would do, and waiting names no option, no target and no point for changing course."

Nabil's work: someone asked for password reset emails again and again on one account, which filled the owner's inbox. Nabil added a limit of 5 reset emails a day for each account.   - "I added a rate limiter on the reset endpoint, using a sliding window of 24 hours. The limit is 5 for each account, and the check runs before the email is sent."
  - "I used a cache with a 24-hour expiry and wrote 12 tests with mocks. The reset code is covered, and the build passes on the main branch."
  - "One account got reset emails again and again, and the owner's inbox filled up. I added a limit of 5 a day, since support said 3 is enough. None went over it in two months."
  - "I built a feature in 4 days that the team needed after a customer complaint. The team liked how it worked once it was finished, and I had no problems."
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "'Rate limiter', 'endpoint' and 'sliding window' need a technical reader. The founder said he does not write code, so he stops following at the first sentence."
  - "It lists tools and tests but never says what problem it solved for a person. The founder has nothing to ask about except the tools."
  - "Right. It gives the problem in the owner's terms, what he built in one sentence, the key choice and why, and the result with its source in plain words."
  - "It has no problem, no choice and no result that a founder can check. It sounds like progress but gives nothing to ask about."
