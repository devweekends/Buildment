## Course checklist

- [ ] I can tell one real time, say which part was mine, and name the decision, the options and what mattered then
- [ ] Every number I say has a before, an after, a time window and a source, or I say plainly that I did not measure it
- [ ] I can give the reason I had at the time for any line on my resume, including "that was not my decision"
- [ ] I can work a case question aloud, and explain my best technical work to a founder and then to an engineer
- [ ] I can name a narrow strength and a real weakness with evidence, and I know how to take time to think
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapter 7.2, finished the mixed review in 7.3, submitted my capstone in 7.4, and submitted my plan below

## Your interview kit

The table below has one line for each tool in the course. It is short on purpose. In an interview you will not have time to reread seven modules. You will have time to remember one line, and the line will bring back the chapter.

| Tool | One line to remember |
|---|---|
| One time | Tell one time: one day, one problem, one decision. Keep the background short |
| My part | Say "we" for the team's work and "I" for mine, and make the line between them clear |
| The decision | Name the options, what mattered then, what happened, how you know and what you would change |
| An honest number | A before, an after, a time window, a source and your share. Or: "I did not measure that, and here is how I would" |
| The real reason | Give the reason you had then, even "that was not my decision". Check each resume line: what, why, result, proof |
| The case shape | Clarify, assume aloud, give two or three options, choose one, name your target and when you would change course |
| The two technical versions | Founder version first: problem, build, choice, result. Engineer version if asked: why this, what if it fails, the test |
| A strength and a weakness | A narrow strength with a recent example and a witness. A real weakness with an example and what you do about it |
| Time to think | Say "Let me think for a moment." Ask "Do you mean...?" Use short sentences, one idea in each |
| Reading feedback | Tag your own answers first. Compare with the card. Question lines your own words do not support |

Copy the table where you will see it: the notes app on your phone, or the first page of a notebook. Read it the evening before an interview. You do not need every line every time. A question about a project needs "one time", "my part" and "the decision". A question about a result needs "an honest number". Part of the skill is choosing which two lines fit the question in front of you.

### How to look back at your first story

The first question matters most. In gate 1 you wrote a story before you knew most of the tools in this course. Reading it now is the clearest way to see how far you have come.

Here is an example from a fictional fellow, Shahd. She is a data assistant at a pharmacy. In gate 1 she wrote, "I built a sales sheet for the pharmacy and the owner liked it." Reading it now, she sees three things. First, it tells what she built, but not one time or the problem. Second, it names no decision: she chose a weekly sheet over a daily one, and she never said why. Third, it has no number and no honest gap. The owner's words are not a result. She would add the decision and the other option, and she would say, "I did not measure how often the owner uses it, and I would count how many weeks he opened it." She would cut "the owner liked it", because she cannot check it.

Write your own look-back the same way: what you would add, what you would cut, and one thing you already did well. Four or five honest sentences show your mentor more than a page of general words.

## Your task: your plan for the next interview

This is the last piece of work in the course. You will write a plan for your next real interview, using your story bank, your audited resume and your feedback. Keep the bank as you have kept it so far, on one paper page or in one note for each story. It should have five stories now. The plan is short, but it forces three decisions: which stories you lead with, which resume line is weakest, and which gap you fill first. These are the three things that change an interview, and you can do all three before it starts.

A strong plan names the stories by their time, not by a topic. "The offline mode in March" can be checked, and "my best project" cannot. It says why the weakest line fails, at which why, and shows the old and the new line. It names one gap, one honest way to fill it, a step and a date. A weak plan says: "I will lead with my best projects, practise more and be more confident. My gap is confidence." Under the next why, it has no answer. Which projects? What was your part? Which line is weakest? What will you do about confidence, and by when? A plan is useful when someone could look at it on the day and see whether you did it.

If you prefer not to use your own work for a part, use Hadeel's, and say so. She would lead with line A, the three-step booking form, because she owned it. She would lead with line B, with the honest figure of about 6 points in the four clinics that only had reminders, and with her own part, the reminder rule and the 18:00 choice. She would lead with line C, her best technical work, in both versions. Her weakest line is D, which she rewrites as "Wrote a short test checklist we used before each release for my last three months". Her gap is a number for line A or for the bakery work, with a plan to count.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads four parts. Each part has three levels. Two fellows can lead with different stories and both be Met.

**Part 1: three stories.**
- Met: Three stories, each with the time, your part and the decision in one line, chosen to fit different questions.
- Partly: Three stories, but one has no part or no decision.
- Not yet: Fewer than three stories, or lines that describe habits.

**Part 2: your weakest line.**
- Met: The line is named, with the why where it fails, the old line and the new line.
- Partly: The line is named, but there is no rewrite, or the new line adds an unsourced number.
- Not yet: No weak line is named.

**Part 3: the gap.**
- Met: One gap, an honest way to fill it, a step and a date.
- Partly: A gap and a way, but no step or no date.
- Not yet: No gap, or a gap filled with an invented number.

**Part 4: honest numbers.**
- Met: Every number has a source or is named as a gap.
- Partly: One number has no source.
- Not yet: Numbers that are invented or rounded up.

**Pass rule:** your plan passes when no part is "Not yet" and at most one part is "Partly".

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Which of your three stories would you drop if the interviewer had only ten minutes?" or "Where does the number in your first story come from?" You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look for it after you submit.
