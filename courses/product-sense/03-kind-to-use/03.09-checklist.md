This is the gate for module 3. The work itself was in the practice chapter, 3.7, where you wrote the unhappy paths of one flow. This gate asks something different: how do you think about a screen now? Tick each box only when it is true. Your answers save as you type.

## Module 3 checklist

- [ ] I can say what a screen shows when it is empty, loading and broken, in exact words, and why a screen checks loading before it checks empty
- [ ] I put a label above every field, write errors in words next to their cause, and check a field after the person leaves it
- [ ] I size targets for fingers at about 44 to 48, keep focus visible, never use colour alone, and give every meaningful image words
- [ ] I use motion only to show a change, and I say that change in words when the reader asks for reduced motion
- [ ] I put the valuable moment on the first screen and teach inside the screen, not in a tour
- [ ] I answered the questions in chapters 3.2 to 3.6, finished the practice in 3.7 and the quiz in 3.8

## Learning log

1. **The state you forget:** Think of an app you use or one you built. Which of the four states does it forget most, and what do you see on your screen when it happens?
2. **The words you will change:** Which words in an error, a label or a button in your own project would you rewrite first? What would they say now?
3. **The rule that changed your mind:** Which rule in this module was different from what you believed before: placeholders, checking on every key, a welcome tour, reduced motion, or another? What changed your mind?
4. **The path you will never skip:** In your next project, which unhappy path will you design first, and at what point in the work will you do it?

## What changed

In module 2 you learned what a person sees first on a happy screen: one thing first, readable type, space that groups, colour with a job. Module 3 asks what that person sees when the screen is not happy. The data has not arrived. The list is empty. The request failed. The form was filled in wrongly. The button is too small for the finger. Or the reader has asked for less movement. A screen built quickly, with or without an AI tool, can show only the happy case, because that is the one the builder sees on a fast connection.

The change this gate looks for is in your first question. Before, the first question about a screen was "does it work?". Now it is "what does a person read here when it does not?". That question is cheap to ask and it finds problems that a polished happy screen hides. It also gives you words to write, such as "Groups did not load. The connection dropped. Your search is kept.", and exact words are something a mentor can check.

A habit helps here: before you call a screen finished, cause its unhappy cases yourself. Switch to flight mode, slow the connection, clear the data, type a bad value, press Tab, zoom the text, turn on reduced motion. Each takes a minute, and together they show most of what this module taught.

Notice also what the module did not ask you to do. It did not ask for a new look. Most of the fixes cost words, an order of checks, a bit of padding or a few lines of CSS. That is why they are worth making first.

## How your mentor will read it

Your mentor reads your answer for the habit, not for the exact list. They want to see that you look at a screen from the side of the person who meets the unhappy case. That person may be on weak data, on a small phone, using a keyboard, or using reduced motion. They read your fixes for exact words and numbers. "Add a loader" is advice. "Finding rooms..." with a line about the wait is a fix. They also read your first choice. There is no single right order, but there must be a reason that names the person and what they lose.

The common weak answer lists the problems it can name and fixes each with a general sentence, such as "show better errors" or "make the button bigger". It sounds right and could be written without looking at the page. A strong answer writes the words and the numbers, and ties each problem to a rule from the module, so that the mentor can check it.

After you submit, the mentor usually asks one follow-up question. For example: "You put the cancel icon first. What would the student see at second 3 if you had put the loading state first? Who would that hurt more?" A good reply points to the page and gives a reason you can show.

## Samir's study-room page

Samir is a student who built a page where students book a study room in the university library. It is a fictional app. Here are six facts about it. First, on library wifi the page shows a title and white space for 8 seconds, and then the rooms appear. Second, when no room is free tonight, the list says "No results". Third, the booking form has four fields that show only a placeholder, "Room", "Date", "Start" and "Hours", and when something is wrong the top of the form says "Invalid input". Fourth, the "Book" button pulses all the time. Fifth, the first time a student opens the page, a four-step tour explains rooms, hours, rules and fees before any room is shown. Sixth, the icon that cancels a booking is 16 by 16 pixels and sits next to the Edit icon with no space between them.

Read the six facts as the person would meet them. A student with a lecture in ten minutes opens the page, sees white space and waits. The same student, on another night, sees "No results" and does not know whether the library is full or the page is broken. Each fact is a moment where a person has to guess. Your job is to remove the guess, in words and numbers.

If you prefer, review one screen of your own project in the same way. Name at least five problems on it, each with the rule and a fix.

The rubric below is for Samir's page.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problems found | At least five of the six named, each with the rule or source behind it | Three or four named, or five named without a rule | Two or fewer named |
| Fixes in words and numbers | Every fix written as exact words or numbers a developer could use | Most fixes are exact, but some are only advice | The fixes are advice only, such as "add a loader" |
| First fix and reason | One problem chosen first, with a reason that names the person and what they lose | A problem chosen first, but no reason | No order or no reason |

**Pass rule:** no part is Not yet, and at most one part is Partly.
