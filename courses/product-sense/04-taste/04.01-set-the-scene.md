Maryam posts the group page of Halaqa in the fellows' chat and asks, "Is it good?" Huda writes, "It looks fine." Saeed writes, "It looks cheap."

## Same page, two verdicts, no reasons

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m4-scene" />

Huda and Saeed both look at version A. Maryam asks each of them why. Huda says the colours are okay. Saeed says the font. Neither points at anything on the screen. Switch to version B, which has the same facts. What changed?

## Three questions turn a feeling into a reason

<MermaidDiagram title="Mais's three questions" chart={`flowchart LR
  A["What is it trying to do?"] --> B["Does it do that?"] --> C["Where do I see it?"]
`} legend="Mais, a fellow from the last cohort, asks these three questions instead of saying who is right." />

Mais does not say who is right. She asks these three questions, and each one gives Huda and Saeed something to point at on the screen. You will use them all through this module.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the rest of the story">

Before Mais joins, Maryam asks again: "Why?" Saeed says the font is the problem, so Maryam changes the font. Saeed still says it looks cheap. Huda still says it looks fine. Now Maryam has two opinions and nothing to change.

Then Mais asks her questions. Huda says the page is for joining a group. Saeed says the Join button is small and hard to find. Now they are talking about the same thing, and they can look at the screen together. Huda opens version B, which Maryam made the next day. She sees that the number of seats left is the biggest thing on the page, and the Join button is wide.

Notice what changed. Before, each fellow gave a feeling. After, each gave a reason that Maryam can check on the screen. A feeling gives her nothing to fix. A reason tells her what to change and how to look again.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why this matters for your work">

Soon you will show your work to people who do not know you. In module 2, Nadia opened ten project links before a call and remembered none of them. In module 7 you will put your own work in front of a stranger.

You will also review other fellows' work. If you can only say "I like it", the other person learns nothing. If you can say "the seats left are the same size as the date", they know what to fix. That number decides the join, so it is easy to miss.

This is also a skill for remote work. Written feedback has no face and no voice. A short, specific reason travels better than a feeling.

English is a second language for most of you. A short, fixed set of questions helps when you cannot find the right word for a feeling. What is it for? Does it do that? Where do I see it? You can ask these in simple words, and you can answer them in simple words.

</GoDeeper>
