  - "Redo the text colours, since contrast may still be too low for the form"

  - "Fact 4 says spacing and errors already pass. Motion is not on the Best list, and it would add something the form does not need."
  - "Fact 1 says contrast already passes, so redoing the colours solves nothing."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rand pastes a screenshot of his booking page into an AI tool. It replies: 'This page follows the golden ratio, so it is balanced and beautiful. The blue header builds trust. Keep the menu to seven items, because people remember seven things. Score: 9 out of 10.' What is the right next step?"
options:
  - "Ignore all of it, because an AI tool cannot give any useful opinion on a screenshot"
  - "Ask again until the score is lower, because 9 out of 10 is too high for a booking page"
  - "Check each point against its source first: three rest on rules that failed their checks"
  - "Ask for ten more rules and keep the ones that agree, since agreement shows they are right"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "AI critique is often wrong, but it is not always useless. Its points are questions to check against a rule or a person, so throwing all of them away loses the ones that hold."
  - "A score is an opinion in a number. Asking again until it changes only changes the number and not the evidence behind it."
  - "Right. The golden ratio, 'blue builds trust' and the seven-item menu all failed their checks in chapter 4.5. Treat each point as a question and check it against a standard, a study or a person before you act."
  - "Tools can agree and still be wrong, and more rules only add more claims to check. Agreement does not replace a source."
```

Which line is safe to write?"

  - "Users do not scroll, so Join should sit at the very top of the first screen of the page"

  - "People scroll when they have a reason, so the position of Join does not matter at all"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Fact 3 says people do scroll when they have a reason, so the rule as stated is wrong. The conclusion to move Join up still needs a better reason."
  - "Right. Fact 2 says attention is highest at the top, and fact 3 says people scroll with a reason. Together they support moving Join up from three screens down (fact 4)."
  - "Fact 2 is about viewing time, not about users who never scroll. The line claims more than the study found."
  - "Fact 2 says most viewing time is near the top, so position matters. The line keeps fact 3 and drops fact 2."
