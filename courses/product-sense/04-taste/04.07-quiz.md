```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Mais builds a sign-up page for a coding bootcamp. 1) Contrast, labels, focus, targets and states all pass. 2) Spacing follows one step. 3) Blurred, the banner and the Register button look equally strong. 4) Two sponsor logos and a countdown sit above the form. 5) Nobody outside the team has tried it. Which next step moves the page toward Best?"
options:
  - "Add a dark mode, since many students read late at night"
  - "Make Register lead and cut the extras, since Best needs one clear first thing"
  - "Test with thirty students before changing it, since more testers find more problems"
  - "Switch to the bootcamp's own colours, since a brand look shows a clear choice"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Dark mode is not on the Best list. Fact 1 says the standards already pass, so it adds a feature and not a check."
  - "Right. Facts 3 and 4 show nothing leads and extras crowd the form. One clear first thing and relevant content are Best checks, and watching three to five people comes after."
  - "More testers find more problems, but with diminishing returns. Three to five people are enough for a round, and the page should be fixed first."
  - "A brand look can be a choice, but only with a reason tied to the job. It does not fix facts 3 and 4, and it is a top-level idea."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Walid writes four one-line choices for his club page. Each line should say what changed, for which job, and what was checked. Which line has all three parts?"
options:
  - "I made the footer dark blue for a professional look, and I checked that it looks neat."
  - "I switched to a rounded font for a friendly feel, and I asked my sister if she liked it."
  - "I changed the colours to the club's colours for the club's brand, and I checked the logo."
  - "I made Pay the only accent for the job of paying, and I checked its text passes 4.5 to 1."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "'A professional look' is a feeling, not a job, and 'looks neat' is not a check a reader can repeat."
  - "'A friendly feel' names no job, and whether his sister liked it is an opinion, not a check against a rule."
  - "'The club's brand' is a reason for the club, not a job for the person using the page, and the logo is not a check."
  - "Right. It names the change (Pay is the only accent), the job (paying) and a check a reader can repeat (4.5 to 1 for text)."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Walid drafts a log line about a clinic page: '20 Nov: Shifa Clinic hides its prices so that patients call. This is a poor choice.' 1) He saw that the first screen has no prices, on his phone on 20 Nov. 2) His task was to find a price. 3) Nothing on the site says why. Which rewrite follows the chapter?"
options:
  - "20 Nov: Shifa Clinic's first screen shows no prices. The task was to find a price."
  - "20 Nov: Shifa Clinic hides its prices so that patients call. Prices should be public."
  - "Shifa Clinic's page is confusing, and I would not use it to find a price."
  - "20 Nov: Shifa Clinic breaks the three-click rule, since a price takes four taps."
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It keeps the date and what he saw (fact 1), ties it to the task (fact 2) and leaves out the guess about why (fact 3)."
  - "It keeps the guess about the clinic's reason. Fact 3 says nothing on the site gives one, so the sentence is invented."
  - "It has no date and no rule, and 'confusing' is a feeling. A reader cannot check it or repeat it."
  - "It uses a rule that failed its test in chapter 4.5, so a line built on it cannot be checked."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Rand pastes a screenshot of his booking page into an AI tool. It replies: 'Add a hero video, because 70 percent of visitors prefer video. Move the menu to the right, because most people are right-handed. Make every button red, because red raises sales by 21 percent. Score: 8 out of 10.' What is the right next step?"
options:
  - "Make all three changes, since the numbers come from research"
  - "Ignore all three points, since an AI tool cannot give a useful opinion"
  - "Ask what each point is based on, since a bare figure is not evidence by itself"
  - "Ask the tool to score the page again, since a second score would decide it"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The reply names no study, so a reader cannot say the numbers come from research. A figure alone is not evidence."
  - "AI critique is often wrong, but its points can be useful questions. Throwing them all away loses the ones that would hold when checked."
  - "Right. Treat each point as a claim: who tested it, with whom, and does it fit a booking page? Then check it against a rule or a person before you act."
  - "A score is an opinion in a number. A second score would only change the number, not the evidence behind it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A blog says: 'Round buttons convert better than square ones.' Omar looks for the evidence. 1) The blog links to one test of one shop's checkout. 2) The test ran for two days. 3) The shop sells shoes, and Omar's page books lab printers. 4) No other test is named. Which line is safe in his log?"
options:
  - "Round buttons convert better than square ones, so Omar's page should use them."
  - "One shoe-shop test favoured round buttons, so the claim is proven false today."
  - "One shoe-shop test favoured round buttons, so button shape does not matter here."
  - "One shoe-shop test favoured round buttons, so qualify it for booking pages."
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The test covered a shoe shop's checkout, not a booking page. Repeating the claim skips the question of whether the test fits."
  - "One test does not prove a claim false either. A claim with thin evidence is different from a claim that a test has refuted."
  - "Fact 1 says one test favoured round buttons, so shape may matter somewhere. The evidence is thin, but it does not show that shape is irrelevant."
  - "Right. Fact 3 shows the test and Omar's page differ, and fact 4 shows no other test. A real finding used where it was not tested is Misread, so qualify it."
```
