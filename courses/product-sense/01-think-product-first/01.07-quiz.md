```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Ghadeer runs the sign-up sheet of a coding bootcamp (fictional) and gets a request to 'add a countdown timer to the home page'. Four facts: 1) Three learners asked for it this week. 2) Asked about the last time it mattered, two said they missed the 9 am start on Sunday because the new time was posted only in a chat. 3) The third said that timers look nice. 4) Ghadeer has 3 hours. What should she do next?"
options:
  - "Build the timer first, because three learners asked for it in the same week"
  - "Drop the request, because one of the three only said that timers look nice"
  - "Put the start time and changes on the home page, then watch if learners find it"
  - "Build a calendar with reminders for every session of the term, in case it helps"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Counting requests shows how many people asked, not what went wrong. Fact 2 says the real problem was a start time lost in a chat."
  - "Fact 2 shows that two of the three had a real problem with the time. One learner who likes how timers look does not cancel that."
  - "Right. The stories in fact 2 point to a time that was hard to find or had changed. Showing it in one place is small, it fits the 3 hours in fact 4, and Ghadeer can watch whether it works."
  - "A full calendar with reminders is far bigger than the 3 hours in fact 4, and none of the stories asked for it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Jad runs a sports club's app (fictional) and asks an AI assistant what to build. It writes: 'Your users are active adults aged 18 to 35. Users clearly want social features, so add a feed, likes and chat. Survey 100 members, and build them if 70 say they would use social features.' What is the main mistake in this advice?"
options:
  - "It asks about next time and a kind of person, not a moment a member was stuck"
  - "The survey is too small, so Jad should ask 1,000 members the same question instead"
  - "The advice is sound, because 70 yeses out of 100 would be a strong signal"
  - "The advice is sound except that it leaves out how many hours Jad has to build"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The advice describes a kind of person (adults aged 18 to 35) and asks about future use. It never asks for a moment when a member was stuck, so the problem is still unknown. The next check is to ask members about the last time."
  - "A bigger sample of the same question still collects guesses about next time. More guesses are not more facts."
  - "A vote about what people would use is a count of guesses. A yes costs nothing, and it does not show anyone reaching a goal."
  - "Hours matter, but they are not the main mistake. The problem and the question in the advice are the weak parts."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Three facts about Jad's club app: 1) Members open the app on their phones on the way to a match. 2) About once a month, a match time changes at the last minute. 3) Last time, Mazen went to the wrong pitch. Which job sentence do the facts support?"
options:
  - "When I want to talk with my team, I want a group chat in the app, so that we stay in touch"
  - "When the season starts, I want to see every match of the whole year, so that I can plan ahead"
  - "When the club grows, I want a feed with photos and likes, so that every member feels close to it"
  - "When a match may have changed, I want to see its time and pitch, so that I go to the right one"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "None of the three facts is about talking with the team. A chat answers a need that the facts do not show."
  - "Facts 2 and 3 are about the next match and its changes, not about the whole season."
  - "Nothing in the facts is about feeling close to the club. Mazen's problem was a place, not a feeling."
  - "Right. It has a situation (a match may have changed), an action (see its time and pitch) and a result (go to the right one). It matches facts 2 and 3 and names no feature."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sireen has 8 hours to build the first version of a clinic's booking page (fictional). The job: 'When I need a visit and the desk is closed, I want to book from my phone and see that it is booked, so that I do not have to call.' Her estimates: booking form 4 hours, confirmation message 2 hours, patient login 3 hours, health tips page 2 hours, first check with 4 patients 1 hour. She wants to know that booking works before she calls the page done. Which plan fits?"
options:
  - "Booking form, confirmation and login, 9 hours in all, and the check can wait"
  - "Booking form, confirmation and the check, 7 hours; login and tips wait"
  - "Booking form and tips first, 6 hours; confirmation and the check wait"
  - "Booking form and confirmation only, 6 hours, and no check, since 2 hours are left"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Booking form, confirmation and login need 4 plus 2 plus 3, which is 9 hours. The budget is 8, so this plan is over it."
  - "Right. 4 plus 2 plus 1 is 7 hours. The job needs the form and the confirmation, and the check is planned. One hour remains, and login (3) and tips (2) do not fit, so they wait."
  - "Tips do not serve the job of booking a visit. Without the confirmation, a patient cannot see that the visit is booked."
  - "This plan skips the 1-hour check although 2 hours remain. Without the check, Sireen cannot know whether booking works."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sireen's booking page had 20 visitors in week 1, and 10 of them booked a visit. In week 2, after she shared the link in a large group, it had 200 visitors, and 30 booked. What do these facts support?"
options:
  - "The page works better now, because 30 bookings is three times as many as 10"
  - "The shared link brought the wrong visitors, so Sireen should stop sharing it"
  - "The share fell from 10 of 20 to 30 of 200: watch visitors try to book"
  - "Nothing can be said until at least 1,000 visitors have used the booking page"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The count of bookings tripled, but the share fell from half to 15 in every 100. More visitors came, so the count says little."
  - "The facts do not say who the visitors were. Stopping the link would be a guess. Watching a few of them try to book shows what happens."
  - "Right. 10 of 20 is half, and 30 of 200 is 15 in every 100. The share fell, so something may block the new visitors. Watch three to five of them try to book, then fix the biggest problem."
  - "A small check with three to five people can show the biggest problems of one group. Waiting for 1,000 visitors only collects more counts."
```
