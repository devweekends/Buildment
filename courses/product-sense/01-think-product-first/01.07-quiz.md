```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A fellow runs a seat-booking page for a university library (fictional) and gets a request to 'add a map of the building'. Four facts: 1) Three students asked for it this week. 2) Asked about the last time it mattered, two students said they booked a seat on floor 2. They spent ten minutes finding the floor, because the page gave only a seat number. 3) The third said that maps look professional. 4) The fellow has 3 hours. What should the fellow do next?"
options:
  - "Build an interactive map of every floor in the library, starting with floor 2"
  - "Show the floor and the way to the seat on the booking confirmation"
  - "Ask students which three features they would like next"
  - "Drop the request and look for a better idea elsewhere"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A request is one person's guess at a fix. Fact 2 shows that the problem was finding the floor, and a full map is far bigger than the 3 hours in fact 4."
  - "Right. Fact 2 shows the problem: people booked a seat and could not find the floor. Showing the floor and the way on the confirmation is small, it fits the 3 hours in fact 4, and the fellow can watch whether it works."
  - "A vote on features collects guesses about next time. It does not show what happened to anyone."
  - "Fact 2 shows that two of the three students had a real problem. One student who likes how maps look does not cancel that."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A freelancer asks an AI assistant what to build for a tutoring marketplace (fictional). It writes: 'Your users are students aged 16 to 22. Students clearly want rewards, so add points, levels and a leaderboard. Survey 200 students, and build them if 140 say they would use rewards.' What is the main mistake in this advice?"
options:
  - "The survey is too small; 2,000 students should answer before the features are built"
  - "It leaves out how many hours the freelancer has to build points and levels"
  - "It gives one feature list; students should rank three different lists instead"
  - "It asks about a kind of person and about next time, not a stuck moment"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "A bigger sample of the same question still collects guesses about next time. More guesses are not more facts."
  - "Hours matter, but they are not the main mistake. The weak parts are the kind of person and the question about next time."
  - "Ranking lists also asks about preferences for next time, so it repeats the same mistake with more choices."
  - "Right. The advice describes a kind of person (students aged 16 to 22) and asks about future use. It never asks for a moment when a student was stuck, so the problem is still unknown. The next check is to ask students about the last time."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Three facts about a bakery's pre-order app (fictional): 1) Customers open it on their phones on the way to work. 2) About once a week, a tray of bread sells out before noon. 3) Last time, a customer walked ten minutes to the shop and found that the bread she wanted was gone. Which job sentence do the facts support?"
options:
  - "When I leave for work, I want to know what bread is still left at the bakery, so that I do not walk there for nothing"
  - "When the bakery opens a new shop, I want to read the story of its bread, so that I feel close to the place"
  - "When I want to share bread with friends, I want to send them a gift code for the bakery, so that they know that I care"
  - "When the season changes, I want to see the full menu of the whole year, so that I can plan my orders well ahead of time"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. It has a situation (I leave for work), an action (know what is still left) and a result (I do not walk there for nothing). It matches facts 2 and 3 and names no feature."
  - "None of the three facts is about a new shop or the story of the bread. The sentence is a job, but not the one the facts show."
  - "Nothing in the facts is about gifts. It is a possible job for another day, and it does not explain why a customer walked for nothing."
  - "The facts are about bread that sells out within a day, not about a menu for the whole year."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A fellow has 8 hours to build the first version of a car wash's booking page (fictional). The job is 'When the shop is closed, I want to book a wash from my phone and see it booked, so that I do not call'. Estimates: booking form 4 hours, confirmation message 2 hours, customer login 3 hours, about-us page 2 hours, first check with 4 customers 1 hour. The fellow wants to know that booking works before calling the page done. Which plan fits?"
options:
  - "Booking form, confirmation and login for 9 hours, and the check can wait until later"
  - "Booking form and about-us page first for 6 hours; the rest waits"
  - "Booking form, confirmation and the check for 7 hours; login and about-us wait"
  - "Booking form and confirmation only for 6 hours, with the check skipped"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Booking form, confirmation and login need 4 plus 2 plus 3, which is 9 hours. The budget is 8, so this plan is over it."
  - "An about-us page does not serve the job of booking a time. Without the confirmation, a customer cannot see that the time is booked."
  - "Right. 4 plus 2 plus 1 is 7 hours. The job needs the form and the confirmation, and the check is planned. One hour remains, and login (3) and about-us (2) do not fit, so they wait."
  - "This plan skips the 1-hour check although 2 hours remain. Without the check, the fellow cannot know whether booking works."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A fellow built a pickup page for a laundry (fictional). In week 1, 40 visitors came and 20 booked a pickup. In week 2, after the link was shared in a large group, 300 visitors came and 45 booked. What do these facts support?"
options:
  - "The page works better now, as 45 bookings is more than twice 20"
  - "The share fell from 20 of 40 to 45 of 300; watch some visitors book"
  - "The shared link brought the wrong visitors; the fellow should stop sharing it"
  - "The fellow should wait for 1,000 visitors before judging the page"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "The count of bookings more than doubled, but the share fell from half to 15 in every 100. More visitors came, so the count says little."
  - "Right. 20 of 40 is half, and 45 of 300 is 15 in every 100. The share fell, so something may block the new visitors. Watch three to five of them try to book, then fix the biggest problem."
  - "The facts do not say who the visitors were. Stopping the link would be a guess. Watching a few of them try to book shows what happens."
  - "A small check with three to five people can show the biggest problems of one group. Waiting for 1,000 visitors only collects more counts."
```
