```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Shadi builds a bakery order page with an AI tool. His brief names four things: 1) the audience, customers on phones; 2) one main action, 'Order'; 3) one accent colour, only on the Order button; 4) a reference, a bus timetable. The draft has the right shape, but every card says 'Product name' and 'Short description'. What should he add to the brief?"
options:
  - "A second reference, so the layout copies the timetable more closely"
  - "A longer audience line, so the tool knows more about the customers"
  - "More colours, so the tool has a wider range to choose for the cards"
  - "Real bread names, prices and pickup times from the bakery's own menu"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Fact 4 says a reference is already there, and the shape of the draft is right. A second reference would fix the layout, not the empty words in the cards."
  - "Fact 1 says the audience is already named. The draft's problem is not who it is for. It is that the cards hold filler instead of facts."
  - "Fact 3 says the colour is already set, with one job. More colours would add choices, which is the opposite of what the brief asks for."
  - "Right. All four parts are present. The missing part is real content, which only Shadi has: the bread names, the prices and the times. The tool cannot invent them."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Osama is 16 and wants three AI opinions on his screen. At the time of writing (checked 2026-10-25): Claude is for people 18 or over; Gemini is for people 13 or over; ChatGPT is for people 13 or over, with a guardian's permission under 18. His guardian has not agreed to ChatGPT. What is the best plan?"
options:
  - "Borrow a friend's Claude account, since it is only one opinion"
  - "Use Gemini, and add his own paper pass, labelled as his"
  - "Ask Gemini in three separate chats, which gives three opinions"
  - "Wait until he is 18, then use all three tools together"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Claude is for people 18 or over, and Osama is 16. Using a friend's account does not change the age rule, so this plan breaks the tool's terms."
  - "Right. Gemini is open to his age. A paper-checklist pass can be a third opinion, as long as he labels it as his own and does not present it as a tool's answer."
  - "Three chats with one tool come from one company and share its habits. The method asks for tools from different companies, so this is one opinion repeated."
  - "Waiting is not needed. Gemini is for people 13 or over, and a paper pass costs nothing, so he can do the method now with a clear note about what he used."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A tool critiques Maryam's screen: 'The hint text fails the contrast rule, at about 3 to 1. Change it to #595959.' Maryam's hint is #767676 on white. What is the right next step?"
options:
  - "Compute the ratio of her real colours, then decide from it"
  - "Change it to #595959 now, because the tool named an exact colour"
  - "Ask the tool again until two answers give the same ratio"
  - "Drop the whole critique, since the tool got one number wrong"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. A ratio is a number, so she computes it with the WCAG formula. #767676 on white is 4.54 to 1, which passes 4.5 to 1. The tool's 'about 3 to 1' is wrong, so she leaves it."
  - "An exact colour sounds sure, but it is only a claim. The tool did not show its working, and her own colours pass. Changing it would answer a problem she does not have."
  - "Two answers that match are still two claims. Tools can share a habit, so agreement is not a measurement. The ratio is quick to compute herself."
  - "One wrong number does not make every point wrong. Each other point in the critique still goes through its own check, and some may be right."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tala's tool said what it built: body text 16 px, one accent colour, and a visible focus mark on every button. Tala opened the page in a browser and measured: body text 14 px, two accent colours, and a visible focus mark on every button. Which conclusion do these facts support?"
options:
  - "The tool is not reliable, so she should ignore everything it built"
  - "About a quarter of reasons fail, as one study found, so this is normal"
  - "Two stated choices are missing, so she fixes them or asks again"
  - "Nothing is wrong, because the tool listed its choices with reasons"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The focus mark matches what the tool said, so part of its work holds. Ignoring all of it throws away a point that passed the check."
  - "In the facts, two of three stated choices fail, not a quarter. The study is about five tools in one test, so it is no rate for this tool, and the check still has to be done."
  - "Right. The text size and the accent colours do not match what the tool said, and the focus mark does. She fixes the two, or asks the tool again, and keeps what held."
  - "A list of choices with reasons is a claim. The measurements show two of those claims are not in the page, which is why she opens the browser."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Tala writes a checklist for her café app that a tool will read as a SKILL.md file. Which line is best to include?"
options:
  - "Make every screen feel warm, premium and modern"
  - "Hint text must pass 4.5 to 1 against its background"
  - "Be bold and creative with the layout on every screen"
  - "Add a gradient header so the app stands out"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "'Warm, premium and modern' cannot be measured. A checklist line should be something she can check on a finished screen, and this one is a mood."
  - "Right. It is a standard she can check with a number: normal text needs 4.5 to 1. A good checklist line is something you can confirm on the finished screen."
  - "It cannot be checked, and 'bold on every screen' is the opposite of spending boldness in one place, which makes a page noisy."
  - "It names no problem a customer has, and a gradient header is a default the checklist is meant to catch. Without a reason in the brief, it should be left out."
```
