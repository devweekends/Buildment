```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Shadi's tool says of his bakery page 'customers will probably miss the delivery note box'. He has no data about it yet. What is the right next step?"
options:
  - "Measure the note box against the text contrast rule"
  - "Drop the point and move on to the next point"
  - "Watch three customers order and see where they stop"
  - "Ask a second tool if the first tool is right"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Contrast is a number, but the point does not say the box is hard to see. It says customers may not notice it, and a rule cannot answer what customers do."
  - "The point names a real problem a customer could have, so it is not empty. Dropping it would throw away something that a short check can test."
  - "Right. The point is about what customers do, and no rule or number answers that. Watching three to five people order shows where they stop."
  - "A second tool gives a second claim, not a check. It can share the first tool's habits, and agreement is not evidence."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Three tools from three companies all say the Confirm button on Haneen's booking screen is too small. She sent the screenshots at full size. She measures the button: 38 pixels tall and as wide as the screen. What should she do?"
options:
  - "Raise the button height to about 44 pixels"
  - "Leave the button at 38 pixels"
  - "Raise the button height to 60 pixels"
  - "Ask five classmates before measuring anything else"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. 38 pixels is above the AA floor of 24 but below the aim of about 44 to 48, so the point is partly true. The tools agreeing was not the reason; the measurement was."
  - "38 is above the AA floor of 24, but the floor is not the aim. The aim for a finger is about 44 to 48, so the button can be better."
  - "Bigger is not always better. Past the aim of 44 to 48, a taller button only takes space on a small screen and gives up other content."
  - "Classmates could add a view later, but size is a number and she already has it. The rule answers the question first."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Only two of the three AI tools Mohannad planned to use open for him. He wants three opinions on his menu page. What should he do for the third?"
options:
  - "Ask one of the two tools again in a new chat"
  - "Use a friend's account for the third tool"
  - "Report the two answers as three opinions"
  - "Add his own paper pass, labelled as his"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Two chats with one tool share that tool's habits, so the second chat is the same opinion again. The method asks for different companies."
  - "The step is to check whether a tool opens for you, at your age. Borrowing an account skips that check and may break the tool's terms."
  - "Calling two answers three misreports his own evidence. A reader would trust the count, and the count would be wrong."
  - "Right. A paper pass with the same five checks is a real third opinion, if he labels it as his own and not as a tool's answer."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Shadi's tool lists what it built into his bakery page. One accent colour, used only on the Order button. Hint text that passes 4.5 to 1. A 28 px headline. He opens the page and finds the accent colour on the Order button and on every price. The hint text is #6B6B6B on white, 5.33 to 1. The headline is 28 px. Which conclusion do these facts support?"
options:
  - "The list can be trusted, because two of its three claims held up"
  - "One claim failed, because the accent also marks prices, so he fixes it"
  - "The draft is fine, because the tool gave reasons for its choices"
  - "The draft should be dropped, because one stated reason was wrong"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Two claims held, but a tool's list is checked claim by claim. A list with one false claim is not safe to trust as a whole."
  - "Right. The accent claim does not match the page, and the other two hold. He fixes the accent, or asks again, and keeps what held."
  - "A reason is a claim, not a result. The measurements decide, and one of them does not match what the tool said."
  - "One wrong claim does not make the whole draft useless. The hint text and the headline match, so he keeps those and fixes the one."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Shadi's brief for his bakery page names the audience: customers on phones. It names the real content: his breads, prices and pickup times. It sets one font with 16 px body text and one accent colour for the prices, and gives a bus timetable as the reference. The draft has the right feel, but it shows three equal buttons: 'Order', 'Call us' and 'Share'. Which part should he add to the brief?"
options:
  - "A second reference to copy the layout from"
  - "More real content, such as the bakery's history"
  - "The one main action, which is the Order button"
  - "A second accent colour for the other buttons"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "A reference is already there, and the layout has the right feel. A second one would not tell the tool which button matters."
  - "The real content is already in the brief. The history would add words, but the problem is three buttons with equal weight."
  - "Right. Four of the five places hold a fact. The missing one is the one main action, so the tool made three equal buttons. Naming Order fills the gap."
  - "More colour adds choices, which is the opposite of the brief's aim. The tool needs to be told which button is first, not how to tell them apart."
```
