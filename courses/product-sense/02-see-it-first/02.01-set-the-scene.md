Nadia leads a small team at a remote company (a fictional company). She wants to hire one fellow for a four-week contract. Ten fellows sent her a link to a project. She has 30 minutes before a call.

## Two of the ten pages, and a third

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m2-scene" />

Switch between the three pages. Fares's page and Yasmin's page were two of the ten. Each says true things about a real project. On Fares's page, every line looks alike. On Yasmin's page, her name is the loudest part. The third page is Laith's, a week later, with the result first. Notice where your eyes go on each page.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the rest of Nadia's morning">

Nadia opens the ten links one after another on her laptop. She spends about two minutes on each page and writes one or two words in her notes: "ok", "fine", "maybe". Every page has the facts she needs. There is a name, a project, a list of tools and a link to a demo.

When the call starts, her cofounder asks which two fellows she would like to talk to. Nadia looks at her notes and cannot say. She cannot say what Fares built, although she read his page. She remembers that Yasmin's name was large, but not what her project did. Laith's page began with a row of tool names, like the others, and she does not remember it either.

Later, Nadia opens a few of the apps behind the pages, and the work is good. The pages hid it. Nadia did not do anything wrong: she is one person with ten links and a call. The page has to do the work of telling her what matters, because the fellow is not in the room.

That evening she writes to all ten fellows and says she could not tell the pages apart. Laith rebuilds his page that week so that the result comes first. His new headline says that waiting time at a clinic desk went from 25 minutes to 10. When the next call comes, Nadia can tell her cofounder what he built.

All the people, pages and numbers in this story are fictional.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why this matters for your career">

If you want remote work, people will often meet your work before they meet you. A link in a message may be the only thing a team lead sees. She may be looking at ten of them, as Nadia was, and she will decide quickly which to open again.

This is not only about portfolio pages. The same thing happens inside your apps. A person who opens an app with ten things on the screen has to guess which one matters. A person who opens a screen with one clear first thing knows where to start. The skill is the same in both places: decide what a person should see first, and build the page so that they do.

This module teaches that skill in four parts, then asks you to use it on a real screen. Module 3 is about what happens when a screen is empty, slow or broken. Module 7 comes back to project pages and demos, and asks how to show your work to someone like Nadia.

</GoDeeper>

## Her eyes land on one thing first

<MermaidDiagram title="What Nadia keeps from a page" chart={`flowchart TD

On the first ten pages, that was a name or a list of tools, not the result. You will learn to choose the first thing on purpose.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what this module covers">

Each chapter in this module gives you one tool for deciding what a person sees. You use the tools together in the practice chapter, on one screen of your own project or on the provided Wasel print shop app.
