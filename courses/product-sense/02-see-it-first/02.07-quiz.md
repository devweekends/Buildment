```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A tutoring site shows each tutor on one card. After a squint, a designer lists four facts. 1) The tutor's name is 28 bold at the top. 2) A bright orange badge, Top rated, is the strongest colour shape on the card. 3) The Message tutor button is a thin grey outline, the same grey as the price text. 4) The card already uses three text sizes. The site wants students to message tutors. Which change do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Raise the name from 28 to 36 and keep the rest"
  - "Add a fourth text size to set the price apart"
  - "Fill the button and make the badge weaker"
  - "Move the price above the name and make it bold"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The name is already the largest text, so making it larger changes little. The action the site wants is still weak."
  - "A fourth size breaks the guide of three sizes, and it does nothing for the Message tutor button. The price is not the thing the site wants students to act on."
  - "Right. The button is the action the site wants, so it needs the strongest signals. Making the badge weaker stops it from competing, and no new size is needed."
  - "A bold price above the name would put the price first. The site wants students to message tutors, not to read the price first."
```

question: "A language school's timetable page is checked four ways. 1) On a phone, the body text is 14. 2) The line height is 1.5. 3) At 200 percent, all the content and buttons are still there. 4) On a laptop, each line holds about 70 characters. Which change do the checks support?"
options:
  - "Raise the body text on phones from 14 to 16"
  - "Cut the lines on laptops to 45 characters"
  - "Raise the line height from 1.5 to 2.0"
  - "Stop the page from scaling text past 150 percent"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Sixteen is the common start for body text, and 14 is under it. The other three checks are fine, so the body size is the one to change."
  - "Seventy characters is inside the range of 45 to 90 that one typography guide recommends. Shorter lines would not fix anything here."
  - "A line height of 1.5 is the common default. At 2.0 the lines begin to drift apart, so this would make the paragraphs harder to follow."
  - "Limiting scaling would fail WCAG 1.4.4, and the page already passes at 200 percent. It would also take away help from readers who need larger text."

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A fellow asks an AI assistant to review a list screen. It answers: 'The hierarchy is clear. Five sizes, 24, 20, 18, 16 and 14, give every line its own level, and the sizes step down in order.' What is wrong with this answer?"
options:
  - "It is right: stepping the sizes down in order is the goal"
  - "It should have judged the order by colour and weight, not by size"
  - "It should have asked for more sizes, for finer levels"
  - "It counted sizes, but close sizes look alike when squinted"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "An order of steps on paper is not the same as an order the eye can see. Sizes that are two apart look almost equal, so the levels blur."
  - "Colour is one signal, but size is a real signal too. The mistake is not which signal was used. It is that nobody checked whether the levels can be told apart."
  - "More sizes would make the levels blur more. The guide is three sizes or fewer, with clear gaps between them."
  - "Right. Counting sizes is not a check. A squint shows whether the levels can be told apart, and five sizes two apart usually cannot."
```

type: mcq
question: "Salma builds a sign-up form for a swimming club. Her spacing step is 4. Each label sits 16 above its field, and there are 16 between a field and the next label. A tester points to the wrong field when asked where to type the club name. Which gaps fit?"
options:
  - "16 under the label, 16 under the field"
  - "10 under the label, 22 under the field"
  - "4 under the label, 24 under the field"
  - "24 under the label, 12 under the field"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "These are the gaps she has now. Equal gaps leave each label halfway between two fields, which is why the tester chose the wrong one."
  - "The label would stay closer to its own field, so it groups well. But 10 and 22 are not multiples of her step, 4, and stray amounts break the rhythm that a step gives."
  - "Right. Four and 24 are both multiples of 4, and the label is much closer to its own field than to the field above it."
  - "This puts more space between a label and its field than between a field and the next label. Each label would then look like it belongs to the field above."
