```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Khalil runs a sports club (fictional) and has 6 hours this week for its booking app. Four facts: 1) In a check with 5 members, 3 gave up when they tried to book a court. 2) All 3 said they could not see which courts were free. 3) Members are asking for a leaderboard, which would take about 10 hours. 4) Showing free courts on the first screen would take about 3 hours. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Build the leaderboard that members asked for"
  - "Show free courts up front and repeat the check"
  - "Ask five more members which features they would like"
  - "Add a short tour that explains how booking works"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A request names a feature, and fact 3 says this one needs 10 hours against his 6. The time is fixed, so the scope has to change, not the clock."
  - "Right. Facts 1 and 2 name a problem he watched happen, and fact 4 fits his time. Repeating the same check afterwards shows whether it worked."
  - "Fact 1 shows what members do when they try to book, which is stronger than asking what they would like. Another round of opinions adds little."
  - "A tour explains how booking works, but the members in fact 2 did not lack an explanation. They could not see which courts were free."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Kinan's lab booking app (fictional) loads a list of free slots on weak data, and the list arrives after 25 seconds. Four facts about what the screen shows: 1) At 0.5 seconds it shows nothing. 2) At 4 seconds it shows a spinner and the words 'Finding slots'. 3) At 15 seconds it shows the same spinner and words. 4) At 20 seconds it shows 'No slots found'. Which change do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Start the spinner at 0.5 seconds, and keep the later screens as they are"
  - "Swap the spinner for a skeleton screen, and keep the words and later screens"
  - "Keep the screen as it is, and wait for the list to arrive after 25 seconds"
  - "Show 'No slots found' only after loading ends, and say how long the wait will be"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Fact 1 is fine as it is. Under about a second, the guidance in 3.2 is to show nothing special, because a spinner would only flash."
  - "Fact 2 is fine as it is: a spinner with words suits a wait of a few seconds. A skeleton changes how a wait feels, not how long it is, and it does not fix the screen at 20 seconds."
  - "Waiting is not a fix. The screen says 'No slots found' for 5 seconds before the list arrives, and a wait over about 10 seconds needs words about how long it will take."
  - "Right. Fact 4 shows a false empty message while the list is still loading, and fact 3 shows a wait over 10 seconds with no time given. Both are fixed by this change."
```

```quiz
question: "Nabil's gym class list (fictional) has the class name, the time, the price and a Book button in each row. Four facts: 1) All four lines are 16 px and regular weight. 2) The price is #808080 on white, a ratio of 3.95 to 1. 3) The Book label is white on #4CAF50, a ratio of 2.78 to 1. 4) In a squint test, no line stood out. Which change do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Make the class name larger, and darken the price and the button"
  - "Make the four lines larger and bolder, and keep the colours as they are"
  - "Make the class name larger and bolder, and keep the colours as they are"
  - "Darken the price and the button colour, and keep the sizes as they are"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Facts 1 and 4 show that nothing comes first, so the class name needs more size. Facts 2 and 3 are under 4.5 to 1 for normal text, so the price and the button label need a darker colour."
  - "Larger text for all four lines keeps them equal, so nothing would come first (facts 1 and 4). The ratios in facts 2 and 3 would not change either."
  - "This fixes the hierarchy in facts 1 and 4. The price at 3.95 to 1 and the button label at 2.78 to 1 still fail 4.5 to 1."
  - "This fixes the contrast in facts 2 and 3. All four lines would still share one size and weight, so the squint test in fact 4 would still find nothing first."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Joud's tutoring site (fictional) has a line on its first screen, written by an AI tool. It reads: 'Unlock a seamless, powerful learning journey that elevates every student.' What is the best next step?"
options:
  - "Swap the banned words for fresher praise words"
  - "Add two more sentences that praise the tutors"
  - "Replace it with a price, a time or a student's action"
  - "Run it through an AI-text detector and edit what it flags"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Word lists date quickly, and fresher praise words would leave the line just as vague. The test is whether a hundred products could say it (5.3)."
  - "More praise adds more words that any product could say. A line like this needs something specific, not more length."
  - "Right. The line says nothing about tutoring, a price, a time or a student, so a hundred sites could print it. A specific fact makes it a line only this site could write."
  - "Who wrote the line does not matter, and detectors that look at words cannot show it reliably. The line is weak whoever wrote it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Wissam asks an AI tool to review the storyboard of her 80-second demo (fictional). The tool replies: 'Your demo is well paced. Start with a 15-second introduction about your background. Record a calm voiceover for the whole video to build trust.' Her storyboard opens on the app at 0:00 with words on screen. Her reviewer will watch in a feed that starts videos muted. What is the best next check?"
options:
  - "Add the introduction and the voiceover that the tool advised"
  - "Play it muted for a friend and ask what the app does"
  - "Ask the tool to score the demo out of 10"
  - "Ask two more tools and follow the majority answer"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "An AI critique can sound sure and still be wrong. This advice would push the core later than 0:15 and add a voice that a muted feed never plays."
  - "Right. An AI critique is a set of questions, to be checked against a rule or a person. A muted play by a friend shows whether the words on screen carry the story."
  - "A score is an opinion in a number. AI judges can favour the first answer and their own style, so a score is not a check."
  - "Tools that agree can all be wrong, and agreement is not evidence (6.4). A different kind of check, a person, is what the critique needs."
```
