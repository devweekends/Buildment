```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Khalil runs a sports club (fictional) and has 6 hours this week for its booking app. Four facts: 1) In a check with 5 members, 3 gave up when they tried to book a court. 2) All 3 said they could not see which courts were free. 3) Members are asking for a leaderboard, which would take about 10 hours. 4) Showing free courts on the first screen would take about 3 hours. What should he do?"
options:
  - "Build the leaderboard, since members asked for it"
  - "Show free courts on the first screen, then repeat the check"
  - "Ask five more members whether they would like a leaderboard"
  - "Build half of the leaderboard in the six hours he has"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Fact 3 says the leaderboard takes about 10 hours, and he has 6. The time is fixed, so the scope has to change, not the clock."
  - "Right. Facts 1 and 2 name a problem he watched happen, and fact 4 fits his time. Repeating the same check afterwards shows whether it worked."
  - "Fact 1 shows what members do when they try to book, which is stronger than asking what they would like. Another round of opinions adds little."
  - "Fact 2 says the members who gave up named free courts, not a ranking. Half a leaderboard would leave the problem they named untouched."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Farah's tutoring app (fictional) loads a list of tutors on weak data, and the list arrives after 25 seconds. Four facts about what the screen shows: 1) At 0.5 seconds it shows nothing. 2) At 4 seconds it shows a spinner with no words. 3) At 15 seconds it shows the same spinner. 4) At 20 seconds it shows 'No tutors found'. Which change do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Add a spinner from the first half second so the wait feels shorter"
  - "Swap the 4 second spinner for a skeleton, because skeletons load faster"
  - "Keep the screen as it is, because the list does arrive in the end"
  - "Hide the empty message until loading ends, and say how long at 15 s"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Fact 1 is fine as it is: at 0.5 seconds nothing is the right thing to show. Under about a second, the rule is to show nothing special."
  - "Fact 2 is fine as it is: at 4 seconds a spinner is right. A skeleton changes how the wait feels; it does not make the data arrive sooner."
  - "Fact 4 is the problem: at 20 seconds the screen says 'No tutors found', but the list arrives at 25. The message is false while the page is still loading."
  - "Right. The empty message in fact 4 is wrong while loading, and the 15 second wait in fact 3 needs words and a time, not the same silent spinner."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Sufian's team lead says: 'Our menu can have at most seven items, because of Miller's law.' Four facts: 1) Miller's 1956 paper is about how many items people can hold in immediate memory. 2) NN/g says large menus can work, on qualitative evidence. 3) Sufian's menu has nine items under three headings. 4) He can test it with five classmates this afternoon. What does the evidence support?"
options:
  - "Test the nine items with five classmates and fix what they miss"
  - "Cut the menu to seven items, because Miller's law says so"
  - "Keep nine items and skip testing, since Miller's law does not apply"
  - "Add four more items, because large menus always work well"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Fact 1 shows the rule was never about menus, and fact 2 says big menus can work. Fact 4 gives a cheap way to find out whether this one does."
  - "Fact 1 says the paper is about memory, not menus. Cutting to seven on that basis uses a rule that does not apply."
  - "Fact 4 offers a quick, cheap check. A rule that does not apply does not prove the menu works, so skipping the check throws away free evidence."
  - "Fact 2 says large menus can work, on qualitative evidence. That is not the same as always working, and more items is not a goal."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Iyad's tutoring site (fictional) has this line on its first screen, written by an AI tool: 'Unlock a seamless, powerful learning journey that elevates every student.' What is the mistake in it, and what is the right next step?"
options:
  - "It uses banned AI words, so he should swap them for fresh praise words"
  - "It is too short, so he should add two more sentences of praise"
  - "It could describe any product; swap it for a price or a time"
  - "He should run it through an AI-text detector before editing it"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Word lists date quickly, and fresh praise words would leave the line just as vague. The test is whether a hundred products could say it."
  - "More praise adds more words that any product could say. A line like this needs something specific, not more length."
  - "Right. The line says nothing about tutoring, a price, a time or a student. A specific fact, such as a price or a time, makes it a line only this site could write."
  - "Who wrote the line does not matter, and word lists cannot show it reliably. The line is weak whoever wrote it."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Manar asks an AI tool to review her case study page. It replies: 'Your page looks professional and clear. Add a longer About section and a skills carousel above the project to build trust.' Her first screen shows her photo and a skills list. The result is in the last section. What is the best next check?"
options:
  - "Add the About section and carousel, because the tool sounded sure"
  - "Test the first screen in a ten-second skim with someone new to it"
  - "Ask the tool to score the page out of 10 and follow the score"
  - "Ask the tool the same question again and keep its most common answer"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "AI critiques can be wrong and still sound sure. Her page already leads with her, and the advice would push the result even further down."
  - "Right. An AI critique is a set of questions, to be checked against a rule or a person. A ten-second skim by a stranger shows what the first screen says."
  - "A score is an opinion in a number. AI judges can favour the first answer and their own style, so a score is not a check."
  - "Asking the same tool again gives you the same habits again. A different kind of check, a person, is what the critique needs."
```
