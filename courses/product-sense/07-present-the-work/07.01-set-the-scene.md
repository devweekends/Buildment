Maryam has redesigned Halaqa, her study-group app. In her check with five classmates, four joined a group in their first visit, up from two. She sends Dalia, a product manager at a remote startup, a link and a video. Halaqa, Maryam and Dalia are fictional. Dalia opens the link and meets a <GlossaryTerm term="login wall" definition="A page that asks you to sign in or sign up before it shows you anything." />.

## What Dalia met on her phone

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m7-scene" />

Dalia taps the link between two meetings. The page asks her to create an account, so she closes it. Later she plays the video with the sound off. A voice she cannot hear explains a settings page. She closes that too. Maryam never learns that anyone looked.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the rest of the story">

Maryam was proud of version 2, and she had reason to be. The check showed a real change: more classmates joined, and they joined faster. She added a sign-up page early because her AI coding tool made one in minutes, and it felt like a real app. She never thought of it as a door that a stranger would have to open.

The video was the same. She recorded it at her desk, in a quiet room, with a headset. It sounded clear to her. It was two minutes and ten seconds long. It started with the sign-up page, went on to the settings page and the menu, and showed a student joining a group at 1:40. She checked it on her laptop with the sound on, and it made sense.

Dalia had no sound because she was between calls and her phone was on silent. She had a few minutes and ten other links to open that day. Nobody told her anything was wrong with Maryam's work. She simply never saw it. This is the quiet part of the story: no one complains, and no one says no. The link is just not opened any further.

Nothing in the app was wrong. The path from the link to the work was wrong. This module teaches that path: a short demo, a case study page, and a link that opens the core in one step.

</GoDeeper>

## Two routes, both end early

<MermaidDiagram title="Two links, no work seen" chart={`flowchart TD
  L["Link from Maryam"] --> W["Login wall"]
  W --> X["Dalia closes it"]
  V["Demo video"] --> M["Sound is off"]

Neither route needed Dalia to dislike the app. The first asked for an account. The second asked for sound. Maryam made both choices without picturing a stranger on a phone. This module is about the path from a link to your best work.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why this matters for your career">

Remote teams often meet your work before they meet you. A reviewer opens a link, looks for a short time and decides whether to look longer. You may never hear the decision. Good code and a good design do not help if the reviewer leaves before they see them.

We do not have data on how reviewers open links. What we do have is narrower. NN/g, a research firm on user experience, writes that people who review portfolios are often recruiters or managers who are not designers, and that they rarely read closely. It wrote this about design portfolios, not about engineering projects, so treat it as a reason to be careful, not as a rule.

So the module builds for a reader who is busy and not an expert. It teaches three things you can do and check. Make a demo that works with the sound off. Write a case study page whose first screen carries the result. Make the link open the core of your app in one step, with a fallback when it is down. Then the capstone asks you to do all of it for one project.
