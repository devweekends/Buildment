## Three ways the same link can open

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m7-one-click-access" />

A login wall shows nothing of the work. A guest button opens the core with sample data in one tap. If the live demo is down, a page with a short video and screenshots still shows the work. Touch each version and see what a reviewer gets in the first moment.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: guest mode, demo mode and demo accounts">

There are three ways to let a stranger in. A guest button opens the app with no account. A demo mode loads sample data in a safe copy of the app. A demo account is a shared email and password that you print on the page. The first two are better, because a reviewer does not have to type anything. A demo account is acceptable when your app cannot work without a login. If you use one, make it read-only or reset it often, and never put real people's data in it.

Sample data means invented names, invented groups and invented times. Halaqa's guest mode shows "Data structures, exam Thursday" and a few seats left, not real classmates. Guest mode must also be separate from anything private: it should not reach real data, an admin screen or a key.

Is this a rule? Not quite. We found no source on whether reviewers run deployed apps or open repository links, so "open in one click" is this course's recommendation. There is one useful comparison. App stores ask developers for demo account details when an app has a login [B83]. That is an analogy, not hiring evidence. It shows that people who review apps for a living expect a way in that needs no sign-up.

</GoDeeper>

## A chain of fallbacks, not one link

<MermaidDiagram title="The fallback chain" chart={`flowchart TD
  L["Live demo in guest mode"] -->|"if it is down"| V["70 second video"]
  V -->|"if it will not play"| S["Screenshots on the case study page"]
  S -->|"if the page is gone"| R["README in the repository"]
`} legend="Each step is a different file in a different place, so one failure does not remove them all." />

Any live demo can break. Free hosts pause or limit sites. Plan a chain: the live demo first, then a short video, then screenshots on the page, then the README. A broken link should never make your case study useless.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: building each step of the chain">

The video is the one from 7.2: 60 to 90 seconds, working muted, with captions. Host it where it will not depend on your app, and link it from the case study page. The screenshots go directly on the page, each with a short text alternative, so the page still works when every link outside it fails.

The README is the first thing a visitor sees in a repository. Put four things in its first screen: one sentence about the job, the result, the link to the guest demo and the link to the video. Below that, say how to run the project. This is practice, not research.

Free tiers have limits and rules, and they change. GitHub Pages has soft limits of 100 GB of bandwidth a month and 10 builds an hour [B78]. Cloudflare Pages lists 500 builds a month on its free plan [B81]. Neither limit is likely to stop a small demo, but a host can pause a site that breaks its rules or terms. That is why the chain exists. You do not plan for a failure you expect. You plan so that a failure you did not expect does not cost you the first impression.

</GoDeeper>

## Put the link where people look

<MermaidDiagram title="Where the link goes" chart={`flowchart TD
  M["Message to a reviewer"] --> C["Case study page"]
  R["First lines of your resume"] --> C
  F["LinkedIn Featured"] --> C
  C --> G["Guest demo"]
`} legend="Every place a person might find you leads to the same case study page, and that page leads to the guest demo." />

People meet your work in a message, on a resume or on a profile. Each of those should lead to the same case study page. That page leads to the guest demo. Course 16 teaches the words for the resume and the profile. This chapter is about where the link goes, and that it opens.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: places to put the link">

This is practice, with one tool fact. At the time of writing (checked 2026-10-25), LinkedIn's Featured section can hold posts, articles, links to other sites and uploaded media such as images, documents and videos. You must have already published a post or article to feature it. The help page states no maximum number of items [B69]. It is an old page, so menus may have changed. Read the wording of any remove button before you press it. One option only takes an item out of Featured and keeps it, and deleting a post cannot be undone.

LinkedIn plays some feed videos without sound unless the reader changes the setting [B66]. That is one more reason the demo must work muted.

A GitHub repository is another place. Its README is its front page, so the guest link and the result belong in the first screen. A personal site is a third. The best choice is the place a reviewer in your target field actually looks, and only you can find that out, by asking people who hire.

Public posting is your choice. The capstone accepts a private submission to your mentor. If you do post in public, never show a client's or employer's screens or data without permission. Course 13 teaches the full privacy habit.

</GoDeeper>

## Check the host's rules before you share

<MermaidDiagram title="Which host fits" chart={`flowchart TD
  Q["Are you paid for this site?"] -->|"Yes"| A["A free personal plan may forbid it. Read the terms"]
  Q -->|"No"| B["Does the demo need a server?"]
  B -->|"No"| C["Static site: GitHub Pages, public repository"]
  B -->|"Yes"| D["Use a host with a server, or a video"]
`} legend="Two questions decide most cases. Always read the host's current terms on the day you publish." />

Free plans have rules. Vercel's free Hobby plan is for personal, non-commercial use, and paid client work counts as commercial. GitHub Pages needs a public repository on a free plan and serves static files. A demo that needs a server needs another host, or a video. Read the terms before you share a link.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: hosting terms in plain words">

These are the hosts' terms, not design rules, and they change. On the same check date, Vercel's terms say the free Hobby plan is for personal or non-commercial use. Its fair use page says that "receiving payment to create, update, or host the site" counts as commercial, and that donations do not. People using it must be at least 16 [B48, B49]. So if a clinic pays Manar to build its booking site, the Hobby plan is not for that site.

GitHub Pages on a free plan needs a public repository [B79]. A published site can be up to 1 GB, and GitHub states soft limits of 100 GB of bandwidth a month and 10 builds an hour. GitHub also says Pages is not for running an online business [B78]. It serves static files, which is our inference from those limits, so check before you rely on it. A demo with a server needs a mock mode with sample data, or the video.

Netlify and Cloudflare Pages also list free plans [B81, B82], but we did not check whether they open in Gaza, and the free numbers change often. GitHub's trade controls page does not name Palestine, Gaza or the West Bank [B80]. Payment and network blocks are a separate matter. Check whether each tool opens for you, and keep the video as the path that always works.

A public repository is public to everyone. Before you switch it on, search it for keys, passwords and real people's data, using the secret check from course 13.

</GoDeeper>

## Open your own link every week

<MermaidDiagram title="The weekly check" chart={`flowchart TD
  O["Open the link, signed out, on a phone"] --> K["Does the core open in one step?"]
  K --> F["Do the video and screenshots still open?"]
  F --> D["Write the date you checked"]
`} legend="Two minutes a week protects the first impression." />

Links break quietly. Once a week, open your link on a phone, signed out, on mobile data if you can. Check that the core opens in one step and that the fallbacks still work. Write the date you checked. It takes two minutes and protects your first impression.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what to look at in the weekly check">

Signed out matters. If you open your own link while you are signed in, you see what you built, not what a stranger sees. Use a private window or a different phone.

Check the guest button first. Does it still open the core, with sample data, in one step? Then check the video: does it play, and are the captions still right? Open one screenshot. Open the README and read its first screen. If a host paused your demo, you will see it here before a reviewer does.

Keep a short note with the date and what you found. Over a month, the note shows whether a free host keeps pausing your site, and then you can change the host. The check is practice, not research. It costs two minutes and a little mobile data, which is cheaper than a lost first impression.

</GoDeeper>
