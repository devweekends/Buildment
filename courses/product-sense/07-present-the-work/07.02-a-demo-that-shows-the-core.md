## One app, a tour and a story

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m7-demo-storyboard" />

The tour follows the app's menus: sign-up, settings, then the main moment at 1:40. The story starts with one student and one job. Tonight's groups are on screen at 0:00, and the join happens at 0:10. Touch both plans and compare the time of the join. The story is also shorter: 70 seconds, against 130.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: one person, one job, one result, in five shots">

A tour is organised by the app: its menu, its settings, its features. A story is organised by a person. In Maryam's story the person is a student with an exam on Thursday. The job is the one she found in module 1: find two or three classmates to revise with tonight. The result is that the student is in a group, and that four of five classmates in her check did the same.

A story hides most of the app. Halaqa had 14 features in version 1, and the story shows one job. That is a trade-off, and it is fine to name it. A person who wants voice rooms will not see them in the demo. They can find them on the case study page or in the README, which you will plan in the next chapters.

Here is the storyboard in full. A storyboard gives each shot a time and the words that appear on screen.

| Time | On screen | Words on screen |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Tonight's groups with seats left | Find a group for tonight |
| 0:10 | A finger taps Join on the 8 pm group | Tap Join |
| 0:25 | The confirmation screen | You are in. 1 seat left. |
| 0:40 | The result | 4 of 5 classmates joined in their first visit |
| 1:00 | End card | Try it as a guest. Link below. |

The last shot starts at 1:00 and lasts 10 seconds, so the story ends at 1:10, which is 70 seconds. Give each shot one idea and at most about 10 words. Keep each caption on screen long enough to read twice, slowly. That last rule is practice, not research.

The first shot already shows the core. Maryam gave up the sign-up page, the settings and the menu. A reviewer who wants those will find them on the case study page. She also writes the result honestly: the small print under the fourth shot says "in a check with 5 classmates". That check finds problems. It does not prove the design is good.

Two numbers in this chapter are targets of this course, not facts about viewers. One is a demo of 60 to 90 seconds. The other is the core of the app within the first 15 seconds. They rest on two findings. An analysis by NN/g in 2011 looked at 205,873 web pages. Users often left within 10 to 20 seconds. Pages that made their value clear in the first 10 seconds could hold people for minutes. The data came from one browser plug-in and describes web pages, not video [B58]. A study of 6.9 million video sessions on a learning platform found that shorter videos were much more engaging [B63]. Those were lecture videos, so the study supports "short", not "90 seconds". Both are research, and both are limited.

Use real content in the story: real group names, real times and sample students, never placeholder text. Never show a client's or employer's screens or data, or other people's private details, without permission. Course 13 teaches the full privacy habit.

</GoDeeper>

## The same frame, with and without sound

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m7-muted-frame" />

Many feeds start a video with the sound off. Then a voice carries nothing, and the words on the screen carry everything. Touch the three versions. A muted viewer cannot ask what the app does, so the screen must say it. Only the caption bar changes between the last two. Put key words in large type, and add captions to anything you say.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why sound cannot carry the story">

This is a mix of practice and a standard. First, the practice. At the time of writing (checked 2026-10-25), LinkedIn says some feed videos may play automatically without sound [B66]. LinkedIn is for people 16 or over [B84]. Chrome always allows muted autoplay and limits autoplay with sound [B70]. Safari on iPhone allows muted autoplay inside a page and needs a tap for sound [B71]. So a video that starts silent is normal, not a mistake by the viewer.

Now the standard. WCAG 2.2, success criterion 1.2.2 (Level A), asks for captions on prerecorded audio in video [B65]. Captions also help. A 2015 review of more than 100 studies found that captions improve comprehension, attention and memory, most of all for people watching in a second language [B64]. That fits your reviewers and fellows well. The same review notes that automatic captions still contain errors, so read every line. These findings support captions for viewers. They do not prove that captions raise your chances of getting a job.

On LinkedIn, you attach a caption file in the desktop upload flow [B67]. We did not find a way to add captions from the mobile app. If you only have a phone, put the words into the picture itself as text on screen, and the video needs no caption file.

</GoDeeper>

## Check the file size before you upload

<MermaidDiagram title="Check the size, then upload" chart={`flowchart TD
  R["Record only the story"] --> Z["Check the file size"]
  Z --> Q{"Small enough for weak data?"}
  Q -->|"Yes"| C["Add captions or words"]
  Q -->|"No"| T["Cut seconds where nothing happens"]
  T --> Z
  C --> U["Upload or share"]
`} legend="Size in MB is megabits a second, times seconds, divided by 8." />

Phones and Windows have a built-in screen recorder. Record only the story, then look at the size in your file manager. At 2 megabits a second, 90 seconds is about 22.5 MB. If the file is large, cut the empty seconds and check again. A reviewer may have weak, costly data too.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: recording paths and file size">

These recording paths are practice, taken from the makers' help pages at the time of writing (checked 2026-10-25). On Android, pull the quick settings down and tap Screen record. The steps differ by phone maker, and some need Android 11 or later [B72]. On iPhone, open Control Center and tap Record. Press and hold the button to turn the microphone on. Some apps block recording [B73]. On Windows, open Snipping Tool, press the Windows key, Shift and R, choose an area, then start and stop [B74]. OBS Studio is free and open-source recording software for Windows 10 and 11, macOS and Linux [B75]. None of these pages gives a file size per minute.

So the size below is arithmetic, not a measurement. A <GlossaryTerm term="bitrate" definition="How much video data is stored for each second of video, usually counted in megabits a second." /> of 2 megabits a second means 2 megabits for every second. Divide the total megabits by 8 to get megabytes.

| Length | Data rate | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 60 seconds | 2 megabits a second | 15 MB |
| 90 seconds | 2 megabits a second | 22.5 MB |
| 90 seconds | 5 megabits a second | 56.25 MB |

Check the real size in your file manager, because a phone may record at a higher rate than you expect. If the file is large, first cut the seconds where nothing happens. Then, if needed, compress it with a free converter such as HandBrake, which is open source for Windows, macOS and Linux [B77]. We found no source that a GIF is smaller than a video for a demo, so do not switch to a GIF to save data.

A 60 to 90 second demo is far below the video limits we found. LinkedIn lists limits for company Pages [B68], and we could not confirm limits for personal posts. Check the upload page for your own account.

</GoDeeper>

## Mute it and say it in one sentence

<MermaidDiagram title="The mute test" chart={`flowchart TD
  M["A friend plays it once, sound off"] --> S["They say what the app does in one sentence"]
  S --> Q{"Is it the job you meant?"}
  Q -->|"Yes"| OK["Share it"]
  Q -->|"No"| F["Fix the first 15 seconds"]
  F --> N["Test with a different friend"]
  N --> S
`} legend="Use a new friend each round, because a friend who saw it already knows the answer." />

Ask a friend, not only yourself, because you already know the answer. They watch once with the sound off and say in one sentence what the app does. If that matches the job you meant, share it. If not, change the first shot.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: a mute test you can run on a friend">

Give a friend your phone with the sound off. Do not explain anything. Play the video once. Then ask three questions: what does the app do, who is it for, and what did it do for the person in the video? Write their answers word for word. This test is practice, not research.

If they cannot say the job, the first 15 seconds did not work. Do not argue with them. Change the first shot, or move the main moment earlier, and test with another friend. The squint test from 2.2 helps here too: look at one still frame from the video and ask what comes first. If everything has the same size and weight, nothing comes first.

Check the captions by reading them once with the sound off. Automatic captions still contain errors [B64], and a wrong word in a caption can change the meaning. Ask your friend before you show their face or any private detail on the screen. A demo made from sample data needs no such permission.

</GoDeeper>
