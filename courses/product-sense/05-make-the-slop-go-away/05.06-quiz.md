```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Hamza builds a booking page for a tailor's shop with an AI tool. Four facts: 1) The tailor's sign and shop front are dark red, and the page header uses the same dark red. 2) The page uses the font that comes with the phone. 3) Under the headline sit three identical cards with icons, labelled 'Quality', 'Trust' and 'Style'. 4) The booking button says 'Book a fitting'. Which change do the facts support?"
options:
  - "Change the header colour, because tool colours are defaults"
  - "Replace the phone's own font, because any default is a fault"
  - "Replace the three cards with the tailor's prices and times"
  - "Rename the booking button, because short labels are defaults"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "Fact 1 says the red matches the sign and shop front. Hamza can say why it is there, so it is a choice and not a default."
  - "Fact 2 shows no problem. A default is a fault only when it fails the person, and the phone's own font needs no download. Nothing here says it fails."
  - "Right. Fact 3 describes the part with no job: identical cards that make claims with nothing to check. Prices and times give a customer something to use."
  - "Fact 4 shows the label already says what happens next. 'Book a fitting' is a clear label, and being short is not a fault."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "An AI tool critiques Razan's page for a car wash: 'Your page uses a purple gradient and the font Inter, which are signs of AI design. Remove both.' What is the right next check?"
options:
  - "Ask whether she chose each for this car wash, and why"
  - "Remove both at once, because the tool's list is a rule of design"
  - "Keep both, because advice from an AI tool is always wrong"
  - "Ask a second AI tool, and remove both if it says the same"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Anthropic's own list calls these patterns defaults rather than choices. A gradient or a font with a reason tied to the car wash is a choice and can stay."
  - "The lists are not rules of design. A purple gradient that matches the car wash's sign, with a reason, is a choice, so removing it without asking loses something."
  - "AI critique can be wrong, but not always. Each point is worth checking against a rule or a person before you keep or drop it."
  - "A second tool may repeat the same list, and agreement is not a reason. The check is Razan's own reason, or a rule she can measure."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A tool writes the top line for Ehab's laundry page: 'Experience seamless, effortless laundry that elevates your day.' What is the mistake in it?"
options:
  - "The words 'seamless' and 'elevates' prove that an AI tool wrote it"
  - "It needs a list of three, such as 'fast, fresh and friendly'"
  - "It should say 'improves' for 'elevates', which is a plainer word"
  - "It could describe any laundry, so it tells a customer nothing"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "Word lists cannot label one text, and people use these words too. The problem is what the sentence tells the reader, not who wrote it."
  - "Three vague words are still vague. A list of three adds a pattern, not a fact a customer can check."
  - "Swapping one word keeps the same empty meaning. The sentence needs a price, a time or a thing a customer did."
  - "Right. Swap 'laundry' for 'bakery' and the line still works. A price, a pickup time or a place would make it specific."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Razan squints at four versions of a copy shop's order page. The job is: send a file before noon and collect it by 4 pm, and the student's worry is missing the deadline. Version 1: nothing stood out, and every line looked the same. Version 2: the line 'Ready by 4 pm' stood out first, then the Upload button. Version 3: the purple glow and the headline stood out equally. Version 4: only the Upload button stood out, and the time and the prices faded away. Which version should she keep?"
options:
  - "Version 1, because every line gets the same attention"
  - "Version 2, because the time comes first, then the button"
  - "Version 3, because the glow and headline are easy to remember"
  - "Version 4, because the button is the action the job needs"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "Fact 1 says nothing stood out. With no first thing, the eye has no place to start, which is the all-bold failure from this module."
  - "Right. The time answers the student's worry about the deadline, and the button is the next step. One thing comes first, and it matches the job."
  - "Fact 3 shows the first things are decoration and a headline, not what the job needs. Being easy to remember is not the same as being useful."
  - "Fact 4 shows the time and prices faded away. The deadline is the student's worry, so a button alone leaves out what the job depends on."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "Bayan's AI-made page for a school tutor fades and slides in every section as the reader scrolls. A reader's phone is set to reduce motion. The tutor still wants the page to feel alive. What is the best change?"
options:
  - "Keep the fades on every section, because the setting only affects games and videos"
  - "Make the fades faster and softer, so that most readers will not notice them"
  - "Drop most fades, keep one change that answers a tap, and honour the setting"
  - "Replace the fades with a short zoom on every section, which is not a fade"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "The reduce-motion setting applies to web pages too, and a fade and slide on every section is also a generic default. The reader asked for less."
  - "Faster does not mean fewer. The reader's setting asks for less motion, and motion on every section is still a default nobody chose."
  - "Right. Motion should show a change, such as the result of a tap, and it must stop or soften when the reader asks. A page can feel alive without moving everywhere."
  - "A zoom is a scaling motion, a common trigger for people who ask for less motion, and it is still motion on every section. It swaps one default for another."
```
