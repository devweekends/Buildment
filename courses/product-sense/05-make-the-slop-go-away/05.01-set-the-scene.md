Sami owns Wasel, a print and copy shop near the university gate (a fictional shop). In exam weeks it gets about 30 to 40 orders a day, mostly from students with an assignment due tomorrow morning and no printer at home. Sami's nephew Karam built an order app for the shop in one evening with an AI tool.

Sami opens the first page on his phone and says, "It works. But it does not sound like my shop." Karam asks what he means.

## The same page fits three different shops

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m5-scene" />

Switch between the shops. Only one word in the headline changes. The glow, badge, cards and button stay the same. Only the two small grey lines belong to one shop. Karam's tool made this page from one short prompt. A short prompt gets common choices, and module 6 shows how to brief a tool.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what Nour sees on her phone">

Nour is a student in the library. Her assignment is due tomorrow morning, and she has no printer. She opens Wasel's page on her phone. She wants three answers: how much is a page, can she collect her pages today, and where is the shop.

The first words she reads are "Revolutionize your printing experience". Around them are a purple glow, a badge that says "AI-powered", three cards that say "Fast", "Secure" and "Smart", and a glowing button that says "Get started". She scrolls past all of it. At the bottom, in small grey text, she finds 0.50 shekels a page, "Ready by 4 pm" and the opening hours, 8 am to 6 pm.

All three answers were on the page. She had to search for them, and the page spent most of its space on words that any shop could use. The 4 pm promise is also not complete. Wasel's real rule is that orders sent before noon are ready by 4 pm. A student who sends a file at 1 pm may expect it at 4. Chapter 5.3 comes back to this, because a specific sentence still has to be complete.

Nobody was careless here. Karam asked for an order app and got one that works. The page is a first draft, and the rest of this module is about the next draft.

</GoDeeper>

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why this module uses the word slop">

The module title uses a strong word. In 2025 Merriam-Webster named <GlossaryTerm term="slop" definition="Low-quality digital content, usually made in large amounts with AI. Merriam-Webster chose it as its word of the year for 2025." /> its word of the year. Its definition is about content that is low in quality and made in quantity with AI.

Here the word names a look, not a person. A page can show that look because nobody had time to choose, and the fix is not to blame the person or the tool. The fix is to decide, part by part, what the page should say. That is a skill, and it is the skill of this module.

There is a business reason to learn it. NN/g, the research company from chapter 1.5, wrote in 2026 that making screens with AI now costs less than checking them. Teams spend time cleaning up generic, unclear output. The person who can tell a chosen page from a default one is useful in that work.

It also matters for your own portfolio. The course sponsor has seen that when a reviewer opens many fellows' projects in one morning, pages made with the same defaults are hard to tell apart. That is an observation, not a measured fact, so treat it as a reason to practise and not as a rule about reviewers.

</GoDeeper>
