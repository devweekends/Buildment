Karam's page works, and its button works. Yet Sami says it could belong to any shop, and he cannot say why.

## Five parts of Wasel's page nobody chose

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m5-spot-the-slop" />

Tap the five parts that carry the generic look: the glow, the badge, the headline, the icon cards and the button. Each one answers no question Nour has. Then tap the price and the hours. They are real content, so they should move up. When all five are found, switch to the version that leads with the real content.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: why each of the five was flagged">

The glow is colour with no job. It says nothing about printing. OpenAI's guidance for its models asks them to limit dominant purple gradients and not to add gradient orbs. Its design article lists decorative gradients among defaults to avoid. The badge tells a customer how the page was made, but a customer wants pages printed. The headline is the aspirational kind of line that the same article lists as filler.

The three icon cards are a row of identical boxes, each with a tinted tile. Anthropic's design skill calls identical rounded cards with one radius and the same soft shadow part of "the SaaS-card kit". OpenAI lists ornamental icons that do not help a person scan. The sources do not say that a tinted square is the problem. They say icons must do a job. Here each card holds a claim with no proof, so the icon only decorates.

The button says "Get started", and nothing tells Nour where it leads. Anthropic's skill treats words on buttons and errors as design: it prefers "Save changes" to "Submit". On Wasel, "Upload a file" says what happens.

Two parts are marked fine on purpose. The price and the hours are real content. Their only fault is where they sit: at the bottom, in small grey text. Small grey text can also fail the contrast rule you measured in chapter 2.5.

Look closely at the fixed version. It has a small all-caps label above the headline. That label holds the shop's name, which a customer needs. If it said "PRINTING, REIMAGINED", it would be the kind of label Anthropic's skill lists as a tell. The same shape can be a choice or a default.

The canvas cannot show everything on the lists. Anthropic's skill also names numbered markers such as 01, 02, 03 when the content is not a sequence. It names arrows on links, and a fade and slide on every section. Look for them on your own pages.

Evidence label: practice. This is published guidance from two companies about what their tools tend to produce. It is not a test of how common these parts are, and it is not a rule of good design.

</GoDeeper>

## Ask of each part: did you choose it?

<MermaidDiagram title="Default or choice" chart={`flowchart TD
  P["Pick one part of the page"] --> Q{"Can you say why it fits this product?"}
  Q -->|"Yes"| K["A choice: keep it"]
  Q -->|"No"| D["A default: choose it or remove it"]
  D --> W["Ask what it does for the person"]
`} legend="Any part can end up on either side. A gradient with a reason is a choice. The same gradient with no reason is a default." />

A default is a choice you did not make. Nothing on the list is wrong in itself. The test is the question in the picture: can you say why this part is right for this product? If you can, keep it. If you cannot, choose it on purpose or take it out.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: defaults are not sins">

Anthropic's own design skill says the patterns it lists are "defaults rather than choices", and that the brief always wins. If a client asks for a look on the list, you build that look. A default is only a problem when nobody decided.

Take a purple gradient. Suppose Sami's shop sign were purple, and Karam used the same purple in the header. That colour would be a choice with a reason a customer can see. A purple glow behind a headline that could sell anything is a default. The colour is the same. What differs is whether anyone could say why it is there.

The same holds for fonts. At the time of writing (checked 2026-10-25), one OpenAI article lists default font stacks, Inter among them, as defaults to avoid. An older OpenAI cookbook recommends Inter among its options. These are not a contradiction. A font is a default when nobody chose it. A fellow who keeps the phone's own font because it needs no download on weak data has a reason, and that is a choice.

This is why the course never gives you a banned list. A list would teach you to remove things. The question teaches you to decide.

Evidence label: practice. The vendors' own words support the idea. The test "can you say why" is the course's way of using it.

</GoDeeper>

## Two AI companies publish the same warnings

<MermaidDiagram title="Two lists, one overlap" chart={`flowchart TD
  A["Anthropic's design skill"] --> L["Looks their own tools produce by default"]
  O["OpenAI's frontend guidance"] --> L
  L --> G["Gradients and glows"]
  L --> C["Identical cards"]
  L --> P["Pills and decorative icons"]
  L --> T["Template chrome"]
`} legend="Both companies describe looks that their tools tend to produce when a prompt leaves gaps." />

Anthropic and OpenAI each publish guidance that lists looks their own tools produce by default. The lists overlap: gradients, identical cards, <GlossaryTerm term="pill" definition="A small rounded label, such as a badge or a tag." /> clusters, decorative icons and <GlossaryTerm term="template chrome" definition="Small labels, arrows and numbers that decorate a template." />. We read them as questions to ask, not as rules of good design.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what each list names">

| Pattern | What the guidance says | Kind of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Gradients and glows | Purple gradients on white; dominant purple gradients; gradient orbs and blobs; decorative gradients | Vendor guidance |
| Identical cards | Identical rounded cards, one radius, one shadow; a card grid as the first impression; cards inside cards | Vendor guidance |
| Pill clusters | "Pill soup", info chips | Vendor guidance |
| Ornamental icons | Icons that do not improve scanning | Vendor guidance |
| Template chrome | Small all-caps labels above headings, arrows on links, numbered markers on content that is not a sequence | Vendor guidance |
| Fades on every section | A fade and slide on every section is a generic default; one planned moment is better | Vendor guidance |

Anthropic's skill also names whole styles as defaults. One is a cream page with a serif font and a terracotta accent. Another is a near-black page with one bright accent. That matters. If you remove a purple glow and put in a cream page because it feels more refined, you have swapped one default for another. Nothing was chosen for Wasel.

OpenAI's guidance adds two points that help a small shop. It asks for one primary action, and it says the first screen of a working app can be the app itself, not a page that describes it. For Wasel, a page where the first thing is the order form may serve Nour better than any landing page. Chapter 5.4 comes back to this.

No Google page on this subject was found. We name only the two companies whose guidance we read. Their lists change with each model version, so use them as of the date checked.

Evidence label: practice. Both lists are vendor guidance, with no measure of how common each pattern is.

</GoDeeper>

## A vague prompt gets the most common page

<MermaidDiagram title="Why tools give common pages" chart={`flowchart TD
  T["Training on common pages"] --> A["The most common answer"]
  V["A vague prompt"] --> A
  K["The same component kits"] --> A
  A --> L["Pages that look alike"]
  F["A specific brief and real content"] --> G["A page for one product"]
`} legend="The tool does not pick a bad answer. It picks the most common one when you leave gaps." />

Tools learn from many common pages, and a vague prompt asks for the most common one. In one study, five AI tools gave similar layouts and looks for the same tasks. Web pages were already getting more alike before AI, partly because of shared kits. The cure is a specific brief, real content and your own decisions.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: what the studies show, and what they do not">

Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g) tested several AI prototyping tools, including v0 and Figma Make. Without visual instructions, the screens tended to look flat and interchangeable. NN/g links this to the training data, and notes that the tools often default to shadcn/ui and Tailwind CSS. Detailed prompts, or an attached design frame, came closer to a human design. Even good output had problems with hierarchy, grouping, contrast and spacing.

A 2026 research paper from a conference on AI, ethics and society (AIES) compared 120 screens from five tools on 24 tasks. The tools converged in how the screens looked and how they were laid out, and varied more in colour. Its limit matters: there was no set of human-designed screens to compare with. The study shows that the tools resemble each other, not how far they sit from human designers. The same paper found that about a quarter of the design reasons the tools stated were not carried out in the screen they built.

Sameness is older than AI. A 2021 study, described in the AIES paper, found that web page layouts grew more alike after 2007. The difference between layouts fell by over 30 percent. The study linked this to shared frameworks such as Bootstrap.

In the same check, Tailwind's default palette includes indigo, and shadcn/ui's default base colour is neutral. These are facts about defaults. They do not prove why any one look spread. A popular story blames one indigo button style, but we found no test of that cause, so the course does not repeat it.

Evidence label: research for the NN/g test and the AIES study, with the limits above.

</GoDeeper>

## Cards are fine when they hold something

<InteractiveCanvas slug="product-m5-card-with-a-job" />

Switch between the two. Both use three cards. The first cards hold a word and a claim, so Nour cannot compare anything. The second cards each hold a price, so she can compare black and white, colour and binding at a glance. The card is the same. The job is different. The test is not whether it is a card, but what the card does.

<GoDeeper title="Go deeper: the same test for glows, fonts, icons and motion">

The test from the second beat works on every part of a page. Here is how it sorts a few parts of Wasel's page.

| Part | A default when | A choice when |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient | It sits behind a headline that says nothing | It matches the shop's sign and you can say so |
| Font | Nobody picked it | You kept the phone's own font because it needs no download |
| Icon | It decorates a claim | It helps Nour scan, such as a tick next to "Ready" |
| Pill | A cluster of tags with no use | One pill that says "Ready" or "Open", next to a word |
| Motion | Every section fades in | One change is shown, and motion stops when the reader asks |

Each row repeats one idea. The part is not wrong. What matters is whether it does a job you can name. That is also why a plain list of prices would be fine here. Cards are one choice, not the only one.

When you cannot name the job, you have two moves. You can give the part a job: make the cards hold prices. Or you can take it out. Do not take a third route, which is to swap one default for another and call it done.

This is the habit the rest of the module builds. In chapter 5.3 you apply it to words, and in 5.4 to the layout of the whole page.

Evidence label: practice, with the course's own test.

Which part is most likely a default nobody chose?"
options:
  - "The dark green header, because any colour band is a default"
  - "The pitch cards, because every card is a default"
  - "The button, because every button is a default"
  - "The row of icon cards that say 'Fun', 'Fair' and 'Friendly'"
correct: 3
explanations:
  - "The green matches the club's shirts and sign, so Ziyad can say why it is there. That makes it a choice."
  - "Each pitch card holds a time and a price that a player compares. The card has a job, so it is a choice."
  - "'Book a pitch' says what happens next. A button with a clear label is a choice, not a default."
  - "Right. These cards hold claims with nothing to check, in identical boxes with icon tiles. Ziyad cannot say what they do for a player, which is the sign of a default."
