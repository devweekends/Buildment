# Course tracks

Not every course is the same kind of product. A track sets the size of a course, what the learner
produces, and which gates it passes before learners see it. Pick the track in the course brief and
record it in `course.yaml` as a comment at the top, for example `# track: soft-skills`.

| | Engineering track | Soft-skills track |
|---|---|---|
| Example | FreshMarket multi-vendor marketplace | Decision-Making & Professional Judgment |
| Size | 210 to 280 hours over 3 to 4 weeks | 8 to 20 hours, about 25 to 40 chapters |
| Learner produces | A deployed product they can defend | Written and recorded evidence: memos, messages, plans, reflections |
| Chapter shape | Build, verify, reflect | Scenario, concept, practice, reflect |
| Variants | 5 or 6 differentiated variants | None by default |
| Detailed process | The agent IP (`agent-ip/`, see Aqib's course factory) | This folder |

## Engineering track

Engineering courses follow the full course factory process in `agent-ip/`: research dossier,
curriculum roadmap confirmed by at least two instructors, detailed blueprint, sample chapter,
full draft, blind critics, variants and publish sign-off. The verification and import steps in
[`README.mdx`](README.mdx) still apply, because they are how any course reaches the platform.

## Soft-skills track

Soft-skills courses teach judgment and communication through scenarios. They are shorter and have
no product to deploy, so the engineering gates would cost more than they protect. They still need
real checks, because a wrong answer key or a careless scenario teaches the wrong lesson to every
learner.

### Gates

| Gate | What passes it | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| S0 Brief | The brief names the learners, topics, practice and constraints | Course owner |
| S1 Plan | `course.yaml` with every module and chapter, each with a goal | Course owner approves before writing |
| S2 Draft | Every planned chapter written; validate and audit clean | Author |
| S3 Independent check | A reviewer who did not write the course re-answers every quiz and predict cold, and reviews wording for second-language readers | A second person or a separate agent pass |
| S4 Platform check | Imported locally and clicked through as a student at phone width | Author |
| S5 Content review | A mentor or subject expert reads the course before learners use it | Mentor or subject expert |

No author approves their own S3. An agent may run S3 for another agent's draft, but S5 is always a
person.

### What a soft-skills course contains

- **Short teaching chapters**, 10 to 20 minutes each, with a `BigWordAlert` for the central term.
- **Scenario quizzes** with one defensible answer and an explanation for every option.
- **Predict-then-reveal** at "what would you do?" moments.
- **Open questions** for anything opinion-based, and for evidence a mentor should read: a memo, a
  message draft, a plan, or a link to a recorded explanation.
- **A gate per module**: checklist, learning log and, where useful, an open question.
- **A capstone** where the learner applies the course to a real situation of their own.

### Recordings and low bandwidth

An open question can accept a link to a video or audio recording. The platform only counts a
link-only submission as complete when `minWords` is 0. When a written answer is the main evidence,
keep `minWords` and make the link optional; when a recording can replace the writing, set
`minWords: 0` and say so in the prompt. Never make a recording the only way to complete a chapter.

## Variants and combinations

A variant keeps a course's outcomes and changes its domain, users, hardest constraint and hero
feature, so two learners finish with different portfolio projects. Variants are planned as
roadmaps first and only written after the roadmap is approved. Variant plans live next to the
other planning documents in [`plans/`](plans/).
