This is the gate for module 11. Your evidence is an explanation of your own tunnel, for a friend. In challenge 8 you captured the same traffic from three places: the café's outside plug, the VPN box's `wg0`, and the lab website's answer. The gate asks you to put those captures side by side and say what each one shows. You have met every piece in the last eight chapters, so nothing here is new. Your answer saves as you type, so you can stop after a power cut and come back.

A mentor reads your answer within three days. You do not have to wait. You can carry on to module 12 as soon as you submit, and if the mentor has a question, it reaches you while you work. You answer it by editing and sending your answer again. Your four `test.sh` runs from challenge 8 are part of the evidence too, so check that they pass and that your `results.md` files are committed before you submit.

## What your explanation must cover

Your friend uses a VPN app every day and has never seen a capture. They believe the app makes them invisible. Four things make an explanation they can follow, and each one points at a line of your own files.

The first is the outer packet. Take one line from your `cafe-outside.txt` in part 3. Say what its two addresses are, why they are public addresses and not your laptop's own, and why the protocol is UDP on port 51820. Give its size, and show the sum from the inner packet to the outer one, with the padding.

The second is the inner packet. Take the matching line from your `wg0.txt`. Say what its two addresses are, where `10.99.0.2` comes from, and why only the VPN box could read this line. Your friend should understand that the two lines are one ping, seen from two places.

The third is what each network saw. Use your four-row "who sees what" table from part 4. For the full tunnel, say what the café saw, what the office saw, and which address the website saw. Mention the name questions: where did `web.test` go before you changed the name server, and where after? Take care with one word. The office did not "spy" on you. It is the other end of the tunnel, and the other end always sees what you send through it.

The fourth is what changed with the split tunnel. Use your `cafe-outside-split.txt`. Say which of your traffic was still inside the tunnel, which was not, and what the café could now read that it could not read before. End with one honest sentence your friend can repeat about what their own VPN app does and does not hide.

Write for your friend, not for a mentor. Explain each technical word, such as NAT or AllowedIPs, the first time you use it. About 300 words are enough, or about four minutes if you speak. A voice note is as good as text. A labelled diagram is good too: draw the outer packet around the inner one, and mark who can read each layer.

## A worked example, for a different capture

Here is the same shape of explanation for a different file: `forward-test.txt` from part 1. It is not about the tunnel, so it shows the shape without giving away your answer.

A weak answer says: "Line 2 arrived because the rule was still working." It is wrong, and it explains nothing. The rule had been deleted.

A strong answer says: "Line 2 says `rule off, old NAT state kept: arrived at the VPN box`. The forward rule had just been deleted, yet the packet arrived. The office router keeps a row for each conversation in its conntrack table, and it reads the rules only for the first packet of a new conversation. Send 1 had made a row that said: answers come from `10.20.0.2`. Send 2 used the same addresses and ports, so the router used the row and never looked at the rules. After `conntrack -F`, line 3, the row was gone, and the packet stopped." This answer quotes the line, names the mechanism, and ends with the evidence that proves it: the very next line.

Look at what the strong answer does. It quotes a real line. It says why that line looks the way it does. And it points to a second line that confirms the reason. Your answer has the same three moves for each of the four points above: the line, the reason, and the check.
