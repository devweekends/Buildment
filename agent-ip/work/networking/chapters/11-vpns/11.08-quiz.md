```quiz
type: mcq
question: "The VPN box's wg0 shows IP 10.99.0.2 > 10.20.0.5: ICMP echo request. At the same moment, a capture on the café router's outside plug shows the packet that carried it. Which addresses does that packet have?"
options:
  - "198.51.100.2 to 203.0.113.2"
  - "10.99.0.2 to 10.20.0.5"
  - "192.168.50.23 to 10.20.0.5"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. Outside the café only the outer packet exists: from the café's public address to the office's, after masquerade, as UDP."
  - "Those are the inner addresses. They are locked inside the outer packet and become visible only on wg0, after the VPN box unlocks it."
  - "The café's outside plug never shows the laptop's own address, because the café router masquerades it. And 10.20.0.5 is never on the outside."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "You ping the office with ping -s 200. The inner packet is 200 + 8 + 20 = 228 bytes. How big is the outer IPv4 packet on the café's outside plug?"
options:
  - "288 bytes"
  - "272 bytes"
  - "300 bytes"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "That skips the padding. 228 is not a multiple of 16, so WireGuard pads it to 240 before adding anything."
  - "272 is 240 + 32, the UDP length. The outer packet also has an 8-byte UDP header and a 20-byte IPv4 header."
  - "Right. 228 pads to 240, the next multiple of 16. 240 + 32 = 272, + 8 = 280, + 20 = 300."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "A laptop has two WireGuard peers. The office peer has AllowedIPs 10.20.0.0/16. The lab peer has AllowedIPs 10.20.7.0/24. A route sends 10.20.7.40 into wg0. Which peer gets the packet?"
options:
  - "The office peer, its block is bigger"
  - "The lab peer, by the longest match"
  - "Neither, two matching peers is an error"
correct: 1
explanations:
  - "A bigger block is a less specific match. WireGuard, like a router, uses the most specific one that matches."
  - "Right. 10.20.7.40 is inside both blocks, and the /24 is the longer match, so the lab peer gets it."
  - "Nested blocks are allowed. Across all peers the longest match decides, exactly as in a routing table."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "The laptop in the café uses a split tunnel: only 10.20.0.0/24 goes into wg0. It visits the lab website at 192.0.2.80 and pings 10.20.0.5. What does a capture on the café's outside plug show?"
options:
  - "Only UDP 51820 to the office"
  - "The ping to 10.20.0.5 in clear"
  - "Website TCP and office UDP 51820"
correct: 2
explanations:
  - "That is a full tunnel. In a split tunnel the website is not in 10.20.0.0/24, so its visit takes the default route outside the tunnel."
  - "The ping to 10.20.0.5 goes into wg0, so outside it is wrapped in UDP. The café never sees the inner address."
  - "Right. The website visit leaves as plain TCP from 198.51.100.2, and the office ping travels as UDP 51820 to 203.0.113.2."
```

```quiz
type: mcq
question: "The laptop, 192.168.50.23, sends from its port 51820 to 203.0.113.2 port 51820. The café router masquerades to its outside address, 198.51.100.2, and no other device uses port 51820. Which source does the office router see?"
options:
  - "198.51.100.2:51820"
  - "192.168.50.23:51820"
  - "203.0.113.2:51820"
correct: 0
explanations:
  - "Right. The NAT table rewrites the source address to the router's outside address, and keeps the port because nothing clashes. The VPN box's wg show listed exactly this endpoint."
  - "That is the laptop's private address. The café router replaces it on the way out, so it never reaches the internet."
  - "That is the office router's own outside address, the destination. The source is the café's side of the conversation."
```
