## What a strong answer looks like

Paste your trace, or the sample from 4.1 if yours is all stars. You may scrub the public addresses first. Then go through it line by line. A strong answer does four things.

The first is to sort the hops, and to say how you know. Use what you learned in 2.4. A private address means the hop is inside a home or provider network. A shared address means the provider is sharing addresses between customers. A public address means the hop is on the internet side. Do not stop at the label. Say how you know. "It is private" is a label. "It is private and it is my gateway from chapter 1.3, so it is my home router" is a reason.

The second is to explain what a line is. Every line is the device that stopped one probe. Traceroute sends probes with TTL 1, 2, 3 and so on. Each router that lowers a TTL to zero discards the probe and sends back an ICMP Time Exceeded message from its own address. So the address on line N is the device that stopped probe N. If you can say this in a sentence, you have understood chapter 4.4.

The third is to explain stars as silence. A line of three stars means that no answer came back. The router may be set to stay silent, or a filter may have dropped the probe or the reply. If later lines answer, the path is not broken at that hop. Do not call a star line broken.

The fourth is to write honest questions. For each hop you cannot explain, write the question and what you would check. A good question comes from your own trace and has a check you could run. A weak question is one that nobody could answer, such as "how does the internet work?".

## A worked example, on the sample trace

Here is an answer for the sample trace from 4.1. Your own trace will differ, so use this one only for its shape.

Line 1 is `192.168.1.1`. It is private, and it is the gateway that I wrote down in chapter 1.3, so it is my home router. Line 2 is `192.168.0.1`, also private. So there is a second router in front of the first, and my packet crosses two private networks before it leaves home. Line 3 is `10.20.0.1`, private again. It comes after my own routers, so I think it is inside the provider's network. I cannot prove that from the number alone.

Lines 4, 5, 7 and 8 are public, so they are routers on the internet side. In the sample their addresses are rewritten to the documentation ranges. Line 9 is `203.0.113.80`. It is the last line, and the website itself answers there. Line 6 shows three stars. I do not know why. A router set to stay silent, or a filter, would give stars. But line 7 answered, so the path is not broken at line 6.

Each line is the device that stopped one probe. The probe with TTL 4 died at the router on line 4, and that router sent Time Exceeded from its own address. Two questions I cannot answer yet. How does the router on line 4 choose its next hop? I would need its table, and a trace never shows tables. Why does my packet cross two private routers? I would check whether line 2 is the modem from my provider, on its settings page.

Look at what this answer does. It sorts every hop and gives a reason. It explains the line in terms of TTL and Time Exceeded. It treats the stars as an unknown and not as a fault. And it ends with two questions that each come with a check.

## Two weak answers, and why they fall short

The first weak answer is a list. It says: "1 is private, 2 is private, 3 is private, 4 to 9 are public, 6 is broken." It sorts the hops, which is a good start. But it gives no reasons, so the reader cannot check any of it. It calls line 6 broken, and the trace does not show that, because line 7 answered. And it has no question and no check, so it hides what the writer does not know.

The second weak answer is a guess about everything: "Line 1 is my router, line 2 is my provider, line 3 is a server in my city, line 5 is a submarine cable." Some of these may be true. None of them can be known from a trace. A trace shows addresses and nothing else. It does not show cities or cables or owners. A strong answer says "I think" when it is a guess, and says what it would check. A weak one writes a guess as a fact.

## If your result was different

Three results change the answer a little. None of them is a mistake.

If your trace is all stars, use the sample from chapter 4.1 and say so at the top of your answer. All stars is normal on some networks. You can still explain why stars appear, which is the part that matters.

If your trace has only one or two lines before the destination, check where you typed it. A trace typed inside the lab shows `172.17.0.1` and then little else, because the lab sits behind Docker's own networking. Run it again on your own computer.

If a hop starts with `100.64` up to `100.127`, it is a shared address, and your provider shares addresses between customers. You do not have to explain why yet. Say what it is, and write a question for it. Module 6 answers it.
