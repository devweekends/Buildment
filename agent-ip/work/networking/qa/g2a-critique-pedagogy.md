# G2A critique, pedagogy and instructor lens: networking roadmap

## Verdict

The roadmap has a strong spine. The course question is clear, module openers are real questions from the learner's world, the build-from-nothing challenges with a test that must fail are the right shape, the capstone is demoable, and the minute totals add up (2,420 minutes, checked per module). It is not ready for instructor sign-off. The sponsor's rule "why before what" is met at module level but not shown for any of the 29 Learn chapters, where the outcome lines read fact-first. The payoff comes late: the course question is answered about 22 hours in, and the first network the learner builds appears after about 11 hours of theory and arithmetic. The Build chapters (about 11 hours of the 40) are timed for a learner who never gets stuck, and the roadmap does not say who writes the scripts. Module 3 uses routers, broadcast, ping and namespaces before they are taught. The environment, shell and own-machine commands assume things many target learners do not have (8 GB RAM, a Windows Pro machine, a router they can open, a laptop at all), with no repair path. One S1, thirteen S2. Recommendation: revise, then re-run this critique before the instructors see it.

What works and should be kept: the Module 1 and 7 openers; the "predict, then play" Learn pattern; the entry diagnostic that returns in the capstone (1.1 and 9.4); the "list three questions you cannot answer yet" gate (1.6); negative tests in every challenge; the capstone's fault injection and its defence probes; documentation ranges in labs; the stretch path; the honest note that the lab container hides the real traceroute path.

## Answers to the seven instructor questions

1. **Right sequence?** Mostly, but not safe yet. Modules 3 and 4 use routers, broadcast, ping and tcpdump before they are taught (D05), and the NAT payoff comes about 22 hours in (D03). Fix those and the order is defensible.
2. **Outcomes an instructor would expect?** Yes in scope. Evidence is weak for O3 by hand and O6 diagnosis (D10, D14), and O7 is only reachable by learners with a working laptop environment (D06, D12).
3. **Time budget realistic?** No for beginners using a second language on a phone, especially Build chapters, binary and the capstone (D01, D10, D11, D15). Expect about 55 to 60 hours unless the Build chapters are scaffolded and re-estimated.
4. **Missing, would refuse to teach without?** Ping and ICMP, reading a capture, bind address versus loopback, a shell on-ramp, an isolation rule for the capstone (D05, D07, D11, D19).
5. **Included, would cut?** Class A, B and C as its own chapter, the repeated shortage story, the AS lab mode, the IPv6 Build chapter, and the written-answer gates that duplicate work (D16, D18).
6. **Do variants represent the audiences?** The roadmap names none. That is acceptable for a foundations course, but phone-only and hotspot-only learners are an audience this course must name and serve, not a footnote (D08, D12).
7. **Capstone proud to review and defend?** Close. Negative tests, two-sided captures, fault injection and probes are good. It needs checkpoints, a taught isolation rule and a demo that fits five minutes (D11).
