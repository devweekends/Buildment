# Critic A Report: Chapter 2.2 ("An address is one 32-bit number")

## Verdict

Verdict: Revise. Chapter 2.2 establishes clear mathematical foundations for binary place values and provides responsive, tactile interactive canvases for exploring 8-bit and 32-bit representations, but it fails multiple strict beats and authoring standards. Most critically, Beat 4 violates the rule to never ask before you teach by presenting an unassisted conversion drill canvas before the greedy subtraction algorithm has been taught in visible prose or worked through in an accessible example. The opening prediction block uses an ungrounded toy analogy about eight lamps while immediately spoiling its answer in the preceding introductory sentence, and the summative quiz tests verbatim recall of GoDeeper numbers against transparently implausible distractors rather than authentic hand calculation. Furthermore, the Beat 3 Mermaid diagram simply restates the text without arguing or showing a conversion flow, visible prose falls below the 300-word beats minimum, the assessment interaction budget is exceeded, and the stated roadmap outcome of converting 32-bit integers to dotted form by hand is neither taught as a viable manual method nor assessed for a second-language learner on a phone. The chapter requires targeted structural, visual, and assessment revisions before it can meet the publication threshold.
