## New defects

### B2-01

- Severity: S3
- Location: `courses/founder-interviews/course.yaml`, `projectGoal`
- Observation: The course description says learners will “survive five whys on every resume line,” but the project goal says “a resume where every line survives two whys.”
- Why it matters: This is learner-facing metadata and changes the promised level of practice.
- Required fix: Change “two whys” to “five whys,” or align the course title, outcomes and chapter language to two whys.

### B2-02

- Severity: S3
- Location: `07.01-set-the-scene.md`, `07.04-capstone-full-interview.md`, `00-course-brief.md`, `roadmap-confirmation.md`
- Observation: Learner text says “three weeks” and “Over three weeks,” while the confirmed roadmap says “about four weeks at 5 hours a week.” The brief still says “3 weeks.”
- Why it matters: A fellow may not know whether the course takes three or four weeks, and the round 1 timeline fix introduced a new mismatch with the confirmed plan.
- Required fix: Choose one learner-facing duration and update the brief, roadmap confirmation, course text and metadata together.

### B2-03

- Severity: S3
- Location: `03.01-set-the-scene.md`, `curriculum-roadmap.md`, `course.yaml`
- Observation: The course repeatedly promises “five whys,” but the gate evidence says the learner will “defend one line through four whys.” The chapter also says the gate uses “four whys.”
- Why it matters: The learner cannot tell whether the final evidence requires four or five follow-up questions.
- Required fix: Define whether “five whys” includes the starting claim or means five follow-up questions, then use the same count in the title, outcomes, gate prompt, roadmap and mentor rubric.

### B2-04

- Severity: S4
- Location: `01.04`, `01.05`, `02.02`, `02.05`, plus repeated Hadeel and Yazan examples
- Observation: The revision reduced the old scenario explosion but still uses the charity-shop and load-time patterns across modules. For example, Adam’s charity-shop work appears in four chapters, while multiple other cases use “before and after” load-time examples.
- Why it matters: Repetition makes separate cases harder to distinguish and reduces the sense that each chapter teaches a new decision.
- Required fix: Keep the strongest running example within one module, then change the product, role and evidence pattern in later modules.
