# Curriculum roadmap: Think Clearly: Logic for the AI Era

> Chapters were drafted before G2B instructor sign-off, on the sponsor's instruction (see `decision-log.md`), as with courses 3 to 8. If instructors change this roadmap, the chapters will be revised to match.

## In one paragraph

A gentle, playful, non-coding course that builds logic and clear thinking before the soft-skills and tech courses. Fellows break problems into steps, reason with if-then rules, spot hidden assumptions and weak arguments, make rough estimates, and check what an AI tool tells them before they trust it. Every module starts with a puzzle from everyday life. The capstone asks each fellow to think one real choice (or a provided scenario) through in five parts and walk a mentor through it in five minutes.

## Learner and entry prerequisites

- **Learner:** Gaza fellowship fellows at the start of the programme, from mixed backgrounds, many not technical. English is a second language. Mostly phones, weak data, power cuts.
- **Prior knowledge:** basic English reading and simple arithmetic. No coding, no maths beyond the four operations.
- **Time available:** about 13 hours, suggested as about 1 hour a day over 2 to 3 weeks, or alongside another course.
- **Entry diagnostic:** none graded. Chapter 1.1 opens with an ungraded warm-up puzzle to set the tone.

## Capstone

- **What the learner submits:** for one real choice or one of three provided scenarios: the steps, two hidden assumptions and a test for one, a rough number with assumptions, an AI check (their own AI question, or the provided captured AI answer), and a decision with what would change their mind. Written, with an optional voice note link.
- **Five-minute demo:** the fellow walks a mentor through the five parts.
- **Defense questions:** Which assumption worries you most? What would change your estimate by ten times? What did the AI get wrong, and how did you find out? What would make you change your decision?
- **Review:** a five-line rubric, about 15 minutes per capstone.

## Variants

Not applicable for version 1: a foundation course has no product to vary. Scenario packs (freelancing, study, small business) are an open decision.

## Out of scope

Coding and pseudocode; formal logic notation, proofs and statistics formulas; tool-specific AI tutorials or paid accounts; deep decision frameworks and biases (course 3); presentation skills (course 4); politics, religion and war as material.
