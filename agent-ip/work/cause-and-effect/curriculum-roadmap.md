# Curriculum roadmap: Why Things Happen: Cause, Effect and Systems Thinking

> Chapters were drafted before G2B instructor sign-off, on the sponsor's instruction (see `decision-log.md`), as with courses 3 to 9. If instructors change this roadmap, the chapters will be revised to match.

## In one paragraph
A practical course on finding out why things really happen. Fellows learn to tell a cause from a coincidence, test a cause fairly, dig past symptoms to root causes and strong fixes, solve problems step by step like a detective, see feedback loops, delays and bottlenecks in a system, and think through second-order effects before they act. Every module uses everyday cases: a home bakery, a family shop, a freelancer, a laptop that will not charge. The capstone is a full investigation of one real or provided problem, walked through with a mentor in five minutes.

## Learner and entry prerequisites
- **Learner:** Gaza fellowship fellows, mixed backgrounds, second-language English, mostly on phones.
- **Prior knowledge:** plain English reading and simple arithmetic. Course 9 helps; modules 1 and 2 repeat what is needed.
- **Time available:** about 19 hours, for example 1 hour a day over 3 to 4 weeks.
- **Entry diagnostic:** none graded; the predict in 1.1 sets the tone.

## Capstone
- **What the learner submits:** for one real problem or one of three provided cases (each with a data table and an AI answer that makes one causal mistake): an exact description, a cause map, a root cause with evidence and what the AI got wrong, a fair test of the fix, and two side effects with one to watch. Written, with an optional voice note link.
- **Five-minute demo:** the fellow walks a mentor through the five parts.
- **Defence questions:** What evidence rules out your second-best cause? What would your fair test show if you were wrong? Why is your fix strong, not weak? What will you watch for, and when?
- **Review:** a five-line rubric with three levels per line (Met, Partly, Not yet) and a pass rule, a mentor key for each provided case in the model answer, about 15 minutes per capstone.
