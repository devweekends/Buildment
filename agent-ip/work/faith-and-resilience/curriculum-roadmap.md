# Curriculum roadmap: Steady Hearts: Islamic Belief for Uncertain Times

> Chapters were drafted before G2B instructor sign-off, on the sponsor's standing instruction (see `decision-log.md`), as with courses 3 to 10. If instructors or the scholar change this roadmap, the chapters will be revised to match. **A qualified Islamic scholar must review this roadmap and every chapter before the course is published** (open decision 1).

## In one paragraph
A gentle, practical course on Islamic belief for fellows who live with uncertainty every day. It starts from what only Allah knows and what is ours to do, then teaches tawakkul with taking the means, sabr that keeps acting through loss and waiting, gratitude and hope in balance, deciding with research, shura and istikhara, sincere intention and good work, starting again after failure, and serving others and asking for help. Every teaching comes from a referenced verse or a sahih or hasan hadith in the research dossier, and every chapter ends with something the fellow does this week. The capstone is a steady plan for one uncertain situation (the fellow's own, or a provided case), walked through with a mentor in five minutes.

## Learner and entry prerequisites
- **Learner:** Gaza fellowship fellows, Arabic first, English second, mostly on phones with weak data and power cuts. Almost all are Muslim.
- **Prior knowledge:** plain English reading; basic familiarity with Islam (prayer, the Quran, the Prophet, peace be upon him). No study of Islamic sciences needed.
- **Time available:** about 16 hours, for example 45 minutes a day over 3 to 4 weeks.
- **Entry diagnostic:** none graded; the predict in 1.1 sets the tone.
- **Wellbeing:** the course names the learner's mentor and the fellowship coordinator as the people to reach. It is not counselling and does not replace professional care.

## Capstone
- **What the learner submits:** a steady plan for one real uncertain situation, or one of three provided cases (always available, so nobody must share personal circumstances), in six parts: (1) what only Allah knows and what is yours to do; (2) the means and a backup plan; (3) how you will wait well, with the words and dua you will use; (4) the people you will consult and, if there is a choice, istikhara; (5) your intention and the small daily habit that keeps you going; (6) who you will serve and who you will reach for help if it gets heavy. Written, with an optional voice note link.
- **Five-minute demo:** the fellow walks a mentor through the six parts.
- **Defence questions:** Where in your plan is the trust, and where is the means? What will you do if the backup also fails? Which source in the course is behind each part? Who is the real person you will tell?
- **Review:** a six-line rubric with three levels (Met, Partly, Not yet), a pass rule, and a mentor key for each provided case in the `modelAnswer`; about 15 minutes per capstone. The rubric grades the plan and its reasoning, never the learner's faith or circumstances.

## Out of scope
Fiqh rulings beyond what a cited source states plainly; theology debates on qadr and free will; comparison of schools or sects; weak, fabricated or disputed hadith; battle narratives and political readings; dream interpretation and "signs"; counselling techniques; self-harm rulings or methods. See `research.md` → What not to teach.
