# Course brief: one-percent-better

## Why this course exists

A community member read the first chapter of *Atomic Habits* (James Clear) and applied its main idea, getting 1% better every day, to their daily worship. They made a private page, "Daily Islamic Habits, 1st Oct to 30th Dec", listing eleven habits, and added one new habit a week while keeping the rest going. They asked for the idea to reach other members so that they could build their own habits, but chose not to share their page, to avoid showing off (riya). This course turns that idea into a structured, sourced course, and keeps the member's own choice at its centre: share the idea, not the score.

The eleven habits from the request:

1. Fajr with the first takbir (takbir al-ula)
2. Dhuhr with the first takbir
3. Asr with the first takbir
4. Maghrib with the first takbir
5. Isha with the first takbir
6. Daily Quran recitation (tilawah)
7. Morning adhkar
8. Evening adhkar
9. Adhkar after the prayers
10. The last two verses of Surah al-Baqarah (Amana al-Rasul)
11. Surah al-Mulk

## One sentence

Learners build eleven daily Islamic habits one week at a time, using the 1% idea and the Sunnah's teaching that the most beloved deeds are the most consistent even if small, with a private tracker, a weekly review and a restart plan.

## Where it sits

A standalone soft-skills course. It pairs naturally with `building-habits` (the habit science, credited to its researchers) and `faith-and-resilience` (belief and steadiness), and reuses the latter's verified sources where they overlap. It is not a fiqh course.

## Learner

- **Who:** Muslim learners in the community: students, developers and working professionals, many of them Urdu speakers. Women, people far from a mosque and shift workers are first-class readers, not exceptions.
- **Language:** plain English, short sentences. Urdu equivalents are given for common terms (takbir-e-ula, tilawat, azkar, riyakari). Arabic for a few duas and verses, copied from checked sources, with transliteration and meaning.
- **Device:** mostly phones; light pages, one canvas per Learn chapter.
- **Time:** about 12 hours, for example 45 minutes a few times a week while running the plan.

## Outcome

By the end, the learner can:

1. Explain why small, consistent deeds are beloved (Sahih al-Bukhari 6465) and plan one new habit a week.
2. Keep worship for Allah with a private tracker, and share the idea instead of the score (Sahih al-Bukhari 1; Sahih Muslim 1893, 2642).
3. Catch the first takbir by leaving at the adhan and walking with calm (Sahih al-Bukhari 636), with a home version (Sahih al-Bukhari 527); plan each prayer.
4. Keep a daily Quran portion with a minimum, a make-up time (Sahih Muslim 747) and a restart rule.
5. Keep a sourced starter set of morning and evening adhkar, and the adhkar after each prayer.
6. Keep a bedtime routine with Amana al-Rasul and Surah al-Mulk that protects Fajr.
7. Check a saying for a collection, a number and a grading before passing it on.
8. Count days done, review weekly, restart after a hard week, and defend a 13-week plan.

## Production bar

- Every verse cited with surah and ayah; every hadith with collection and number, and grading when outside the two Sahih collections. Only sahih or hasan hadith are used as the Prophet's words; disputed ones are named as disputed.
- Arabic is copied by script from the source text (see `research.md`), never typed from memory; at most two Arabic blocks per chapter.
- No rulings beyond what a source states plainly. Women, people at home and shift workers have their own version of every prayer habit.
- The five obligatory prayers are never treated as optional habits to add later.
- No sectarian judgements: community practices after the prayer (collective dua, Ayat al-Kursi) are respected, not graded as wrong.
- Nothing personal is graded; every gate offers a provided case; nobody is asked to share a tracker.
- **A qualified Islamic scholar reviews every chapter before publishing.**

## Non-goals

- No fiqh: who must pray in the mosque, joining prayers, details of missed obligatory prayers.
- No dream interpretation, numerology, or promised worldly results for any dhikr.
- No public leaderboards, streak sharing or "accountability" posts.

## Version 2: the whole of daily life

The requester then asked that the course cover every area of daily life the Sunnah teaches, not only the eleven worship habits, and supplied a longer list: morning and night routines, the washroom, eating and drinking, salah and Quran habits, home, intention, taqwa and ihsan, character, discipline, learning, hygiene, daily duas, iman, guarding the eyes, ears and tongue, wudu, the rights of others, humour, friendship, haya, mobile and internet, halal provision, money, family, the heart, mercy, courage, time, responsibility, animals, the akhirah, family golden rules, Jumu'ah, sadaqah, neighbours and guests, and Ramadan.

The course grew from 7 to 15 modules (82 chapters, about 30 hours). The worship modules gained three chapters (inside the prayer; understanding, tajweed and hifz; the night routine), and eight new modules (7 to 14) cover the rest of the list. Every item on the list appears in a chapter, a checklist or a gate. Where the list's own references were weak or could not be checked, the course uses sound alternatives; see the corrections table in `research.md`.
