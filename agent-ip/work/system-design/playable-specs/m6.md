# Module 6 playable specs

Six reserved playable slugs for Module 6 ("APIs, the front door and protecting it"). None has a folder yet; each chapter already places `<InteractiveCanvas slug="..." />` and tells the learner what to drag or tap (quoted under "Beat text"). Source ids refer to `agent-ip/work/system-design/01-research-dossier.md`. Every playable sits in a `BeatFrame` with at most two controls outside the picture. Every verdict below is 38 characters or fewer. Kitabi, Huda and every setting marked "made up" are course examples.

## sysdesign-m6-api-style-weigh (6.2, beat 4: "Weigh what matters, see who wins")

- **Beat text:** "Tap how much each need matters for one API and watch the totals change. Start with a public API for partners ... Switch to your own mobile screens ... Switch to two of your own services talking all day ... The scores are course judgments, not measurements."
- **Claim:** The right API style depends on the caller: weighting reach and caching picks REST, round trips from far-away screens pick GraphQL, small fast calls between services pick gRPC.
- **Model:** weighted sum, total = sum over needs of (weight x score). Scores (1 to 5, course judgments):

  | Need | REST | GraphQL | gRPC |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Reach: partners and browsers | 5 | 4 | 2 |
  | Few round trips for varied screens | 2 | 5 | 3 |
  | Small, fast calls between services | 2 | 2 | 5 |
  | HTTP caching for free | 5 | 2 | 1 |
  | Simple to run and debug | 5 | 3 | 3 |

  Weight levels: 0 "not needed", 1 "nice", 3 "must".
- **Inputs and snaps:** tap a need's weight chip to cycle 0, 1, 3. Three preset chips (at most two controls outside the picture, so put presets inside the picture): "Partner API" (3, 1, 0, 3, 1), "Mobile screens" (1, 3, 0, 1, 1), "Internal services" (0, 1, 3, 0, 1). Default: Partner API.
- **Worked totals (REST / GraphQL / gRPC):** Partner API 37 / 26 / 15. Mobile screens 21 / 24 / 15. Internal services 13 / 14 / 21.
- **What moves:** three option cards (REST, GraphQL, gRPC) with bars that grow as weights change; the leader's card gets the accent ring; each need is a row of chips inside the picture.
- **Verdicts:** REST leads by 2 or more: "REST wins: reach and caching". GraphQL leads: "GraphQL wins: one trip per screen". gRPC leads: "gRPC wins: small typed calls". Lead under 2 points: "Close: pick what your team runs".
- **Primitive:** the existing factor renderer (`renderers/factor`, `FactorSpec`) carries the model as data. The IP prefers the picture as the control, so draw it as cards with in-picture chips; note that `FactorCanvas.tsx` still needs `FOCUS_RING` and `TONES`.
- **Limits text:** "Scores are course judgments. Real choices also weigh what your team already runs."
- **Sources:** [S3] round trips (for the chapter's framing); [S12] HTTP/2; [S22] caching. Scores are not sourced numbers.

## sysdesign-m6-paging-insert (6.3, beat 4: "Add new books while you page")

- **Beat text:** "Drag how many new books arrive between page 1 and page 2, then switch between offset and cursor. With offset, each new book pushes one book from page 1 onto page 2 again. Delete a book from page 1 instead, and offset skips a book Huda never saw. The cursor shows every book exactly once."
- **Claim:** Offset pagination repeats rows when rows are added in front of the reader and skips rows when rows are removed; cursor pagination does neither.
- **Model:** books 9 down to 1, newest first, 3 per page. Page 1 = 9, 8, 7. Then a change k is applied, then page 2 is taken.
  - k = -1: book 8 is deleted. k = 0: no change. k = 1, 2, 3: books 10, 11, 12 are added at the top, in that order.
  - Offset page 2 = rows 4 to 6 of the changed list. Cursor page 2 = the 3 books older than book 7.
- **Inputs:** a rail inside the picture with 5 snaps: "8 deleted", "no change", "+1", "+2", "+3" (default "+1"). A toggle outside the picture: Offset / Cursor (default Offset).
- **Worked values (offset page 2):** k = -1: 5, 4, 3 (book 6 skipped). k = 0: 6, 5, 4. k = 1: 7, 6, 5 (7 repeated). k = 2: 8, 7, 6 (8 and 7 repeated). k = 3: 9, 8, 7 (all repeated). Cursor page 2 is 6, 5, 4 for every k.
- **What moves:** a column of book cards; new cards slide in at the top, a deleted card fades out; a "page 1" bracket and a "page 2" bracket; on page 2 a repeated card is marked amber with "again", a skipped card shows as a red ghost with "never shown".
- **Verdicts:** k = 0: "No change: both pages are right". Offset k = 1: "Offset: book 7 shown twice". k = 2: "Offset: books 8 and 7 twice". k = 3: "Offset: all of page 1 again". k = -1: "Offset: book 6 never shown". Cursor, any k: "Cursor: no repeats, no gaps".
- **Primitive:** `picture-slider` for the rail, with a custom list drawing on `BeatFrame`.
- **Limits text:** "Pages of 3 are illustrative. A book whose sort key changes can still appear twice with a cursor, because it really moved."
- **Sources:** none needed; it follows from the definitions. Keyset pagination is a source-needed note in the chapter.

## sysdesign-m6-retry-key (6.4, beat 4: "Retry with and without a key")

- **Beat text:** "Drag how slow the provider is, and pick how the API treats keys. The app waits 250 ms, then retries, up to 4 tries. With no key, every slow try charges again. With 'check, then save', a retry that lands during the charge charges again. With 'claim first', every purchase is charged exactly once."
- **Claim:** Without a key every timed-out retry charges again; check-then-save still double-charges when the retry lands during the charge; claiming the key first charges exactly once.
- **Model (one purchase, the challenge 5 part 4 settings):** the app times out after T = 250 ms; after a timeout it waits 50 ms and retries with the same key; after a 409 it waits 200 ms; at most 4 tries. The provider takes p ms for every try that reaches it, and the charge is real at (try start + p) even if the app has stopped waiting. Server work other than the provider is instant.
  - No key: if p <= 250, 1 try, 1 charge. Otherwise tries start at 0, 300, 600, 900 ms, all time out, 4 charges, never confirmed.
  - Check, then save: the reply is saved at time p. Try 2 starts at 300. If p <= 300, try 2 finds the saved reply: 1 charge. If p > 300, try 2 finds nothing and charges again; try 3 at 600 finds the saved reply: 2 charges.
  - Claim first: try 1 claims the key at 0. A try that finds it pending gets 409 at once; one that finds it done gets the saved reply. Tries at 0, 300, 500, 700 (as needed). Always 1 charge; confirmed by try 2 if p <= 300, try 3 if p <= 500, try 4 otherwise.
- **Inputs:** provider time on a rail inside the picture, 100 to 600 ms, step 10, snaps at 200, 280, 450 and 600 (default 450). Mode chips: "No key", "Check, then save", "Claim first" (default "No key").
- **Worked values (charges for no key / check / claim):** p = 200: 1 / 1 / 1. p = 280: 4 / 1 / 1. p = 450: 4 / 2 / 1. p = 600: 4 / 2 / 1.
- **What moves:** lanes for Huda's phone, the API with its key store, and the provider (scene card style); each try is a dot that flies; a 250 ms wait ring per try; each charge drops a coin tile onto the provider card with a running count; 409 replies fly back amber.
- **Verdicts:** p <= 250, any mode: "Answer in time: 1 charge, any mode". No key, p > 250: "No key: 4 charges, app says failed". Check, 250 < p <= 300: "Saved in time: 1 charge". Check, p > 300: "Check, then save: 2 charges". Claim first, p > 250: "Claim first: 1 charge, always".
- **Primitive:** custom on `BeatFrame`, drawn with the scene renderer's parts (`SvgNode`, `DotGrid`, `VerdictBanner`), with `picture-slider` for the rail.
- **Limits text:** "One purchase, instant servers, and a provider that takes the same time on every try. Challenge 5 runs 60 purchases with real HTTP and timers: 180, 93 and 60 charges in one run."
- **Sources:** [S89] timeouts and retries; [S80] Stripe idempotency keys. The numbers are challenge 5's settings.

## sysdesign-m6-limiter-burst (6.5, beat 6: "Same burst, five limiters")

- **Beat text:** "Send one burst pattern through all five limiters at once. Drag the second burst closer to the first and across the minute boundary. The fixed window lets all 10 through as soon as the boundary falls between them. The sliding log and the counter stay near 5. The token bucket allows only what has dripped back; the leaky bucket makes requests wait instead."
- **Claim:** With the same traffic, a fixed window lets 2 times the limit through across a boundary, while sliding windows and buckets do not.
- **Model:** limit 5 per minute for every limiter. Burst A: 5 requests at 0:59 (59 s), all allowed by every limiter (each starts fresh). Burst B: 5 requests at time b, dragged from 59 to 119 s. Allowed in burst B:
  - Fixed window (minute windows): 5 if b >= 60, else 0.
  - Sliding log (60 s): 5 if b >= 119, else 0 (an entry at 59 leaves the log when b - 60 >= 59).
  - Sliding window counter: for b in the window starting at 60, w = (60 - (b - 60)) / 60, allowed = floor(5 - 5w) = floor(5 (b - 60) / 60); for b < 60, 0.
  - Token bucket: capacity 5, refill 1 token per 12 s. Allowed = min(5, floor((b - 59) / 12)).
  - Leaky bucket: drains 1 request every 12 s; holds 5 including the one being served. Burst A: 1 served at once, 4 queued. At b, queued = max(0, 4 - floor((b - 59) / 12)); B accepted = 5 - queued (the rest refused); accepted requests are delayed, not refused, and the last one waits about 12 s times its place in the queue.
- **Inputs:** a rail inside the picture for b, 59 to 119 s, step 1, snaps at 59, 60, 75, 90 and 119 (default 60). One Replay button outside the picture.
- **Worked values (fixed / log / counter / token / leaky accepted):** b = 59: 0 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 1. b = 60: 5 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 1. b = 75: 5 / 0 / 1 / 1 / 2. b = 90: 5 / 0 / 2 / 2 / 3. b = 119: 5 / 5 / 4 / 5 / 5.
- **What moves:** five rows, one per limiter, each a short time strip from 0:50 to 2:00 with the minute line at 1:00; burst A and burst B as dots on every row; allowed dots turn good, refused dots bad, queued dots (leaky row) slide right to show their delay. Each row's label shows "B: n of 5".
- **Verdicts:** b < 60: "Same window: nobody allows more". b = 60 to 74: "Only the fixed window let 10 in". b = 75 to 118: "Others allow only what has expired". b = 119: "A full minute later: all allow 5".
- **Primitive:** custom on `BeatFrame` with `picture-slider` for the rail; the five rows could reuse the scene strip and tag drawing.
- **Limits text:** "5 a minute is a made-up limit. The counter's estimate assumes last minute's requests were spread evenly; challenge 5 shows a bunched pattern where it slips."
- **Sources:** [S29] sliding window counter; [S30] and [S32] token bucket; [S31] leaky bucket (nginx). Fixed window and sliding log are derived designs (dossier: no primary source).

## sysdesign-m6-gateway-jobs (6.6, beat 4: "Switch gateway jobs on and off")

- **Beat text:** "Turn the gateway's jobs on and off, then follow one request through. Turn off the token check, and anyone can reach payments. Turn off the limit, and the scraper floods search. Turn on order rules, and the gateway needs a copy of every order. Keep the checks every service shares; leave business rules in the services."
- **Claim:** A gateway should run the checks every service shares and nothing that needs a service's data.
- **Model:** six job blocks inside the gateway, each on or off: TLS (default on), token check (on), rate limit (on), routing (on), request id (on), order rules (off). A request from Huda (GET /orders/512) and one from a scraper follow the path through every block that is on. Each block that is on is a step on the path; the hop as a whole costs about 50 microseconds [S3]. Do not show a per-job time: the dossier has no such number.
- **Inputs:** tap a block to switch it (blocks are the controls, inside the picture). Replay outside the picture.
- **What moves:** the request dot passes through the blocks that are on; a switched-off protection shows who gets in (an unknown caller reaching Payments, the scraper's dots piling onto Search); order rules on draws a red dashed arrow from the gateway to the orders database labelled "copy of every order".
- **Verdicts (first matching, in this order):** token check off: "Anyone can call payments". Rate limit off: "Scraper floods search". TLS off: "Traffic readable on the wire". Routing off: "Client must know every service". Request id off: "No id to trace this request". Order rules on: "Gateway now needs every order". Default: "Thin gateway: checks, then routes".
- **Primitive:** `block-map` (tap a block, "Break it" shows what users lose); or `design-stepper` with one flip per step.
- **Limits text:** "One gateway copy is drawn; real gateways run several behind a load balancer. The 50 microsecond hop is napkin-math's figure for a proxy."
- **Sources:** [S3] proxy hop; [S30] API Gateway; [S31] nginx limits.

## sysdesign-m6-revoke-timeline (6.7, beat 4: "Ban a seller: who notices, and when?")

- **Beat text:** "Ban a seller and watch each design notice. Drag the token lifetime. With sessions, the very next request is refused. With signed tokens alone, the seller keeps working until the token expires. Add a small list of banned tokens that services refresh every few seconds, and the gap shrinks to that refresh time, at the price of a lookup again."
- **Claim:** How fast a ban takes effect depends on where the proof is checked: sessions at once, signed tokens only at expiry, a denylist within its refresh time.
- **Model:** the seller is banned at t = 0 and keeps sending requests. Worst case: the seller's token was issued just before the ban.
  - Sessions: refused at the next request, gap 0 s. Lookups: 1 per request.
  - Signed tokens only: gap = L, the token lifetime. Lookups: 0 per request.
  - Signed tokens plus a denylist refreshed every 10 s (made up): gap = 10 s whatever L is. Lookups: 0 per request, plus one list fetch per service every 10 s.
- **Inputs:** token lifetime L on a rail inside the picture, 1 to 60 minutes, snaps at 1, 5, 15 and 60 (default 15). Ban button (Replay) outside the picture.
- **Worked values:** L = 15: gaps 0 s / 15 min / 10 s. L = 60: 0 s / 60 min / 10 s. L = 1: 0 s / 1 min / 10 s.
- **What moves:** three lanes on a log time axis from 1 s to 60 min; the seller's requests are dots that keep flowing along each lane after the ban line, green while accepted, red from the moment each design refuses; each lane's end shows its gap and its lookups per request.
- **Verdicts:** L <= 5: "Short tokens: gap of 5 min or less". L > 5: "Tokens alone: up to {L} min after ban" (with L filled in, at most 38 characters). The sessions and denylist lanes carry their own labels: "Sessions: refused at once", "Denylist: within 10 s".
- **Primitive:** `log-ruler` for the time axis and rail, with lanes drawn on `BeatFrame`; or the timeline renderer (`renderers/timeline`).
- **Limits text:** "The 15-minute lifetime and the 10-second refresh are made-up Kitabi settings. Short access tokens with revocable refresh tokens behave like the tokens-only lane with a small L."
- **Sources:** [S3] lookup cost (500 microseconds). JWT and OAuth refresh tokens are source-needed notes in the chapter.
