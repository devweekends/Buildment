# Module 12 playable specs (consensus, leader election and coordination)

Eight reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All eight sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. The 19 watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m12-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data; they use the same actor names (S1 to S5, N1 to N5) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with the course code: the timeout figures and gossip figures with seeded simulations (the same `seededRandom` as `toolkit/stats.js`), the Raft figures with the challenge 11 cluster (`challenges/challenge-11-raft`), the rest by the arithmetic shown. The Raft playables use fixed, scripted timers and a fixed 10 ms message delay so that a given button press always gives the same picture; the random part is shown as a choice from a short list.

## sysdesign-m12-timeout-slider

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.2 beat 2, "Drag the timeout, watch the price".
- **Claim:** A short failure timeout notices a real crash sooner but raises many false alarms on healthy nodes. A long timeout is calm and slow. The false alarms fall steeply at first and then flatten.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the timeout rail (inside the picture), a strip of beat gaps drawn as bars, `SvgNode` for the monitor and the node, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** timeout T on a linear rail from 900 ms to 2,000 ms, step 10 ms. Snap points at 900, 1,000, 1,050, 1,100, 1,150, 1,200, 1,300, 1,500 and 2,000. Arrow keys step 10 ms, Page keys step 100 ms. Default 1,100.
- **Model:** A node sends a beat every I = 1,000 ms. A beat takes 10 ms plus a random extra with mean 30 ms (exponential). The monitor suspects the node when the gap between two arrivals is longer than T. The gap is I plus the difference of two delays, which is Laplace with scale 30 ms.
- **Formulas:**
  - For T at least 1,000 ms: P(false alarm per beat) = 0.5 x exp(-(T - 1,000) / 30).
  - For T below 1,000 ms: P = 1 - 0.5 x exp((T - 1,000) / 30).
  - False alarms per hour = 3,600 x P.
  - Average time to notice a real crash = T - 460 ms (T minus half a beat, plus 10 + 30 ms of delay).
- **Picture:** a strip of the last 40 gaps between beat arrivals, drawn as bars from a fixed seeded list (seed 7, delays as in the model), with a horizontal line at T. Bars above the line are red and counted. Under the strip, two big numbers: "false alarms per hour" and "a real crash is noticed after". The monitor card pulses red when a bar crosses the line.
- **Numbers to check** (formula; a simulation of 720,000 beats agrees to the second decimal):

  | T | False alarms per hour | Notice a real crash after |
  |---|---|---|
  | 900 ms | about 3,535 | 440 ms |
  | 1,000 ms | 1,800 | 540 ms |
  | 1,050 ms | 340 | 590 ms |
  | 1,100 ms | 64.2 | 640 ms |
  | 1,150 ms | 12.1 | 690 ms |
  | 1,200 ms | 2.29 | 740 ms |
  | 1,300 ms | 0.08 | 840 ms |
  | 1,500 ms | 0.0001 | 1,040 ms |
  | 2,000 ms | 0 | 1,540 ms |

- **Verdicts:** at least 10 false alarms: "340 false alarms an hour" (the number filled in; bad). From 0.5 up to 10: "About 2 false alarms an hour" (warn). Under 0.5: "Calm: a crash is seen in 840 ms" (good, number filled in). Never longer than 38 characters.
- **Reduced motion:** the bars are drawn in place; no pulsing.
- **Accessibility:** the rail is a `role="slider"` whose value text reads "timeout 1,100 ms, 64 false alarms an hour, a crash seen after 640 ms".
- **Sources:** Raft timing requirement and ranges [S68]; phi accrual trade-off [S73]. The delay model and all figures are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m12-quorum-explorer

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.3 beat 2, "Move N, W and R, find the gaps".
- **Claim:** A read group and a write group must share a node exactly when W + R is more than N. The same choice sets how many failures reads and writes survive, and how slow they are.
- **Primitives:** three `picture-slider.tsx` rails (N, W, R) or `rail-slider.tsx`, `SvgNode` circles for the replicas in a row (tappable to fail), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle outside the picture.
- **Input:** N on a snap rail 3, 5, 7, 9 (default 5). W and R on snap rails 1 to N (defaults 3 and 3; clamp when N changes). Tap a replica to fail or revive it (44px targets). Arrow keys step 1.
- **Model:**
  - majority(N) = floor(N / 2) + 1.
  - Replica response times in ms, in order: 10, 15, 25, 40, 70, 120, 200, 300, 500. Use the first N.
  - Write group = the W fastest replicas that are up. Read group, for the picture, = the R slowest replicas that are up, so the two groups are as far apart as they can be.
  - Shared nodes = write group intersect read group.
  - Guaranteed overlap when W + R > N, with at least W + R - N shared nodes.
  - Writes survive N - W failures; reads survive N - R failures.
  - Write time = response time of the W-th fastest up replica, or "blocked" when fewer than W are up. Read time likewise with R.
- **Picture:** the replicas in a row, fastest on the left, each with a ms label. Write group ringed blue, read group ringed amber, shared nodes filled green with a check. When W + R is at most N, the picture shows the two rings apart with a gap marker and a small "a read can miss a write" tag. Below, three pills: "writes survive 2", "reads survive 2", "write 25 ms, read 25 ms". A failed replica turns grey with a cross.
- **Numbers to check** (response times 10, 15, 25, 40, 70, 120, 200, 300, 500 ms; write time is the W-th fastest up replica, read time the R-th fastest):

  | N | W | R | Overlap guaranteed | Writes survive | Reads survive | Write time | Read time |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 3 | 2 | 2 | yes, at least 1 | 1 | 1 | 15 ms | 15 ms |
  | 3 | 1 | 3 | yes, at least 1 | 2 | 0 | 10 ms | 25 ms |
  | 3 | 1 | 1 | no | 2 | 2 | 10 ms | 10 ms |
  | 5 | 3 | 3 | yes, at least 1 | 2 | 2 | 25 ms | 25 ms |
  | 5 | 2 | 3 | no | 3 | 2 | 15 ms | 25 ms |
  | 5 | 1 | 5 | yes, at least 1 | 4 | 0 | 10 ms | 70 ms |
  | 5 | 5 | 1 | yes, at least 1 | 0 | 4 | 70 ms | 10 ms |
  | 9 | 5 | 5 | yes, at least 1 | 4 | 4 | 70 ms | 70 ms |

  The picture's rings show the worst case for overlap (the write group is the W fastest replicas, the read group the R slowest), not the replicas a read waits for. With N = 5, W = 3, R = 3 and replicas 4 and 5 failed, reads and writes still work (3 up; write and read time 25 ms). With replica 3 also failed, both are blocked.
- **Verdicts:** W + R above N: "Overlap: every read meets a write" (good). Otherwise: "No overlap: a read can miss a write" (warn). Fewer than W up: "Writes blocked: too few nodes up" (bad). Fewer than R up: "Reads blocked: too few nodes up" (bad). Blocked verdicts win over overlap verdicts.
- **Reduced motion:** rings appear in place.
- **Accessibility:** each rail is a slider; each replica is a button ("replica 3, 25 ms, up; press to fail"); the verdict is a live region.
- **Sources:** R + W > N and latency set by the slowest replica [S45]; majority [S52]. Response times are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m12-fencing-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.4 beat 3, "Turn on fencing, drag the pause".
- **Claim:** A lease cannot stop a paused leader that wakes after its lease ended. A fencing token that storage checks stops its write, whatever the length of the pause.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the pause rail, a timeline strip drawn like `year-strip.tsx` (0 to 24 s), `svg-chip.tsx` or `svg-radio-chip.tsx` for the fencing toggle inside the picture (44px), a storage `SvgNode`, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** (1) pause P on a linear rail from 0 to 20 s, step 0.5 s, snap points 0, 2, 5, 8, 8.5, 9, 12, 14, 20; default 14. (2) fencing toggle Off | On, default Off. Arrow keys step 0.5 s, Page keys step 5 s.
- **Model:** Lease 10 s. A takes the lock at t = 0 with token 33 and freezes at t = 2 s. The lease ends at t = 10 s. B takes the lock at t = 10.5 s with token 34 and writes x = B at t = 11 s. A wakes at t = 2 + P and writes x = A with token 33 at once. Storage with fencing off accepts every write in arrival order; with fencing on it keeps the highest token seen and rejects a lower one. A tie at t = 11 goes to B first.
- **Outcome by pause:**

  | Pause | A wakes at | Off | On |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 to 8 s | 2 to 10 s | A writes inside its lease: fine | Same |
  | above 8 to under 9 s | above 10 to under 11 s | Lease over, B has not written yet: A's write lands, then B's | Same: token 33 is still the highest seen |
  | 9 s or more | 11 s or more | A overwrites B: x = A | A is rejected: x = B |

- **Picture:** a timeline from 0 to 24 s. A's lease bar (0 to 10 s, grey after the freeze), A's frozen span (2 to 2 + P) as a hatched bar, B's lease bar from 10.5 s. Write markers: B's at 11 s, A's at 2 + P. Storage card on the right shows the value and "highest token seen". A rejected write bounces off the card with a cross.
- **Verdicts:** P up to 8 s: "A woke inside its lease: all fine" (good). Above 8 s and under 9 s: "Late, but B had not written yet" (warn). 9 s or more, fencing off: "A overwrote B's newer write" (bad). 9 s or more, fencing on: "Token 33 rejected: B's write stands" (good).
- **Reduced motion:** the write markers appear in place.
- **Accessibility:** the rail is a slider ("pause 14 s, A wakes at 16 s"); the toggle is a real switch.
- **Sources:** fencing token and the 33 and 34 example [S70]; Chubby sequencers [S71]. Lease and pause lengths are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m12-raft-election-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.5 beat 6, "Kill, split and cut: drive the cluster".
- **Claim:** A crashed leader is replaced after about one election timeout. A tie costs one more timeout. A cut-off follower raises its term alone, and its return forces one election.
- **Primitives:** custom canvas on `BeatFrame` with `SvgNode` cards for the five servers (S1 to S5, laid out as in the scenes), `flow-pulse.tsx` or `link-line.tsx` for message dots, `svg-button.tsx` for the actions inside the picture. This is the `[cluster: raft]` renderer of the simulation kit.
- **Input:** four buttons (44px): "Kill the leader", "Force a tie", "Cut off S4", "Reconnect S4"; plus "Kill another server" (kills the next follower) and "Reset". A speed toggle 1x | 0.5x outside the picture.
- **Model:** Five servers, majority 3. Fixed message delay 10 ms each way, heartbeat every 50 ms. Start: S1 leader of term 1, all logs equal at 3 committed entries. Election timers, in ms, from fixed lists (the learner's actions pick the list): default list S2 240, S3 190, S4 270, S5 210. Tie list: S2 220, S3 220, S4 260, S5 280, then retry timers S2 160, S3 290. A server that grants a vote resets its timer. Votes: one per term, first come first served; S4 answers S2 first and S5 answers S3 first in the tie list.
- **Scripted results:**
  - Kill the leader, default timers: S3 fires at 190 ms (term 2), votes arrive at 210 ms, S3 leads at 210 ms. S2, S4 and S5 voted for S3; S5's timer at 210 ms is reset by its own vote at 200 ms. No leader for 210 ms. Verdict: "S3 won term 2 with 4 of 5 votes".
  - Force a tie, then kill: S2 and S3 fire at 220 ms (term 2), S4 votes S2, S5 votes S3, each has 2 of 5, no leader. Retry timers 160 and 290 ms: S2 fires at 380 ms (term 3), S3 and the others vote at 400 ms, S2 leads at 400 ms. No leader for 400 ms. Verdict: "Tie: S2 won term 3 after a retry".
  - Cut off S4: S4's timer list 270, 200, 250, 180 ms: it becomes candidate at 270 ms (term 2), then at 470 ms (term 3), 720 ms (term 4), 900 ms (term 5). The other four stay in term 1 with S1 leading. Verdict at 900 ms: "S4 alone: term 5, no majority". Reconnect: S4's RequestVote (term 5) reaches S1, which steps down and adopts term 5; S4's log is one entry behind (a write was committed while it was away), so every other server refuses it; the next timer (S3, 190 ms after the reset) wins term 6. About 210 ms without a leader. Verdict: "S4's old term forced a new election".
  - Kill the leader and two followers: three servers down, two up, no majority. Both live servers become candidates and their terms rise by 1 at every timeout; no leader. Verdict: "No majority: no leader is possible".
- **Picture:** five server cards in a ring: role colour, term number, votes tally on candidates (dots), a thin arc that drains as the election timer runs. A strip under the cards shows "no leader for 210 ms" counting up while no server leads. Cut links drawn as a broken line.
- **Verdicts:** as in the scripted results; each at most 38 characters.
- **Reduced motion:** no travelling dots; each step is a short list of lines below the picture ("S3 asks for votes, term 2").
- **Accessibility:** buttons have text labels; the state is also a text list ("S1 down, S3 leader term 2, S2 follower term 2...") in a live region.
- **Sources:** terms, 150 to 300 ms timers, split votes [S68]. The scripted timers are course numbers. The challenge 11 part 1 table (median failover 218 ms) supports the 210 ms order of magnitude.

## sysdesign-m12-raft-log-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.6 beat 5, "Send, cut, heal: watch logs repair".
- **Claim:** An entry is committed only when a majority holds it. A leader cut off with a minority stores entries it can never commit, and the new leader overwrites them after the cut heals.
- **Primitives:** custom canvas on `BeatFrame`, `SvgNode` cards for S1 to S5, each with a row of log boxes (numbered slots, the term written in each box), a commit marker line, `svg-button.tsx` for actions inside the picture. This is the `[cluster: raft]` renderer.
- **Input:** buttons (44px): "Send an entry", "Cut S1 and S2 off", "Heal", "Kill a follower", "Reset". "Send an entry" goes to the node that currently calls itself leader; when two nodes do, a small chooser (S1 | S3) appears.
- **Model:** Five servers, majority 3. S1 leads term 1 at the start, all logs empty. Entries are named a, b, c, ... (the commands). Replication takes one step (about 10 ms each way); the step is shown. Commit rule: a leader commits an entry of its own term when a majority holds it. After the cut, S3, S4 and S5 elect S3 for term 2 after one timeout; the new leader adds an empty entry (shown as a small dot) when it takes office. Healing makes S3 send its log to S1 and S2: they refuse once (the entry before does not match), drop their conflicting entries, then copy.
- **Script and checkpoints:**

  | Step | State |
  |---|---|
  | Send a, b, c | All five logs hold 3 entries of term 1; commit marker at 3 on every server. Verdict: "Majority holds it: committed". |
  | Cut S1 and S2 off, send d to S1 | d (term 1, slot 4) on S1 and S2 only, hollow; commit stays at 3. Verdict: "2 of 5: stored, not committed". |
  | Wait one timeout | S3 leads term 2 and appends an empty entry at slot 4 (term 2) on S3, S4 and S5. S1 still shows leader of term 1. |
  | Send e to S3 | e at slot 5 (term 2) on S3, S4, S5; commit marker at 5 on those three. S1 and S2 stay at 3. |
  | Heal | S3 sends term 2 entries. S1 and S2 drop d, copy the empty entry and e. All five logs match; commit 5 everywhere. Verdict: "d was never committed: erased". |
  | Then Kill a follower twice and send f | f commits with 3 of 5 up. A third kill leaves f hollow with "2 of 5: no majority". |

- **Verdicts:** "Majority holds it: committed" (good); "2 of 5: stored, not committed" (warn); "d was never committed: erased" (warn, with the entry letter filled in); "All five logs match again" (good); "Two leaders, only one can commit" (warn, shown while both claim the role).
- **Reduced motion:** entries appear in place; a text line below says what moved.
- **Accessibility:** buttons have labels; each log is a text list ("S1: a b c d(term 1)") in a live region; solid and hollow boxes differ by outline style and by a "committed" word on the marker.
- **Sources:** commit rule, Leader Completeness, log repair [S68]. Challenge 11 part 3 supports the numbers: 5 of 5 writes lost on the small side, 0 committed, logs equal after healing in 100 of 100 runs.

## sysdesign-m12-paxos-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.7 beat 2, "Two proposers, three acceptors".
- **Claim:** Once a majority has accepted a value, any later proposer that gets promises from a majority is forced to propose that same value. Two values are never chosen.
- **Primitives:** `lane-sequence.tsx` (sequence of messages between proposers and acceptors) or `design-stepper.tsx` for the step list, `SvgNode` cards for A1, A2, A3, P1, P2, `svg-chip.tsx` for choosing which acceptors a message reaches (44px, tap to toggle), `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** For the active proposer: "Prepare" and "Accept" buttons; before sending, tap the acceptors that receive the message (default all three; the learner may choose any subset). P1 uses numbers 1, 3, 5 and wants A; P2 uses 2, 4, 6 and wants B. A "Reset" button.
- **Model:** Each acceptor keeps promised (number or none), acceptedNumber and acceptedValue. prepare(n): if n is above promised, set promised = n and reply promise with (acceptedNumber, acceptedValue) if any; otherwise reply no. accept(n, v): if n is at least promised, set promised = n, acceptedNumber = n, acceptedValue = v and reply accepted; otherwise reply no. A proposer may send accept only after promises from at least 2 acceptors; its value is the acceptedValue with the highest acceptedNumber among the promises, or its own if there is none. A value is chosen when at least 2 acceptors hold the same (number, value).
- **Scripted run (the chapter's predict):**

  | Step | Result |
  |---|---|
  | P1 Prepare(1) to A1, A2, A3 | Three promises, nothing accepted yet |
  | P1 Accept(1, A) to A1, A2 | A1 and A2 accepted (1, A); A is chosen: 2 of 3 |
  | P2 Prepare(2) to A2, A3 | A2 promises and reports (1, A); A3 promises with nothing; P2 holds 2 promises |
  | P2 Accept: the value box is locked to A | The tooltip says "A2 reported (1, A): P2 must propose A" |
  | P2 Accept(2, A) to A2, A3 | All three acceptors hold A; still one chosen value |

  Any majority P2 can reach includes A1 or A2, so the value is always forced to A. If P2 prepares to A3 alone, it holds 1 promise and "Accept" stays disabled with "wait for a majority".
- **Duel run:** P1 Prepare(1) to all, P2 Prepare(2) to all, P1 Accept(1, A): all three refuse (promised 2). P1 Prepare(3) to all, P2 Accept(2, B): all three refuse (promised 3). A "Duel" hint shows after two refusals: "Two proposers can keep outbidding".
- **Verdicts:** "P2 must propose A, not B" (warn); "A is chosen: 2 of 3 accepted it" (good); "Refused: promised 2" (bad, the number filled in); "No majority: P2 must wait" (warn); "Still one value: A" (good).
- **Reduced motion:** messages appear as list lines, no travelling.
- **Accessibility:** acceptor chips are toggle buttons ("A2, receives the message: on"); each acceptor's state is written as text ("A2: promised 2, accepted 1 A").
- **Sources:** phases, majorities and the distinguished proposer [S67]. The rule that a promise reports the highest accepted proposal is from the same paper (section 2.2) and is flagged in the chapter as a source needed note.

## sysdesign-m12-gossip-spread

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.8 beat 2, "Drag the size and the fanout".
- **Claim:** Gossip reaches every node in about log N rounds, with a fixed load per node. A bigger fanout cuts the rounds. A false suspicion spreads as fast as true news, so the suspicion time must be longer than the spread.
- **Primitives:** two `picture-slider.tsx` rails (cluster size, fanout), a growth curve drawn in the picture, `SvgNode` dots for a small sample of nodes, `VerdictBanner`. One button inside the picture: "Suspect a healthy node".
- **Input:** (1) cluster size N on a log rail with snap points 7, 16, 100, 1,000, 10,000 (default 100). (2) fanout f on a snap rail 1, 2, 3 (default 1). Arrow keys move one snap point.
- **Model:** One node knows at round 0. Each round, each informed node tells f other nodes chosen at random. The expected number of informed nodes x follows x(r+1) = x(r) + (N - x(r)) x (1 - (1 - 1 / (N - 1))^(f x x(r))), starting at x(0) = 1. "Rounds to reach everyone" = the first round where N - x is below 0.5. The result matches a seeded simulation (300 runs; 40 runs for 10,000 nodes) within 0.5 round.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | N | f = 1 | f = 2 | f = 3 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 7 | 5 (sim 5.0) | 3 (sim 3.2) | 3 (sim 2.5) |
  | 16 | 7 (sim 7.4) | 5 (sim 4.6) | 4 (sim 3.6) |
  | 100 | 12 (sim 12.3) | 7 (sim 7.4) | 6 (sim 5.7) |
  | 1,000 | 18 (sim 18.1) | 11 (sim 10.7) | 8 (sim 8.2) |
  | 10,000 | 24 (sim 23.9) | 14 (sim 14.0) | 11 (sim 10.7) |

  Curve for N = 1,000, f = 1 (informed nodes after each round): 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 31, 61, 117, 215, 367, 562, 751, 882, 951, 981, 993, 997, 999, 1,000. Half the cluster knows after round 10.
  Messages per node per round: f. Total messages per round: f x informed nodes.
- **False suspicion:** the button marks one healthy node as suspected at round 0. The suspicion reaches half the cluster by round 10 (N = 1,000, f = 1), which is when the target is likely to hear it and start its "alive #2" correction. The correction then needs about 18 more rounds. So the cluster is mixed for about 28 rounds. The picture shows two curves, red for the false news and green for the correction, and a line "suspicion time must be above about 28 rounds".
- **Picture:** the growth curve (informed share against rounds) with a marker at the "all know" round; a row of up to 49 dots (7 x 7 sample) that fill as the curve moves for small N, and a coloured band for large N. Under it: "rounds to reach everyone: 18", "messages per node per round: 1".
- **Verdicts:** "1,000 nodes hear it in 18 rounds" (good, numbers filled in); after the button: "A false rumor spreads just as fast" (warn); "Correction done after about 28 rounds" (good).
- **Reduced motion:** the curve is drawn complete; no dot animation.
- **Accessibility:** both rails are sliders ("1,000 nodes, fanout 1, 18 rounds"); the button is a real button.
- **Sources:** infection-style spread and suspicion in SWIM [S72]. The round counts are the course simulation. The log N shape is flagged as a source needed note in the chapter.

## sysdesign-m12-registry-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 12.9 beat 2, "Register, beat, expire, watch".
- **Claim:** A crashed instance stays in the registry until its lease runs out, so callers can be sent to it for up to a lease length. A lease shorter than about three beat intervals removes healthy instances when beats are lost.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the lease and beat rails, `lane-sequence.tsx` or `rail-strip.tsx` for the time strip, `SvgNode` cards for three instances and the registry, `svg-button.tsx` for "Crash" and "Lose next beat" per instance (44px), `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** (1) lease TTL on a snap rail 4, 6, 8, 10, 15, 30 s (default 10). (2) beat interval on a snap rail 1, 2, 3, 5 s (default 3). (3) per instance: "Crash" and "Lose next beat". (4) "Reset". The simulation clock runs at 1 s of model time per 0.4 s of real time; a pause button stops it.
- **Model:** Instances i1, i2, i3 register at t = 0 and beat every I seconds from their own offsets 0, 1 and 2 s. A beat that reaches the registry sets the key's expiry to that moment plus TTL. A lost beat does not reach it. A key is deleted when the clock reaches its expiry; the watcher is told 0.01 s later (etcd's watch is about 10 ms in a healthy cluster [S54]). A crashed instance sends no more beats.
- **Formulas:**
  - Stale window after a crash = expiry - crash time, between TTL - I and TTL.
  - Average stale window = TTL - I / 2.
  - Consecutive lost beats a healthy instance survives = ceil(TTL / I) - 2, but not below 0.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | TTL | Beat interval | Stale window (worst) | Stale window (average) | Lost beats survived |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10 s | 3 s | 10 s | 8.5 s | 2 |
  | 10 s | 1 s | 10 s | 9.5 s | 8 |
  | 8 s | 5 s | 8 s | 5.5 s | 0 |
  | 15 s | 5 s | 15 s | 12.5 s | 1 |
  | 30 s | 3 s | 30 s | 28.5 s | 8 |
  | 4 s | 3 s | 4 s | 2.5 s | 0 |

  Worked example (default): i1 beats at 0, 3, 6, 9. Crash at t = 4 right after the beat at 3: expiry at 13, so callers are told 9 s after the crash. Lose the beats at 3 and 6: the beat at 9 arrives before the expiry at 10, so i1 stays. Lose 3, 6 and 9: the key expires at 10 and a healthy instance is removed.
- **Picture:** a time strip with each instance's beats as ticks and its lease as a bar that shrinks to zero; the registry card lists live keys; a watcher card lists the events it has been told ("payments-2 added", "payments-2 gone"). A crashed instance turns grey; the "stale" part between the crash and the expiry is hatched red.
- **Verdicts:** after a crash: "Dead one listed for 9 s more" (warn, number filled in); after the expiry of a crashed instance: "Gone after 9 s; watcher told" (good); a healthy instance expiring: "Healthy one expired: lease too short" (bad); lost beats survived: "Survived 2 lost beats" (good).
- **Reduced motion:** the strip updates in steps of one second; no sliding.
- **Accessibility:** rails are sliders; the crash and lose-beat buttons are labelled per instance ("Crash payments-2"); the registry and watcher lists are text in a live region.
- **Sources:** etcd leases, transactions and watches [S53, S54]. The beat and lease figures are course numbers.
