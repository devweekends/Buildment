# Module 22 playable specs (chat, presence and notifications)

Six reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All six sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The 17 watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m22-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same names (Dardasha, Layla, Sami, Gateway, Chat, Registry, Presence, Notifier) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with code (the formulas are short and shown). All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time. Course numbers shared by the playables, defined in chapters 22.1 to 22.5: 10 million daily users; 40 messages per user a day; peak 3 times the average; 3 recipients per message; 1.5 devices per user; 20% of devices connected at the busiest time; 300 bytes per stored message; a day is 86,400 seconds; a gateway holds 50,000 lines in the plan (100,000 at most) and accepts 500 new connections a second; a chat server finishes 4,000 messages a second and is planned at 2,000; a store node finishes 5,000 writes a second and is planned at 2,500; a registry node finishes 100,000 operations a second and is planned at 50,000; a heartbeat every 30 seconds with a key that lives for two beats; 3,125 presence changes a second at peak (15 million devices x 6 changes a day x 3 peak / 86,400); an SMS provider that accepts 150 messages a second.

## sysdesign-m22-estimate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 22.1 beat 2, "Drag the users, find the load".
- **Claim:** For a chat app, the open connections are the load that is easy to miss: messages a second can be modest while millions of lines need dozens of gateway servers. The ringed card is the number that drives the design.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links, some cards are inputs: tap, then drag sideways or use arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** three draggable cards. Daily users: 1M, 5M, 10M, 25M, 50M (default 10M). Messages per user a day: 10, 20, 40, 80, 160 (default 40). Devices online at peak: 10%, 20%, 30% (default 20%). Four fixed cards labelled "assumption": 1.5 devices per user, peak 3 times, 3 recipients per message, 300 B per message, plus the gateway plan of 50,000 lines.
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Messages a day = users x messages per user.
  - Messages a second, average = messages a day / 86,400. Peak = average x 3. Deliveries a second at peak = peak x 3.
  - Open lines = users x 1.5 x share online. Gateways = ceil(open lines / 50,000).
  - Storage a year = messages a day x 365 x 300 B.
- **Picture:** three chains from the same first card. Upper: Daily users > (x messages) Messages a day > (/ 86,400 s) Messages a second > (x 3 peak) Peak messages a second > (x 3 recipients) Deliveries a second. Middle: Daily users > (x 1.5 x online) Open lines > (/ 50,000) Gateways. Lower: Messages a day > (x 365 x 300 B) Storage a year. Dots flow along the links at a speed set by the value. Format: `4,630 /s`, `13,889 /s`, `41,667 /s`, `3.0 million`, `60 gateways`, `43.8 TB`. Ring (tone warn) on the driver card (see verdicts).
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above):

  | Users | Msgs per user | Online | Msgs per day | Avg msgs /s | Peak msgs /s | Peak deliveries /s | Open lines | Gateways | Storage a year | Ring |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10M | 40 | 20% | 400M | 4,630 | 13,889 | 41,667 | 3,000,000 | 60 | 43.8 TB | open lines |
  | 1M | 40 | 20% | 40M | 463 | 1,389 | 4,167 | 300,000 | 6 | 4.4 TB | none |
  | 50M | 40 | 20% | 2000M | 23,148 | 69,444 | 208,333 | 15,000,000 | 300 | 219.0 TB | open lines |
  | 10M | 10 | 20% | 100M | 1,157 | 3,472 | 10,417 | 3,000,000 | 60 | 10.9 TB | open lines |
  | 10M | 160 | 20% | 1600M | 18,519 | 55,556 | 166,667 | 3,000,000 | 60 | 175.2 TB | open lines |
  | 10M | 40 | 10% | 400M | 4,630 | 13,889 | 41,667 | 1,500,000 | 30 | 43.8 TB | open lines |
  | 10M | 40 | 30% | 400M | 4,630 | 13,889 | 41,667 | 4,500,000 | 90 | 43.8 TB | open lines |
  | 1M | 160 | 10% | 160M | 1,852 | 5,556 | 16,667 | 150,000 | 3 | 17.5 TB | peak messages |
  | 1M | 10 | 10% | 10M | 116 | 347 | 1,042 | 150,000 | 3 | 1.1 TB | none |

- **Verdicts** (the first rule that holds):
  - `Open connections drive it` (gateways 20 or more; warn)
  - `Message rate drives it` (peak messages 5,000 a second or more; warn)
  - `Storage drives it` (storage 10 TB a year or more; warn)
  - `One small cluster could serve this` (otherwise; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the cards show their final values.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "10 million daily users, 40 messages each, 3 million open lines, 60 gateways"; the ring is also written as text in the verdict.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions (chapter 2.3 for the method). No external source.

## sysdesign-m22-design-stepper

- **Chapter and beat:** 22.2 beat 3, "Step through the design at peak".
- **Claim:** The numbers decide the blocks. One server cannot hold 3 million lines; gateways fix the lines, partitions fix the writes, and the registry and notifier come last. Each block is sized against the number that stresses it.
- **Primitives:** `design-stepper.tsx` with a `DesignSpec` (`models/design-stepper.ts`): five steps, Back and Next under the picture, step chips on top, one choice in step 3 (partition count, a row of three chips inside the picture). `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** Back and Next (or tap a step chip). In step 3 only: partition count 4, 6 or 8 (default 8).
- **Model:** peak load is 13,889 messages a second, 41,667 deliveries a second, 3,000,000 open lines, 3,125 presence changes a second. Steps:
  1. One box: lines, rules and storage on one server. Lines 3,000,000 against a plan of 50,000.
  2. Gateways and chat servers: 3,000,000 / 50,000 = 60 gateways; 13,889 / 2,000 = 7 chat servers (ceil). The store still takes 13,889 writes a second against a plan of 2,500.
  3. Split the store by a hash of the conversation id into k partitions: each gets 13,889 / k writes a second.
  4. A registry: lookups 41,667 + writes 3,125 = 44,792 operations a second against 50,000 per node (one node 90%; two nodes 45% each).
  5. A push queue and a notifier take the 10% of deliveries that find a phone off: 4,167 jobs a second. The load on every other block is unchanged.
- **Formulas:**
  - Times its plan = load / plan.
  - Per partition share of plan = (13,889 / k) / 2,500.
  - Gateways = ceil(3,000,000 / 50,000) = 60; chat servers = ceil(13,889 / 2,000) = 7.
- **Picture:** left to right: Phones > Gateways (x N) > Chat servers (x N) > Store (x k), with the Registry above and the Queue and Notifier below. A pill on the card under stress shows how many times its plan it carries (tone bad above 1.0, warn from 0.8 to 1.0, good below 0.8). Step 4 adds the Registry card with the pill `45% each, 2 nodes`. Step 5 adds the Queue and Notifier with `4,167 jobs /s`.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Step | Card | Load | Times its plan |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | One box lines | 3,000,000 | 60.0 |
  | 2 | Store | 13,889 writes /s | 5.6 |
  | 3, k = 4 | Each partition | 3,472 /s | 1.39 |
  | 3, k = 6 | Each partition | 2,315 /s | 0.93 |
  | 3, k = 8 | Each partition | 1,736 /s | 0.69 |
  | 4 | Registry, one node | 44,792 /s | 0.90 |
  | 4 | Registry, two nodes | 22,396 /s each | 0.45 |
  | 5 | Push queue | 4,167 jobs /s | n/a |

- **Verdicts:** step 1 `One server holds 60 times its plan` (bad); step 2 `Lines fit; the store is 5.6x its plan` (warn); step 3 `Each partition has room` (good, k = 8), `Tight: 93% of one node's plan` (warn, k = 6), `Still 1.4x one node's plan` (bad, k = 4); step 4 `Lookups fit on two registry nodes` (good); step 5 `Off-line phones go through a queue` (good).
- **Reduced motion:** the cards jump to their final values with no flow.
- **Accessibility:** step chips and partition chips are buttons with names such as "Step 3, split the store, 8 partitions"; each card has a text label with its load.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course numbers; the 2,500 write plan follows chapter 20.2.

## sysdesign-m22-connections-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 22.3 beat 3, "Restart a few gateways, spread the return".
- **Claim:** Restarting a gateway sends 50,000 phones to the others. If many restart at once, or the phones come back at the same moment, the fleet cannot accept the new connections. A few gateways at a time, with the phones spread over time, is a trickle the fleet absorbs.
- **Primitives:** `traffic-playground.tsx` or `block-map.tsx` for the gateway grid (tap cards to restart a block), `picture-slider.tsx` for the spread rail (inside the picture), `SvgNode`, `VerdictBanner`. A small chart of arrivals per second against the accept line.
- **Input:** gateways restarted at once k: 1, 6, 15, 30 (chips inside the picture, default 6). Spread of the return J: 0 (all at once), 5, 15, 30 seconds on a snap rail (default 5). Arrow keys step through the snap points. One button outside the picture: Restart (replays the return).
- **Model and formulas** (60 gateways, 50,000 lines each, 500 new connections a second each, 100,000 lines at most):
  - Phones to move P = 50,000 x k. Healthy gateways H = 60 - k.
  - Fleet accepts C = H x 500 a second. Arrival rate R = P / max(J, 1).
  - Lines on each healthy gateway after the move = 50,000 + P / H.
  - Time to return = max(max(J, 1), P / C) seconds.
- **Picture:** a grid of 60 small cells (the gateways); the k restarting cells go dark and their phones are drawn as dots flowing to the lit cells. Under it, an arrivals-per-second area over time with the accept line C drawn across; the area is red above the line. Format: `R = 20,000 /s`, `C = 27,000 /s`, `lines each: 55,556`.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | k | J | Phones | Fleet accepts /s | Arrivals /s | Result | Time | Lines per healthy gateway |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 0 | 50,000 | 29,500 | 50,000 | over | 1.7 s | 50,847 |
  | 1 | 5 | 50,000 | 29,500 | 10,000 | fine | 5 s | 50,847 |
  | 6 | 0 | 300,000 | 27,000 | 300,000 | over | 11.1 s | 55,556 |
  | 6 | 5 | 300,000 | 27,000 | 60,000 | over | 11.1 s | 55,556 |
  | 6 | 15 | 300,000 | 27,000 | 20,000 | fine | 15 s | 55,556 |
  | 6 | 30 | 300,000 | 27,000 | 10,000 | fine | 30 s | 55,556 |
  | 15 | 30 | 750,000 | 22,500 | 25,000 | over | 33.3 s | 66,667 |
  | 30 | 30 | 1,500,000 | 15,000 | 50,000 | over | 100 s | 100,000 |

- **Verdicts** (the first rule that holds):
  - `Gateways full: lines at the limit` (lines per healthy gateway 100,000 or more; bad)
  - `Too many at once: retries pile up` (R above C; bad)
  - `Back in {t} s, no overload` (otherwise, t as in the Time column; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the chart shows the final area and the verdict.
- **Accessibility:** the k chips and the J rail are controls with text such as "6 gateways restart, spread 15 seconds, 20,000 arrivals a second, fleet accepts 27,000"; the verdict is also text.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course numbers. The 500 new connections a second and the 50,000 and 100,000 lines are course assumptions (chapters 22.1 and 22.3).

## sysdesign-m22-sequence-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 22.4 beat 2, "One counter per conversation sets the order".
- **Claim:** Two phones that sort by arrival time can disagree about the order of a chat when one path is slow. If every message carries a number from its conversation and a phone shows message n only after n - 1, all phones agree, at the price of a short wait when a message arrives early.
- **Primitives:** `lane-sequence.tsx` for the service's numbered lane and the two phone lists, `picture-slider.tsx` or a snap rail for the delay (inside the picture), `beat-segmented.tsx` for the toggle outside the picture, `SvgNode`, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** two tap targets inside the picture, "Send" under Dana and under Omar (default: tap Dana, then Omar). The first one tapped is sent at t = 0 and the second at t = 100 ms. A delay rail for the slow path to Sami, which applies to the message sent first: 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000 ms (default 200). One toggle outside the picture: "Phones sort by": arrival or number (default arrival). One button: Replay.
- **Model:** each sender is 30 ms from the service; the service numbers by arrival (1 for the first sent, 2 for the second). The service is 40 ms from Layla for both. For Sami, message 1 takes D ms and message 2 takes 40 ms. Arrival at Layla: message 1 at 70 ms, message 2 at 170 ms. Arrival at Sami: message 1 at 30 + D, message 2 at 170. By arrival, Sami sees 2 before 1 when 30 + D > 170, that is D > 140. By number, Sami holds message 2 until message 1 arrives: the hold is 30 + D - 170 = D - 140 ms when D > 140, else 0.
- **Picture:** the service in the middle with a numbered lane (1, 2). Above, Dana and Omar with their Send targets. Below, Layla's and Sami's phones, each with a short list of the messages as the phone shows them. Dots fly along the paths at speeds set by their delay. With the number toggle on, a message that arrives early appears greyed with the text `waits for no. 1`.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | D (ms) | Layla by arrival | Sami by arrival | Phones agree by arrival | Sami by number | Message 2 held (ms) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 50 | 1, 2 | 1, 2 | yes | 1, 2 | 0 |
  | 100 | 1, 2 | 1, 2 | yes | 1, 2 | 0 |
  | 200 | 1, 2 | 2, 1 | no | 1, 2 | 60 |
  | 500 | 1, 2 | 2, 1 | no | 1, 2 | 360 |
  | 1000 | 1, 2 | 2, 1 | no | 1, 2 | 860 |

  The snap points avoid D = 140, where the two arrivals tie.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Phones disagree on the order` (arrival, D above 140; bad)
  - `Same order, only by luck` (arrival, D below 140; warn)
  - `Same order on every phone` (number; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the lists show their final state at once; the hold is written as text.
- **Accessibility:** Send targets and the toggle are buttons; the lists are real lists with text such as "Sami's phone shows: Dana's message, then Omar's".
- **Sources:** None needed (course model). Chapters 10.1 and 10.3 for the ordering ideas.

## sysdesign-m22-presence-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 22.4 beat 7, "Drag the ping gap, watch the cost".
- **Claim:** Presence is a heartbeat from every connected phone, so its cost is connected phones divided by the gap, not the message rate. A shorter gap finds offline phones faster and costs more; ending the ping at the gateway moves the cost off the presence store.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` or `rail-slider.tsx` for the gap (inside the picture), `beat-segmented.tsx` for the toggle outside the picture, `SvgNode`, `VerdictBanner`. Two bars: pings a second and messages a second, on one scale.
- **Input:** gap between pings: 5, 15, 30, 60, 120 seconds on a snap rail (default 30). One toggle outside the picture: "A ping ends at": the store or the gateway (default the store).
- **Model and formulas** (3,000,000 connected phones; 13,889 messages a second at peak):
  - Pings a second = 3,000,000 / gap.
  - Times the message rate = pings a second / 13,889.
  - A phone shows offline after two missed pings = 2 x gap seconds.
  - Store writes a second: the store mode is pings a second; the gateway mode is 3,125 (status changes) plus 60 gateway leases.
- **Picture:** a phone cloud on the left, a gateway column and the presence store on the right. In store mode the pings flow all the way to the store card; in gateway mode they stop at the gateway and only a thin stream of changes reaches the store. A bar for pings a second next to a bar for messages a second.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Gap (s) | Pings /s | Times the message rate | Shows offline after | Store writes /s (store mode) | Store writes /s (gateway mode) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5 | 600,000 | 43.2 | 10 s | 600,000 | about 3,125 |
  | 15 | 200,000 | 14.4 | 30 s | 200,000 | about 3,125 |
  | 30 | 100,000 | 7.2 | 60 s | 100,000 | about 3,125 |
  | 60 | 50,000 | 3.6 | 120 s | 50,000 | about 3,125 |
  | 120 | 25,000 | 1.8 | 240 s | 25,000 | about 3,125 |

- **Verdicts** (the first rule that holds):
  - `Pings are {n}x the message rate` (store mode and n 3 or more, n rounded; bad)
  - `Cheap, but a dot 4 minutes late` (gateway mode and gap 120; warn)
  - `Cheap: store sees only changes` (gateway mode; good)
  - `Pings below 2x the message rate` (store mode and n under 3; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** the pings do not flow; the bars and the verdict show the final values.
- **Accessibility:** the rail and the toggle are controls with text such as "ping every 30 seconds, 100,000 pings a second, 7.2 times the message rate, offline shown after 60 seconds".
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course numbers. The 3,125 changes a second is 15 million devices x 6 x 3 / 86,400 (course assumptions).

## sysdesign-m22-outage-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 22.5 beat 5, "Drag the outage, drop the stale".
- **Claim:** While an SMS provider is down, login codes pile up in the queue. After it returns, the queue shrinks only by the spare capacity, so a 5 minute outage takes 10 minutes to clear. Dropping codes that have waited longer than they are worth clears it much sooner.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the outage length (inside the picture), `beat-segmented.tsx` for the stale limit (a four-option segmented control, outside the picture), `SvgNode`, `VerdictBanner`. The queue is an area over time.
- **Input:** outage length L: 1, 2, 5, 10 minutes (snap rail inside the picture, default 5). "Drop codes older than": never, 10 min, 5 min, 2 min (default never). One button: Replay.
- **Model** (a first-in, first-out queue; codes arrive at 100 a second; no sending during the outage; 150 a second after it; a code that has waited longer than the limit when it reaches the front is dropped, and costs no sending time):
  - Waiting at the end of the outage = 100 x 60 x L.
  - Catch-up time without a limit = L x 100 / (150 - 100) = 2 x L.
  - Longest wait = L minutes when no code is dropped.
- **Picture:** the queue length over time (area), with the outage band shaded, a dashed line for the stale limit on the age of the oldest code, and three numbers: `waiting 30,000`, `dropped 18,000`, `back to normal in 4 min`.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with a one-second simulation of the model):

  | Outage | Drop older than | Waiting at end | Dropped | Longest wait | Back to normal in |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 min | never | 6,000 | 0 | 1 min | 2 min |
  | 2 min | never | 12,000 | 0 | 2 min | 4 min |
  | 5 min | never | 30,000 | 0 | 5 min | 10 min |
  | 10 min | never | 60,000 | 0 | 10 min | 20 min |
  | 5 min | 10 min | 30,000 | 0 | 5 min | 10 min |
  | 5 min | 5 min | 30,000 | 0 | 5 min | 10 min |
  | 5 min | 2 min | 30,000 | 18,000 | 2 min | 4 min |
  | 10 min | 5 min | 60,000 | 30,000 | 5 min | 10 min |
  | 10 min | 2 min | 60,000 | 48,000 | 2 min | 4 min |
  | 2 min | 2 min | 12,000 | 0 | 2 min | 4 min |

- **Verdicts** (the first rule that holds):
  - `{n} stale dropped; clear in {t} min` (dropped above 0; good)
  - `Old codes sent late: clear in {t} min` (nothing dropped and the longest wait is 2 min or more; warn)
  - `Short outage: clear in {t} min` (otherwise; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the area shows the final state with no growth animation.
- **Accessibility:** the rail and the segmented control are controls with text such as "5 minute outage, drop codes older than 2 minutes, 18,000 dropped, back to normal in 4 minutes".
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course numbers (100 codes a second, 150 a second provider limit). The metastable pattern is chapter 16.4 [S94].
