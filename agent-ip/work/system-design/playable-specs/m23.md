# Module 23 playable specs (search, typeahead and probabilistic data structures)

Five reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All five sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The ten watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m23-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same names (Dalil, Suggest, Search, Index) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with code (the formulas are short and shown). All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time. Course numbers shared by all five, defined in chapter 23.1: 200 million products; 1 KB of text each; 100 different words in each product; 4 bytes for one entry of the index; 50 million shoppers a day; 4 searches for each shopper; 5 suggestion calls for each search; a busiest second that is 3 times the average; a shard of 10 GB; a day is 86,400 seconds.

## sysdesign-m23-estimate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 23.1 beat 2, "Drag the shoppers, find the load".
- **Claim:** For a search box, the suggestion calls and the index size shape the design; the search rate alone does not. The cards that matter are ringed.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links, some cards are inputs: tap, then drag sideways or use arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** four draggable cards. Shoppers a day: 10M, 25M, 50M, 100M, 200M (default 50M). Searches per shopper: 1, 2, 4, 8 (default 4). Suggestion calls per search: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8 (default 5). Products: 20M, 50M, 200M, 500M, 1B (default 200M). The peak factor (3), the words per product (100), the bytes per entry (4) and the shard size (10 GB) are fixed cards labelled "assumption".
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Searches a day = shoppers x searches per shopper.
  - Searches a second, average = searches a day / 86,400; peak = average x 3.
  - Suggestion calls a second, peak = searches a day x calls per search / 86,400 x 3.
  - Index GB = products x 100 x 4 B / 10^9.
  - Shards = max(1, ceil(index GB / 10)).
  - Shard calls a second at peak = searches peak x shards.
- **Picture:** three chains from a shared start. Upper chain: Shoppers a day > (x searches each) Searches a day > (/ 86,400 s, x 3 peak) Searches a second, peak. Middle chain: Searches a day > (x calls each, / 86,400 s, x 3 peak) Suggestion calls a second, peak. Lower chain: Products > (x 100 words x 4 B) Index size > (/ 10 GB) Shards. Dots flow along the links at a speed set by the value. Format: `6,944 /s`, `34,722 /s`, `80 GB`, `8 shards`. Rings (tone warn): on the suggestion card when its peak is 10,000 or more; on the shards card when shards are 4 or more.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above):

  | Shoppers | Searches each | Calls each | Products | Search peak /s | Suggestion peak /s | Index | Shards | Verdict |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 50M | 4 | 5 | 200M | 6,944 | 34,722 | 80 GB | 8 | Suggestions and index size drive it |
  | 10M | 2 | 3 | 50M | 694 | 2,083 | 20 GB | 2 | One machine could serve this |
  | 100M | 8 | 5 | 500M | 27,778 | 138,889 | 200 GB | 20 | Suggestions and index size drive it |
  | 25M | 1 | 1 | 20M | 868 | 868 | 8 GB | 1 | One machine could serve this |
  | 200M | 4 | 5 | 200M | 27,778 | 138,889 | 80 GB | 8 | Suggestions and index size drive it |
  | 50M | 4 | 2 | 200M | 6,944 | 13,889 | 80 GB | 8 | Suggestions and index size drive it |
  | 50M | 4 | 5 | 1B | 6,944 | 34,722 | 400 GB | 40 | Suggestions and index size drive it |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Suggestions and index size drive it` (both rings; warn)
  - `Suggestions drive the traffic` (suggestion peak 10,000 or more and shards under 4; warn)
  - `Index size drives: shard it` (shards 4 or more and suggestion peak under 10,000; warn)
  - `One machine could serve this` (otherwise; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the cards show their final values.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "50 million shoppers a day, 6,944 searches a second at peak, 34,722 suggestion calls a second"; the rings are also written as text in the verdict.
- **Limits shown to the learner:** averages and guesses; the peak factor is one number for both traffic types; real apps debounce key presses; the index size ignores compression and replicas.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions (chapter 2.3 for the method). No external source.

## sysdesign-m23-tiny-index

- **Chapter and beat:** 23.2 beat 2, "Build an index from three documents".
- **Claim:** A search index is a table flipped: from "product to words" into "word to products". A query reads the lists of its words, and a rare word makes a short list.
- **Primitives:** `design-stepper.tsx` or `stepper.ts`/`use-stepper.ts` for the four steps (Back and Next under the picture, step chips on top), with a row of query-word chips inside the picture in the last step. `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** Back and Next (or tap a step chip). In step 4 only: tap word chips to add or remove query words (red, shoe, running, waterproof, hiking, blue, short). Default query: red + shoe.
- **Model:** three products and the tokenizer of the chapter (lower case, split on non-letters, strip a trailing s from words longer than 3 letters). Steps:
  1. Three products: 1 "Red running shoes", 2 "Blue running shorts", 3 "Red waterproof hiking shoes".
  2. Split into words: 1 red, running, shoe; 2 blue, running, short; 3 red, waterproof, hiking, shoe. The stripped "s" is shown as a small change (shoes becomes shoe, shorts becomes short).
  3. Flip the table: blue [2], hiking [3], red [1, 3], running [1, 2], shoe [1, 3], short [2], waterproof [3]. The seven word cards appear with their lists.
  4. Ask: the chips pick the query words. The picture lights the lists of the chosen words, shows the products that are in every list (AND), and shows a score for every product that matches any word.
- **Formulas:**
  - Weight of a word = ln(3 / number of products that contain it): red, shoe, running 0.405; blue, short, waterproof, hiking 1.099.
  - Score of a product = sum of the weights of the chosen words it contains (each word appears once in each product).
  - AND result = products present in every chosen list. If no word is chosen, nothing is lit.
- **Picture:** left, three product cards; right, the word cards with lists as small numbered dots; links from a product to its words in step 2 and from each word to its products in step 3. In step 4 a bar under each product shows its score, and a banner shows the AND result.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the chapter code):

  | Query | AND result | Ranked (score) |
  |---|---|---|
  | red | 1, 3 | 1: 0.405, 3: 0.405 |
  | red + shoe | 1, 3 | 1: 0.811, 3: 0.811 |
  | red + waterproof + shoe | 3 | 3: 1.910, 1: 0.811 |
  | blue + red | none | 2: 1.099, 1: 0.405, 3: 0.405 |
  | running + shoe | 1 | 1: 0.811, 2: 0.405, 3: 0.405 |
  | hiking | 3 | 3: 1.099 |
  | waterproof + hiking | 3 | 3: 2.197 |
  | running | 1, 2 | 1: 0.405, 2: 0.405 |

- **Verdicts (step 4):**
  - `Found: product 3 matches every word` (one product in AND; the digit is the product number; 35 characters; good)
  - `2 products have every word` (two products; good)
  - `No product has every word` (empty AND with a word chosen; warn)
  - `Tap words to ask a question` (no word chosen; accent)
  - Steps 1 to 3 show a short caption instead: `Three products, in plain text`, `Words are cleaned and split`, `Now each word lists its products`.
- **Reduced motion:** links appear without drawing; the final step shows the default query result.
- **Accessibility:** each chip is a toggle button with a label such as "waterproof, in 1 product, adds 1.10"; the result is written as text in the banner.
- **Limits shown to the learner:** three tiny products; every word appears once in each product, so only rarity affects the score; real engines compress lists and also use how often a word repeats and how long the text is.
- **Sources:** Lucene term dictionary and posting lists [S100]; arithmetic and code in chapter 23.2.

## sysdesign-m23-trie-walk

- **Chapter and beat:** 23.3 beat 3, "Keep the best three at every node".
- **Claim:** If every node of a trie stores its best three queries ahead of time, a suggestion costs one step per letter typed and reads nothing below the node.
- **Primitives:** a custom canvas on `BeatFrame` with letter keys as `svg-pill-button.tsx` or `svg-chip.tsx` (44px targets), the tree drawn with `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`. Model in `models/trie-walk.ts` with a unit test that builds the trie with the chapter's code and compares every prefix.
- **Input:** tap letter keys to type (keys: s, h, o, e, r, w, a, t, i, u, c, l, space, and a backspace key). Only letters that have a child at the current node are tinted as "next"; any other key is still tappable and leads to the empty answer. Reset on the second control. Default: empty box.
- **Data:** nine made-up queries with counts: shoes 9200, shirt 7000, watch 5200, shorts 4100, shoes red 3100, sheets 2600, shower curtain 1800, shoulder bag 1500, shoelaces 900. The trie has 50 nodes (71 characters, shared prefixes).
- **Model:** the typed text walks from the root one letter at a time. At each node the best three queries by count are stored. The answer is the stored list of the node reached. If a letter has no child, the answer is empty and the walk stops.
- **Picture:** the tree on the left, shown only along the typed path plus the next-letter children (dimmed); the current node is ringed; a counter "Steps: n letters, queries read below the node: 0"; a result card on the right listing up to three queries with their counts.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the chapter code and by filtering the list):

  | Typed | Answer (count) |
  |---|---|
  | s | shoes 9200, shirt 7000, shorts 4100 |
  | sh | shoes 9200, shirt 7000, shorts 4100 |
  | she | sheets 2600 |
  | shi | shirt 7000 |
  | sho | shoes 9200, shorts 4100, shoes red 3100 |
  | shoe | shoes 9200, shoes red 3100, shoelaces 900 |
  | shoes | shoes 9200, shoes red 3100 |
  | shoes + space | shoes red 3100 |
  | shor | shorts 4100 |
  | show | shower curtain 1800 |
  | shou | shoulder bag 1500 |
  | w | watch 5200 |
  | wa | watch 5200 |
  | x | none |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Type a letter to walk down` (empty box; accent)
  - `Answer read from 1 node` (a non-empty answer; good)
  - `No query starts with this` (empty answer after letters; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** the walk jumps to the final node with no travelling dot.
- **Accessibility:** each key is a button; the live result is an `aria-live` region reading the typed text and the answers.
- **Limits shown to the learner:** nine queries; k is 3 so the picture fits a phone (Dalil shows up to ten); counts are made up and frozen, whereas a real trie is rebuilt from counts every few minutes.
- **Sources:** Course design; the code and output are in chapter 23.3.

## sysdesign-m23-bloom-size

- **Chapter and beat:** 23.4 beat 2, "Drag the bits and the hashes".
- **Claim:** A Bloom filter's wrong-maybe rate falls as bits per word grow, and for each size there is a best number of hashes. Too few let typos through; too many fill the row.
- **Primitives:** `rail-slider.tsx` and `SliderRail` from `picture-slider.tsx`, two rails inside the picture (only the rails are drag surfaces); curve and row drawn in the canvas. Reuse `models/bloom.ts` from the chapter 7.3 canvas if it exposes `FP = (1 - e^(-k/b))^k`; otherwise add the function to the canvas model. Do not copy the 7.3 picture: this one varies the hash count.
- **Input:** bits per word b, snaps 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20 (default 10); hashes k, every whole number from 1 to 20 (default 7). Arrow keys step, Page keys jump, Home and End go to the ends.
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Wrong-maybe rate FP = (1 - e^(-k / b))^k [S113].
  - Best hashes k* = max(1, round(b x ln 2)); ratio = FP(b, k) / FP(b, k*).
  - Share of bits set = 1 - e^(-k / b).
  - Memory for 100 million words = b x 100,000,000 / 8 bytes.
  - One wrong maybe in round(1 / FP) typos.
- **Picture:** a row of 50 cells for the bits; the first round(share set x 50) cells are filled; the share is written beside it. A curve of FP against k for the chosen b, with a marker at the chosen k and a tick at k*; the rate is written next to the marker. Below, a bar for the memory with its value in MB, and a strip of 100 typo dots where the first round(FP x 100) are amber.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | b | k | FP | Ratio to best | Bits set | Memory (100M words) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10 | 7 (best) | 0.82% | 1.00 | 50.3% | 125 MB |
  | 10 | 1 | 9.52% | 11.61 | 9.5% | 125 MB |
  | 10 | 4 | 1.18% | 1.44 | 32.9% | 125 MB |
  | 10 | 10 | 1.02% | 1.24 | 63.2% | 125 MB |
  | 10 | 14 | 1.90% | 2.32 | 75.3% | 125 MB |
  | 10 | 20 | 5.46% | 6.66 | 86.5% | 125 MB |
  | 5 | 3 (best) | 9.18% | 1.00 | 45.1% | 62.5 MB |
  | 15 | 10 (best) | 0.07% | 1.00 | 48.7% | 187.5 MB |
  | 15 | 1 | 6.45% | 86.69 | 6.4% | 187.5 MB |
  | 20 | 14 (best) | 0.007% | 1.00 | 50.3% | 250 MB |

  Measured with the filter of chapter 23.5 at b = 10 (10,000 words, 200,000 typos): k = 1 gives 9.51%, k = 7 gives 0.86%, k = 20 gives 5.59%.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Too few hashes: typos slip through` (ratio above 1.15 and k below k*; warn)
  - `Too many hashes: the row fills up` (ratio above 1.15 and k above k*; warn)
  - `Near the best hash count, few errors` (ratio 1.15 or less and FP under 1%; good)
  - `Best hashes, but add more bits` (ratio 1.15 or less and FP from 1% to under 5%; warn)
  - `Best hashes, still too many errors` (ratio 1.15 or less and FP 5% or more; bad)
- **Reduced motion:** the ring on the typo dots does not walk; values show their final state.
- **Accessibility:** each rail is a `role="slider"` whose value text gives the bits, the hashes, the rate, the share of bits set, the memory and the verdict.
- **Limits shown to the learner:** the formula assumes independent hashes and a filter filled with exactly the planned number of words; real filters add some overhead, so the memory is a floor; a Bloom filter cannot remove a word.
- **Sources:** [S113] for the formula and the best k; the sweep is measured with the chapter 23.5 code.

## sysdesign-m23-hll-registers

- **Chapter and beat:** 23.4 beat 4, "HyperLogLog counts different shoppers".
- **Claim:** HyperLogLog counts different items with a fixed, tiny memory, and its error shrinks as the square root of the number of registers; 12 KB gives under 1% for 50 million shoppers.
- **Primitives:** `rail-slider.tsx` with `SliderRail` for one rail inside the picture; a number line with a band drawn in the canvas.
- **Input:** the number of registers m, snaps 16, 64, 256, 1,024, 4,096, 16,384 (powers of 2 from 2^4 to 2^14; default 1,024). Arrow keys step, Home and End go to the ends.
- **Model and formulas:**
  - True distinct shoppers N = 50,000,000 (fixed, from chapter 23.1).
  - Standard error s = 1.04 / sqrt(m) [S114].
  - Band of one standard error = N x (1 - s) to N x (1 + s); band of two = N x (1 - 2s) to N x (1 + 2s).
  - Memory = 6 bits x m / 8 bytes (Redis uses 16,384 registers of 6 bits, 12 KB, 0.81% [S115]).
  - Exact set = 50,000,000 x 8 bytes = 400 MB.
- **Picture:** a number line from 0 to 100 million with a marker at 50 million; a dark band for one standard error and a light band for two; the percentage written beside the band; two bars at the bottom: the HyperLogLog memory and the exact 400 MB, with the ratio written ("1 / 32,552 of the exact memory" at 12 KB).
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Registers | Memory | Standard error | One-error band (millions) | Two-error band (millions) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 16 | 12 B | 26.00% | 37.00 to 63.00 | 24.00 to 76.00 |
  | 64 | 48 B | 13.00% | 43.50 to 56.50 | 37.00 to 63.00 |
  | 256 | 192 B | 6.50% | 46.75 to 53.25 | 43.50 to 56.50 |
  | 1,024 | 768 B | 3.25% | 48.38 to 51.63 | 46.75 to 53.25 |
  | 4,096 | 3,072 B (3 KB) | 1.63% | 49.19 to 50.81 | 48.38 to 51.63 |
  | 16,384 | 12,288 B (12 KB) | 0.81% | 49.59 to 50.41 | 49.19 to 50.81 |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Too rough to trust` (s above 5%; bad)
  - `Fine for a dashboard` (s from 1% to 5%; warn)
  - `Under 1% error in 12 KB` (s under 1%; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the bands do not grow when the rail moves; they jump to their size.
- **Accessibility:** the rail is a `role="slider"` whose value text gives the registers, the memory, the error and the verdict.
- **Limits shown to the learner:** the error is a typical (standard) error, so a single count can be outside the band; HyperLogLog cannot list shoppers or remove one; the formula holds for large counts, and tiny counts use a built-in correction.
- **Sources:** [S114] for 1.04 / sqrt(m) and about 2% with 1.5 KB; [S115] for Redis (12 KB, 0.81%).
