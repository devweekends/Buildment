# Module 24 playable specs (geospatial search and ride matching)

Six reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All six sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The twelve watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m24-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same names (Safar, Driver map, Dispatch) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with code (scratch scripts, formulas shown). All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time. Course numbers shared by the module, defined in chapter 24.1 unless noted: 1 million drivers online at the busiest time; a location report every 4 seconds; 50 bytes per report; 100 bytes to hold one driver's latest place; 10 million rides a day; peak 5 times the average; 200,000 riders with the app open, map refresh every 5 seconds. Defined in chapter 24.3: a database node is planned at 2,500 writes a second (the course number from chapter 20.2); a memory node is planned at 50,000 updates a second (illustrative). Geohash box sizes use 111,195 m per degree at the equator (geohash letter p has ceil(5p/2) longitude bits and floor(5p/2) latitude bits).

## sysdesign-m24-estimate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 24.1 beat 2, "Drag the crowd, find the load".
- **Claim:** For a ride service the stream of location reports is the load. Searches matter less and ride requests are tiny. The ringed card is the number that drives the design.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links, some cards are inputs: tap, then drag sideways or use arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** three draggable cards. Drivers online: 100K, 250K, 500K, 1M, 2M (default 1M). Seconds between reports: 1, 2, 4, 8, 15 (default 4). Riders with the app open: 50K, 100K, 200K, 500K (default 200K). Fixed cards labelled "assumption": 5 s map refresh, 50 B per report, 100 B per driver, 10 million rides a day, peak factor 5.
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Reports a second = drivers / seconds between reports.
  - Bandwidth in = reports a second x 50 B.
  - Searches a second = riders / 5.
  - Ride requests a second at peak = 10,000,000 / 86,400 x 5 (about 579, fixed).
  - Latest places in memory = drivers x 100 B.
- **Picture:** four chains. Upper: Drivers online > (divide by seconds between reports) > Reports a second > (x 50 B) > Bandwidth in. Second: Riders with app open > (divide by 5 s) > Searches a second. Third: Rides a day > (divide by 86,400, x 5 peak) > Ride requests a second at peak. Fourth: Drivers online > (x 100 B) > Latest places in memory. Dots flow along links at a speed set by the value. Formats: `250,000 /s`, `12.5 MB/s`, `40,000 /s`, `580 /s`, `100 MB`. Ring (tone warn) on the driver card: Reports when reports are 25,000 or more and at least the searches; Searches when searches are larger and 10,000 or more; none otherwise.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above; searches at 200K riders unless noted):

  | Drivers | Gap (s) | Riders | Reports /s | MB/s in | Searches /s | Memory |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1M | 4 | 200K | 250,000 | 12.5 | 40,000 | 100 MB |
  | 100K | 4 | 200K | 25,000 | 1.25 | 40,000 | 10 MB |
  | 2M | 4 | 200K | 500,000 | 25 | 40,000 | 200 MB |
  | 1M | 1 | 200K | 1,000,000 | 50 | 40,000 | 100 MB |
  | 1M | 15 | 200K | 66,667 | 3.3 | 40,000 | 100 MB |
  | 250K | 8 | 50K | 31,250 | 1.56 | 10,000 | 25 MB |
  | 1M | 4 | 500K | 250,000 | 12.5 | 100,000 | 100 MB |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Updates drive it: keep them in memory` (reports 25,000 or more and at least the searches; warn)
  - `Searches drive it: cache the map` (searches larger than reports and 10,000 or more; warn)
  - `One database could serve this` (reports and searches both under 2,500; good)
  - `Busy, but a few servers can do it` (otherwise; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the cards show their final values.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "1 million drivers, a report every 4 seconds, 250,000 reports a second"; the ring is also written as text in the verdict.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions (chapter 2.3 for the method). No external source.

## sysdesign-m24-geohash-zoom

- **Chapter and beat:** 24.2 beat 2, "A short code for every box".
- **Claim:** A geohash names a box. Each added letter picks one of 32 sub-boxes, so a longer code names a smaller box inside the bigger one.
- **Primitives:** a thin custom canvas on `BeatFrame` (a tappable grid in the picture). Closest reusable primitive: `design-stepper.tsx` for the Back and Reset controls and the step count. Two controls outside the picture: Back (one letter), Reset.
- **Input:** tap a sub-box to zoom into it (or move with the arrow keys and press Enter). Start at the whole world (0 letters); stop at 7 letters (the last tap is disabled). The default state is the world, with the grid of the first letter shown.
- **Model:**
  - Odd letters (1, 3, 5, 7) cut the current box into 8 columns by 4 rows (3 longitude bits and 2 latitude bits). Even letters (2, 4, 6) cut it into 4 columns by 8 rows. North at the top, west at the left.
  - Letters for an odd level (rows from north to south): `b c f g u v y z` / `8 9 d e s t w x` / `2 3 6 7 k m q r` / `0 1 4 5 h j n p`.
  - Letters for an even level: columns west to east, rows north to south: `p r x z` / `n q w y` / `j m t v` / `h k s u` / `5 7 e g` / `4 6 d f` / `1 3 9 c` / `0 2 8 b`.
  - Box size at the equator: 1 letter 5,004 km x 5,004 km; 2 letters 1,251 km x 625 km; 3 letters 156 km x 156 km; 4 letters 39.1 km x 19.5 km; 5 letters 4.89 km x 4.89 km; 6 letters 1.22 km x 0.61 km; 7 letters 153 m x 153 m.
- **Picture:** the current box fills the picture as a grid of 32 tappable cells, each labelled with its next letter. Above it, the code so far as letter chips (the last one accented), and a line `Box: 1.2 km by 0.6 km` that uses the sizes above. A small inset shows the chain of boxes (each smaller one drawn inside the last).
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Longer code, smaller box` (after any zoom; good)
  - `Same start, same neighbourhood` (shown when the learner goes Back one letter and picks a different sub-box; accent)
- **Reduced motion:** zooming jumps without animation.
- **Accessibility:** each cell is a button with a name such as "letter s, zoom in"; the code chips and the size line are live text.
- **Sources:** Geohash alphabet, 5 bits per letter and box sizes: Wikipedia "Geohash", read 2026-10-24 (source needed in the dossier); the grids and sizes were computed with the module's own code.

## sysdesign-m24-cell-edge

- **Chapter and beat:** 24.2 beat 3, "Close drivers can have different codes".
- **Claim:** A driver 30 m away can sit in another box. Reading only the rider's box misses nearby drivers; the rings needed grow with the radius and shrink with the box size.
- **Primitives:** custom canvas on `BeatFrame`; `picture-slider.tsx` for the draggable rider; `radio-chip-row.tsx` for the letters and the radius; one segmented control for the search mode. Controls outside the picture: none beyond the chip rows (two rows: letters 5, 6, 7; radius 0.5, 1, 2 km), the mode is a chip row inside the picture.
- **World (metres; the equator and the 0 degree meridian run through the origin, so their lines are edges at every letter count):**
  - Rider starts at (-15, 10). Draggable anywhere within its box.
  - D1 (15, 10), 30 m east, across the meridian edge. D2 (-15, 410). D3 (-15, 1590). D4 (700, 10). D5 (-15, 2610).
  - Box width and height in metres: 5 letters 4,887 x 4,887; 6 letters 1,222 x 611; 7 letters 153 x 153. Boxes are rows and columns of these sizes with an edge at x = 0 and y = 0.
- **Model:** the search circle has the chosen radius around the rider. Modes: Own box only (0 rings), Nine boxes (1 ring), Rings to reach (k = max(ceil(radius / width), ceil(radius / height)) rings, so (2k + 1) squared boxes). A driver inside the circle is found when its box is within k rings of the rider's box (box column and row differ by at most k), otherwise missed. Boxes read, area read (boxes x width x height) and drivers scanned (area in km² x 100 drivers per km²) are shown.
- **Picture:** a 5 x 5 patch of boxes (scaled to the letters), box edges drawn, the circle, the rider as a draggable dot, the five drivers as dots (green found, red ring missed, grey outside the circle). The boxes read are shaded. Beside the picture, a line `9 boxes read, 21,500 drivers to measure`.
- **Numbers to check** (rider at the start point (-15, 10)):

  | Letters | Radius | Rings to reach | Own box | Nine boxes | Rings to reach |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5 | 1 km | 1 | misses D1, D4 | finds D1, D2, D4 | finds D1, D2, D4 (9 boxes) |
  | 6 | 0.5 km | 1 | misses D1 | finds D1, D2 | finds D1, D2 (9 boxes) |
  | 6 | 1 km | 2 | misses D1, D4 | finds D1, D2, D4 | finds D1, D2, D4 (25 boxes) |
  | 7 | 0.5 km | 4 | misses D1, D2 | finds D1, misses D2 | finds D1, D2 (81 boxes) |

  Rider dragged north to (-15, 600), which is the top of its 6-letter box:

  | Letters | Radius | Rings to reach | Nine boxes | Rings to reach |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 6 | 1 km | 2 | finds D1, D2, D4, misses D3 (990 m) | finds all four (25 boxes) |
  | 6 | 2 km | 4 | misses D3 | finds all four (81 boxes) |
  | 5 | 1 km | 1 | finds all four (9 boxes) | finds all four (9 boxes) |

  Cost at 100 drivers per km² (rings to reach): 5 letters, 1 km, 9 boxes, 215 km², 21,500 drivers; 6 letters, 1 km, 25 boxes, 18.7 km², 1,870 drivers; 7 letters, 1 km, 225 boxes, 5.3 km², 530 drivers.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Missed a driver across the edge` (any driver inside the circle is outside the read boxes; bad)
  - `All drivers in range were found` (every driver inside the circle is in the read boxes; good)
  - `Found, but a very big box to scan` (all found and more than 5,000 drivers to measure; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** the rider moves with no trail.
- **Accessibility:** the rider is a `role="slider"` with arrow-key steps of 50 m; each driver has a text label (D1 found, 30 m).
- **Sources:** Arithmetic and geohash code from the module (scratch folder). Box sizes: Wikipedia "Geohash" (source needed in the dossier).

## sysdesign-m24-quadtree-split

- **Chapter and beat:** 24.2 beat 5, "Crowded places need smaller boxes".
- **Claim:** A quadtree cuts only the crowded box into four again and again until no box holds too many drivers. Quiet places keep big boxes. A fixed grid puts the whole crowd in one box.
- **Primitives:** `rail-slider.tsx` or `snap-stops-rail.tsx` for the crowd size, `radio-chip-row.tsx` for the limit, custom drawing for the tree. Controls: crowd size (snap points) and limit chips (100, 500).
- **Input:** crowd in the stadium: 0, 100, 500, 1,000, 3,000, 6,000 drivers (default 3,000). Limit per box: 100 or 500 (default 500).
- **Model:** the city holds 40 drivers spread evenly. The stadium crowd is spread evenly inside one square that is 1/16 of the city (a level-2 box). Start with one box. If a box holds more than the limit, cut it into four equal boxes and divide the drivers by area, repeating down the tree (at most 12 levels). The hot square is the first quadrant at levels 1 and 2. Count the boxes (leaves), the most drivers in one box and the deepest level.
- **Picture:** the square city with the hot square shaded, the quadtree boxes drawn, and each box's count shown only for the fullest box. Two stat chips: `Fixed grid: fullest box 3,040` (crowd + 40 for a 3,000 crowd, if no quadtree) and `Quadtree: 22 boxes, fullest 188`.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the model):

  | Crowd | Limit 500: boxes, fullest, levels | Limit 100: boxes, fullest, levels |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | 1, 40, 0 | 1, 40, 0 |
  | 100 | 1, 140, 0 | 10, 26, 3 |
  | 500 | 10, 126, 3 | 22, 31, 4 |
  | 1,000 | 10, 251, 3 | 22, 63, 4 |
  | 3,000 | 22, 188, 4 | 70, 47, 5 |
  | 6,000 | 22, 375, 4 | 70, 94, 5 |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Quiet city: one box is enough` (crowd 0)
  - `Dense places split deeper` (otherwise; good)
  - `A fixed grid would hold it all` (shown with the fixed-grid chip when the crowd is 1,000 or more; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** boxes appear without the cutting animation.
- **Accessibility:** the crowd slider is a `role="slider"` with value text "3,000 drivers in the stadium, 22 boxes, fullest 188".
- **Limits fold text:** the real crowd is not even, drivers move, and a real quadtree is rebuilt as they move.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions; the module's own model script. A flat scan of 3,000 drivers took about 0.1 ms in plain JavaScript on one laptop (module benchmark, chapter 24.2).

## sysdesign-m24-update-load

- **Chapter and beat:** 24.3 beat 3, "Move the dials, see the load".
- **Claim:** The same stream of location reports needs about 100 disk database nodes or about 5 memory nodes. Halving the gap between reports doubles the load.
- **Primitives:** `traffic-playground.tsx` (dots from a source into a store) with `flow-pulse.tsx` for the dots, `radio-chip-row.tsx` for the store toggle; two sliders. Controls: drivers (snap points), seconds between reports (snap points), store toggle (inside the picture).
- **Input:** drivers online: 100K, 250K, 500K, 1M, 2M (default 1M). Seconds between reports: 1, 2, 4, 8, 15 (default 4). Store: Disk database or Memory map (default Disk database).
- **Model:**
  - Reports a second = drivers / seconds.
  - Disk nodes = ceil(reports / 2,500). Memory nodes = ceil(reports / 50,000).
- **Picture:** a phone group on the left sends dots (speed and count follow the rate) into a store block on the right. The store block shows a row of node icons (up to 20, then `+ n more`) and the count. Numbers on the picture: `250,000 /s`, `100 nodes`.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Drivers | Gap (s) | Reports /s | Disk nodes | Memory nodes |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 100K | 4 | 25,000 | 10 | 1 |
  | 250K | 4 | 62,500 | 25 | 2 |
  | 500K | 4 | 125,000 | 50 | 3 |
  | 1M | 4 | 250,000 | 100 | 5 |
  | 1M | 1 | 1,000,000 | 400 | 20 |
  | 1M | 2 | 500,000 | 200 | 10 |
  | 1M | 8 | 125,000 | 50 | 3 |
  | 1M | 15 | 66,667 | 27 | 2 |
  | 2M | 4 | 500,000 | 200 | 10 |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `100 disk nodes for old data` (disk and 10 or more nodes; the number follows the model, so `27 disk nodes for old data`; bad)
  - `Disk handles it, at a high price` (disk and fewer than 10 nodes; warn)
  - `Memory: 5 nodes carry it` (memory and 10 or fewer nodes; good)
  - `Even memory needs 20 nodes` (memory and more than 10 nodes; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** dots are static; the node count updates.
- **Accessibility:** the two sliders are `role="slider"` with value text; the node count is also live text.
- **Limits fold text:** no replicas, failures or search load; node capacities are course numbers (2,500 from chapter 20.2, 50,000 illustrative).
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions.

## sysdesign-m24-batch-match

- **Chapter and beat:** 24.4 beat 5, "Match together, not one by one".
- **Claim:** Giving each rider the nearest free driver in turn can leave the next rider a far driver. Matching two riders together can pick a shorter total.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` (drag Driver 1 along a road) plus a two-way toggle (One by one, Together) outside the picture. Controls: the toggle and a reset-to-0.4 chip.
- **World (kilometres along a straight road):** Rider A at 0. Rider B at 1.0. Driver 2 at -0.5. Driver 1 draggable between 0.1 and 0.9 in steps of 0.1 (default 0.4). Speed for the time line: 30 km/h.
- **Model:**
  - One by one: A takes the nearer of the two drivers (a tie goes to Driver 1); B gets the other. Total = distance A to that driver plus distance B to the other.
  - Together: total = the smaller of (A to D1 + B to D2) and (A to D2 + B to D1).
  - Saved = one by one minus together.
- **Picture:** a horizontal road with A and B as rider icons and the two drivers as cars. Pairing lines are drawn for the chosen mode with the distance on each, and a total chip. In One by one mode a numbered A then B step order is shown. A small chip shows the saving in minutes: saved km / 30 x 60 (for example `0.8 km saved, 1.6 min`).
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Driver 1 at | One by one | Together | Saved |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.1 | 1.6 km | 1.4 km | 0.2 km |
  | 0.2 | 1.7 km | 1.3 km | 0.4 km |
  | 0.3 | 1.8 km | 1.2 km | 0.6 km |
  | 0.4 | 1.9 km | 1.1 km | 0.8 km |
  | 0.5 | 2.0 km | 1.0 km | 1.0 km |
  | 0.6 | 0.9 km | 0.9 km | 0 |
  | 0.7 | 0.8 km | 0.8 km | 0 |
  | 0.8 | 0.7 km | 0.7 km | 0 |
  | 0.9 | 0.6 km | 0.6 km | 0 |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `One by one wastes 0.8 km` (One by one and saved above 0; the number follows the model; bad)
  - `Together: 1.1 km in total` (Together mode; good)
  - `Same pairing either way` (saved is 0; good)
- **Reduced motion:** cars jump to their pairings.
- **Accessibility:** Driver 1 is a `role="slider"` with value text "Driver 1 at 0.4 km, one by one 1.9 km, together 1.1 km".
- **Limits fold text:** two riders and two drivers on a line; real roads are not straight lines, and a window of thousands of riders needs a proper assignment method.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on a course example (scratch script).
