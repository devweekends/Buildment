# Module 26 playable specs (web crawler)

Five reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All five sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The thirteen watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m26-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same names (Dalil, DalilBot, Frontier, Fetcher) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with code (the formulas are short and shown). All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time. Course numbers shared by all five, defined in chapters 26.1 and 26.2: DalilBot fetches 2 billion pages a month; a month is 2,592,000 seconds; a page is 100 KB of HTML; a page has 50 links; one fetch takes 0.5 s with a cold DNS lookup and 0.4 s with a DNS cache (100 ms cold lookup, 0.5 ms cached, 80 ms per round trip, S3); pages are compressed 4 to 1 for storage; the default wait between requests to one site is 1 second; a fetcher machine holds 100 downloads in flight and is planned at half its limit; the seen set holds 20 billion URLs.

## sysdesign-m26-estimate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 26.1 beat 2, "Drag the volume, find the load".
- **Claim:** For a polite crawler, bandwidth and machines are spread out and easy. The number that drives the design is how many different sites must have work ready at the same moment, which is pages a second times the wait between requests to one site.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links, some cards are inputs: tap, then drag sideways or use arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** three draggable cards. Pages per month: 0.5 billion, 1 billion, 2 billion, 5 billion, 10 billion (default 2 billion). Page size: 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 KB (default 100). Wait between requests to one site: 0.5, 1, 2, 5 seconds (default 1). The time for one fetch (0.5 s), links per page (50) and the storage saving (4 to 1) are fixed cards labelled "assumption".
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Pages a second = pages per month / 2,592,000.
  - Data in = pages a second x page size x 8 bits (Mbit/s, with 1 KB = 1,000 bytes).
  - Sites busy at once = pages a second x wait.
  - Fetches in flight = pages a second x 0.5 (Little's law).
  - Link checks a second = pages a second x 50.
  - Stored a month (TB) = pages per month x page size / 4 / 1,000,000,000 (KB to TB).
- **Picture:** two chains that start at the same card. Upper chain: Pages per month > (divide by 2,592,000 s) Pages a second > (x wait) Sites busy at once. Lower chain: Pages a second > (x page size, x 8) Data in; Pages a second > (x 0.5 s) In flight; Pages a second > (x 50 links) Link checks a second; Pages per month > (x size, / 4) Stored a month. Dots flow along the links at a speed set by the value. Format: `772 /s`, `617 Mbit/s`, `772 sites`, `386`, `38,580 /s`, `50 TB`. Ring (tone warn) on the driver card, chosen in this order: Data in when it is 5,000 Mbit/s or more; otherwise Sites busy at once when it is 100 or more; otherwise Stored a month when it is 20 TB or more; otherwise none.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above):

  | Pages | Size | Wait | Pages/s | Data in | Sites at once | In flight | Link checks/s | Stored a month | Ring |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2B | 100 KB | 1 s | 771.6 | 617 Mbit/s | 772 | 386 | 38,580 | 50 TB | Sites at once |
  | 0.5B | 100 KB | 1 s | 192.9 | 154 Mbit/s | 193 | 96 | 9,645 | 12.5 TB | Sites at once |
  | 10B | 100 KB | 1 s | 3,858 | 3,086 Mbit/s | 3,858 | 1,929 | 192,901 | 250 TB | Sites at once |
  | 2B | 500 KB | 1 s | 771.6 | 3,086 Mbit/s | 772 | 386 | 38,580 | 250 TB | Sites at once |
  | 10B | 500 KB | 1 s | 3,858 | 15,432 Mbit/s | 3,858 | 1,929 | 192,901 | 1,250 TB | Data in |
  | 2B | 100 KB | 5 s | 771.6 | 617 Mbit/s | 3,858 | 386 | 38,580 | 50 TB | Sites at once |
  | 2B | 100 KB | 0.5 s | 771.6 | 617 Mbit/s | 386 | 386 | 38,580 | 50 TB | Sites at once |
  | 0.5B | 20 KB | 0.5 s | 192.9 | 31 Mbit/s | 96 | 96 | 9,645 | 2.5 TB | none |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Bandwidth drives it: spread the load` (ring on Data in; warn)
  - `Politeness: need many sites at once` (ring on Sites busy at once; warn)
  - `Storage drives it: plan the disks` (ring on Stored a month; warn)
  - `One machine could crawl this` (no ring; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the cards show their final values.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "2 billion pages a month, 772 pages a second, 772 sites busy at once"; the ring is also written as text in the verdict.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions (chapter 2.3 for the method, chapter 2.5 for Little's law). No external source.

## sysdesign-m26-design-stepper

- **Chapter and beat:** 26.2 beat 3, "Step through the design at peak".
- **Claim:** The numbers decide the blocks. A fetcher that waits for each page is hundreds of times too slow; many downloads in flight make a small fleet; a DNS cache trims it; the frontier and the seen check are split by host so each shard holds a share of the memory and the work.
- **Primitives:** `design-stepper.tsx` with a `DesignSpec` (`models/design-stepper.ts`): five steps, Back and Next under the picture, step chips on top, one choice in step 4 (shard count, a row of three chips inside the picture). `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** Back and Next (or tap a step chip). In step 4 only: frontier shards 4, 8 or 16 (default 8).
- **Model:** the target is 2 billion pages a month = 771.6 pages a second. Steps:
  1. One fetcher: waits for each page, so 1 / 0.5 s = 2 pages a second.
  2. Many downloads in flight: a machine holds 100, finishes at most 100 / 0.5 = 200 pages a second and is planned at half, 100. Machines = ceil(771.6 / 100) = 8.
  3. Add a DNS cache: a fetch takes 0.4 s, so a machine finishes at most 250 and is planned at 125. Machines = ceil(771.6 / 125) = 7.
  4. Split the frontier and the seen check by a hash of the host into k shards. The seen set is a Bloom filter for 20 billion URLs at 1% error, 24 GB in all, and link checks are 38,580 a second in all. Each shard holds 24 / k GB and answers 38,580 / k checks a second.
  5. Store the pages: 771.6 pages a second x 25 KB compressed = 19.3 MB/s written, and 50 TB a month stored.
- **Picture:** left to right: Frontier shards (x k, from step 4) > Fetchers (x N) > DNS cache (from step 3) > Web. A pill on the fetcher card shows "2 pages/s" (step 1), "8 machines" (step 2), "7 machines" (step 3). A pill on each shard shows its memory and checks (step 4). In step 5 a Storage card appears with "50 TB a month". Requests run as dots; in step 3 the dots that used to stop at a Resolver card turn back at the cache.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Step | Result |
  |---|---|
  | 1 | 2 pages/s, 386 times too slow |
  | 2 | 8 machines |
  | 3 | 7 machines |
  | 4, k = 4 | 6 GB and 9,645 checks/s each |
  | 4, k = 8 | 3 GB and 4,823 checks/s each |
  | 4, k = 16 | 1.5 GB and 2,411 checks/s each |
  | 5 | 19.3 MB/s written, 50 TB a month |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `One fetcher: 386 times too slow` (step 1; bad)
  - `8 machines carry 772 pages a second` (step 2; good)
  - `DNS cache: 7 machines, fewer lookups` (step 3; good)
  - `4 shards: 6 GB and 9,600 checks each` (step 4, k = 4; warn)
  - `8 shards: 3 GB and 4,800 checks each` (step 4, k = 8; good)
  - `16 shards: 1.5 GB and 2,400 each` (step 4, k = 16; good; it says nothing about the cost of more nodes, the chapter fold does)
  - `Pages to object storage: 50 TB/month` (step 5; good)
- **Reduced motion:** dots are replaced by arrows with counts; the final frame of each step shows its pill.
- **Accessibility:** step chips and shard chips are buttons with visible labels; the pills are read out as text.
- **Sources:** Course numbers (chapter 26.2 fold). Little's law and planning at half load: chapter 2.5. Round trip of 80 ms and cache query of 0.5 ms: S3. TLS 1.3 in one round trip: S11. The Bloom filter size is chapter 26.4.

## sysdesign-m26-host-rate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 26.3 beat 3, "Sites in play set the speed". A predict ("20 sites, 800 slots, 1 second: how many pages a second?") sits right before it.
- **Claim:** The crawl speed is the smaller of two limits: sites with work waiting divided by the wait, and download slots divided by the fetch time. With few sites, extra slots do nothing. A big site at one request a second takes weeks.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` (log rail inside the picture) for the number of sites, `snap-stops-rail.tsx` or `rail-slider.tsx` (inside the picture) for the wait, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle. A custom picture: a column of site lanes on the left and a Fetch slots card on the right.
- **Input:** sites with work waiting on a log rail with snap points 10, 20, 50, 100, 300, 772, 1,000, 3,000, 10,000 (default 20). Wait between requests to one site: 0.5, 1, 2, 5 seconds (default 1). Arrow keys step one snap on each. Fixed: 800 download slots, 0.4 s per fetch (capacity 2,000 pages a second), a big site of 1,000,000 pages.
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Politeness limit = sites / wait (pages a second).
  - Capacity limit = 800 / 0.4 = 2,000.
  - Speed = min(politeness limit, capacity limit). Limit in force = whichever is smaller (both when equal).
  - Sites needed for 772 pages a second = 771.6 x wait.
  - Days to finish the big site = 1,000,000 x wait / 86,400.
- **Picture:** up to 24 lane icons drawn for the sites (a "x N" label gives the true number), each releasing a dot at most once per wait; the dots flow into the Fetch slots card, whose fill shows the speed against 2,000. Two readouts: "Speed: N pages/s (limit: sites | slots | both)" and "Needed for 772/s: N sites". A small card at the bottom: "One site of 1 million pages: N days". A ring (warn) on the limiting card.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Sites | Wait | Speed | Limit | Sites needed for 772/s | Big site |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 20 | 1 s | 20/s | sites | 772 | 11.6 days |
  | 100 | 1 s | 100/s | sites | 772 | 11.6 days |
  | 772 | 1 s | 772/s | sites | 772 | 11.6 days |
  | 1,000 | 1 s | 1,000/s | sites | 772 | 11.6 days |
  | 3,000 | 1 s | 2,000/s | slots | 772 | 11.6 days |
  | 1,000 | 5 s | 200/s | sites | 3,858 | 57.9 days |
  | 772 | 5 s | 154/s | sites | 3,858 | 57.9 days |
  | 1,000 | 0.5 s | 2,000/s | both | 386 | 5.8 days |
  | 20 | 2 s | 10/s | sites | 1,543 | 23.1 days |
  | 10,000 | 5 s | 2,000/s | both | 3,858 | 57.9 days |

- **Verdicts** (N is the speed, rounded to a whole number with a thousands comma):
  - `Too few sites: N pages a second` (speed under 386; bad)
  - `Short of 772: N pages a second` (speed from 386 to 771; warn)
  - `Enough sites: N pages a second` (speed 772 or more and the limit is sites; good)
  - `Slots are the limit: 2,000 a second` (the limit is slots or both; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the fill and readouts show final values.
- **Accessibility:** each rail is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "20 sites, 1 second wait, 20 pages a second, limited by sites"; the verdict repeats it.
- **Sources:** Course numbers (chapters 26.1 to 26.3). Little's law: chapter 2.5.

## sysdesign-m26-bloom-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 26.4 beat 2, "Drag the error, watch the memory". A predict ("20 billion URLs: how much memory does a 1% Bloom filter need?") sits right before it.
- **Claim:** A Bloom filter holds the seen set in a small fraction of the memory of exact fingerprints, at the price of a small share of new URLs wrongly skipped. Each 10 times lower error costs about 4.8 more bits per URL.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links; input cards are tapped then dragged or moved with arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** two draggable cards. URLs known: 1 billion, 5 billion, 20 billion, 50 billion, 100 billion (default 20 billion). Error rate: 10%, 1%, 0.1%, 0.01% (default 1%). Fixed cards: exact fingerprint 8 bytes; average URL 100 bytes.
- **Model and formulas** (S113):
  - Bits per URL = -ln(p) / (ln 2)^2.
  - Hash functions k = round(bits per URL x ln 2).
  - Bloom filter memory (GB) = URLs x bits per URL / 8 / 1,000,000,000.
  - Exact fingerprints (GB) = URLs x 8 / 1,000,000,000.
  - URL strings (TB) = URLs x 100 / 1,000,000,000,000.
  - New URLs wrongly skipped per billion new = p x 1,000,000,000.
- **Picture:** a chain from URLs known through "bits per URL" and "hash functions" to Bloom filter memory. Beside it, a bar chart on one scale of three bars: Bloom filter, exact fingerprints, URL strings; the Bloom bar is ringed. Format: `9.6 bits`, `7 hashes`, `24 GB`, `160 GB`, `2 TB`, `10 million skipped per billion`.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | URLs | Error | Bits per URL | k | Bloom | Exact | URL strings | Skipped per billion |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 20B | 1% | 9.59 | 7 | 24.0 GB | 160 GB | 2 TB | 10 million |
  | 20B | 10% | 4.79 | 3 | 12.0 GB | 160 GB | 2 TB | 100 million |
  | 20B | 0.1% | 14.38 | 10 | 35.9 GB | 160 GB | 2 TB | 1 million |
  | 20B | 0.01% | 19.17 | 13 | 47.9 GB | 160 GB | 2 TB | 100,000 |
  | 1B | 1% | 9.59 | 7 | 1.2 GB | 8 GB | 0.1 TB | 10 million |
  | 100B | 1% | 9.59 | 7 | 119.8 GB | 800 GB | 10 TB | 10 million |
  | 100B | 0.01% | 19.17 | 13 | 239.6 GB | 800 GB | 10 TB | 100,000 |

- **Verdicts** (B is the Bloom memory and E the exact memory, each with one decimal under 10 GB and none above, "GB" always shown):
  - `Small, but 1 in 10 new URLs lost` (error 10%; warn)
  - `Bloom: B GB instead of E GB` (error 1%; good)
  - `B GB, only 1 in 1,000 URLs lost` (error 0.1%; good)
  - `B GB, 1 in 10,000 URLs lost` (error 0.01%; good)
- **Reduced motion:** no animation; bars and numbers update at once.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "20 billion URLs, 1 percent error, 24 gigabytes for a Bloom filter against 160 for exact fingerprints".
- **Sources:** S113 for the formulas; chapter 7.3 for the small filter; a measured run (chapter 26.4 fold) gives 10.10%, 1.01% and 0.096% on 1 million URLs against targets of 10%, 1% and 0.1%.

## sysdesign-m26-recrawl-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 26.3 beat 6, "When to visit a page again".
- **Claim:** Visiting a page as often as it changes keeps it fresh without wasting fetches. Too often, and most visits find nothing new. Too rarely, and changes pile up unseen. Both depend only on change rate times the gap between visits.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` or `snap-stops-rail.tsx` (inside the picture) for the gap, `svg-chip.tsx` or `radio-chip-row.tsx` (inside the picture) for the page kind, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** page kind, three chips: News front page (changes 6 times a day), Product page (once a week), Old article (once in 90 days); default News. Visit gap on a snap rail: 1 hour, 4 hours, 12 hours, 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 14 days, 30 days, 90 days (default 1 day).
- **Model and formulas** (c = changes a day: 6, 1/7, 1/90; T = gap in days): with changes modelled as random events at an average rate c,
  - Fresh share of time = (1 - e^(-cT)) / (cT).
  - Wasted share of visits = e^(-cT).
  - Visits a page a month = 30 / T. Visits a day for 10 million pages = 10,000,000 / T.
- **Picture:** a time strip with the page's changes marked as ticks (spaced at the average rate, evenly, so the picture is deterministic) and the visits marked below at the chosen gap. Two bars: "Fresh" and "Wasted visits", each with its percentage. A readout: "Visits a day for 10 million pages: N".
- **Numbers to check** (fresh / wasted, rounded to whole percent):

  | Kind | 1 hour | 4 hours | 12 hours | 1 day | 3 days | 7 days | 14 days | 30 days | 90 days |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | News (c = 6) | 88 / 78 | 63 / 37 | 32 / 5 | 17 / 0 | 6 / 0 | 2 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
  | Product (c = 1/7) | 100 / 99 | 99 / 98 | 97 / 93 | 93 / 87 | 81 / 65 | 63 / 37 | 43 / 14 | 23 / 1 | 8 / 0 |
  | Old article (c = 1/90) | 100 / 100 | 100 / 100 | 100 / 99 | 99 / 99 | 98 / 97 | 96 / 93 | 93 / 86 | 85 / 72 | 63 / 37 |

  Visits a day for 10 million pages: 240 million at 1 hour, 60 million at 4 hours, 20 million at 12 hours, 10 million at 1 day, 3.3 million at 3 days, 1.4 million at 7 days, 0.7 million at 14 days, 0.33 million at 30 days, 0.11 million at 90 days.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Most visits find nothing new` (wasted share above 70%; warn)
  - `Stale most of the time` (fresh share below 50% and wasted at most 70%; bad)
  - `Fresh enough, little wasted` (otherwise; good)
- **Reduced motion:** no animation; the strip and bars update at once.
- **Accessibility:** the kind chips are buttons with visible labels; the gap rail is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "News front page, visit every 1 day, fresh 17 percent, wasted 0 percent"; the verdict repeats it.
- **Sources:** the change model is the usual random-change model for web pages (source needed: Cho and Garcia-Molina, ACM TOIT 2003, not in the dossier); the two formulas were derived from it and the table computed with code. Page kinds and rates are course numbers.
