# Module 25 playable specs (Booking and payments: contention and money)

Four reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All four sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The twelve watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m25-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same names (Tazkara, Seats, Payments, Ledger, Provider) and the same numbers as the playables below. The seat hold race of 25.2 beat 2 is the existing `ticketline-seat-hold-race` and needs no work.

Every number below was computed with code (the formulas are short and shown). All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time. Course numbers shared by the module, defined in chapters 25.1 and 25.2: 400,000 fans and 20,000 seats at the default; half of the fans arrive in the first 10 seconds; each fan makes 4 requests in that time and tries 1 hold; a buyer takes 120 seconds to pay; a ticket averages 80; a seat store is planned for 5,000 requests a second; half of the fans who are admitted end up buying. All are assumptions, not measurements.

## sysdesign-m25-estimate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 25.1 beat 2, "Drag the crowd, find the pain".
- **Claim:** In a ticket drop the request rate grows with the crowd, but the number that matters is fans per seat: it decides how many holds must fail. Payments stay few. The design has two problems, contention and exact money.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links, some cards are inputs: tap, then drag sideways or use arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** two draggable cards. Fans waiting: 100,000, 200,000, 400,000, 1,000,000 (default 400,000). Seats: 5,000, 20,000, 50,000 (default 20,000). The first-10-seconds share (50%), the requests per fan (4), the checkout time (120 s) and the ticket price (80) are fixed cards labelled "assumption".
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Fans in the first 10 s = fans x 0.5. Fans a second at the start = that / 10.
  - Requests a second at peak = fans a second x 4.
  - Fans per seat = fans / seats.
  - Hold attempts in 10 s = fans x 0.5 (one each). Share that must fail = max(0, 1 - seats / attempts).
  - Payments a second = seats / 120.
  - Money moved in 2 minutes = seats x 80.
- **Picture:** three chains from the input cards. Chain 1: Fans > (x 50%, divide by 10 s) Fans a second > (x 4) Requests a second, peak. Chain 2: Fans and Seats > (divide) Fans per seat > Share of holds that must fail. Chain 3: Seats > (divide by 120 s) Payments a second > (x 80) Money moved. Dots flow along the links at a speed set by the value. Formats: `80,000 /s`, `20 per seat`, `90%`, `167 /s`, `1.6 million`. Ring (tone warn) on the failed-share card when the share is 50% or more, on the payments card otherwise.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above):

  | Fans | Seats | Requests /s | Fans per seat | Must fail | Payments /s | Money moved | Verdict |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 100,000 | 5,000 | 20,000 | 20 | 90.0% | 41.7 | 400,000 | Contention: 90% of holds must fail |
  | 100,000 | 20,000 | 20,000 | 5 | 60.0% | 166.7 | 1,600,000 | Contention: 60% of holds must fail |
  | 100,000 | 50,000 | 20,000 | 2 | 0.0% | 416.7 | 4,000,000 | Every hold can succeed |
  | 200,000 | 5,000 | 40,000 | 40 | 95.0% | 41.7 | 400,000 | Contention: 95% of holds must fail |
  | 200,000 | 20,000 | 40,000 | 10 | 80.0% | 166.7 | 1,600,000 | Contention: 80% of holds must fail |
  | 200,000 | 50,000 | 40,000 | 4 | 50.0% | 416.7 | 4,000,000 | Contention: 50% of holds must fail |
  | 400,000 | 5,000 | 80,000 | 80 | 97.5% | 41.7 | 400,000 | Contention: 98% of holds must fail |
  | 400,000 | 20,000 | 80,000 | 20 | 90.0% | 166.7 | 1,600,000 | Contention: 90% of holds must fail |
  | 400,000 | 50,000 | 80,000 | 8 | 75.0% | 416.7 | 4,000,000 | Contention: 75% of holds must fail |
  | 1,000,000 | 5,000 | 200,000 | 200 | 99.0% | 41.7 | 400,000 | Contention: 99% of holds must fail |
  | 1,000,000 | 20,000 | 200,000 | 50 | 96.0% | 166.7 | 1,600,000 | Contention: 96% of holds must fail |
  | 1,000,000 | 50,000 | 200,000 | 20 | 90.0% | 416.7 | 4,000,000 | Contention: 90% of holds must fail |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Contention: N% of holds must fail` (share 50% or more, N rounded; warn; at most 34 characters)
  - `Some contention: N% of holds fail` (share above 0 and below 50%; good)
  - `Every hold can succeed` (share 0; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the cards show their final values.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "400,000 fans, 20,000 seats, 80,000 requests a second at peak, 20 fans per seat, 90 percent of holds must fail, 167 payments a second"; the ring is also written as text in the verdict.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions (chapter 2.3 for the method). No external source.

## sysdesign-m25-waiting-room

- **Chapter and beat:** 25.2 beat 7, "Let fans in at a steady rate".
- **Claim:** A waiting room protects the seat store by admitting fans at a steady rate. Too fast and the store carries many times its plan; too slow and the show takes an hour to sell out. A good rule keeps about as many fans inside as there are seats, which is Little's law: fans inside = admit rate x time inside.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` or `snap-stops-rail.tsx` for the admit rate (a log rail inside the picture), `VerdictBanner`, `SvgNode` cards for Waiting room, Booking and Seat store. No toggle.
- **Input:** admit rate a in fans a second, on a snap rail with points 10, 33, 100, 167, 500, 1,250, 5,000, 20,000 (default 167). Arrow keys step one snap.
- **Model and formulas** (400,000 fans, 20,000 seats, 120 s inside, 4 requests per admitted fan, store plan 5,000 requests a second, half of the admitted fans buy):
  - Fans inside = a x 120 (Little's law, L = lambda W).
  - Store load = a x 4 requests a second. Times its plan = load / 5,000.
  - Sell-out time = 20,000 / (a x 0.5) seconds.
  - Verdict rule: if times its plan is above 1, bad; otherwise if sell-out is above 600 s, warn; otherwise good. Show sell-out in seconds below 90 s, otherwise in minutes (rounded).
- **Picture:** left to right: a Waiting room card with a line of small dots (the 400,000 fans; the line shortens as the sell-out progresses), a Booking card, a Seat store card. A pill on the Seat store card shows the load as a share of plan (`13% of plan`, `16x plan`). A pill on the Booking card shows `20,040 inside` next to a mark for `20,000 seats`. Dots flow at a speed set by a. A small readout under the rail shows the admit rate and the sell-out time.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Admit rate /s | Fans inside | Store load /s | Share of plan | Sell-out | Verdict |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10 | 1,200 | 40 | 0.8% | 4,000 s | Safe but slow: sold out in 67 min |
  | 33 | 3,960 | 132 | 2.6% | 1,212 s | Safe but slow: sold out in 20 min |
  | 100 | 12,000 | 400 | 8.0% | 400 s | Within plan: sold out in 7 min |
  | 167 | 20,040 | 668 | 13.4% | 239.5 s | Within plan: sold out in 4 min |
  | 500 | 60,000 | 2,000 | 40.0% | 80 s | Within plan: sold out in 80 s |
  | 1,250 | 150,000 | 5,000 | 100.0% | 32 s | Within plan: sold out in 32 s |
  | 5,000 | 600,000 | 20,000 | 400.0% | 8 s | Store at 4x its plan |
  | 20,000 | 2,400,000 | 80,000 | 1,600.0% | 2 s | Store at 16x its plan |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Safe but slow: sold out in N min` (load at most plan, sell-out above 600 s; warn)
  - `Within plan: sold out in N min` or `... in N s` (load at most plan, sell-out 600 s or less; good)
  - `Store at Nx its plan` (load above plan; bad; N rounded, one decimal below 10)
- **Reduced motion:** no flowing dots; the line of fans and the pills update at once.
- **Accessibility:** the rail is a `role="slider"` with value text "167 fans a second, 20,040 inside, store at 13 percent of plan, sold out in 4 minutes".
- **Sources:** Little's law [S5]. The 5,000 plan, the 4 requests, the 120 s and the half who buy are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m25-post-entries

- **Chapter and beat:** 25.3 beat 3, "Post a sale, then break the books".
- **Claim:** A sale is one transaction of three entries whose debits and credits are equal. Take an entry out and the books are off by exactly its amount. A refund is not an edit: it adds three reversing entries, so the history keeps six.
- **Primitives:** `block-map.tsx` style toggles (tap a card in or out) with a two-bar balance readout, `VerdictBanner`, and a step toggle (Sale, Refund) as one of the two outside controls. `design-stepper.tsx` is an alternative if Sale and Refund are shown as two steps.
- **Input:** three entry cards for the sale, each tappable in or out (all three in by default): `Debit provider-clearing 100`, `Credit organizer-payable 90`, `Credit fee-revenue 10`. A step control switches to Refund, which shows three more cards, each tappable in or out (all in by default): `Credit provider-clearing 100`, `Debit organizer-payable 90`, `Debit fee-revenue 10`.
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Debits = sum of the included debit cards. Credits = sum of the included credit cards.
  - Off by = |debits - credits|. Balanced when debits = credits and at least one card is in.
  - Account balance = debits minus credits for that account, over all included cards (sale and refund).
- **Picture:** left, the cards as a stack (included cards solid, excluded cards dashed and grey). Right, two bars labelled `Debits` and `Credits` with totals, and a beam or a gap marker that tilts by the difference. Under them, the three account balances as small pills (`provider-clearing 100`, `organizer-payable -90`, `fee-revenue -10`). In Refund mode with everything in, the three pills read 0 and a count shows `6 entries`.
- **Numbers to check** (sale only):

  | Entries in | Debits | Credits | Verdict |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Dr 100 + Cr 90 + Cr 10 | 100 | 100 | Balanced: debits equal credits |
  | Cr 90 + Cr 10 | 0 | 100 | Off by 100: a debit is missing |
  | Dr 100 + Cr 10 | 100 | 10 | Off by 90: a credit is missing |
  | Cr 10 | 0 | 10 | Off by 10: a debit is missing |
  | Dr 100 + Cr 90 | 100 | 90 | Off by 10: a credit is missing |
  | Cr 90 | 0 | 90 | Off by 90: a debit is missing |
  | Dr 100 | 100 | 0 | Off by 100: a credit is missing |
  | none | 0 | 0 | Nothing posted yet |

  Sale plus refund, all six in: debits 190, credits 190, all three balances 0, verdict `Refunded: 6 entries, balances 0` (31 characters). With any card out, the off-by rule applies to the six cards together.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Balanced: debits equal credits` (good)
  - `Off by N: a credit is missing` or `Off by N: a debit is missing` (bad)
  - `Nothing posted yet` (neutral, accent)
  - `Refunded: 6 entries, balances 0` (good)
- **Reduced motion:** the beam jumps to its final tilt; no animation.
- **Accessibility:** each card is a toggle button with its text and state ("Debit provider-clearing 100, included"); the verdict is read out as text; colour is never the only signal (the missing card is also labelled "missing").
- **Sources:** Course convention (a balance is debits minus credits). The accounting convention itself is a `source needed` note in chapter 25.3.

## sysdesign-m25-webhook-order

- **Chapter and beat:** 25.4 beat 3, "Duplicate, late, out of order".
- **Claim:** Webhooks arrive in any order and sometimes twice. A rule that sets the status to the last event received ends in the wrong state. A payment that only moves forward, ignoring any event that would move it back, always ends in the right one.
- **Primitives:** `snap-stops-rail.tsx` (a rail with named stops) for the arrival order, two chips inside the picture, `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`. `sorter.tsx` is an alternative if the learner places the events one at a time.
- **Input:** three events in the order they happened: `A` authorized (rank 1), `C` captured (rank 2), `R` refunded (rank 3). The learner picks the arrival order on a rail of six stops: A C R, A R C, C A R, C R A, R A C, R C A (default C A R). A chip `Captured arrives twice` adds a second C at the end of the arrival order (default off).
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Rank: created 0, authorized 1, captured 2, refunded 3.
  - Last event wins: status = state of the final arrival.
  - Forward only: start at created; for each arrival, if its rank is above the current rank, move to it; otherwise ignore it (the event is still saved).
  - The true final state is refunded.
- **Picture:** top, the arrival sequence as numbered cards (1, 2, 3, and 4 when the duplicate is on) in the chosen order, each showing its event. Two rows below: `Last event wins` and `Forward only`, each a small state strip (created, authorized, captured, refunded) with a marker that steps through the arrivals in turn, ending on the final state. A tick or cross sits next to each final state, compared with the truth `refunded`. An ignored event is drawn dimmed with the word "ignored" on the forward-only row.
- **Numbers to check** (final status of each rule; the true state is refunded):

  | Arrival order | Last wins | Forward only |
  |---|---|---|
  | A C R | refunded | refunded |
  | A R C | captured | refunded |
  | C A R | refunded | refunded |
  | C R A | authorized | refunded |
  | R A C | captured | refunded |
  | R C A | authorized | refunded |
  | any of the six, plus a second C at the end | captured | refunded |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Both end on refunded` (both rules reach the truth; good)
  - `Last wins: captured. Truth: refunded` or `Last wins: authorized. Truth: refunded` (38 characters at most; bad)
- **Reduced motion:** the markers jump to the final state; no stepping animation.
- **Accessibility:** the rail is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "Arrival order captured, authorized, refunded; last event wins ends on refunded; forward only ends on refunded". The chip is a toggle button.
- **Sources:** Course design. State ranks and "ignore events that move backwards" are the rule taught in chapter 25.4. (Source needed: whether providers promise webhook order. The course assumes they do not.)
