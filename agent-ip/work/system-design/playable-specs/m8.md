## sysdesign-m8-leader-followers (8.2, beat 4: "Send writes, read anywhere, kill the leader")

- **Beat text:** "Tap Write to send a change to the leader, and watch the copies follow. Tap Read on any node to see what it answers. Then tap Kill leader. Reads still work on the followers. Writes stop: nobody may accept them until a follower becomes the new leader (chapter 8.5)."
- **Claim:** Followers copy the leader's log in order and can answer reads, possibly one write behind. When the leader dies, reads continue and writes stop.
- **Model:** three nodes: Leader, Follower 1, Follower 2. The leader holds a log of entries numbered 1, 2, 3, and so on, with the current value `x = n` after entry n. Each Write tap appends the next entry on the leader and sends it to the followers. Copy delay in model time: Follower 1 about 300 ms, Follower 2 about 800 ms (made up; real lag is usually under one second [S38]). A follower applies entries in the leader's order and shows `x = n` for the last entry it applied. A Read tap on a node returns that node's `x`.
- **Inputs:** tap the Write chip beside the app icon (repeat as often as you like, at most 9 entries then it wraps to a Reset hint); tap any node card to Read from it; tap the leader's Kill chip. Outside the picture: Kill leader and Reset (each 44px).
- **What moves:** a log strip under each node with numbered boxes that fill; a message dot with a label chip travels leader to follower on each write; the Read result appears as a chip beside the node, green when equal to the leader's `x`, amber with "one behind" when older. After Kill, the leader card turns red and dims; a Write tap shows a refused chip "no leader".
- **Verdicts:** after a write: "Followers copy the leader's log" (31). After a stale read: "That read is one write behind" (29). After a fresh read: "That copy is up to date" (23). After Kill: "Reads work; writes are stopped" (30). Write after Kill: "No leader: nobody can take writes" (33).
- **Primitive:** `traffic-playground` carries tap-to-send requests and node cards; the log strip and the refusal chip are custom drawing on `BeatFrame`. If it cannot carry a log strip, a custom component on `BeatFrame` and `useBeatClock` is justified, with the model in a tested `model.ts`.
- **Limits text:** "Two followers, made-up copy times. Promoting a follower is left out on purpose (chapter 8.5)."
- **Sources:** [S38] hot standby, asynchronous streaming replication; [S39] Aurora replicas; [S44] each DynamoDB partition has a leader.

## sysdesign-m8-confirm-count (8.3, beat 3: "How many followers must confirm?")

- **Beat text:** "Drag the picture to choose how many followers must confirm before the leader says OK: none, one or two. Watch the write time and how many copies exist at the moment of OK. Then switch off the West follower and drag to two. Notice what happens to writes."
- **Claim:** Each confirmation you wait for adds the round trip to a farther follower and buys one more copy at OK time. Asking for more confirmations than there are live followers stops writes.
- **Model:** leader plus two followers: West (round trip 60 ms, one-way 30 ms) and EU (round trip 80 ms, one-way 40 ms) [S3]. Leader disk write with fsync 0.3 ms [S3]. Write rate 500 writes a second (made up). Let k be the number of confirmations required, from 0 to 2.
  - Write time = 0.3 ms + the k-th smallest round trip among live followers; 0.3 ms when k = 0. If fewer than k followers are live, writes stall.
  - Copies at OK time = 1 + k.
  - Writes at risk on a leader crash with k = 0: write rate x the smallest one-way delay of a live follower = 500 x 0.030 = 15 (West live) or 500 x 0.040 = 20 (West down). With k at least 1, 0 acknowledged writes are lost to one crash.
  - Worked values, both followers live: k = 0: 0.3 ms, 1 copy, 15 at risk. k = 1: 60.3 ms, 2 copies. k = 2: 80.3 ms, 3 copies. West down: k = 0: 0.3 ms, 20 at risk. k = 1: 80.3 ms. k = 2: stalled.
- **Inputs and snaps:** drag the OK marker along a rail with three snap points: 0, 1, 2 (default 1). One toggle outside the picture: "West follower: on / off" (default on). One hint line: "Drag the OK marker; switch off West."
- **What moves:** a timeline of one write: the client sends, the leader saves (0.3 ms), copy dots travel to each follower and back; the OK marker sits at the k-th confirmation; the client's wait ring grows to the write time; copy chips (1, 2 or 3) light up at the OK moment; a switched-off follower greys out and its dot never returns.
- **Verdicts:** k = 0: "Fast, but 15 writes at risk" (27); with West down: "Fast, but 20 writes at risk" (27). k = 1: "60 ms a write; survives 1 failure" (33); with West down: "80 ms a write; survives 1 failure" (33). k = 2: "80 ms a write; survives 2 failures" (34). Stalled: "Writes stuck: not enough followers" (34).
- **Primitive:** `picture-slider` (snap points) with a custom timeline drawing; the toggle uses the shared `BeatToggle` control in `beat-frame.tsx`.
- **Limits text:** "Leaves out disk contention, queueing and jitter. Write rate, copy times and the 500 a second are made up."
- **Sources:** [S3] napkin-math round trips (60 ms North America East to West, 80 ms EU West to North America East) and fsync disk write 0.3 ms; [S38] a synchronous commit waits at least one round trip and may never complete if a synchronous standby crashes.

## sysdesign-m8-split-brain (8.5, beat 3: "Cut the link: two leaders")

- **Beat text:** "Cut the network between the leader and the others, but leave the leader running. The followers elect a new leader. Now both accept writes, and the two histories grow apart. Turn Fencing on and repeat: the old leader's writes are refused. Notice which writes remain."
- **Claim:** A leader that is cut off keeps accepting writes while the followers elect another, so two histories grow. A database that checks the epoch number refuses the old leader.
- **Model:** three nodes A (leader, epoch 1), B and C (followers), and one shared Database that stores the writes. Cut tap: the A to B and A to C links drop. After a made-up timeout (2 s of scene time standing for 10 s [S68, S60 for the idea]), B is promoted with epoch 2. A does not know. Write taps: tapping A or B (the current leaders) sends a write to the Database. Without fencing, the Database accepts both; with fencing, it keeps the highest epoch seen (2 after B's first write) and refuses writes with epoch 1 after that.
  - Worked run, 2 writes on each side: no fencing: A's history `a1, a2`, B's history `b1, b2`, 4 writes accepted by two leaders. Fencing on: `b1, b2` accepted; `a1` accepted only if sent before B's first write, `a2` refused with "epoch 1 < 2".
  - Heal tap: the link returns. Without fencing, the two histories cannot be merged: one is chosen and the other is lost. With fencing, only the new leader's history remains.
- **Inputs:** tap Cut / Heal on the link in the picture; tap a leader node to write; one toggle outside the picture: "Fencing: on / off" (default off); Replay resets. At most two controls outside the picture.
- **What moves:** link lines between nodes (solid, dashed when cut); the epoch number badge on each node; a countdown ring on B and C during the timeout; log chips growing under each leader; refused writes bounce off the Database with a red "refused" chip.
- **Verdicts:** before the cut: "One leader, one history" (23). During the cut, before promotion: "Followers wait out the timeout" (30). After promotion, no fencing: "Two leaders, two histories" (26). After heal, no fencing: "One history must be thrown away" (31). Fencing on, old write refused: "Old leader's write is refused" (29). After heal, fencing on: "Only the new leader's writes remain" (35).
- **Primitive:** a custom component on `BeatFrame` and `useBeatClock` (nodes, links, epoch badge); no existing primitive carries two linked histories. Keep the model in a tested `model.ts`. The `scene` renderer is not enough because the learner changes the story.
- **Limits text:** "One database, three nodes, a made-up timeout. Real systems elect with a majority vote, and every storage node must check the epoch."
- **Sources:** [S38] STONITH and the need to tell the old primary it is no longer primary; [S70] fencing tokens; [S71] Chubby sequencers; [S68] Raft terms and election timeouts; [S134] the 43-second partition at GitHub.

## sysdesign-m8-two-regions-edit (8.6, beat 2: "Two regions edit one document")

- **Beat text:** "Drag the Singapore edit along the timeline. Each region accepts its edit at once and sends it to the other, which takes about 80 ms. If the second edit starts before the first one arrives, the picture flags a conflict. Move the edits apart until it clears, and notice how far apart they must be."
- **Claim:** Two leaders conflict when each accepts its edit before the other's copy arrives, which is when the edits are closer together than the one-way copy time. Last writer wins settles it by silently dropping one edit.
- **Model:** the EU edit happens at t = 0. A copy takes 80 ms one way (half of the 160 ms EU West to Singapore round trip [S3]). The Singapore edit happens at t = b, with b from -150 ms to +150 ms in 5 ms steps. Conflict if and only if |b| < 80 ms. If b >= 80 the Singapore leader already had the EU edit, so Singapore's edit builds on it. If b <= -80 the EU leader already had the Singapore edit.
  - Last writer wins (toggle on): the edit with the larger timestamp is kept. At b > 0 Singapore's edit wins; at b < 0 the EU edit wins; at b = 0 break the tie by region name (EU). The other edit is dropped, with no message to its author.
  - Worked values: b = 12: conflict; Singapore kept, EU dropped. b = -40: conflict; EU kept. b = 79: conflict. b = 80: no conflict.
- **Inputs and snaps:** drag the Singapore edit marker along the timeline, 5 ms steps, with soft snaps at -80, 0 and +80 (default 12 ms). One toggle outside the picture: "Rule: none / last writer wins" (default none). Hint line: "Drag the Singapore edit."
- **What moves:** two lanes (EU, Singapore) on a time axis from -150 to +250 ms; an edit blob on each lane; a copy arrow from each blob toward the other lane that lands 80 ms later; where an arrow lands after the other lane's edit, the landing point turns red ("did not know"); with the rule on, the dropped edit fades with a strike-through.
- **Verdicts:** conflict, no rule: "Concurrent: neither saw the other" (32). No conflict: "Second edit saw the first: no clash" (34). Conflict, rule on: "Later edit kept; the other is lost" (34).
- **Primitive:** `picture-slider` with a custom two-lane timeline drawing; `log-ruler` could draw the axis.
- **Limits text:** "Fixed 80 ms copy time and two edits. Real clocks differ by milliseconds, so \"later\" can be wrong (chapter 10.6)."
- **Sources:** [S3] 160 ms round trip EU West to Singapore; [S43] any Region accepts writes, last writer wins per item; [S52] last writer wins implies data loss with concurrent updates.

## sysdesign-m8-quorum-nodes (8.7, beat 2: "Write to three of five, read from two")

- **Beat text:** "Tap three nodes to write, then tap two nodes to read from. If the nodes you read include at least one that got the write, the read finds the newest value. Try reading from the two nodes that missed it. Then write to four nodes and read from two, and notice when a read can no longer miss."
- **Claim:** A read is guaranteed to see the newest confirmed write when R + W is more than N, because every read group shares a node with every write group. With R + W = N a read can miss it.
- **Model:** N = 5 nodes, each shows `x = 6` (version 1). Write phase: the learner taps a set of nodes (W = their count, from 1 to 5); on Confirm those nodes become `x = 7` (version 2) and the others stay at 6. Read phase: the learner taps a set of nodes (R = their count, from 1 to 5); the read returns the newest version among the tapped nodes. Found = at least one tapped read node has version 2.
  - Guarantee: if R + W > 5, found is true for every choice. If R + W <= 5, there is a choice that misses.
  - Worked values: W = 3, R = 2 (sum 5): reading the two nodes that missed returns x = 6. W = 3, R = 3 (sum 6): always found. W = 4, R = 2 (sum 6): always found. W = 5, R = 1: always found. W = 1, R = 5: always found.
  - A node-down chip (tap a node twice) greys a node out; it cannot be in either set; with two nodes down, W = 4 cannot be confirmed (stalled).
- **Inputs:** tap nodes to build the write set, tap Confirm in the picture, tap nodes to build the read set; a header shows "W = 3, R = 2, R + W = 5 (not more than 5)". Outside the picture: Reset and Auto-miss (chooses the worst read set for the current W and R, to show it can miss).
- **What moves:** five node cards in two rows; selected write nodes ring blue and turn `x = 7` with a version chip; selected read nodes ring amber; the result chip appears on the client; when R + W > N, the overlap node gets a double ring.
- **Verdicts:** found: "Read found the newest write" (27). Missed: "Read missed the newest write" (28). R + W > N header verdict: "Overlap guaranteed: R + W is over 5" (35). R + W <= N: "A read can miss: R + W is not over 5" (36). Stalled: "Not enough nodes up to confirm" (30).
- **Primitive:** `block-map` carries five tappable blocks with states; the two phases and the header are custom drawing on `BeatFrame`.
- **Limits text:** "One key, no concurrent writes, strict quorums. Sloppy quorums and concurrent writes need versions and repair (module 13)."
- **Sources:** [S45] Dynamo: N, R, W, (3, 2, 2) common; [S52] majority quorum (n + 1) / 2 and the limits of quorum reads.
