# Module 28 playable specs (The system design interview)

Three reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All three sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer and every status pill at 15 characters or fewer. The eight watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m28-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. Two of the playables use the house `design-stepper.tsx` (`DesignSpec` in `models/design-stepper.ts`, like `sysdesign-m20-design-stepper`); the third uses `sorter.tsx` (like `sysdesign-m1-requirement-sorter`).

Every number below was computed with code. Course numbers shared by the two steppers: the Wasla estimate of chapter 20.1 and the design of chapter 20.2 and 20.4: peak 100,000 opens (redirects) and 1,000 creates a second; a cache hit rate of 90%; an app server planned at 5,000 opens a second; a database node planned at 2,500 operations a second; 5 partitions. The interview time boxes are course choices (chapter 28.2): clarify 5, estimate 5, sketch 10, deep dive 15, trade-offs and failure 7, wrap-up 3 minutes, 45 in all.

## sysdesign-m28-interview-stepper

- **Chapter and beat:** 28.2 beat 1, "Six boxes fill 45 minutes".
- **Claim:** A 45-minute round is six boxes of time, each ending with one thing said or drawn. Minutes spent drawing the sketch come out of the deep dive.
- **Primitives:** `design-stepper.tsx` with a `DesignSpec`: six steps, Back and Next under the picture, step chips on top, one two-state action in step 3. `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`. Chip titles (one or two words): Clarify, Numbers, Sketch, Dive, Cost, Wrap-up.
- **Input:** Back and Next (or tap a step chip). Step 3 only: a toggle button "Draw 8 min longer" (and "Back to 10 min") that is also the tap target on the Clock card.
- **Picture (420 x 330):** three cards in a row at y = 100: Clock (kind clock, x = 62), Board (kind note, x = 210), Interviewer (kind user, x = 358); links Clock to Board and Board to Interviewer. Under the Board card a column of at most five chips centred at x = 210, rows at y = 190, 218, 246, 274, 302, one new chip per step that stays in later steps. A labelled request dot runs between Board and Interviewer in every step (label = what that step produces).
- **Model:** the plan is clarify 5, estimate 5, sketch 10, deep dive 15, trade-offs and failure 7, wrap-up 3. The clock pill shows the minute at the end of the step. The action in step 3 adds 8 minutes to the sketch: the clock pill reads "minute 28" and the deep dive that follows has 15 minus 8 = 7 minutes.
- **Steps** (clock pill, board pill, chip added, verdict, tone):

  | Step | Clock pill | Board pill | Chip added to the board | Verdict (tone) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 Clarify | minute 5 | scope | create + open, no edits | Minute 5: scope agreed (good) |
  | 2 Numbers | minute 10 | numbers | 100,000 opens/s, 9 TB | Minute 10: opens drive it (good) |
  | 3 Sketch | minute 20 | 4 boxes | app > cache > store | Minute 20: main path drawn (good) |
  | 3 Sketch, action on | minute 28 (bad) | 4 boxes | app > cache > store | Sketch ran long: dive gets 7 min (bad) |
  | 4 Dive | minute 35 | 1 deep dive | dive: cache, 90% hits | Minute 35: one dive, done (good) |
  | 5 Cost | minute 42 | cost + failure | 302 costs a hop; cache restart | Minute 42: cost and failure named (good) |
  | 6 Wrap-up | minute 45 | done | (none) | Minute 45: three-sentence summary (good) |

  The Interviewer card's pill: "listening" (step 1), "asks why" (step 5), "can score it" (step 6), empty otherwise. The action does not carry to step 4, which shows the plan.
- **Sentence under the picture (`says`):** step 1 "Four questions fix what to build and what to leave out."; step 2 "Say each sum with its unit and name the driving number."; step 3 "Draw the path of each main action and give each box one job."; step 3 action on: "Eight more minutes of drawing come out of the deep dive."; step 4 "One hard part, with options and the number that chose it."; step 5 "One choice with its cost and switch point, then one failure."; step 6 "Three sentences: what you built, the driver, what next."
- **Numbers to check:** 5 + 5 + 10 + 15 + 7 + 3 = 45. After the action: 5 + 5 + 18 = 28, and 45 minus 28 minus 7 minus 3 = 7 left for the deep dive.
- **Reduced motion:** no moving dot; the chip column and pills show the final state of the step.
- **Accessibility:** step chips are buttons with `aria-current`; the action is a real button with `aria-pressed`; the live region reads the minute, the chip and the verdict.
- **Limits (fold):** the minute counts are the course's plan, not a rule from any company. Real rounds vary in length and in how much the interviewer talks.
- **Sources:** Course plan (chapter 28.2); design loop (chapter 1.4).

## sysdesign-m28-probe-stepper

- **Chapter and beat:** 28.3 beat 2, "Step through six follow-ups".
- **Claim:** A follow-up is answered by scaling the numbers, dividing each load by its plan, fixing the part that breaks first and pricing the fix. The same design answers six probes.
- **Primitives:** `design-stepper.tsx` with a `DesignSpec`: six steps, Back and Next, step chips (Today, 10x, Fix, Cold, Hot link, Cost), actions in steps 3, 4 and 5. `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`, chips for numbers.
- **Input:** Back and Next (or tap a chip). Step 3: a three-state button cycling the partition count 5, 8, 10 (also the tap target on the Database card). Step 4: a toggle "Spread the cache" (tap target: Cache card). Step 5: a toggle "Copy in each app" (tap target: Apps card). Actions do not carry to later steps; step 4 and 5 assume the step 3 result of 8 partitions and a 99% hit rate.
- **Picture (420 x 330):** Browsers (user, x = 62, y = 100), Apps (server, x = 210, y = 100), Cache (cache, x = 358, y = 100); Database (database, x = 210, y = 230). Links Browsers to Apps, Apps to Cache, Apps to Database. A chip under Browsers (x = 62, y = 160) shows the load. A labelled request dot runs Browsers to Apps to Cache. Result chips sit at y = 290 and 316.
- **Model and formulas:** database operations = opens x (1 minus hit rate) + creates. Share of plan = (operations / partitions) / 2,500. App servers = opens / 5,000.
- **Steps** (pills, chips, verdict, tone):

  | Step | State | Pills | Chip | Verdict (tone) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 Today | 100,000 opens, 1,000 creates, 90% hits, 5 partitions: 11,000 ops/s, 2,200 each | Apps 20 servers; Cache 90% hits; Database 88% of plan | 100,000 opens/s | Today: every part inside its plan (good) |
  | 2 10x | 1,000,000 opens, 10,000 creates, 90% hits: 110,000 ops/s, 22,000 each | Apps needs 200 (warn); Database 8.8x plan (bad) | 1,000,000 opens/s | 10x: database at 8.8x its plan (bad) |
  | 3 Fix | 99% hits: 20,000 ops/s | Apps 200 servers; Cache 99% hits; Database by partitions | 20,000 db ops/s | 99% hits: database at 160% of plan (bad, 5 partitions) |
  | 3, 8 partitions | 2,500 each | Database 100% of plan (good) | same | 8 partitions: each at 100% of plan (good) |
  | 3, 10 partitions | 2,000 each | Database 80% of plan (good) | same | 10 partitions: each at 80% of plan (good) |
  | 4 Cold | cache empty, 8 partitions: 1,010,000 ops/s, 126,250 each | Cache broken; Database 50x plan (bad) | 1,010,000 db ops/s | Empty cache: database at 50x plan (bad) |
  | 4, spread | cache on 10 nodes, one restarts: 9,000 + 100,000 + 10,000 = 119,000 ops/s, 14,875 each | Cache 10 nodes (good); Database 6x plan (warn) | 119,000 db ops/s | One node empty: database at 6x plan (warn) |
  | 5 Hot link | 1 open in 10 is one code: 100,000 reads/s on one cache node | Cache 1 hot code (warn) | 100,000 reads/s on 1 node | One cache node takes 100,000 reads/s (bad) |
  | 5, copies | a copy kept 5 s in each of 200 app servers: 200 / 5 = 40 reads/s | Apps 5 s copy (good) | 40 reads/s on cache | App copies cut it to 40 reads/s (good) |
  | 6 Cost | summary | none | chips: bigger cache; 3 more db nodes; 5 s copy in apps | Name the cost before they ask (good) |

- **Sentence under the picture (`says`):** 1 "Where the sketch ended: 11,000 database operations a second on 5 partitions."; 2 "Scale every number by 10, then divide each load by its plan."; 3 "Apps: add copies. Database: more hits, then more partitions."; 4 "Every open misses: 1,010,000 operations a second on 8 partitions."; 4 spread "A restart empties a tenth. Merge duplicate misses to cut it more."; 5 "One viral code can load one cache node."; 5 copies "Each app keeps the hottest codes for a few seconds."; 6 "Cost: cache memory, 3 more nodes (5 to 8), data to move. Switch if hits stay under 99%."
- **Numbers to check** (computed with code):

  | Case | Database ops a second | Per partition | Share of plan |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Today | 11,000 | 2,200 (5) | 88% |
  | 10x, 90% hits | 110,000 | 22,000 (5) | 880% |
  | 99% hits | 20,000 | 4,000 (5) / 2,500 (8) / 2,000 (10) | 160% / 100% / 80% |
  | Cold cache, 8 partitions | 1,010,000 | 126,250 | 5,050% (50.5x) |
  | Ten cache nodes, one empty | 119,000 | 14,875 (8) | 595% (about 6x) |
  | Hot link | 100,000 reads on one node | | 40 reads/s with 200 copies of 5 s |

- **Reduced motion:** dots are replaced by lit links; pills and chips show the final numbers.
- **Accessibility:** chips and the partition button are real buttons with visible labels; `aria-pressed` on toggles; the live region reads the step, the pills and the verdict.
- **Limits (fold):** cache nodes have limits of their own that the picture ignores; real traffic is not smooth; the 5 second copy is an example value (chapter 20.4 says "a few seconds"). Wasla is fictional.
- **Sources:** Course numbers (chapters 20.1, 20.2, 20.4). The 2,500 plan follows chapter 2.5.

## sysdesign-m28-answer-sorter

- **Chapter and beat:** 28.3 beat 5, "Strong answers name a cost and a switch".
- **Claim:** An answer to "why that choice?" has three levels. A claim only says what was chosen. A better answer names a cost. A strong answer gives a number and says when to switch.
- **Primitives:** `sorter.tsx` (`SorterConfig`: buckets, cards, deckLabel, prompt, hint, endNote), data in `data.ts` with `makeSorterCanvas` like `sysdesign-m1-requirement-sorter`. Three buckets, eight cards.
- **Buckets:** `claim` "Claim only" / "no cost named"; `cost` "Names a cost" / "no number"; `strong` "Strong" / "number + switch".
- **Deck label:** "Candidate says". **Prompt (banner):** "Which bucket does this answer go in?" **Hint:** "Tap the bucket this answer belongs in, or drag the card onto it." **End note:** "A strong answer names what it costs, gives a number, and says when to switch."
- **Cards** (text, answer, reason for each wrong bucket, each reason 60 characters or fewer):

  | # | Card text | Answer | Why not the other buckets |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | I would add a cache, because it makes reads faster. | claim | cost: "It names no cost. 'Faster' is the benefit."; strong: "It has no number and no switch point." |
  | 2 | Pushing posts to followers makes feeds fast, but each post costs one write per follower. | cost | claim: "It names a cost: one write per follower."; strong: "It gives no number and no point to switch." |
  | 3 | Latest places in memory: 100 MB. A restart empties it for 4 s. A standby if that hurts. | strong | claim: "It has a number, a cost and a switch point."; cost: "It also gives numbers and a switch point." |
  | 4 | I would number messages in each chat so the order is right. | claim | cost: "It says what it does, not what it gives up."; strong: "No cost, no number, no switch point." |
  | 5 | A Bloom filter saves memory, but it can wrongly skip a new page. | cost | claim: "It names a cost: skipped pages."; strong: "It gives no size and no point to switch." |
  | 6 | Authorize, then capture: 330 provider calls a second. Charge at once if the provider cannot split. | strong | claim: "It has a number and a switch point."; cost: "It also gives a number and a switch point." |
  | 7 | I would send a 302, because that is what people usually do. | claim | cost: "'Usual' is not a cost."; strong: "No cost, no number, no switch point." |
  | 8 | Push below 100,000 followers: a 20M post is 20M writes. Move the line if write capacity changes. | strong | claim: "It has a number and says when to move."; cost: "It also gives a number and a switch point." |

  Card lengths (characters): 51, 88, 87, 59, 64, 98, 59, 96. The longest is under 100, so each card wraps to at most three lines at 40 characters.
- **Inputs, what moves, accessibility:** as `sysdesign-m1-requirement-sorter` (tap a bucket or drag the card; a right card flies into its bucket; a wrong card shakes and shows the reason; bucket buttons are labelled with name, gloss and count; a live region reads each result).
- **Numbers on screen:** the three bucket counts and the end verdict.
- **What the learner should notice:** a number and a switch point are what lift an answer from "names a cost" to "strong"; a cost with no number is still only the middle bucket.
- **Limits (fold):** real answers are messier, and where one stops being "strong" is a judgment. The sentences are condensed from chapters 20, 21, 24, 25 and 26. Hikaya, Wasla and the others are fictional.
- **Sources:** Chapters 21.3, 24.1, 25.4 and 26.4 (numbers); trade-off shape from the authoring standards.
