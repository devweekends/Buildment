# Module 15 playable specs (queues, logs and stream processing)

Eight reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All eight sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The 24 watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m15-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same actor names (Queue, Mailer, Topic, Email, Receipts, Stats, Part. 0, Part. 1) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with code: the fluid models by the loop shown, the windows and watermark figures with the same rules as the code exercise in chapter 15.8 (`createAggregator`), and the key balance figures with the generator printed in the slug's section. All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time.

## sysdesign-m15-backlog-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.2 beat 3, "Drag the burst, watch the line".
- **Claim:** While a burst brings more jobs than the workers can finish, the backlog grows by the difference every second. After the burst it shrinks only by the spare capacity, so the line outlives the burst. With too few workers it never shrinks. A queue limit refuses jobs instead of letting the line grow.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the burst rate (rail inside the picture), `rail-slider.tsx` or a snap rail for the workers, one toggle outside the picture (queue limit off or 5,000), `SvgNode` for the queue and the workers, `VerdictBanner`. Backlog drawn as an area over time.
- **Input:** burst rate B on a rail from 150 to 400 jobs per second, step 10, snap points at 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, default 300. Workers W on a snap rail 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, default 5. Toggle "Queue limit": off, or 5,000 jobs. Arrow keys step 10 on the burst and 1 on the workers.
- **Model:** normal arrivals are 150 per second. The burst runs from second 30 to second 90 (60 seconds). Each worker finishes 40 jobs per second, so capacity C is 40 x W. Every second: backlog = max(0, backlog + arrivals - C). With a limit L, anything above L is refused and counted.
- **Formulas:**
  - Peak backlog = (B - C) x 60 when B is above C, else 0.
  - Age of the last job in line at the peak = peak / C seconds.
  - Seconds to empty after the burst = peak / (C - 150) when C is above 150, else "never".
  - With limit 5,000: refused = max(0, peak - 5,000) summed over the burst; the backlog stays at 5,000 until the burst ends, then empties in 5,000 / (C - 150) seconds.
- **Picture:** a time axis from 0 to 420 seconds. The burst is a shaded band from 30 to 90. The backlog is an area that rises during the band and falls after it. A dashed line marks the limit when it is on. Three numbers sit on the picture: "peak", "oldest job waits" and "empty after". The queue card shows the count and the worker cards light up as a row of W small icons.
- **Numbers to check** (computed by the loop above, 1 second steps):

  | Workers | Capacity | Burst | Peak | Oldest waits | Empty after burst |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5 | 200 | 250 | 3,000 | 15 s | 60 s |
  | 5 | 200 | 300 | 6,000 | 30 s | 120 s |
  | 5 | 200 | 350 | 9,000 | 45 s | 180 s |
  | 5 | 200 | 400 | 12,000 | 60 s | 240 s |
  | 6 | 240 | 300 | 3,600 | 15 s | 40 s |
  | 6 | 240 | 400 | 9,600 | 40 s | 107 s |
  | 4 | 160 | 250 | 5,400 | 34 s | 540 s |
  | 4 | 160 | 300 | 8,400 | 53 s | 840 s |
  | 3 | 120 | 300 | grows for ever | | never |
  | 8 | 320 | 300 | 0 | | no line |
  | 5, limit 5,000 | 200 | 300 | 5,000 | 25 s | 100 s, 1,000 refused |
  | 5, limit 5,000 | 200 | 400 | 5,000 | 25 s | 100 s, 7,000 refused |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `No line forms` (capacity at or above the burst; good)
  - `Spike absorbed, empty in 60 s` (the number filled in, up to 120 s; good)
  - `Line outlives the burst by 3 min` (above 120 s; warn)
  - `The line never empties` (capacity at or below 150; bad)
  - `Queue full: 1,000 jobs refused` (a limit is on and jobs are refused; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** the final frame shows the whole curve; no growing animation.
- **Accessibility:** the burst rail is a `role="slider"` whose value text reads "burst 300 jobs per second, 5 workers, peak 6,000, empty 120 seconds after the burst".
- **Sources:** Little's law and queueing [S5, S6]; the numbers are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m15-rebalance-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.3 beat 3, "Add a consumer, watch the partitions move".
- **Claim:** In a consumer group each partition has exactly one consumer. The group's top speed is set by the consumer that holds the most partitions, so it rises in steps, and consumers beyond the partition count sit idle. A change of membership moves partitions (a rebalance).
- **Primitives:** `block-map.tsx` for the six partitions as blocks coloured by owner, `picture-slider.tsx` for the number of consumers, `SvgNode` for the consumers, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** consumers c on a snap rail 1 to 8, default 3. Arrow keys step 1. Each press of the rail ends in a short rebalance animation of 1 second (a course number, labelled "illustrative") in which the moved partitions turn grey.
- **Model:** 6 partitions, numbered 0 to 5, each carrying one sixth of a load. Round-robin assignment: partition p goes to consumer p mod c. Each consumer processes 100 events per second in total. The offered load is 450 events per second.
- **Formulas:**
  - Largest share k = ceil(6 / c) for c up to 6, else 1.
  - Group limit = 600 / k events per second.
  - Idle consumers = max(0, c - 6).
  - Partitions that move when c goes to c + 1 = the number of p with p mod c different from p mod (c + 1).
- **Picture:** six partition blocks in a row, each coloured by its owner; below, up to eight consumer cards, each listing its partitions, with idle ones greyed. A bar compares the group limit with the 450 offered load line.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Consumers | Assignment | Group limit | Idle | Partitions that move when one more joins |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 012345 | 100 | 0 | 3 |
  | 2 | 024 / 135 | 200 | 0 | 4 |
  | 3 | 03 / 14 / 25 | 300 | 0 | 3 |
  | 4 | 04 / 15 / 2 / 3 | 300 | 0 | 2 |
  | 5 | 05 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 | 300 | 0 | 1 |
  | 6 | one each | 600 | 0 | 0 |
  | 7 | one each, one idle | 600 | 1 | 0 |
  | 8 | one each, two idle | 600 | 2 | not applicable |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Behind: handles 300 of 450` (limit below 450, numbers filled in; bad)
  - `Keeps up: one partition each` (c = 6; good)
  - `2 consumers sit idle` (c above 6, number filled in; warn)
- **Reduced motion:** the blocks change colour at once; no sliding.
- **Accessibility:** each partition block has a text label with its owner; the rail's value text reads "4 consumers, limit 300 events per second, none idle".
- **Sources:** Kafka design: one consumer per partition per group [S82]; Kinesis shards [S132]. The 100 events per second, the 450 load and the 1 second pause are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m15-retry-reorder-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.4 beat 3, "A retry can swap two events".
- **Claim:** If two updates for one key are in flight and the first fails and is retried, the log holds them in the wrong order and the final state is wrong. Waiting for each ack, or numbering each update so the consumer can skip old ones, keeps the state right.
- **Primitives:** `log-ruler.tsx` for the time axis in milliseconds, `SvgNode` for producer, broker log and consumer, a toggle outside the picture ("First send is lost") and a three-way segmented control outside the picture ("Send freely", "One at a time", "Version numbers"), `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** toggle "First send is lost": off or on (default on). Segmented control: send freely (default), one at a time, version numbers.
- **Model:** the producer sends update 1, "paid", at t = 0 and update 2, "refunded", at t = 10 ms (version 1 and version 2). Network and write take 5 ms each way for an ack. The producer's timeout is 50 ms. If the first send is lost, the producer sends it again at t = 50 ms and it is written at t = 55 ms.
- **Formulas:**
  - Send freely, first send lost: update 2 is written at t = 15, update 1 at t = 55. Log order: refunded, paid. Final state by log order: paid (wrong). Total time 55 ms.
  - Send freely, nothing lost: written at t = 5 and t = 15. Log: paid, refunded. Final: refunded. Total 15 ms.
  - One at a time, first lost: update 1 is written at t = 55, acked at t = 60. Update 2 is sent at t = 60 and written at t = 65. Log: paid, refunded. Final: refunded. Total 65 ms (about 4.3 times the 15 ms of the lucky run).
  - Version numbers, first lost: log order: refunded (version 2), paid (version 1). The consumer applies version 2, then skips version 1 because it is older. Final: refunded. Total 55 ms.
- **Picture:** three lanes (producer, log, consumer) on a millisecond axis. Messages travel; the lost send ends in a cross. The log shows two boxes in the order written. The consumer card shows the order status, with a red badge when it is wrong.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Order shows paid, not refunded` (send freely, lost; bad)
  - `Order holds, nothing failed` (send freely, not lost; good)
  - `Safe, but 65 ms instead of 15` (one at a time, lost; warn)
  - `Safe: the old version is skipped` (version numbers, lost; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the final frame shows both boxes in the log and the consumer's status.
- **Accessibility:** the verdict is text; the toggle and the segmented control are native buttons with visible labels.
- **Sources:** Kafka idempotent producer and sequence numbers [S82]; the ordering effect of retries is course reasoning (see the source note in chapter 15.4).

## sysdesign-m15-partition-key-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.4 beat 5, "Choose a key: order against balance".
- **Claim:** The key decides which events stay in order and how evenly the partitions are loaded. A key that keeps related events together can overload one partition, and a key that spreads well can split related events.
- **Primitives:** `beat-segmented.tsx` for the four keys inside the picture (tap a card), four partition bars, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** one of four keys: order id, customer id, country, no key (default order id).
- **Model:** 1,000 events, 4 partitions. The data comes from this generator (seeded random as in `toolkit/stats.js`, `seededRandom(15)`):

  ```js
  const R = seededRandom(15);
  const w = Array.from({ length: 50 }, (_, i) => 1 / (i + 1)); // customer c0 is the most popular
  const pick = (arr, r) => { let x = r * arr.reduce((a, b) => a + b); for (let i = 0; i < arr.length; i++) { x -= arr[i]; if (x <= 0) return i; } return arr.length - 1; };
  const cust = Array.from({ length: 50 }, (_, i) => ({ id: "c" + i, country: ["PS", "EG", "JO"][pick([80, 15, 5], R())] }));
  const orders = Array.from({ length: 200 }, (_, i) => ({ id: "o" + i, cust: pick(w, R()) }));
  const events = []; // 5 events per order
  orders.forEach((o) => { for (let k = 0; k < 5; k++) events.push({ order: o.id, cust: cust[o.cust].id, country: cust[o.cust].country }); });
  const fnv = (s) => { let h = 2166136261; for (const ch of s) { h ^= ch.charCodeAt(0); h = Math.imul(h, 16777619) >>> 0; } return h; };
  // partition of an event with key K: fnv(event[K]) % 4. With no key: Math.floor(R() * 4), drawn after the lines above.
  ```

- **Formulas:** events per partition; largest share = max / 1,000; "one order stays in order" is true when all 5 events of every order share a partition; "one customer stays in order" is true when all events of every customer share a partition.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the generator; the customer c0 alone holds 200 of the 1,000 events, c1 holds 155, c2 holds 60; PS holds 900, EG 60, JO 40):

  | Key | Events per partition | Largest share | One order in order | One customer in order |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | order id | 250 / 250 / 250 / 250 | 25.0% | yes | no |
  | customer id | 170 / 320 / 325 / 185 | 32.5% | yes | yes |
  | country | 900 / 60 / 40 / 0 | 90.0% | yes | yes |
  | no key | 249 / 264 / 243 / 244 | 26.4% | no | no |

  The perfectly even 250 for order id is a property of this data set and hash (each order has exactly 5 events); say so in the limits fold.
- **Picture:** four vertical bars for the partitions, with a dashed line at 25%. Two badges under the key card: "orders in order" and "customers in order" (check or cross, with text). The hottest bar turns amber above 30% and red above 50%.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Even load, one order stays in order` (order id; good)
  - `Hot partition: 33% of all events` (customer id; warn)
  - `One partition holds 90% of events` (country; bad)
  - `Even load, but no order at all` (no key; bad)
- **Reduced motion:** the bars are drawn at their final height.
- **Accessibility:** each bar has a text label with its count; the key cards are native buttons.
- **Sources:** Kafka keys and partitions [S81]; hash partitioning and hot keys, chapters 9.2 and 9.4. The data set is a course example.

## sysdesign-m15-poison-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.5 beat 2, "Wait in place, or park it".
- **Claim:** A message that always fails stops an ordered partition for as long as it is retried in place. A dead letter queue with a retry limit parks it so the line moves on. A limit that is too low can park a message that was only flaky.
- **Primitives:** `traffic-playground.tsx` or a custom lane for 1,000 messages as a strip, a rail for the retry limit inside the picture (`rail-slider.tsx`), two toggles outside the picture ("Dead letter queue" off or on, "Message 10" always fails or fails twice), `SvgNode` for the consumer and the dead letter queue, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** dead letter queue off or on (default off). Retry limit n on a snap rail 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 (default 5; used only when the queue is on). Message 10 is "always bad" (default) or "flaky: fails twice, then works".
- **Model:** 1,000 messages in one partition, handled in order. A good message takes 50 ms. A failed try takes 50 ms and is followed by a wait of 1 second before the next try. Message 10 is the special one.
- **Formulas:**
  - Dead letter queue off, always bad: messages 1 to 9 finish at 0.45 s; message 10 is retried for ever; 991 messages wait and the oldest waiting message gets older every second.
  - On, always bad, limit n: total time to clear everything else = 9 x 0.05 + n x 1.05 + 990 x 0.05 seconds.
  - On, flaky, limit 1 or 2: the message is parked although it would have worked; total = 9 x 0.05 + n x 1.05 + 990 x 0.05.
  - On or off, flaky, limit 3 or more (or off): it works on try 3; total = 9 x 0.05 + 2 x 1.05 + 0.05 + 990 x 0.05 = 52.10 seconds.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Queue | Message 10 | Limit | Total time | Parked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | off | always bad | none | never finishes | 0 |
  | on | always bad | 1 | 51.00 s | 1 |
  | on | always bad | 3 | 53.10 s | 1 |
  | on | always bad | 5 | 55.20 s | 1 |
  | on | always bad | 10 | 60.45 s | 1 |
  | on | flaky | 1 | 51.00 s | 1 (a good message lost to the dead queue) |
  | on | flaky | 2 | 52.05 s | 1 (same) |
  | on | flaky | 3 or more | 52.10 s | 0 |
  | off | flaky | none | 52.10 s | 0 |

- **Picture:** a strip of messages as small squares, done ones green, the current one pulsing, waiting ones grey. A counter "waiting behind it" and a clock "oldest has waited". The dead letter queue card shows its count.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Blocked at message 10 for ever` (queue off, always bad; bad)
  - `Parked after 5 tries, 55 s in all` (queue on, always bad; good)
  - `A good message was parked too soon` (limit below 3, flaky; warn)
  - `Flaky message worked on try 3` (flaky, limit 3 or more; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the strip shows the end state.
- **Accessibility:** the counters are text; the toggles are native buttons.
- **Sources:** SQS dead-letter queues and maxReceiveCount [S85]; FIFO message groups [S84]. The 50 ms, 1 second and 1,000 messages are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m15-window-shapes-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.6 beat 2, "Three ways to cut time into windows".
- **Claim:** The same events give different counts under tumbling, sliding and session windows. Tumbling puts each event in one window, sliding in several, and session follows activity.
- **Primitives:** `beat-segmented.tsx` for the three shapes (inside the picture), `log-ruler.tsx` for the time axis, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** shape: tumbling (60 s), sliding (60 s window, 20 s slide), session (40 s gap). Default tumbling.
- **Model:** eight events at seconds 5, 20, 50, 70, 75, 130, 140 and 200. Tumbling windows start at multiples of 60. Sliding windows start at multiples of 20 and last 60 seconds; an event belongs to every window that contains it. A session ends when the gap to the next event is more than 40 seconds.
- **Numbers to check:**
  - Tumbling: 0 to 60 has 3 (5, 20, 50), 60 to 120 has 2 (70, 75), 120 to 180 has 2 (130, 140), 180 to 240 has 1 (200). Each event is in 1 window.
  - Sliding: windows from -40 to 20 (1), -20 to 40 (2), 0 to 60 (3), 20 to 80 (4), 40 to 100 (3), 60 to 120 (2), 80 to 140 (1), 100 to 160 (2), 120 to 180 (2), 140 to 200 (1), 160 to 220 (1), 180 to 240 (1), 200 to 260 (1). Each event is in 3 windows, so 24 memberships for 8 events.
  - Session: 5 to 75 has 5 events, 130 to 140 has 2, 200 to 200 has 1. Three sessions, with gaps of 55 and 60 seconds between them.
- **Picture:** a time axis from 0 to 240 s with the events as dots; the windows as bars stacked in rows under the axis, each labelled with its count; each dot shows the number of windows it is in.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Each event is in one window` (tumbling; good)
  - `Each event is in 3 windows` (sliding; warn, with the cost note "3 times the work")
  - `Windows follow activity: 3 sessions` (session; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the bars are drawn in place.
- **Accessibility:** a text table below the picture lists windows and counts; the segmented control is native buttons.
- **Sources:** Flink windows [S87]. The event times are course numbers.

## sysdesign-m15-watermark-delay-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.6 beat 4, "Drag the delay: fast or complete".
- **Claim:** A short watermark delay closes windows soon, so results come fast, but late events find the window closed and are dropped. A long delay keeps more events and delays every result. No setting is both fast and complete.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the delay (rail inside the picture), `log-ruler.tsx` for the event-time axis, `VerdictBanner`. One optional toggle outside the picture: "Allowed lateness" off or 30 s (default off).
- **Input:** delay d on a snap rail 0, 5, 10, 20, 30, 45, 60 seconds, default 10. Arrow keys step 5.
- **Model:** window 60 s, tumbling. 40 events with event times 0, 3, 6, ..., 117 and arrival times from this list, sorted by arrival (each pair is [event time, arrival time] in seconds):

  `[[0,1.4],[3,5.6],[6,8.6],[9,12],[12,13.3],[15,20.4],[21,22.8],[24,24.3],[30,31.9],[18,32],[33,33.1],[39,40.3],[36,42.4],[42,43.6],[45,45.5],[57,59.2],[60,60.3],[48,63],[54,63.8],[63,66.3],[51,66.9],[69,69.8],[75,75.6],[27,77],[72,79],[78,82.5],[81,83.2],[84,89],[90,90.4],[87,91.4],[96,96.8],[99,99.8],[102,102.5],[108,114.4],[111,115.7],[105,116.1],[66,118],[114,119.4],[117,127.4],[93,153]]`

  The watermark is the newest event time seen so far minus d, read before each event, exactly as in `createAggregator` of chapter 15.8. An event is dropped when the watermark is at or past its window's end plus the allowed lateness.
- **Formulas:** results are produced d seconds after the newest event for a window arrives, so every result is at least d seconds behind. Dropped count = events whose window had closed (beyond the allowed lateness) when they arrived.
- **Numbers to check** (no allowed lateness):

  | Delay | Dropped of 40 | Share counted |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 s | 4 | 90% |
  | 5 s | 1 | 97.5% |
  | 10 s | 1 | 97.5% |
  | 20 s | 0 | 100% |
  | 30 s | 0 | 100% |
  | 45 s | 0 | 100% |
  | 60 s | 0 | 100% |

  The slowest events (the last four with the largest lateness) arrive 60, 52, 50 and 15.9 seconds late, in order. With the toggle on (allowed lateness 30 s) nothing is dropped at any delay; the events that would have been dropped become late corrections instead: 4 at delay 0, 1 at delay 5, 1 at delay 10, and 0 from delay 20 up. Show them as amber dots with the text "corrected".
- **Picture:** the event-time axis with each window as a bar; dots for the events, those dropped turn red with a cross; a small clock shows "result is d seconds behind". The delay rail sits inside the picture.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `4 events dropped, results instant` (d = 0; warn, number filled in)
  - `1 dropped, results 10 s behind` (the number and delay filled in; warn)
  - `Nothing dropped, results 30 s late` (d at or above 20; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the final state is drawn at once.
- **Accessibility:** the rail value text reads "delay 10 seconds, 1 event dropped of 40, results 10 seconds behind".
- **Sources:** Flink windows and allowed lateness [S87]; Flink time concepts page (source note in chapter 15.6). The events are a course example.

## sysdesign-m15-slow-subscriber-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 15.7 beat 2, "One slow subscriber, two designs".
- **Claim:** If the order service calls every subscriber in turn, one slow subscriber slows every order. With a queue for each subscriber, orders stay fast and only the slow subscriber's queue grows.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for Receipts' speed (rail inside the picture), `traffic-playground.tsx` or a custom lane for the events, `SvgNode` for the order service, the topic or direct links, and three subscribers, a toggle outside the picture ("Call each in turn" or "A queue for each"), `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** Receipts' speed r on a snap rail 2, 4, 6, 10, 20, 30 events per second, default 6. Toggle: "Call each in turn" or "A queue for each" (default "A queue for each"). Arrow keys step along the snaps.
- **Model:** 20 orders arrive per second. Email and Stats need 33.3 ms per event (30 per second). Receipts needs 1,000 / r ms. The order service has a pool of 5 workers. In the direct design a worker calls the three subscribers one after another and waits. In the queue design a worker only writes the event, which takes 5 ms.
- **Formulas:**
  - Direct: time per order = 33.3 + 33.3 + 1,000 / r ms; pool capacity = 5 x 1,000 / that, in orders per second. When capacity is below 20, the order service falls behind and every order waits.
  - Queues: order time = 5 ms. Backlog of a subscriber after 600 seconds = max(0, 20 - speed) x 600.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Receipts speed | Direct: time per order | Direct: pool capacity | Queues: Receipts backlog after 10 min |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 30 | 100 ms | 50.0 per s | 0 |
  | 20 | 117 ms | 42.9 per s | 0 |
  | 10 | 167 ms | 30.0 per s | 6,000 |
  | 6 | 233 ms | 21.4 per s | 8,400 |
  | 4 | 317 ms | 15.8 per s (falls behind) | 9,600 |
  | 2 | 567 ms | 8.8 per s (falls behind) | 10,800 |

  Email and Stats have a backlog of 0 in every row of the queue design.
- **Picture:** the order service on the left, the three subscribers on the right. In the direct design, one link per subscriber and a growing "orders waiting" counter at the order service. In the queue design, a queue card in front of each subscriber with its own count. Counts are drawn after a fast-forward of 10 minutes (the "after 10 minutes" label is on the picture).
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Orders slow: 233 ms each` (direct, capacity at or above 20; warn)
  - `Orders fall behind: only 16 a second` (direct, capacity below 20; bad)
  - `Orders fast; only Receipts waits` (queues, Receipts slower than 20; good)
  - `Everyone keeps up` (queues, Receipts at 20 or more; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the fast-forward is replaced by its final counts.
- **Accessibility:** counts are text; the toggle is a native button with a visible label.
- **Sources:** SNS to SQS fan-out page (source note in chapter 15.7) and Little's law [S5]. The speeds and the pool of 5 are course numbers.
