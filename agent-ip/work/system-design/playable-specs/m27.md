# Module 27 playable specs (video platform at scale)

Five reserved slugs. The builder creates each folder (`frontend/src/canvas/<slug>/`), registers it and writes its `spec.md`. All five sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 11.5 units, use `SvgNode` cards and `VerdictBanner`, take theme tokens only, and keep every verdict at 38 characters or fewer. At most two controls sit outside the picture, each a 44px target with a visible label; every other input is inside the picture. The eleven watch-only scenes of the module (`sysdesign-m27-*`, kind `scene`) already exist as data in `frontend/src/canvas/`. They use the same names (Lamha, API, CDN edge, Storage) and the same numbers as the playables below, so the picture and the play agree.

Every number below was computed with code (the formulas are short and shown). All models are deterministic; none uses a random number at play time. Course numbers shared by the module, defined in chapter 27.1: 1 billion views a day; 3 minutes watched per view; peak 3 times the average; 500,000 uploads a day; a source video of 5 minutes at 8 Mbit/s (300 MB); a ladder of five versions at 0.4, 0.8, 1.5, 3 and 6 Mbit/s (together 11.7 Mbit/s, 439 MB for 300 seconds, so 739 MB stored per video with the source); an average of 2 Mbit/s sent to a viewer; making the whole ladder costs 15 worker-minutes for a 5 minute video (3 times real time); a day is 86,400 seconds. Defined in later chapters: a sending machine pushes 100 Gbit/s and is planned at half (50 Gbit/s); the origin budget is 1 Tbit/s at peak; a shard row takes 1,000 writes a second [S40] and is planned at half (500).

## sysdesign-m27-estimate-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 27.1 beat 2, "Drag the views, find the load".
- **Claim:** For a video platform, views a second are small, but each view lasts minutes, so millions watch at once and the bandwidth is the load. Storage and transcoding are real costs; the ringed card is the number that drives the design.
- **Primitives:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards joined by links, some cards are inputs: tap, then drag sideways or use arrow keys), `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** three draggable cards. Views a day: 100M, 500M, 1B, 2B, 5B (default 1B). Minutes watched per view: 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 (default 3). Uploads a day: 100k, 250k, 500k, 1M, 2M (default 500k). Fixed cards labelled "assumption": the average sent rate (2 Mbit/s), the peak factor (3), the stored size per video (739 MB), the transcode cost (15 worker-minutes per video).
- **Model and formulas:**
  - Views a second = views a day / 86,400.
  - Viewers at once = views a second x minutes x 60 (Little's law, chapter 2.5).
  - Video sent, average = viewers at once x 2 Mbit/s. Video sent, peak = average x 3.
  - Stored a year = uploads a day x 739 MB x 365.
  - Busy machines, average = uploads a day x 15 / 1,440. Busy machines, peak = average x 3.
- **Picture:** three chains that start at their own input card. Chain 1: Views a day > (divide by 86,400 s) Views a second > (x minutes x 60) Viewers at once > (x 2 Mbit/s) Video sent, average > (x 3 peak) Video sent, peak. Chain 2: Uploads a day > (x 739 MB x 365) Stored a year. Chain 3: Uploads a day > (x 15 / 1,440 min) Busy machines, average > (x 3) Busy machines, peak. Dots flow along the links at a speed set by the value. Format: `11,574 /s`, `2.1 million`, `4.2 Tbit/s`, `12.5 Tbit/s`, `135 PB`, `5,200`, `15,600`. Ring (tone warn) on the driver card: the peak video-sent card when it is 1 Tbit/s or more, otherwise the stored-a-year card when it is 10 PB or more, otherwise the busy-machines-at-peak card when it is 1,000 or more, otherwise none.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above):

  | Views a day | Minutes | Uploads a day | Views /s | Viewers at once | Sent, peak | Stored a year | Machines, peak |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1B | 3 | 500k | 11,574 | 2.08 million | 12.5 Tbit/s | 134.9 PB | 15,625 |
  | 100M | 3 | 500k | 1,157 | 208,333 | 1.25 Tbit/s | 134.9 PB | 15,625 |
  | 5B | 3 | 500k | 57,870 | 10.4 million | 62.5 Tbit/s | 134.9 PB | 15,625 |
  | 1B | 10 | 500k | 11,574 | 6.94 million | 41.7 Tbit/s | 134.9 PB | 15,625 |
  | 100M | 1 | 2M | 1,157 | 69,444 | 0.42 Tbit/s | 539.5 PB | 62,500 |
  | 100M | 1 | 100k | 1,157 | 69,444 | 0.42 Tbit/s | 27.0 PB | 3,125 |

  At the lowest snaps (100M views a day, 1 minute, 100k uploads) the peak sent is 0.42 Tbit/s and storage is 27 PB, so the storage card is ringed and the verdict reads "Storage drives it: plan cold tiers". The verdict "A few servers could serve this" cannot be reached inside the input ranges; it stays in the model for a builder who widens them.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Bandwidth drives it: a CDN is a must` (peak sent 1 Tbit/s or more; warn)
  - `Storage drives it: plan cold tiers` (peak sent under 1 Tbit/s and stored 10 PB or more; warn)
  - `Transcoding drives it: size the fleet` (the two above not met and peak machines 1,000 or more; warn)
  - `A few servers could serve this` (otherwise; good)
- **Reduced motion:** the dots do not flow; the cards show their final values.
- **Accessibility:** each input card is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "1 billion views a day, 11,574 views a second, 2.08 million viewers at once, 12.5 terabits a second at peak"; the ring is also written as text in the verdict.
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions (chapter 2.3 for the method, chapter 2.5 for Little's law [S5]). No external source. Bit rates and the ladder are listed as "source needed" in the chapter.

## sysdesign-m27-design-stepper

- **Chapter and beat:** 27.2 beat 4, "Step through the design at peak".
- **Claim:** The numbers decide the blocks. Storage and app servers do not cut the bytes; a CDN with a high hit rate does. Processing and counting then get their own paths.
- **Primitives:** `design-stepper.tsx` with a `DesignSpec` (`models/design-stepper.ts`): five steps, Back and Next under the picture, step chips on top. One toggle in step 3 (an action with two phases). `SvgNode` cards, `VerdictBanner`.
- **Input:** Back and Next (or tap a step chip). In step 3 only: a toggle for the hit rate, 95% or 70% (default 95%).
- **Model and formulas** (peak sent 12.5 Tbit/s, a sending machine planned at 50 Gbit/s, origin budget 1 Tbit/s, 15,625 busy machines at peak, a viral video at 5,000 views a second, shard planned at 500 writes a second):
  1. One box: app and video files on one server. Times its plan = 12,500 Gbit/s / 50 Gbit/s = 250.
  2. Files in object storage, stateless API servers for pages. Storage sends 12.5 Tbit/s, which is 12.5 times the 1 Tbit/s budget.
  3. A CDN with a 95% hit rate: storage sends 12.5 x 0.05 = 0.625 Tbit/s (0.63), under the budget. Toggle to 70%: 12.5 x 0.30 = 3.75 Tbit/s (3.8), 3.75 times the budget.
  4. A queue and workers for processing (the upload path, chapter 27.3). The machines card shows 15,600 at peak (15,625 rounded to the hundred).
  5. Sharded counters (chapter 27.5): a viral video at 5,000 views a second is spread over 16 shards, 313 writes a second each, 63% of the 500 plan.
- **Picture:** left to right: Viewers > (API servers) and > (CDN edge, from step 3) > (Storage). In step 4 a second row appears: Uploader > Raw storage > Queue > Workers > Video storage. In step 5 a Counter card with a row of 16 small shard boxes appears beside the API. A status pill on the main card of each step: step 1 "250x plan", step 2 "12.5 Tbit/s", step 3 "0.63 Tbit/s" (or "3.8 Tbit/s" in the 70% phase, tone bad), step 4 "15,600 peak", step 5 "313 /s each". Requests run through as dots; in step 3 nineteen in twenty (or seven in ten) turn back at the CDN.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Step | Card to watch | Value |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | Server | 250 times its plan |
  | 2 | Storage | 12.5 Tbit/s, 12.5 times the budget |
  | 3, 95% hits | Storage | 0.63 Tbit/s, 0.63 times the budget |
  | 3, 70% hits | Storage | 3.75 Tbit/s, 3.75 times the budget |
  | 4 | Workers | 15,625 machines at peak |
  | 5, 16 shards | Counter | 5,000 / 16 = 312.5 writes a second each |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `One box: 250 times its plan` (step 1; bad)
  - `Storage must send 12.5 Tbit/s` (step 2; bad)
  - `CDN: storage now sends 0.63 Tbit/s` (step 3, 95%; good)
  - `At 70% hits storage sends 3.8 Tbit/s` (step 3, 70%; bad)
  - `Processing runs off the play path` (step 4; good)
  - `Counting spreads over 16 rows` (step 5; good)
- **Reduced motion:** dots are replaced by arrows with counts; the final frame of each step shows its pill.
- **Accessibility:** step chips and the hit-rate toggle are buttons with visible labels; each pill is read out as text.
- **Sources:** Course numbers (chapter 27.2 fold). The 50 Gbit/s plan follows chapter 2.5 (plan a machine at about half busy). The 1,000 write units a second per partition behind the shard plan is [S40].

## sysdesign-m27-backlog-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 27.3 beat 5, "Drag the uploads, watch the backlog".
- **Claim:** The transcode line grows by uploads minus capacity during the busy hour, and shrinks afterwards only by the spare capacity, so the wait at the end of the busy hour is the backlog divided by the capacity.
- **Primitives:** `traffic-playground.tsx` (arrivals, workers, a queue drawn as a line that fills), `picture-slider.tsx` or `snap-stops-rail.tsx` for the two inputs inside the picture, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** uploads a second during the busy hour, on a snap rail: 5.8 (average), 11.6, 17.4 (peak, 3 times), 26, 35 (default 17.4). Transcode machines on a snap rail: 4,000, 8,000, 12,000, 16,000, 20,000 (default 12,000). Arrow keys step one snap.
- **Model and formulas:** a video is one job of 15 worker-minutes = 900 worker-seconds, so capacity is W / 900 videos a second. The day has three hours in one-minute steps: hour 1 at the chosen rate, hours 2 and 3 at the average of 5.8 a second. Each minute: backlog = max(0, backlog + (arrivals - capacity) x 60). Wait at the end of hour 1 = backlog at minute 60 / capacity, in minutes. Empty at = the first minute after 60 at which the backlog is 0 (round the backlog to the nearest 0.5 video before comparing).
- **Picture:** a line chart of backlog (videos waiting) over the three hours, with the busy hour shaded, a dashed line at the 10 minute wait target (backlog = 10 x 60 x capacity), a marker at the end of hour 1 labelled with the backlog and the wait, and a marker where the line returns to zero (or the text "not within 3 hours"). Above the chart: a worker card with the machine count and a small queue icon that fills with the backlog. Arrivals run as dots into the queue at a speed set by the rate; completed videos leave at the capacity.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above):

  | Uploads a second | Machines | Capacity a second | Backlog at end of hour 1 | Wait at that point | Empty at |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 5.8 | 4,000 | 4.44 | 4,880 | 18 min | not within 3 hours |
  | 5.8 | 8,000 | 8.89 | 0 | 0 | no backlog |
  | 11.6 | 4,000 | 4.44 | 25,760 | 97 min | not within 3 hours |
  | 11.6 | 8,000 | 8.89 | 9,760 | 18 min | minute 113 |
  | 11.6 | 12,000 | 13.33 | 0 | 0 | no backlog |
  | 17.4 | 4,000 | 4.44 | 46,640 | 175 min | not within 3 hours |
  | 17.4 | 8,000 | 8.89 | 30,640 | 57 min | not within 3 hours |
  | 17.4 | 12,000 | 13.33 | 14,640 | 18 min | minute 93 |
  | 17.4 | 16,000 | 17.78 | 0 | 0 | no backlog |
  | 26 | 12,000 | 13.33 | 45,600 | 57 min | minute 161 |
  | 26 | 16,000 | 17.78 | 29,600 | 28 min | minute 102 |
  | 26 | 20,000 | 22.22 | 13,600 | 10 min | minute 74 |
  | 35 | 16,000 | 17.78 | 62,000 | 58 min | minute 147 |
  | 35 | 20,000 | 22.22 | 46,000 | 34 min | minute 107 |
  | 35 | 8,000 | 8.89 | 94,000 | 176 min | not within 3 hours |

  A fleet of 15,625 machines has a capacity of 17.36 videos a second, which matches the 17.4 peak almost exactly. It is the value chapter 27.1 quotes. It is not on the snap rail, so the 16,000 row stands for it.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Line never empties: add machines` (the backlog is still above 0 at minute 180; bad; checked first)
  - `No backlog: watchable at once` (backlog at the end of hour 1 is 0; good)
  - `Wait of 8 min: inside the target` (wait of 10 minutes or less, with the real number; good)
  - `Wait of 18 min: over the target` (wait over 10 and under 60 minutes, with the real number; warn)
  - `Wait of 97 min: far over the target` (wait of 60 minutes or more, with the real number; bad)
- **Reduced motion:** the chart is drawn complete; no dots move.
- **Accessibility:** both rails are `role="slider"` with value text such as "17.4 uploads a second, 12,000 machines, 14,640 videos waiting after one hour, a wait of 18 minutes, empty at minute 93".
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on course assumptions; the model is the backlog model of chapter 15.2, with Little's law [S5]. Queues deliver at least once [S83].

## sysdesign-m27-skew-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 27.4 beat 5, "Drag the skew, watch the origin".
- **Claim:** The CDN hit rate depends on how views are spread over the catalogue (the skew) and on the size of the cache. A steep skew needs a small cache; a mild skew leaves a large share of plays to the origin, whatever the cache size within reach.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` or `snap-stops-rail.tsx` (two rails inside the picture), `log-ruler.tsx` for the cache size, `SvgNode` for the Edge and Origin cards, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** the skew s on a snap rail: 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 (default 1.0). The cache size C (videos kept at an edge) on a log rail with snaps 100 thousand, 1 million, 10 million, 100 million (default 1 million). Arrow keys step one snap.
- **Model and formulas** (catalogue N = 1 billion videos; peak sent 12.5 Tbit/s; origin budget 1 Tbit/s): a Zipf shape where the video ranked r gets views in proportion to 1 / r^s. The cache holds the C most-watched videos. Hit rate = H(C, s) / H(N, s) with H(n, s) = sum of r^-s for r from 1 to n. Compute H exactly up to n = 100,000, then add the integral from 100,000 to n of x^-s (for s = 1 use the natural log of n / 100,000), plus the Euler-Maclaurin correction (n^-s - K^-s) / 2 + s / 12 x (K^(-s-1) - n^(-s-1)) with K = 100,000. Origin = 12.5 x (1 - hit rate) Tbit/s.
- **Picture:** top, a rank axis on a log scale from 1 to 1 billion with the popularity curve drawn as a falling line (height is share of views per video), the first C ranks shaded as "kept at the edge", and the share of views under the shaded part written on it ("68% of plays"). Bottom, an Edge card (hit rate as a percentage) and an Origin card (Tbit/s), with a bar for the 1 Tbit/s budget and a marker for the origin load. Requests flow as dots, the share that reaches the origin set by 1 minus the hit rate.
- **Numbers to check** (computed with the formulas above; each cell is hit rate, then origin Tbit/s at peak):

  | Skew | 100 thousand | 1 million | 10 million | 100 million |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0.8 | 14.6%, 10.67 | 24.1%, 9.49 | 39.0%, 7.63 | 62.6%, 4.68 |
  | 0.9 | 31.7%, 8.54 | 43.4%, 7.08 | 58.1%, 5.23 | 76.7%, 2.92 |
  | 1.0 | 56.8%, 5.41 | 67.6%, 4.05 | 78.4%, 2.70 | 89.2%, 1.35 |
  | 1.1 | 79.6%, 2.55 | 86.6%, 1.68 | 92.1%, 0.99 | 96.5%, 0.44 |
  | 1.2 | 92.4%, 0.95 | 95.7%, 0.54 | 97.8%, 0.27 | 99.2%, 0.11 |
  | 1.3 | 97.5%, 0.31 | 98.8%, 0.15 | 99.5%, 0.06 | 99.8%, 0.02 |

  A check of the approximation: H(200,000, 1.0) is 12.78329081 both exactly and by the formula, and H(300,000, 0.8) is 57.84902932 both ways.
- **Verdicts:**
  - `Origin under its 1 Tbit/s budget` (origin 1.0 Tbit/s or less; good)
  - `Origin sends 1.7 Tbit/s: over budget` (origin above 1.0 and up to 3.0, with the real number; warn)
  - `Origin sends 7.1 Tbit/s: over budget` (origin above 3.0, with the real number; bad)
- **Reduced motion:** no flowing dots; the curve and the numbers update at once.
- **Accessibility:** each rail is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "skew 1.0, cache of 1 million videos, 67.6 percent of plays answered at the edge, the origin sends 4.05 terabits a second, 4 times its budget".
- **Sources:** Arithmetic on a Zipf shape with invented exponents (source needed for a real figure, listed in the chapter). Origin budget and catalogue size are course numbers. The 80 ms read and 160 ms round trip used in the next beat are napkin-math [S3].

## sysdesign-m27-shards-play

- **Chapter and beat:** 27.5 beat 3, "Drag the views, count the shards".
- **Claim:** One row takes at most 1,000 writes a second, so a viral video needs enough shards that the busiest shard stays under half of that (500). More shards fix the writes and make a full read touch more rows, which is why the page reads a merged total.
- **Primitives:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the views a second and `snap-stops-rail.tsx` for the number of shards (both inside the picture), `block-map.tsx` or `SvgNode` boxes for the shard row, `VerdictBanner`. No toggle.
- **Input:** views a second for one video on a snap rail: 500, 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 10,000, 30,000 (default 5,000). Shards on a snap rail: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64 (default 4). Arrow keys step one snap.
- **Model and formulas:** writes a second per shard = views / shards (even spread; no random number at play time). Share of the row limit = per shard / 1,000 [S40]. Share of the plan = per shard / 500. Reads for one full read of the count = shards. Merge reads every 5 seconds = shards. Fewest shards that fit the plan = ceil(views / 500).
- **Picture:** a row of shard boxes (up to 64 small boxes, wrapped in rows of 16), each filled in proportion to its share of the 1,000 limit, with a line across at half. Boxes at or above the limit are drawn in the bad tone, between the plan and the limit in the warn tone, under the plan in the good tone. Two numbers under the row: "per shard: N writes a second" and "a full read: N rows". A dashed marker "fewest that fit: N" beside the shard rail.
- **Numbers to check** (writes a second per shard, rounded):

  | Views a second | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | Fewest that fit |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 500 | 500 | 250 | 125 | 63 | 31 | 16 | 8 | 1 |
  | 1,000 | 1,000 | 500 | 250 | 125 | 63 | 31 | 16 | 2 |
  | 2,000 | 2,000 | 1,000 | 500 | 250 | 125 | 63 | 31 | 4 |
  | 5,000 | 5,000 | 2,500 | 1,250 | 625 | 313 | 156 | 78 | 10 |
  | 10,000 | 10,000 | 5,000 | 2,500 | 1,250 | 625 | 313 | 156 | 20 |
  | 30,000 | 30,000 | 15,000 | 7,500 | 3,750 | 1,875 | 938 | 469 | 60 |

- **Verdicts:**
  - `Throttled: 5x the row limit` (per shard above 1,000; the multiple is per shard / 1,000, with one decimal under 10; bad)
  - `Over its plan: busy seconds throttle` (per shard from 501 to 1,000; warn)
  - `Each shard under half its limit` (per shard from 50 to 500; good)
  - `Too many shards for this rate` (per shard under 50; warn, and the picture names the cost: "a full read touches N rows")
- **Reduced motion:** the boxes fill at once; no motion.
- **Accessibility:** each rail is a `role="slider"` with value text such as "5,000 views a second, 16 shards, 313 writes a second each, 31 percent of the row limit, a full read touches 16 rows"; the colour of a box is also written as text in the verdict.
- **Sources:** DynamoDB allows 1,000 write units a second per partition [S40]; planning at half follows chapter 2.5. The even spread is a simplification: with a random choice the busiest shard in a second is a little above the average (stated in the chapter fold).
