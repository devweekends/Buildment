## 7. Playable specs

Module 10 reserves five playable slugs. None of their folders exist yet. Every verdict is 38 characters or fewer; every number below was computed with the reference clocks of chapter 10.8 or by the arithmetic shown.

### sysdesign-m10-drift-sync (10.2 beat "Drag the drift and the sync interval")
- **Claim:** Two clocks that drift in opposite directions and are synced every T seconds are at most 2 x drift x T apart; while that gap is above the gap between two writes, the later write can get the smaller timestamp.
- **Model:** Node A runs d ppm fast, node B runs d ppm slow, both exactly right at each sync. Two writes land 1 ms apart. The gap at t seconds after a sync is 2 x d x t microseconds. A sync leaves no error in this model (the beat's fold says real syncs leave up to about 1 ms [S64]).
- **Formulas:**
  - Largest gap in milliseconds = 2 x d x T / 1000, with d in ppm and T in seconds.
  - The gap passes 1 ms after t1 = 500 / d seconds.
  - Share of each cycle with the gap above 1 ms = max(0, 1 - t1 / T).
- **Check points (d = 50 ppm):**

  | Sync every | Largest gap | Gap above 1 ms for |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 s | 0.1 ms | 0% of the cycle |
  | 10 s | 1 ms | 0% (the gap only touches 1 ms) |
  | 1 min | 6 ms | 83% |
  | 10 min | 60 ms | 98% |
  | 1 h | 360 ms | 99.7% |
  | 1 day | 8.64 s | 99.99% |

  Other drifts: d = 10 ppm gives t1 = 50 s, so a 1 min sync is above 1 ms for 17% of the cycle. d = 1 ppm gives t1 = 500 s, so a 10 min sync is above 1 ms for 17%.
- **Inputs:** two rails drawn in the picture. Drift: 1, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100 ppm (snap points, log spacing). Sync interval: 1 s, 10 s, 1 min, 10 min, 1 h, 1 day (snap points, log spacing).
- **What moves:** a sawtooth line of the gap over time. It climbs, and drops to 0 at each sync. A dashed threshold line sits at 1 ms. The part of each tooth above the line is filled red. Two write tags (1 ms apart) slide along the time axis and flip order when they sit in the red part.
- **Verdicts:**

  | When | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | Largest gap is 1 ms or less | "Safe: the gap never passes 1 ms" |
  | Otherwise | "Flips after N s: P% of each cycle" (N = 500 / d, P = share above) |
  | Interval of a day, drift above 5 ppm | "A day: wrong almost all the time" |

- **Primitive:** `picture-slider.tsx` twice (log rails), with a small sawtooth chart drawn in the picture; `beat-frame.tsx` for the limits fold.
- **Sources:** 1 ppm = 86 ms a day, most clocks within 50 ppm [S52]; NTP error on a quiet network within 1 ms [S64].

### sysdesign-m10-lamport-play (10.3 beat "Predict each counter before it appears")
- **Claim:** A receive sets the counter to the larger of the node's own value and the stamp, then adds 1; a counter never goes down.
- **Model:** Three nodes, A, B and C, start at 0, 5 and 0. A local event adds 1. A send adds 1 and attaches the new value as the stamp. A receive sets the counter to max(own, stamp) + 1. The learner works through a scripted run, then may continue freely.
- **Formulas:**
  - Local event or send: counter = counter + 1.
  - Receive: counter = max(counter, stamp) + 1.
- **Check points (the scripted run):**

  | Step | What the learner does | Counter after |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | tap A (local event) | A = 1 |
  | 2 | A sends to B | A = 2, stamp 2; B = max(5, 2) + 1 = 6 |
  | 3 | B sends to C | B = 7, stamp 7; C = max(0, 7) + 1 = 8 |
  | 4 | C sends to A | C = 9, stamp 9; A = max(2, 9) + 1 = 10 |
  | 5 | A sends to B | A = 11, stamp 11; B = max(7, 11) + 1 = 12 |

- **Inputs:** tap a node for an event; tap a node and then another to send. Before the new value appears, the learner taps a guess from up to three chips: own + 1, stamp + 1, and the larger of the two + 1 (merged when two are equal). A wrong guess shakes and says why; the right one flies in.
- **What moves:** three lanes with message arrows. The receiving counter shows two numbers merging into the larger, then a "+ 1". A faint trail marks past values, so the learner sees that nothing goes down.
- **Verdicts:**

  | When | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | After a receive | "max(5, 2) + 1 = 6" (numbers filled in; always under 24 characters) |
  | Receiver ahead of the stamp | "Receiver was ahead: it only adds 1" |
  | Receiver behind the stamp | "Receiver was behind: it jumps forward" |
  | After the fifth scripted step | "Every receive lands after its send" |

- **Primitive:** none covers three lanes with tap-to-send. Build on `svg-node.tsx`, `beat-frame.tsx` and the tap-and-feedback flow of `sorter.tsx` for the guess chips; the `sim` clocks (Lamport) supply the counters.
- **Sources:** S58 (Lamport 1978: IR1, IR2, Clock Condition).

### sysdesign-m10-vector-compare (10.4 beat "Pick two events and compare")
- **Claim:** Comparing two vectors slot by slot says whether one event happened before the other or the two are concurrent.
- **Model:** Nine events on three nodes. Vectors are [A, B, C]:

  | Event | What happens | Vector |
  |---|---|---|
  | A1 | A, local event | [1,0,0] |
  | A2 | A sends to B | [2,0,0] |
  | B1 | B, local event | [0,1,0] |
  | B2 | B receives from A2 | [2,2,0] |
  | C1 | C, local event | [0,0,1] |
  | B3 | B sends to C | [2,3,0] |
  | C2 | C receives from B3 | [2,3,2] |
  | C3 | C sends to A | [2,3,3] |
  | A3 | A receives from C3 | [3,3,3] |

- **Formulas:** x before y when every slot of x is at most the slot of y and they differ; concurrent when each is bigger in at least one slot. Missing slots count as 0.
- **Check points:** 36 pairs in all: 29 ordered and 7 concurrent. The concurrent pairs are A1-B1, A1-C1, A2-B1, A2-C1, B1-C1, B2-C1 and B3-C1. Other examples: A2 before B2; B2 before C2; A3 after C3; A1 before A3.
- **Inputs:** tap one event, then a second. A third tap starts again. Keyboard: arrow keys move through the events; Enter selects.
- **What moves:** the two chosen events light up with their vectors side by side. The deciding slots are circled: for an ordered pair, a slot that is smaller on the "before" side; for a concurrent pair, one circled slot on each side. If a chain of messages joins the two events, the chain glows. A small counter shows how many of the 7 concurrent pairs the learner has found.
- **Verdicts:**

  | When | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | x before y | "A2 happened before B2" (names filled in) |
  | x after y | "A3 happened after C3" |
  | Concurrent | "Concurrent: each leads in one slot" |
  | All 7 concurrent pairs found | "All 7 concurrent pairs found" |

- **Primitive:** `block-map.tsx` (tap cards, path highlight) for selecting events and showing message paths.
- **Sources:** S45, S52.

### sysdesign-m10-hlc-skewed (10.5 beat "Skew the clocks and watch the stamps")
- **Claim:** A hybrid stamp puts every receive after its send even when the receiver's clock is far behind; the time part runs ahead of the slow clock and the counter counts until the clock catches up.
- **Model:** Three nodes with clock offsets from true time (default A = 0, B = -40 ms, C = +25 ms). True time advances in the scripted run. Stamps follow the hybrid rules of chapter 10.5: on a tick, if the clock is past l then (l, c) = (clock, 0), otherwise c + 1; on a receive, l = max(l, message l, clock), and c is set from the tied inputs (both tie: max + 1; own l: c + 1; message l: message c + 1; clock alone: 0).
- **Check points (offsets 0, -40, +25; 10 ms between send and receive):**

  | True time | Event | Node clock | Stamp |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 100 | A sends to B | 100 | (100,0) |
  | 110 | B receives | 70 | (100,1) |
  | 120 | B local event | 80 | (100,2) |
  | 130 | B sends to C | 90 | (100,3) |
  | 140 | C receives | 165 | (165,0) |
  | 150 | C sends to A | 175 | (175,0) |
  | 160 | A receives | 160 | (175,1) |

  With plain wall-clock stamps the same run gives A's send 100 and B's receive 70: the effect is stamped before its cause. The distance of l ahead of B's own clock is 30 ms at its receive (100 - 70), then 20 and 10 ms at its next two events; the largest anywhere is 30 ms, inside the 65 ms spread of the three offsets.
- **Inputs:** drag a node's clock sideways to set its offset (-50 to +50 ms, snap points 0, +-10, +-25, +-50). Tap "send" on a node and then the node it sends to. One toggle outside the picture: "Wall clock" or "Hybrid".
- **What moves:** three lanes with a true-time axis. Each node shows its own clock reading and its (l, c) stamp. A message carries its stamp. When the receiver's l is ahead of its own clock, a bracket shows the gap in ms. The counter ticks up on later events until the clock passes l.
- **Verdicts:**

  | When | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | Wall mode, receive stamp at or below send | "Receive stamped before its send" |
  | Hybrid mode, any receive | "Receive (100,1) after send (100,0)" (numbers filled in) |
  | l is ahead of the clock | "Time part is 30 ms ahead of the clock" |
  | Clock passes l | "Clock caught up: counter back to 0" |

- **Primitive:** none covers drag-the-clock plus tap-to-send. Build on `svg-node.tsx`, `beat-frame.tsx` and `picture-slider.tsx` (for the offset rails); the `sim` hybrid clock supplies the stamps (same rules as `HybridClock` in chapter 10.8).
- **Sources:** S59.

### sysdesign-m10-snowflake-bits (10.7 beat "Change the bits, then break the clock")
- **Claim:** The bits of a 64-bit ID are a budget between time, machine and count; a clock that steps back repeats IDs unless the generator remembers its last time.
- **Model:** 63 usable bits split into time t, worker w and count s, with t = 63 - w - s. A generator makes r IDs per millisecond (default 20).
- **Formulas:**
  - Machines = 2^w.
  - IDs per millisecond per machine = 2^s.
  - IDs per second for the fleet = 2^w x 2^s x 1,000.
  - Lifetime in years = 2^t ms / 31,557,600,000 ms per year (365.25 days).
  - Clock step back of R ms (the clock reads R ms behind true time): the trusting generator repeats R ms of IDs, so duplicates = r x R. The guarded generator sees a gap of R - 1 ms behind its last time: if R - 1 is at most 10 it waits R - 1 ms with no repeats; otherwise it refuses r x (R - 1 - 10) requests, then waits 10 ms.
- **Check points:**

  | Worker bits | Count bits | Time bits | Machines | IDs per ms per machine | Lifetime | Fleet IDs per second |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10 | 12 | 41 | 1,024 | 4,096 | 69.7 years | 4,194,304,000 |
  | 12 | 10 | 41 | 4,096 | 1,024 | 69.7 years | 4,194,304,000 |
  | 8 | 12 | 43 | 256 | 4,096 | 278.7 years | 1,048,576,000 |
  | 10 | 8 | 45 | 1,024 | 256 | 1,115 years | 262,144,000 |
  | 16 | 16 | 31 | 65,536 | 65,536 | 24.9 days | 4,294,967,296,000 |

  Rollback check points at r = 20 and the default split: R = 2 gives 40 repeated IDs (trusting) and a 1 ms wait (guarded); R = 5 gives 100 and 4 ms; R = 10 gives 200 and 9 ms; R = 50 gives 1,000 and 780 refused requests, then a 10 ms wait.
- **Inputs:** two sliders in the picture for worker bits and count bits (0 to 16, step 1, snap at 8, 10, 12); time bits follow. A "needed per second" input (estimate-chain card, steps 1 thousand, 100 thousand, 10 million, 1 billion). A step-back chip row: 0, 2, 5, 10, 50 ms. A handling toggle: "Trust the clock", "Wait, then refuse".
- **What moves:** a 64-cell bit bar whose time, worker and count segments grow and shrink as the sliders move; the cards for machines, IDs per ms and lifetime update. After a step back, a strip of 20 IDs per ms shows repeated IDs in red, or a wait ring, or refused requests.
- **Verdicts:**

  | When | Verdict |
  |---|---|
  | Fleet rate covers the need | "Enough: N IDs a second" (N shortened, such as 4.2 billion) |
  | Fleet rate below the need | "Too few: the count field runs out" |
  | Lifetime under 10 years | "Time runs out in N years" |
  | Trusting, step back above 1 ms | "Trusting: 100 repeated IDs" (number filled in) |
  | Guarded, small step | "Waits 4 ms, no repeats" |
  | Guarded, big step | "Refuses 780 requests, no repeats" |

- **Primitive:** `estimate-chain.tsx` (cards with inputs and links) for the bit budget and the needed rate; the step-back chips and the strip of IDs are a small addition to the same picture.
- **Sources:** S65 (41, 10 and 12 bits; 69 years; 4,096 per ms per machine; guards against a clock going backwards); S66 (UUIDv7 48-bit time). Guarded-generator behaviour is the one built in challenge 9, part 5.
