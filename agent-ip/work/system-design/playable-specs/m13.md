# Module 13 playable specs (leaderless stores, Dynamo and conflict resolution)

Six reserved playable slugs. None has a folder yet; each chapter already places `<InteractiveCanvas slug="..." />` and tells the learner what to drag or tap (quoted under "Beat text"). All six sit on `BeatFrame` (`primitives/beat-frame.tsx`), draw on a 420 wide viewBox with labels of at least 13 units, use `SvgNode` cards, `DotGrid` and `VerdictBanner`, take at most two controls outside the picture (each a 44px target with a visible label), and end on a verdict of 38 characters or fewer. Each model is pure (no React) in a `model.ts` beside the canvas, with the "numbers to check" below as its unit tests. Source ids refer to `agent-ip/work/system-design/01-research-dossier.md`. Hana, Dina, Souq and every setting marked "made up" or "illustrative" are course examples. The audit flags "no playable beat" on 13.2 to 13.7 until these exist; that flag is expected.

The 18 data-only scenes of the module already exist (`frontend/src/canvas/sysdesign-m13-*`). The orchestrator registers all 24 slugs.

## sysdesign-m13-ring-write (13.2, beat 2: "Drag the key, skip repeated machines")

- **Beat text:** "Drag the key around the ring and tap Write. Three copies appear, each on a different machine. Now turn off Skip repeats and drag the key to 10. Two points of machine A come first, so only two machines hold the key. Skipping keeps every copy on a different machine."
- **Claim:** A key's copies go to the first N distinct machines clockwise from its point. Machines own several points (virtual nodes), so a walk over the next N points can meet one machine twice; the preference list skips repeats.
- **Model:** a ring of positions 0 to 359. Five machines with two points each (illustrative positions, chosen so repeats occur): A at 20 and 55, B at 90 and 175, C at 140 and 320, D at 215 and 345, E at 250 and 290. N = 3. For a key at position p, the walk starts at the first point at or after p (wrapping past 359 to the first point). With Skip repeats on, the list keeps each machine the first time it appears and skips its other points, until it holds 3 machines. With Skip repeats off, the list is the next 3 points, whatever machine they belong to. The first machine in the list is the coordinator.
- **Inputs:** drag the key marker round the ring (integer positions 0 to 359, snap to 1; arrow keys step 1, Page keys step 15). Default 10. Two controls outside the picture: a toggle "Skip repeats" (on by default) and a "Write" button (replays the copy animation).
- **What moves:** the key marker on the ring; the walk highlights points clockwise with a thin trail; three copies fly from the coordinator to their machine cards (on a card row below the ring); machine cards show their points as small dots. With Skip repeats off and a repeat in the walk, the repeated point is shown twice on the same card and the card count under the ring reads "2 machines".
- **Numbers to check** (N = 3):

  | Key at | Skip on | Skip off | Machines with Skip off |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | A, B, C | A, A, B | 2 |
  | 10 | A, B, C | A, A, B | 2 |
  | 20 | A, B, C | A, A, B | 2 |
  | 21 | A, B, C | A, B, C | 3 |
  | 60 | B, C, D | B, C, B | 2 |
  | 130 | C, B, D | C, B, D | 3 |
  | 240 | E, C, D | E, E, C | 2 |
  | 300 | C, D, A | C, D, A | 3 |
  | 335 | D, A, B | D, A, A | 2 |
  | 359 | A, B, C | A, A, B | 2 |

- **Verdicts:** Skip on: "Copies on B, C and D" (names filled in; at most 22 characters). Skip off with 3 machines: "Copies on C, B and D". Skip off with 2 machines: "Only 2 machines hold a copy" (27). Before the first Write tap: no verdict, only the walk.
- **Primitive:** `picture-slider.tsx` for the drag (a circular rail mapped to 0 to 359), `svg-node.tsx` for the machine cards, a ring drawn as an SVG circle as in `sysdesign-m9-ring-play`. No existing primitive draws a ring with a walk.
- **Limits text:** "Five machines with two points each, at made-up positions. A real ring hashes the machine name and many points per machine (chapter 9.3), and real stores also spread copies over racks or zones."
- **Reduced motion:** the copies appear in place; the walk shows as numbered points 1, 2, 3.
- **Accessibility:** the ring is a `role="slider"` with value text "key at 10, copies on A, B and C".
- **Sources:** [S45] (consistent hashing with virtual nodes; preference list), [S40]. The "distinct physical nodes" rule is flagged `source needed` in the chapter.

## sysdesign-m13-cluster-heal (13.3, beat 3: "Take nodes down, write, bring them back")

- **Beat text:** "Tap nodes to take them down or up, then tap Write and Read. Under Strict, writes need the usual three. Under Sloppy, they go to stand-ins. Take the three usual nodes down, write, bring them back and read at once: the read returns the old value. Wait a moment, read again, and it is fixed."
- **Claim:** A sloppy write lands on stand-ins with hints and succeeds through a failure; a read of the usual nodes right after they return can miss it, until the hints are handed over. Without hints, it never heals.
- **Model:** five nodes N1 to N5 on a ring. One key `x`, currently 6 on every node. Ring order for the key: N1, N2, N3, N4, N5. N = 3, W = 2, R = 2. The usual nodes are N1, N2, N3; the stand-ins are N4, N5, in that order. Model time runs in ticks of 1 second of the animation. Rules:
  - **Write** (`x` becomes the next number). The coordinator is the first node in ring order that is up. Strict: targets are the usual three. Sloppy: each usual node that is down is replaced by the next unused stand-in that is up, which stores the value as a hint for the owner; if no stand-in is left, that target is missing. A target that is up stores the value one tick later and acks one tick after that. The write is confirmed when at least 2 targets acked; otherwise it is refused.
  - **Read.** The coordinator asks the usual three. Nodes that are down do not answer. It returns the newest value among the first 2 answers, in node order N1, N2, N3. With fewer than 2 live usual nodes the read is refused.
  - **Hints** (Hints on only). At every even tick, a stand-in that is up offers each hint to its owner if the owner is up; the owner stores the value one tick later and the stand-in drops the hint. A stand-in that is down loses its hints.
- **Inputs:** tap a node card to toggle it up or down. Controls outside the picture: a Strict | Sloppy switch, and a Hints On | Off switch. Tapping the Write chip and the Read chip (inside the picture, next to the client icon). A Reset chip restores all nodes and `x = 6`.
- **What moves:** per-node cards with the current `x`, a small hint badge (`hint for N1: 7`) on a stand-in, message dots from the coordinator to its targets, hint dots from a stand-in to its owner, an answer chip next to each live usual node on a Read, and a tick counter.
- **Numbers to check** (Sloppy, Hints on, start with `x = 6` everywhere):

  | Step | State after | Result |
  |---|---|---|
  | Down N1, N2, N3. Write (x = 7). | N4 holds hint for N1 (7), N5 holds hint for N2 (7). N3 has no stand-in. | "Write confirmed": 2 acks (N4 and N5). |
  | Up N1, N2, N3. Read at once. | N1, N2, N3 all hold 6. | Read returns 6 (stale). |
  | Wait to the next even tick plus 1. Read. | N1 = 7 and N2 = 7. N3 = 6. | Read returns 7 (N1 and N2 answer first). |
  | Same, but Hints Off. Wait 10 ticks. Read. | N1, N2, N3 = 6. N4, N5 = 7 (hints never sent). | Read returns 6 for ever. |
  | Strict. Down N1, N2, N3. Write. | No change. | "Write refused: 0 of 2 acks". |
  | Strict. Down N3 only. Write. | N1 = 7, N2 = 7. | "Write confirmed": 2 acks. |

- **Verdicts:** "Write saved on N4 and N5 (hints)" (34). "Write refused: 0 of 2 acks" (26). "Read returned old x = 6" (23). "Read returned new x = 7" (23). "Read refused: fewer than 2 nodes" (32).
- **Primitive:** `traffic-playground` carries tap-to-send and node cards; the hint badge and ring layout are custom drawing on `BeatFrame`. `design-stepper.tsx` is a fallback for the tick counter if a step-through version is easier.
- **Limits text:** "One key, five nodes, a perfect failure detector and made-up times. The hint check runs every 2 seconds here; a real node checks on its own timer. The ring order and the stand-ins are fixed."
- **Reduced motion:** states change without travelling dots; the tick counter and the badges show the result.
- **Accessibility:** each node is a toggle button with the label "N2, up" or "N2, down"; the read result is announced with a live region.
- **Sources:** [S45] (sloppy quorum, hinted handoff), [S52] (the overlap argument assumes the same N nodes). The "hints are kept apart and checked on a timer" detail is flagged `source needed` in the chapter.

## sysdesign-m13-hot-partition (13.4, beat 5: "A hot partition stays capped")

- **Beat text:** "Drag the hot key's writes per second. Without adaptive capacity, the busy partition is held to its even share, 500, and the rest is rejected. Turn adaptive capacity on: the partition can borrow what the others leave unused, up to its own ceiling of 1,000. Push past 1,000, and nothing helps."
- **Claim:** A table's write throughput is shared out over its partitions, so a hot partition can be throttled while the table is far under its total. Adaptive capacity lets it borrow unused throughput, but a single partition has a ceiling of 1,000 writes per second that no spare capacity lifts.
- **Model (illustrative; the share and the borrowing rule are flagged `source needed` in the chapter):** one table provisioned for P = 2,000 write units per second, in 4 partitions. Even share = P / 4 = 500 each. Three background partitions each receive 200 writes per second, 600 in all. The hot partition receives d writes per second (the dragged value). The per-partition ceiling is 1,000 [S40].
  - Adaptive capacity off: accepted = min(d, 500); rejected = d - accepted.
  - Adaptive capacity on: accepted = min(d, 1,000, P - 600) = min(d, 1,000); rejected = d - accepted.
  - Table use = 600 + accepted.
- **Inputs:** drag the hot partition's incoming bar (the picture is the control) from 0 to 2,000, snap to 100; default 800; arrow keys step 100. One toggle outside the picture: Adaptive capacity On | Off (default Off).
- **What moves:** four partition cards in a row, each with an incoming bar, a dashed line at its share (500) that jumps to the ceiling (1,000) when adaptive capacity is on, and a red "rejected" cap above the line; a thin table-use bar underneath with the 2,000 line.
- **Numbers to check:**

  | Hot demand d | Off: accepted / rejected | On: accepted / rejected | Table use (off / on) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 300 | 300 / 0 | 300 / 0 | 900 / 900 |
  | 500 | 500 / 0 | 500 / 0 | 1,100 / 1,100 |
  | 800 | 500 / 300 | 800 / 0 | 1,100 / 1,400 |
  | 1,000 | 500 / 500 | 1,000 / 0 | 1,100 / 1,600 |
  | 1,500 | 500 / 1,000 | 1,000 / 500 | 1,100 / 1,600 |
  | 2,000 | 500 / 1,500 | 1,000 / 1,000 | 1,100 / 1,600 |

- **Verdicts:** Off and d at most 500: "Within its share: nothing rejected" (34). Off and d above 500: "Rejects 300/s: share is only 500" (numbers filled in; 33). On and d at most 1,000: "Borrowed spare capacity: none rejected" (38). On and d above 1,000: "Ceiling 1,000/s: 500/s still rejected" (37).
- **Primitive:** `traffic-playground.tsx` (a source bar, partition cards with limit lines, a pill that turns good, warn or bad) with `picture-slider.tsx` for the drag; `beat-frame.tsx` for the limits fold.
- **Limits text:** "Made-up table: 2,000 write units shared evenly by four partitions, and 200 writes a second on each of the other three. Real tables, and the way spare throughput is lent out, are described in the DynamoDB documentation. The 1,000 writes per second ceiling is the published design limit."
- **Reduced motion:** the bars jump to their values; no animated caps.
- **Accessibility:** the drag is a `role="slider"` with value text "hot key 800 writes a second, 300 rejected".
- **Sources:** [S40] (3,000 read and 1,000 write units per partition), [S44] (hot partitions and throughput dilution). Chapter 9.4 owns the key-level fix (salting).

## sysdesign-m13-lww-skew (13.5, beat 3: "Drag the skew and count wrong winners")

- **Beat text:** "Drag the clock skew and the gap between the two writes. When the gap is bigger than the skew, the later write wins, as it should. When it is smaller, the later write loses. Switch to Concurrent: now one of the two is lost whatever the clocks say. The counter shows wrong winners out of 20 pairs. At a skew of 20 ms, 19 of 20 pairs go wrong."
- **Claim:** Last write wins compares timestamps, so the write that really came later loses whenever the gap between the writes is smaller than the gap between the two clocks. For concurrent writes it always loses one, whatever the clocks.
- **Model:** write 1 happens on node A at real time 20 ms. A's clock is s ms ahead of B's (A reads 20 + s/2, B reads 20 - s/2 for the same moment). Write 2 happens on node B g ms after write 1.
  - stamp1 = 20 + s/2; stamp2 = 20 + g - s/2.
  - Sequential mode (the second client saw the first write finish): write 2 wins when stamp2 > stamp1, that is g > s. It loses when g < s. If g = s the stamps tie and the tie rule decides (shown as "tie").
  - Concurrent mode (neither client saw the other): one write is dropped in every pair whatever g and s.
  - The counter plays 20 pairs with gaps g = 1, 2, 3, ... 20 ms and reports the number of wrong winners (sequential: pairs with g < s; concurrent: all 20 pairs lose one write).
- **Inputs:** two rails drawn in the picture. Skew s: 0 to 30 ms, snap to 0, 2, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30; default 20. Gap g: 1 to 30 ms, snap 1; default 5. One toggle outside the picture: Sequential | Concurrent (default Sequential). The counter always shows the 20-pair total for the current skew.
- **What moves:** a true-time axis with two write markers (write 1 fixed at 20, write 2 at 20 + g); under each marker a stamp tag (stamp1, stamp2) offset by the clock error, with a small arrow from the real position to the stamp; the larger stamp is crowned; a row of 20 small pair squares fills red for wrong winners and amber for ties.
- **Numbers to check** (20 pairs, gaps 1 to 20):

  | Skew s | Wrong winners (g < s) | Ties (g = s) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0 | 0 | 0 |
  | 2 | 1 | 1 |
  | 5 | 4 | 1 |
  | 10 | 9 | 1 |
  | 15 | 14 | 1 |
  | 20 | 19 | 1 |
  | 30 | 20 | 0 |

  Default (s = 20, g = 5, Sequential): stamp1 = 30, stamp2 = 15; write 1 wins; the later write is lost.
- **Verdicts:** Sequential, g above s: "The later write wins: correct" (29). Sequential, g below s: "Later write lost: its clock ran slow" (36). Tie: "Equal stamps: the tie rule decides" (34). Concurrent: "Concurrent: one of the two is lost" (34).
- **Primitive:** `picture-slider.tsx` twice (two rails), `year-strip.tsx` is not suitable; the pair squares are custom SVG. The `sysdesign-m10-drift-sync` playable (module 10) is the closest reference for the timeline.
- **Limits text:** "Two nodes and two writes. Skew is the gap between two clocks right now; real clocks change as they drift and as they are synced (chapter 10.2). The 20 pairs are an illustration, not a measurement."
- **Reduced motion:** markers and tags jump to their positions.
- **Accessibility:** each rail is a `role="slider"` with value text "skew 20 ms" or "gap 5 ms"; the counter is announced as "19 of 20 pairs have the wrong winner".
- **Sources:** [S52] (drift up to 50 ppm, NTP error, last write wins loses concurrent writes), [S76] (2013 Cassandra test, about 28% of acknowledged writes lost, for 2013 versions).

## sysdesign-m13-cart-merge (13.6, beat 3: "Try four merge rules on one conflict")

- **Beat text:** "Pick a merge rule and see the cart it makes. Start with Hana removing milk while Dina adds tea. Then switch to Dina adding milk back. Last write wins, union and remove-by-name each fail one of the two cases. Only tagged adds, the OR-Set of chapter 13.7, get both right. Try each rule on both cases before you read on."
- **Claim:** Each simple merge rule has a blind spot. Union cannot remove, remove-by-name cannot add again, and last write wins loses one side. Tagged adds (OR-Set) get both cases right.
- **Model:** the cart starts as {eggs, milk}. Two concurrent edits, chosen by the case toggle:
  - Case 1: Hana removes milk, Dina adds tea. Intended cart: {eggs, tea}.
  - Case 2: Hana removes milk, Dina adds milk again. Intended cart: {eggs, milk} (Dina did not see the removal).
  - Rules. Last write wins: the cart with the later stamp wins as a whole; for the picture Dina's save is later (stamp 12 against 10). Union: the union of the two carts. Remove by name: items = (union of both carts) minus (every name removed by either side). Tagged adds: each add has a unique tag; the cart holds an item if one of its tags is not removed; a remove marks only the tags it saw.
- **Results:**

  | Rule | Case 1 (remove milk, add tea) | Case 2 (remove milk, add milk) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Last write wins | {eggs, milk, tea}: milk is back (Dina's cart wins) | {eggs, milk}: right by luck; wrong if Hana's stamp were later |
  | Union | {eggs, milk, tea}: milk is back | {eggs, milk}: right |
  | Remove by name | {eggs, tea}: right | {eggs}: Dina's add is lost |
  | Tagged adds (OR-Set) | {eggs, tea}: right | {eggs, milk}: right |

- **Inputs:** four rule chips in the picture (tap one); one toggle outside the picture: Case 1 | Case 2 (default Case 1), and a second control: a Stamps flip button that swaps which side has the later stamp, so the last write wins row can show its other result (Case 1: {eggs}, the tea is lost; Case 2: {eggs}, Dina's add is lost). Default rule: Union.
- **What moves:** two lanes (Hana, Dina) with their edits as chips on a short timeline; a merge arrow into a result cart; the result is compared with the intended cart: items that are right get a check, a returned item gets a "back" tag, a lost item gets a "lost" tag.
- **Verdicts:** Wrong result with a returned item: "Milk came back" (14). Wrong with a lost add: "Dina's add was lost" (19). Right: "Both people's intent kept" (25). Last write wins, right by luck: "Right only if Dina's stamp is later" (35).
- **Primitive:** `sorter.tsx` (tap a chip, see the answer and why) with a small timeline drawn in SVG; or `block-map.tsx` for the four rule cards.
- **Limits text:** "A cart of three items and two edits. Real merge rules also need clocks, tags and context (chapters 13.6 and 13.7). The stamps 10 and 12 are made up."
- **Reduced motion:** the result cart appears without an arrow animation.
- **Accessibility:** rule chips are radio buttons; the result is announced as "Union, case 1: eggs, milk and tea. Milk came back."
- **Sources:** [S45] (Dynamo cart: merging never loses an add, deleted items can resurface), [S74] (2P-Set and OR-Set), [S43].

## sysdesign-m13-crdt-play (13.7, beat 5: "Merge in any order, three set types")

- **Beat text:** "Three replicas hold different changes: A removes milk and rice, B adds rice again, C adds eggs. Tap to merge any replica into any other, in any order. Switch between the plain set, remove-by-name and the OR-Set. All three agree, in every order. Only the OR-Set agrees on the right cart: eggs and rice."
- **Claim:** Merge functions that are commutative, associative and idempotent make every merge order end in the same state, for any CRDT. Converging does not mean being right: only the OR-Set ends in the cart the people meant.
- **Model:** three replicas A, B, C. All start with {milk, rice}. Local changes: A removes milk and rice, B adds rice again, C adds eggs. Three set types, selected by a switch:
  - **Plain set** (state: names; remove deletes the name locally; merge = union of names).
  - **Remove by name** (2P-Set; state: names and removed names; a removed name stays removed for ever; merge = union of both parts; visible = names minus removed names).
  - **OR-Set** (state: tags per element and removed tags; the starting milk has tag s:1 and rice has s:2; A's removals mark s:1 and s:2; B's add of rice gets tag B:1; C's add of eggs gets C:1; merge = union of tags and of removed tags; visible = elements with a tag that is not removed).
  - A tap on replica X then replica Y merges X's state into Y.
- **Outcomes** (merging all three replicas together, in any of the 6 orders):

  | Type | Every order gives | Is it what people meant? (eggs and rice) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Plain set | {eggs, milk, rice} | No: milk came back |
  | Remove by name | {eggs} | No: Dina's rice was lost |
  | OR-Set | {eggs, rice} | Yes |

- **Inputs:** tap one replica, then another (a directed merge, source then target). A third tap starts again. Keyboard: arrow keys move between replicas, Enter selects. Controls outside the picture: a set type switch (Plain | By name | OR-Set) and a Reset button. Switching type resets the replicas.
- **What moves:** three replica cards with their current sets (OR-Set shows tags as small chips: milk s:1 removed, rice s:2 removed, rice B:1, eggs C:1); a merge arrow from source to target; a counter of merges used; when all three replicas hold the same state, a verdict appears.
- **Verdicts:** All equal, Plain: "All agree, but milk came back" (30). All equal, By name: "All agree, but rice was lost" (28). All equal, OR-Set: "All agree: eggs and rice. Correct." (35). Not equal yet: "Replicas still differ: keep merging" (35).
- **Primitive:** `block-map.tsx` (tap cards, path highlight) for the replica cards and merge arrows; the sim kit `cluster` renderer would fit but is not built.
- **Limits text:** "Three replicas and three changes. State-based merges only (each replica sends its whole state). Real OR-Sets keep one tag per add, so their state grows (chapter 13.8)."
- **Reduced motion:** the target card updates without an arrow animation.
- **Accessibility:** merge is a two-step selection announced as "merge A into B"; the verdict is in a live region.
- **Sources:** [S74] (G-Set, 2P-Set, OR-Set), [S75] (strong eventual consistency). The cart items are a course example.
