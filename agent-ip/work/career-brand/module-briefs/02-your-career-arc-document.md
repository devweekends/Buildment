# Module 2: Your career arc document

## Outcome
The learner has a private career arc document: two or three values shown by actions, two or three pillars each backed by one proof and one number (or an honest gap), a one-line statement of what makes them different, skills of four kinds shown by examples, one human detail, the replies to three best-self asks (or a plan to send them), a role card for every role with "I did" separated from "the team did", a gaps list, and a privacy mark on every item (O2). It is the source every later artifact is drawn from; an AI tool may restructure it but never add to it.

## Why this module exists
Hard constraint 1: evidence before prose. A resume written before the material is gathered fills with "responsible for" and adjectives. The arc collects what the learner did, in detail, with where the proof lives, so module 3 can turn it into claims and modules 6 to 8 can draw from it. The sponsor asked for this "brainstorming document before the resume".

## Prerequisites
Module 1 (reader-signals, baseline). Entry: work-history.

## Cross-links and limits
- Course 14, "The Founder Interview" (module 1, "Your part and the team's"), for telling ownership aloud: one line in 2.3.
- Course method throughout, labelled: pillars, role cards, the "AI may restructure, never add" rule. The research behind it is thin: personal branding research is fragmented with no single tested definition [P55]; the reflected best-self idea is a theory paper [P53]; strengths interventions show wellbeing effects, not hiring effects [P54].
- Never teach as fact: ikigai diagrams, personality-type or strengths tests as career evidence (U-J1); "a personal brand is a logo and a slogan".
- Hardship with care: no prompt requires personal hardship; the human detail is optional and never graded; never paste other people's private details into an AI tool.

## 2.5 Write your career arc document (Build, 70 min, stepType Build)
Artifact: the learner's own career arc document (private). Areej's arc is the worked example. An optional 15-minute warm-up completes two of Areej's unfinished role cards (freelance and study project) from canon facts.

Parts and template (give as a Markdown outline the learner copies into a document they own):
1. Values (two or three), each with one action that shows it.
2. Pillars (two or three), each with: the strength in one line, proof 1 (what and where it lives), proof 2, one number or an honest gap.
3. What makes me different (one line).
4. Skills by kind: hard, soft, leadership, initiative; one example each.
5. One human detail (optional) and where it may appear.
6. Best-self asks: who, sent on, reply summary, what repeats (or "plan to send by" a date).
7. Role cards, one per role: role and dates; situation; what I did; what the team did; result; where the proof lives; may I show it publicly (yes, with names removed, no, ask).
8. Gaps (things not measured, skills not yet shown).
9. Privacy marks on every item: private, public-ok, ask first.

How to use AI on own material: paste your own notes (with names of employers' clients, secrets and other people's details removed) and ask the tool to sort them into the template and to ask you questions about empty fields. The tool may restructure and ask; it may not add a skill, a role, a number or a result. Read every line against your memory and evidence; add one line at the end: "AI use: what I asked, what I kept, what I removed." This rule ("restructure, never add") is the base for every later AI draft (resume, profile, outreach, comments).

No-work-yet path: every learner has at least a study or volunteer role; school projects, helping in a family business and club roles count. A learner who will not describe any role may complete Areej's arc in full from canon and the mentor follows up at the gate (open decision 1).

Acceptance criteria:
- All nine parts present (part 5 may say "skipped").
- Each pillar has two proofs with where they live, and a number or an honest gap.
- Every role card separates "I did" from "the team did".
- Every item has a privacy mark.
- The AI-use line is present if AI was used, and no fact appears that the learner cannot point to.

Progressive hint: start with the role you remember best; write "unknown" in "where the proof lives" and move on. If you are stuck on pillars: read your best-self replies (or Areej's) and write what repeats. Worked example: Areej's complete arc (canon). Prose must reach 1,200 words: explain why each part exists, the restructure rule, privacy, and one failure (an AI draft that added "led a team of five").

## 2.6 Your career arc (Check, 10 min, stepType Check)
Five MCQs, skills not recall:
1. Choose the pillar statement that is backed by proof and a number; the others are adjectives or tools.
2. Choose the rewrite of a "we built" sentence that separates the learner's part from the team's.
3. Choose where a human detail fits best for a given reader (About versus a resume bullet).
4. From three best-self replies, choose the theme that should become a pillar.
5. **Check the AI's answer:** an AI tool turned Areej's notes into an arc and added "led a team of five" and "expert in Kubernetes". Choose the problem (the tool added facts the notes do not support; restructure, never add).

## 2.7 Checklist and learning log (Gate, 30 min, stepType Gate)
Checklist:
- My arc has all nine parts.
- Each pillar has two proofs and a number or an honest gap.
- Every role card says what I did and what the team did.
- Every item has a privacy mark.
- If I used AI, the AI-use line is there and every fact is mine.
- I pasted no client secrets or other people's details into any tool.

Learning log:
1. Which pillar is strongest, and what makes it strong?
2. Which role did you undersell before you wrote its card? (Or: why Areej's volunteer card matters.)
3. What did your best-self asks show you? (Optional; or read Areej's replies and say what repeats.)
4. Which gap will you try to close first?

Written task (openquestion, text, minWords 100, allowSpeechInput true): state one pillar, its two proofs with where each lives, and list what in your arc stays private and why (about 120 words).

Rubric parts:
| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pillar stated | One plain line naming a strength, no superlative | A strength buried in adjectives | No pillar |
| Two proofs | Two proofs, each with where it lives; at least one number or honest gap | One proof, or proofs with no location | None |
| Ownership | Each proof says the learner's part | Part of it blurred into "we" | All "we" |
| Privacy | At least two items named as private with a reason | One item, or no reason | None |

Pass rule: every part at least Partly, and "Two proofs" Met. No AI drill in this module. Mentor follow-up: one question on the weaker proof. ChapterRecap: the arc is private, detailed and the source of everything later.
