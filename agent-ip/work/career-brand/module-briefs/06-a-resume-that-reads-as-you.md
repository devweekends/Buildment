# Module 6: A resume that reads as you

## Outcome
The learner can order a resume by strength of proof in a plain file that passes the paste test, tell applicant tracking facts from myths, turn a ledger row into a bullet with ownership, before, after, window and share, let AI restructure their own material and then cut empty sentences, add two or three labelled links tested on a phone, and tailor by changing what comes first. They have a resume where every line maps to a ledger row and one version tailored to a real posting (O6), and a drill or written answer compared with the module 1 baseline (O9).

## Why this module exists
Evidence before prose: the resume is written last, from the ledger, so every number has a source and the resume can link to the case study and showcase. The sponsor asked for a clean, structured, non-AI-sounding resume with no "AI fluff".

## Prerequisites
Module 3 (claim-card, claim-ledger), module 5 (lasting-links, case study), module 2 (ai-restructure-rule), module 1 (first-look-order, baseline).

## Cross-links and limits
- Course 14, "The Founder Interview", module 3 ("Every resume line survives five whys"): link in 6.3 and 6.8 for defending each line aloud.
- Never teach as fact: "75 percent of resumes are rejected by robots" [S76, S77]; "99 percent of Fortune 500 use an ATS"; "two-page resumes get 2.9 times more interest" [S80]; "tailored cover letters bring 50 percent more interviews" [S81]; "quantified bullets get more interviews" [S45]; "ATS tools reject you with AI"; "columns and text boxes break parsing" as vendor fact (only a cautious habit); "Greenhouse fails over 2.5 MB"; "AI screeners prefer AI-written resumes" in the real market [S21].
- Never teach: keyword stuffing, white text, copying the posting, fake experience, a fixed list of banned words, humaniser tools.
- Credits: the Greenhouse support articles for file facts [S78, S79]; the HBS and Accenture hidden workers report [S24]. Resume-service companies only as "a resume company's simulation".

## 6.6 Write your resume from the ledger (Build, 80 min, stepType Build)
Artifact (own; Areej's resume is the worked example, open decision 1): a resume, a mapping of each line to a ledger row, and one version tailored to a real posting.

Steps: choose the structure (6.2); write bullets from claim cards (6.3); optionally ask AI to draft from the arc and ledger, then run the empty sentence edit yourself (6.4); add two or three labelled links and test them on a phone (6.5); run the paste test; save as PDF or Word; tailor one copy to a real posting the learner might apply to (or one of the two provided postings).

Templates: a resume outline (name and links; summary of two lines; Experience; Projects; Volunteer; Skills in one line; Education); a mapping table (resume line, ledger row ID, or "fact" for education and dates); a tailoring record (posting, three changes, the ledger row behind each).

AI on own material: paste ledger rows (no contact details, no secrets) and ask for bullets in the 6.3 shape and an order suggestion for a given posting; it may not add a number, a tool, a responsibility or a result. Every number is checked against the ledger. The AI-use line goes in private notes, not on the resume.

Repair path: no resume draft at all: start from Areej's outline and replace each of her lines with your own.

Acceptance criteria:
- Every line maps to a ledger row (or is a plain fact such as education or dates).
- No empty sentence survives the edit checklist; no "responsible for".
- The file passes the paste test and is a standard format.
- Two or three labelled links, each opened on a phone.
- The tailored copy changes order and the summary, with no copied keywords.
- No number appears that is not in the ledger.

Hint: write the three strongest bullets first; everything else is ordering. If you are stuck on the summary: start from your arc's "what makes me different" line. Prose must reach 1,200 words: why the order, one failure (Areej's old resume), the AI rule, the paste test, tailoring.

## 6.7 A resume that reads as you (Check, 10 min, stepType Check)
Five MCQs:
1. Choose the section order for an early-career fellow with one strong internship result and a study project.
2. Choose the bullet that has ownership, before, after and a window.
3. A friend says "75 percent of resumes never reach a human, so stuff in keywords". Choose the honest reply.
4. Choose the tailored version that changes what comes first without copying the posting.
5. **Check the AI's answer:** an AI tool rewrote Areej's report bullet as "Spearheaded a transformative initiative that boosted backend performance 10x". Choose the problems (empty phrases; "10x" is not in the ledger: 4.6 s to 0.41 s is about 11 times on p95 but the claim drops the window, the source and her share).

## 6.8 Checklist and learning log (Gate, 50 min with the drill, 45 written, stepType Gate)
Checklist:
- Every line of my resume maps to a ledger row or is a plain fact.
- It passes the paste test and is a standard file.
- My two or three links are labelled and open on a phone.
- I have one copy tailored to a real posting, with the three changes recorded.
- I removed contact details before pasting it into the AI interviewer.
- I finished the AI drill and compared the flags with module 1, or wrote the written option.

Learning log:
1. Which flag from module 1 is gone, and which remains? (Or: compare Areej's flags, canon.)
2. Which bullet was hardest to write honestly, and why?
3. What did you cut that you used to think was important?
4. What changed in the tailored copy, and which ledger row backs it?

Written task (openquestion, text, minWords 100, allowSpeechInput true): compare your old and new resume: the biggest change, one flag or weak line that remains, and what you will do about it. Optional section "If you did not finish the AI drill": three lines of your new resume, each with its ledger row and "what was your part?" (about 150 words).

Rubric parts:
| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ledger mapping | Every line maps to a ledger row or a fact | One or two lines unmapped | Many unmapped or invented |
| Bullets | Bullets show ownership, before, after and window or an honest action | Some bullets missing parts | Duties and adjectives |
| File and links | Paste test passed; links labelled and tested | One problem | Unreadable file or broken links |
| Tailoring | Order and summary changed, each backed by a row | Changes without backing | Copied keywords or none |
| Comparison (drill or written) | Flags compared with module 1, or three lines defended | Partial | Missing |

Pass rule: every part at least Partly; "Ledger mapping" must be Met. Mentor follow-up: one to three questions on the top bullets.

AI drill (aiinterview): `context: resume` (new resume, contact details removed), `focus: [resume, ownership, impact]`, `maxTurns: 10`, `whyDepth: 4`, `required: false`. Opening question idea: "Pick the first line of your resume. What exactly did you do, and how do you know it worked?" Rubric intent: the same probes as module 1, so the flags can be compared; probe ownership and sources. Fallback: written section; no VPN. Course 14, "The Founder Interview", for practising aloud.

ChapterRecap: proof first, owned bullets, AI restructures, empty sentences out, real tailoring.
