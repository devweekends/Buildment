# Module 7: LinkedIn: profile, posts, comments and outreach

## Outcome
The learner can write a headline, About, Featured and Experience that put proof first, using the counter on screen and the free paths LinkedIn describes; tell what LinkedIn says about how posts spread from folklore; write a post about real work and a comment that adds one useful thing; write a short, specific outreach message; spend a small monthly budget of connection notes well; and keep a slow posting rhythm measured over eight weeks, all within LinkedIn's rules. They have a rebuilt profile, three outreach drafts, five comment drafts, a four-week plan and a second ten-second test (O7, O1).

## Why this module exists
The sponsor asked for headline, about, featured, experience, posting, commenting well, outreach messages and real strategies to grow an audience. The profile comes after the resume so it draws from the same ledger and links to the case study. Platform facts expire, so every fact here is dated and the learner trusts the screen (research-platforms hard constraint 1).

## Prerequisites
Module 6 (proof-order, owned-bullet), module 5 (case study link), module 2 (human-detail), module 1 (first-look-order, baseline). Entry: linkedin-account.

## Cross-links and limits
- Course 7, "Professional & Business Communication": first LinkedIn basics and outreach etiquette; link in 7.1 and 7.4; do not reteach.
- Never teach as fact (unverified, research-platforms): headline 220 and About 2,600 characters (U-G1); 100 to 200 invitations a week (U-G2); any algorithm recipe (first hour, link penalty, hashtag count, long posts, comment word counts, best hour) (U-G3); newsletter limits (U-G4); "21x" photo views (U-G5); template reply rates (U-H1); best day, word count or follow-up gap (U-H2); that Arab or Palestinian senders get lower replies (U-H3).
- The US study of connection acceptance by photo-signalled race [P33] stays in mentor notes, not learner text (open decision 7).
- Never teach: pods, bots, auto-commenters, auto-DM, scrapers, mass-mail personalisation, deceptive hooks, Premium-dependent steps. No gate needs Premium.
- Every platform fact carries "checked 2026-10; if your screen differs, follow your screen".
- Credits: "LinkedIn Help Center, checked 2026-10"; "LinkedIn Engineering, 2026"; Rajkumar, Aral and colleagues (Science, 2022); Flynn and Lake (2008); Brooks, Gino and Schweitzer (2015).

## 7.3 Posts and comments that add something (Learn, 20 min, stepType Learn, format: beats)
Outcome: tell what LinkedIn says about how posts spread from folklore; write a post about real work and a comment that adds one useful thing; know what the rules forbid.

| # | Heading | Claim the picture must show | Visual | GoDeeper fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Each feed is built for one viewer | The same five posts reach two people in different orders, driven by what each has read and engaged with | Watch-only scene | LinkedIn Engineering (listed 12 March 2026): the feed uses profile information and engagement history, LLM-generated embeddings for retrieval, a sequential model over more than a thousand past interactions, and "never demographic attributes" [P17]. Help Center: content goes to a subset of connections and followers depending on connection strength and settings [P13]. Phrase: "LinkedIn builds a different feed for every person... Nobody outside LinkedIn can tell you an exact recipe." |
| B2 (predict before) | Folklore and what LinkedIn says | Cards "the first hour decides", "links are punished", "use exactly three hashtags" stamped "no first-party source"; beside them LinkedIn's own tips | Watch-only scene | LinkedIn's tips: write content your audience relates to, avoid being overly promotional, keep the profile complete, share frequently, react and comment on others' posts [P13]. A 2019 newsroom post said no format is favoured [P19] (old). Phrase: "Treat any guide that gives an exact number (a magic hour, a magic word count, a magic hashtag count) as a guess." |
| B3 | A post about real work | A short post: the problem, one number with its source, a screenshot, what you would change | Static picture | Posts can be up to 3,000 characters; LinkedIn may limit visibility for automation or excessive posting [P11]. | B4 (PLAYABLE) | A comment that adds one thing | The learner rates five draft comments on a post (Great post!; a specific true compliment; a small example from own work; a question that shows they read it; a link to their own service); each shows what it adds for the author and for readers | Playable canvas (sorter primitive: "adds something" and "adds nothing") | Course method with first-party anchors: LinkedIn suggests building reputation by reacting and commenting [P13] and asks members to respond authentically [P23]; people underestimate how positively genuine compliments are received [P34]. A good comment adds one thing (a number, an example, a question), is short, true and yours; polite, specific disagreement is fine. Phrase: "A good comment adds one useful thing. It is not a way to be seen." |
| B5 | What the rules forbid | Bots, auto-comment tools, groups that agree to like each other's posts and fake accounts, each crossed out; a "restricted" banner | Static picture | User Agreement (effective 3 November 2025), section 8.2: no bots or unauthorised automation to connect, message, comment, like or share, no inauthentic engagement, no algorithm manipulation, one real account [P22]. Community Policies: do not "agree with others ahead of time to like or re-share each other's content" [P23], which is what a pod does (LinkedIn does not use the word "pod"). ## 7.4 Outreach and honest growth (Learn, 25 min, stepType Learn, format: beats)
Outcome: write a short, specific outreach message; start with people you once knew; spend a few notes well; keep a measured rhythm.

| # | Heading | Claim the picture must show | Visual | GoDeeper fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 (predict before) | People say yes more than you expect | Two bars: how often askers expect a yes, and how often people actually agree to a direct, small request | Watch-only scene | Flynn and Lake: across six studies, people underestimated by as much as 50 percent the chance a stranger would agree to a direct request [P28]. Asking for advice made seekers look more competent, more so on hard tasks and when asking an expert personally [P29]. Phrase: "Send the message. In studies of asking for help (Flynn and Lake), people underestimated, by as much as 50% in some studies, how likely others were to say yes." |
| B2 | Start with people you once knew | Contact rings: people you once knew well (teachers, past leads, clients, classmates), people you know a little, strangers | Static MermaidDiagram or picture | Dormant ties gave advice that compared well with current ties in a study of executive MBA students [P30]. A LinkedIn experiment with more than 20 million people found moderately weak ties helped job mobility most, in an inverted U; it tested connection suggestions, not messages [P21]. Phrase: "Start with people you once knew, then people you know a little." And: "It does not prove that sending a message to a stranger works. It is about who is in your network." |
| B3 | Free accounts: connections and few notes | A free account: messages only to connections; a small monthly allowance of personalised notes; a phone app path that cannot add a note | Static picture | InMail is Premium; a Basic account can message only connections [P27]. LinkedIn's pages say three personalised notes a month on one page and five on another, 200 characters each (checked 2026-10); plan for three and count them (open decision 18) [P9, P10]. Invitations from People You May Know in the mobile app cannot be personalised [P9]. Invitation limits apply to everyone; hitting one restricts sending for about a week; withdrawing pending invitations does not lift it; LinkedIn says to invite people you know and trust [P10]. |
| B4 (PLAYABLE) | Spend a small note budget | A contact list and three note tokens; the learner assigns notes; everyone else is reached another way (connect without a note when already in conversation, comment first, email or GitHub); a warning appears if a note is a promotional message to a stranger | Playable canvas (sorter primitive with a budget) | Course method from LinkedIn's own facts: keep notes for cold, high-value people; connect without a note when you already talk; send the real message after they accept [P9]. Policy: no promotional invitations to people you do not know [P23]. |
| B5 | A message a busy person can answer | A short message: who you are in one line, why this person, one specific question, an easy way to say no | Static picture | Course method. LinkedIn's own recruiter data says shorter and individually sent InMails got more replies (a company claim about the opposite direction) [P32]; personalised invitations got 1.5 times the responses in a health-study recruitment trial (another context) [P31]; a template with a first name inserted is bulk mail. AI-rewritten referral requests showed uneven predicted gains in one preprint, not real replies [P35]. Timing, follow-up gaps and word counts are our suggestions: test them and keep a log [U-H2]. Course 7, "Professional & Business Communication", for etiquette. |
| B6 | Grow slowly, measure eight weeks | An eight-week log: posts, comments, replies, profile views, with a slow line rising | Watch-only scene | No controlled study shows a posting habit causes more followers or offers; LinkedIn says share frequently and gives no number [P13]. Phrase: "No one has a proof of what makes a LinkedIn audience grow... So we post real work and comment well, and we measure our own results over eight weeks." Results are tracked, never promised. |

- Predict before B1: "You ask someone you barely know for one small, specific piece of advice. Compared with what you expect, how often do people say yes?"
- Quick check: choose who gets one of three notes.
- Worked example: Areej's three outreach drafts and her note budget (canon).

## 7.5 Rebuild your profile and plan four weeks (Build, 65 min, stepType Build)
Artifacts (profile is own, open decision 1; Areej's is the worked example):
1. The rebuilt profile: headline, About, Featured (the case study link at least, or a plan if no post exists yet), Experience bullets from the resume and ledger, skills, profile URL, an Open to Work decision with its audience.
2. Three outreach drafts: a person you once knew; a moderately weak tie; a founder reached first through a useful comment, then a message after they accept. Each has a note version (200 characters or fewer) and a message version.
3. Five comment drafts on real posts (or the provided sample posts), each adding one thing.
4. A four-week plan (one post a week, five comments a week) and an eight-week log template (posts, comments, replies, profile views).

Templates: a profile worksheet (field, my text, source in ledger or arc); an outreach card (who, tie type, why them, the one question, note version, message version, channel); a comment card (post, what my comment adds, the comment); the plan and log tables.

AI on own material: paste your arc and resume (no contact details) and ask for an About draft in two paragraphs and three headline options from your words; run the empty sentence edit yourself; never use any tool that posts, comments, connects or messages for you (against the rules). Each comment is written by you.

No-account path: a learner without LinkedIn writes the profile in a document and follows course 7, "Professional & Business Communication", to create it later. Nothing needs Premium.

Acceptance criteria:
- The headline says the role and what you do in the first words; About has two proofs from the ledger.
- Every number on the profile is in the ledger.
- Featured holds the case study link (or a plan to add it).
- Three outreach drafts, each with one specific ask and a note version within 200 characters.
- Five comments, each adding one thing.
- A four-week plan and an eight-week log; no automation; no promotional invitations to strangers.

Hint: write the headline last; it is a summary of the About. If you are stuck on outreach: list five people you once worked or studied with. Prose must reach 1,200 words: why each field, one failure (Amjad), the rules, how to log honestly.

## 7.6 LinkedIn (Check, 10 min, stepType Check)
Five MCQs:
1. Choose the headline that tells a founder what the person does in the first words.
2. Choose the comment that adds something to a given post.
3. With three notes left this month, choose who gets one and how to reach the others.
4. A friend invites you to a group that agrees to like each other's posts. Choose the accurate statement of LinkedIn's rule.
5. **Check the AI's answer:** an AI tool says "Post at 8 am on Tuesday with exactly three hashtags and no links; the first hour decides your reach." Choose what is wrong (no first-party source for any of it; LinkedIn's help says be relevant, not overly promotional, and comment on others' posts).

## 7.7 Checklist and learning log (Gate, 30 min, stepType Gate)
Checklist:
- My headline, About, Featured and Experience are rebuilt, and every number is in my ledger.
- Featured holds my case study link (or my plan to add it).
- I have three outreach drafts and five comment drafts.
- My four-week plan and eight-week log are ready.
- I use no automation tool and send no promotional invitations to strangers.
- A partner (or a timer) ran the ten-second test on my new profile.

Learning log:
1. What did the viewer remember this time, compared with module 1?
2. Which outreach draft are you most nervous to send, and what is the smallest version of it?
3. Which piece of LinkedIn folklore did you believe before?
4. What will you count in your eight-week log?

Written task (openquestion, text, minWords 100, allowSpeechInput true): report the second ten-second test and compare it with your module 1 result (what was seen first then and now, and one change still to make), about 120 words.

| Part | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Profile rebuilt | Headline, About, Featured and Experience done; numbers from the ledger | One field missing | Not rebuilt |
| Test compared | Both results named and the difference explained | One result only | No test |
| Drafts | Three outreach drafts with one ask each and five comments that add something | Fewer, or generic | None |
