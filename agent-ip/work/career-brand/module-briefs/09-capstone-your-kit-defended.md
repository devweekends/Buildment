# Module 9: Capstone: your kit, defended

## Outcome
The learner submits the finished kit, shows that every number in the resume and profile maps to a ledger row or an honest gap, defends the kit in a full AI interview or a recorded five-minute walkthrough (or a written defence plus a short live call), fixes one thing the feedback named, turns feedback into the next thing to build or measure, and compares the result with the module 1 baseline (O9, with evidence for O1 to O8).

## Why this module exists
The brief's demo: a mentor opens the kit, picks any line, and the learner shows where its number came from. The sponsor's item 9: the AI interviewer asks about the learner's own work, pushes for better proof and recommends what to build or measure next. The capstone closes the debug-first loop opened in module 1.

## Prerequisites
Every earlier module's artifact: claim-ledger (3), appetite-plan (4), case-study-shape and stranger-test (5), owned-bullet (6), profile-fields (7), work-post and own-site (8), baseline (1).

## Cross-links and limits
- Course 14, "The Founder Interview", chapter 7.2 (reading feedback critically) and the founder interview itself: link in 9.2 and 9.6. Fellows who have not taken course 14 should take it next.
- The drill and the defence never require a key: the recorded walkthrough or the written defence plus a live call completes 9.4. Never use a VPN or location tricks if a key fails.
- Privacy: no contact details, client secrets, private company data or other people's details in anything pasted or recorded.
- Hardship with care: a recording is never forced (audio only accepted; written plus call as the alternative, open decision 21).
- Drill flags are feedback, never a pass criterion (open decision 5).

## 9.3 Assemble your kit (Build, 40 min, stepType Build)
Artifact: the assembled kit, submitted here.

Steps:
1. Collect the eight parts in one kit index (a private document or repository) with a link or file for each: career arc document, inventory and claim ledger, project plan, showcase plan and case study, resume and tailored copy, LinkedIn profile, post and channel plan, website plan.
2. Kit check: list every number in the resume and profile and map each to a ledger row or an honest gap (Areej: 14 numbers, 7 rows, 1 gap).
3. Privacy check: the arc and ledger stay private; the resume is not posted publicly; the case study has names removed and permission recorded; no secrets, keys, customer data or other people's details anywhere public.
4. Link check: open every public link on a phone without logging in.
5. Mark each part published or private (roadmap table) and note any should-have done (deployed demo, published post, live site).

Templates: the kit index; the number map (line, number, ledger row or gap); the privacy checklist; the link check record.

AI on own material: a tool may help find numbers in a pasted resume and profile (contact details removed) for the map; you do the mapping and the checks.

Areej path: learners who used Areej's material for the case study or post say so in the index (open decision 1); the arc, project plan, resume, profile and website plan are their own.

Submission (one openquestion, submissionMode text, minWords 0 so a links-only kit index is accepted, allowSpeechInput true): the kit index with links and a three-line check summary (numbers mapped, privacy checked, links opened).

Acceptance criteria: all eight parts present; every resume and profile number mapped; privacy check passed; every public link opens on a phone; published and private marked.

Hint: do the number map first; most fixes come from it. Prose must reach 1,200 words: why each check, one failure (a number with no row found at the last minute), the published versus private reasoning.

## 9.4 Defend your kit (Build, 40 min, stepType Build)
Artifact: a defence and one fix.

Three paths, any one completes the chapter:
- **A. Full AI interview** (aiinterview fence): `context: resume-and-project` (the resume, and the case study or project plan; contact details removed), `focus: [resume, impact, case-study, best-technical, ownership]`, `maxTurns: 15`, `whyDepth: 4`, `required: false`. Opening question idea: "Pick any line on your resume. Show me where its number came from, and tell me what your part was." Rubric intent (server only, assessment designer): probe sources, windows, ownership, what else changed, the first real user and what the learner would measure next.
- **B. Recorded five-minute walkthrough** (audio only accepted): open three resume lines and show their ledger rows; open the case study and say what you did and what the team did; open the project plan and name the first real user; answer two of the defence questions from 9.1.
- **C. Written defence plus a short live call** with the mentor, decided by the mentor (open decision 21): answer the six defence questions in writing for your two strongest lines, then a 15-minute call.

After the defence: fix one thing the feedback named (a line, a ledger row, a link) and record it.

Submission (one openquestion, submissionMode text, minWords 0 so a recording link alone is accepted, allowSpeechInput true): the path taken, the recording link or written defence where relevant, the one fix (before and after), and the next thing to build or measure.

AI on own material: none beyond the interview itself; the fix is yours.

Areej path: her defence (canon) is the worked example; learners defend their own kit, including any Areej parts they used, saying which they are.

Acceptance criteria: one path completed; one fix recorded with before and after; one next step with a date.

Prose must reach 1,200 words: how to prepare in 10 minutes, how to answer "how was that measured?" with the ledger open, what to do if the key fails (path B or C; no VPN), recording on weak data.

## 9.5 Mixed review (Check, 10 min, stepType Check)
Five MCQs, each from a different module, each asking "what is missing?":
1. A claim card without a window (module 3).
2. A resume bullet with no share stated (module 6).
3. A showcase whose demo asks for a sign-up and has no static base layer (module 5).
4. A cross-posted copy with no link home (module 8).
5. **Check the AI's answer:** an AI tool summarises Areej's case study as "improved performance by 90 percent across the platform". Choose what it got wrong (a different metric and scope than the ledger's one endpoint p95, no window or source, and her lead's part dropped).

## 9.6 Course checklist and learning log (Gate, 30 min, stepType Gate)
Checklist:
- My kit index lists all eight parts with links.
- Every number in my resume and profile maps to a ledger row or an honest gap.
- Nothing private is public; no secrets or other people's details anywhere.
- Every public link opens on a phone without setup.
- I defended my kit (AI interview, recording, or written defence plus call) and fixed one thing.
- I compared the result with my module 1 baseline.
- I answered my mentor's follow-up questions.

Learning log:
1. Compare your module 1 flags or written answers with today's: what changed? (Or: Areej's, canon.)
2. Which line of your kit are you most confident defending, and why?
3. What is the one thing you will build or measure next, and by when?
4. Which part of the kit will you send with your next real application or message?

Written task (openquestion, text, minWords 100, allowSpeechInput true): compare your baseline with your capstone defence and state your next step (about 150 words).

Capstone rubric (the mentor records it here; under 30 minutes per kit):
| Dimension | Met | Partly | Not yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Every resume and profile number maps to a ledger row or an honest gap | One or two unmapped numbers | Several unmapped or invented numbers |
| Ownership | The learner's part is stated apart from the team's in the resume, case study and defence | Blurred in one place | "We" throughout, or overstated |
| Clarity | Plain, specific writing; no empty sentences in the resume, profile or post | A few empty phrases | Mostly adjectives and duties |
| Access and safety | Public links open on a phone without setup; demo plan safe; no private data public | One gap | Private data or secrets public |
| Defence | Answers "how was that measured?" and "what was your part?" for any line picked; one fix made | Answers for some lines | Cannot answer |

Pass rule (roadmap open decision 5): all eight parts exist; every resume and profile number maps to a ledger row or an honest gap ("Evidence" Met); the stranger test passed on a phone (from gate 5.8); the mentor's one to three follow-up questions are answered by resubmitting. The other dimensions need at least Partly. Drill flags are feedback only.
