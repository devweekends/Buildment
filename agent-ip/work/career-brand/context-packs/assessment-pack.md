## Write `work/career-brand/assessment-plan.md`

1. **Outcome map:** every outcome O1 to O9 to its assessments (quizzes, predicts, gates, the capstone), with the level (application, analysis, evaluation, creation).
2. **Quiz chapters (9):** for each, five item specs: the skill tested, the scenario seed (named fictional actor, role and project; never the scene person's exact case), the decision the learner makes, what makes each wrong option plausible (a real mistake), and which item is a "check the AI's answer" item (at least one in modules 1, 3, 6, 7 and 8). Assign the correct position for each of the 45 items in advance so positions 0 to 3 are spread (no position over 35 percent across the course, each quiz uses at least three positions), and plan which items have the key as the **shortest** option and which as a **middle-length** option, so the key is never the longest by default (course 12's review found correct answers that were never the longest, course 14's found every predict key was the shorter option: avoid both patterns; the key should be the longest in roughly a quarter of items, never more than 10 percent longer than the longest distractor).
3. **Predicts (28, one per Learn chapter):** for each, the prompt seed, the two options, the key, and whether the intuitive answer is right (in at least a third of predicts it is). Assign the key's position (first in about half) and its relative length (shorter in about half, longer or equal in about half, never more than 10 percent longer).
4. **Gates (9):** for each, the open question's prompt (with the written option for drill gates as a marked optional part), `minWords`, the rubric parts with Met, Partly and Not yet and observable thresholds, the pass rule, the mentor follow-up questions, and the **mentor key** outline for Areej's case (what to accept, what not to accept, which of the five flags apply: vague ownership, unsupported number, pseudo-story, invented reason risk, jargon without explanation).
5. **AI drills (gates 1.6, 3.8, 5.8, 6.8 and the defence in 9.4):** for each, the full fence values: `title`, `opening` (under 300 characters, plain), `focus`, `context`, `maxTurns`, `whyDepth`, `required: false`, and the `rubric` string (600 to 1,400 characters, one line, no line breaks). Every rubric must: (1) say what a strong answer shows for this module; (2) probe how each number was measured ("How did you measure it? Over what window? Where can I see it?"), ownership ("What was your part?"), and the alternative on technical choices; (3) accept "I did not measure that", "I don't know" and "that was not my decision" as full answers and never push the learner to guess or invent; (4) judge features, not length, quote the learner's words, and put a level word (Strong, Getting there or Not yet) at the start of each focus-area note, because the block has no numeric score; (5) use the five flags by name; (6) in next steps, recommend one thing to measure next or one small thing to build next around the learner's own needs, without naming a specific project idea; (7) kind, direct tone, about the answer not the person. The drill context the learner pastes comes from their career arc, claim ledger, case study or resume, with names and contact details removed.
6. **Capstone (9.3 and 9.4):** the kit rubric (evidence, ownership, clarity, access and safety, defence) with Met, Partly and Not yet, the pass rule from the roadmap, and the defence questions.
7. **Blind key check plan:** say that every MCQ and predict will be re-answered blind by a second model family with `agent-ip/scripts/blind-keys.mjs`, and that the PR asks for a human key check.

Rules: no em or en dashes anywhere; no real people or companies; no opinion graded in a quiz (opinion goes to an open question); every key follows from something the course teaches or from arithmetic. No git. Absolute paths. Report: counts, the position and length plan in numbers, and any outcome you could not assess well.
