## How your mentor will read it

A mentor will read your two stories part by part. There is no required size of result. A small, honest result can be fully Met.

**Part 1: before, after and window.**

- **Met:** All three are stated in plain numbers, with the unit.
- **Partly:** A percentage with one of the three missing.
- **Not yet:** A percentage with no base.

**Part 2: source.**

- **Met:** A named source for each number.
- **Partly:** A source for one number only.
- **Not yet:** No source.

**Part 3: your share.**

- **Met:** Names what else changed and uses a comparison, or says plainly it cannot separate them.
- **Partly:** Mentions other changes but does not use them.
- **Not yet:** Claims the whole change.

**Part 4: gap or estimate.**

- **Met:** A gap says what you would measure, and each estimate says how you got it.
- **Partly:** A gap with no plan.
- **Not yet:** An unlabelled estimate or an invented number.

**Pass rule:** no part is Not yet, and at most one part is Partly.

The mentor then replies with one or two short questions. For example: "Where does that number come from?" or "What else changed that month?". You answer them in writing or in a voice note. That reply is part of the gate, so look out for it after you submit.

## Practice with the AI interviewer

This drill asks what changed because of one piece of your work, and then asks "how do you know?" about every number you give. Paste 50 to 150 words about one story from your bank, with roles instead of names. It is a good place to test whether your before, after, window and source hold up when someone asks.

Never paste your full name, phone number, email, address, ID numbers, passwords, keys, client or employer names, private company data or other people's details. If you do not have a key, the written task below completes this gate.

```aiinterview
title: "What changed because of your work"
opening: "Pick one piece of your work that made a difference. What changed because of it, and how do you know?"
focus: [impact, ownership]
context: project
maxTurns: 10
whyDepth: 3
required: false
rubric: "A strong interview shows a result stated with a before, an after, a time window and a source, and how much of the change was the learner's own, with other changes named. Look for a clear line between what the learner did and what the team or the season did. Probes: if the answer gives a percentage, ask 'From what?'. After any number ask 'How do you know?' or 'Where does that number come from?'. If the answer says 'we', ask 'What was your part?'. If the answer describes habits, ask for one specific time. Ask what else changed in the same period. Stop rules: accept 'I did not measure that', 'I don't know' and 'that was not my decision' as full answers, reply briefly, and ask what they would measure next. Never push the learner to guess a number or invent one. Scoring: judge features, not length. An honest gap with a plan to measure scores higher than an unsourced number. Quote the learner's words as evidence. Use these flag names when they apply: vague ownership, unsupported number, pseudo-story, invented reason risk, jargon without explanation. Tone: kind and direct, about the answer and not the person. If the evidence is thin, suggest where it might exist, or a small project around a need of their own."
```
