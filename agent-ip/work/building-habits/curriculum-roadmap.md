# Curriculum roadmap: Building Habits That Last

> Chapters were drafted before G2B instructor sign-off, on the sponsor's standing instruction (see `decision-log.md`), as with courses 3 to 10. If instructors change this roadmap, the chapters will be revised to match.

## In one paragraph
A practical, research-grounded course on building good habits and weakening unwanted ones, for fellows who study, freelance and work remotely through power cuts, weak internet, displacement and family duties. Fellows learn what a habit really is (a behaviour a familiar situation starts for you), how long habits take to form, how to start with a tiny step tied to a clear cue, how to let their surroundings do the work, how to weaken a habit by removing its cue rather than relying on willpower, how to track without shame and restart after a missed day, and how to fit habits into a real daily rhythm of study, work, prayer times, sleep and family. From module 2 each fellow may run one small "course habit" and track it, so the capstone can review real data. The capstone is a 30-day habit plan the fellow designs, runs and reviews, or a provided case, walked through with a mentor in five minutes.

## Learner and entry prerequisites
- **Learner:** Gaza fellowship fellows, mixed backgrounds, second-language English, mostly on phones with weak data and power cuts.
- **Prior knowledge:** plain English reading. No course required.
- **Time available:** about 16 hours, for example 45 minutes to an hour a day over three to four weeks.
- **Entry diagnostic:** none graded; the predict in 1.1 sets the tone.

## The course habit
From gate 2, each fellow may choose one small habit, write it as a cue and a tiny step, and track it on paper or a phone. Gates 3, 5 and 6 each ask for one short update, and the capstone reviews the data. This is optional: a fellow who prefers not to, or whose month is too disrupted, uses a provided case at every step, with no penalty.

## Capstone
- **What the learner submits:** for their own 30-day plan, or one of three provided cases (each with a 30-day tracker table and a short AI answer that makes one mistake from the course): the habit as a cue and a tiny step; the surroundings and friction changes; the backup version; the tracker summary with missed days, their causes and the restarts; the decision to keep, change or drop, with one change for the next 30 days. Written, with an optional voice note link.
- **Five-minute demo:** the fellow walks a mentor through the plan and review.
- **Defence questions:** What caused most of your missed days, and how do you know? What did you change after a miss? Why is your next change one change, not three? What will your hardest-day version be next month?
- **Review:** a rubric with three levels per line (Met, Partly, Not yet), a pass rule, and a mentor key for each provided case in the model answer, about 15 minutes per capstone.
- **Timing:** a course taken at one chapter a day gives about 25 days between gate 2 and the capstone. A fellow with fewer than 30 days of data reviews what they have, says so, and may submit the full review later, or uses a provided case.

## Variants
Not applicable for version 1. Scenario packs (study, freelance, family) are open decision 5.

## Out of scope
Therapy and clinical topics (addiction, eating disorders, depression, trauma); neuroscience and "dopamine" explanations; productivity apps and tools; goal-setting and time management as subjects; religious instruction; any one book's framework taught as the method.
