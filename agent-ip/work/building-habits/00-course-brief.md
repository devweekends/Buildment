# Course brief: building-habits (course 12)

## Why this course exists

The sponsor asked for a practical habit-development course, "kind of Atomic Habits type". No fellowship course teaches how habits form or how to build and keep them. Course 6 (Ownership, Initiative and Adaptability) touches personal improvement plans, and course 10 (Why Things Happen) teaches cause and effect, but neither teaches the habit methods themselves. Fellows study, freelance and work remotely with unstable power and internet, displacement and family duties, so routines break often. A course that teaches how to build habits that survive those breaks, and how to restart without shame, serves every other course.

The course is grounded in research, not in one book. Ideas are credited to their authors (for example Wendy Wood, Peter Gollwitzer, BJ Fogg, Phillippa Lally and colleagues, and James Clear's book as a popular summary). The method, the cases and the wording are the course's own.

## One sentence

Fellows learn how habits really form and use that to build one good habit and weaken one bad one: design the cue, the place and the first tiny step, make the good choice easy and the bad one harder, track without shame, restart after a missed day, and keep the habit going through power cuts, weak internet and family life.

## Where it sits

Course 12 in the pipeline numbering. Soft-skills, Beginner to Medium. It has no prerequisite and can be taken at any point; it pairs well with course 6 (ownership) and helps fellows keep a study rhythm through the longer tech courses (8 and the marketplace course). Course 10's "fair test" idea appears once, gently, in the capstone review, and is re-explained there.

## Learner
- **Who:** Gaza fellowship fellows building careers in tech and remote work, from mixed backgrounds.
- **Prior knowledge:** plain English reading. No course is required.
- **Language:** Arabic first, English second. Short sentences, common words, every term defined on first use.
- **Device and connection:** mostly phones, weak or expensive data, power cuts. Light pages, no images, every interaction saves on its own.
- **Life context:** many live with displacement, shared homes, family duties and changing daily schedules. Prayer times are part of many fellows' daily rhythm. The course treats this as ordinary background, never as drama, and never asks fellows to share personal hardship.
- **Time available:** about 15 hours, for example 45 minutes to an hour a day over three to four weeks.
- **Motivation:** keep a study rhythm, finish courses and freelance work, sleep and move better, spend steady time with family and prayer, and stop losing hours to the phone.
- **What they will do with the result:** run their own 30-day habit plan during and after the course, and use the same tools for study, deep work and remote jobs.

## Outcome
By the end, the learner can:
1. **Explain** what a habit is (a behaviour started by a cue in a stable context, with little thought) and **describe** the cue, routine and reward in a real habit.
2. **Write** a habit as a tiny first step tied to a clear cue, using an if-then plan or an anchor after an existing routine.
3. **Redesign** a place or routine so the good behaviour takes less effort and the unwanted one takes more.
4. **Plan** how to weaken a bad habit: find its cue and reward, remove or avoid the cue, add friction and choose a replacement.
5. **Track** a habit simply and **respond** to a missed day with a restart plan instead of giving up.
6. **Adapt** a habit for real conditions: power cuts, weak internet, prayer times, family duties and changed homes, with a smaller backup version.
7. **Design, run and review** a 30-day habit plan with tracking data (their own, or a provided case), and decide what to keep, change or drop.

## Demo
In five minutes the fellow walks a mentor through their 30-day plan and review: the habit written as a cue and a tiny step, the friction they removed or added, the backup version for a hard day, the tracking record with its missed days, what they changed after the review and why.

Not applicable as written: there is no software product. Read as **course scope**:

## Production bar
Not applicable as a code production bar. The equivalent bar:
- Every graded key follows from a rule taught in the course or from arithmetic, and is re-answered blind by an independent reviewer.
- Every claim about research (how long habits take, if-then plans, willpower, streaks, fresh starts, identity) matches the research dossier and its "phrase it this way" notes. Popular claims the dossier marks as overstated (for example "21 days") are taught only as myths.
- No book's wording or named framework is presented as the course's own; ideas are credited.
- No invented statistics. Scenario numbers are labelled as the scenario's own.
- `content:validate:source`, `content:audit` and `agent-ip/scripts/audit-course.mjs` results recorded; import and a click-through at 375px and desktop, light and dark.

## Variants
Not applicable: a foundation course has no product to vary. Scenario packs (for example a study pack and a freelancer pack) can follow later as an instructor decision.

## Constraints
- **Stack:** the buildment reader and existing blocks; canvases on the registered renderers (sequence, state machine, factor, slider), each with a 14A spec, plus a custom canvas only where a claim needs it.
- **Budget:** about 15 hours core, inside the soft-skills band of 8 to 20 hours. Chapters meet the full agent-ip prose minimums (1,200 and 700 prose words).
- **Platform limits:** state machine grids at most 3 columns by 2 rows; canvases only in Learn chapters, none in gates or quizzes; catalog categories are tech and soft-skills only.
- **Care rules:** warm and practical; no hustle culture, no shame, no "just try harder"; hardship appears as ordinary background, never for drama; prayer and faith appear naturally as part of a daily rhythm, with no theological claims; every personal prompt offers a provided case; no politics or war as case material.
- **Legal and IP:** no copied wording from any book; frameworks credited to their authors; short quotations only where the dossier allows.

## Non-goals
- **No therapy or clinical content:** no treatment of addiction, eating disorders, depression or trauma. The course says once, kindly, where a mentor or professional is the right help.
- **No neuroscience lessons:** no "dopamine" explanations or brain-region talk.
- **No productivity-app or tool tutorials:** paper, a notebook or any simple tracker will do.
- **No goal-setting or time-management course:** these appear only where they touch habits.
- **No religious instruction:** prayer appears as a fixed point in the day that can anchor a habit, not as a subject.

## Success signals
- Gate answers show habits written as a cue plus a tiny step, not as wishes ("study more").
- Capstone reviews show real tracking data with missed days and a restart, not a perfect streak.
- Fellows keep a course habit going through the course and report adapting it during power cuts.
- Mentor review time per capstone stays near 15 minutes with the rubric.
