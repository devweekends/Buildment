### S3

- id: N1
  location: all 68 quiz items and 26 predicts (counts above), notably 3.3 (2 of 2 strictly longest), 5.2 (2 of 2), 2.5 and 7.3 (2 of 5 each).
  observation: The correct option is now longest or tied-longest in 35 of 68 quiz items (51%) and strictly longest in 25 (37%); it is the shortest in only 4. "Pick the longest" scores about 44% against 25% chance. Margins sit at 3 to 10% over the longest wrong option, just under the audit's 10% warning, so the audit cannot see it.
  why it matters: This is the round 1 defect reversed and milder. The playbook says to rewrite a set when the correct option is longest in half or more. A test-wise learner can still gain about 19 points by form.
  suggested fix: Re-draft about a third of the 4-option items so the correct option is mid-length or shortest (the rewrite should change the distractors, not trim the key), then re-run the counts: target pick-longest within 30% and the correct option longest or tied in at most a third.

- id: N2
  location: predicts in 5.1 (day 29), 5.2 (tracker week), 5.3 (streak or days done), and to a lesser extent 6.4 (tea at 18:00).
  observation: These are still answered before the learner guesses. 5.2's predict follows "It told a different story from the one in his head" and "Yousef's memory says he misses on tired days"; 5.3's follows the section that has just contrasted "2" and "25 of 30", and the 5.1 key words define the streak; 5.1 asks what a man should do tonight, which anyone answers for someone else (the chapter itself says so); 6.4 follows "The clock time stayed the same, and the day moved around it".
  why it matters: Three of 26 predicts still produce no surprise, in the module where the predict-first pattern has to prepare the learner for self-review.
  suggested fix: Move the 5.2 and 5.3 predicts above the sentences quoted, give the data in the prompt (the three missed days' notes are hidden until step 9; the streak question can ask for the numbers 6 and 29), and replace the 5.1 predict with one that asks about the five empty squares' cause.

- id: N3
  location: 3.6 (exemplar "Ruba is a pharmacist") and 4.2, 4.3 (Ruba, the pharmacist Maha works with).
  observation: The same name and job appear for two different people, one a certificate student with a tablet and the other a calm colleague whose phone lives in a locker.
  why it matters: Second-language readers will assume they are the same person and look for the link; the gate exemplar is meant to be unfamiliar.
  suggested fix: Rename the 3.6 exemplar; while there, separate Sara from Sana and Rana from Reem (the remaining half of S3-14).

- id: N4
  location: 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 6.2, 6.3, 6.4 (the canvas copy blocks).
  observation: In modules 1 to 4 and 7 the four canvas lines ("What this shows", "Try this", "What changed", "Limits of the model") are separate paragraphs; in these six chapters they run together as one paragraph with bold lead-ins.
  why it matters: On a phone this is a wall of text exactly where the learner is told to "check your prediction", and it makes the instructions hard to find for second-language readers.
  suggested fix: Split into four short paragraphs as in 1.2 to 4.4 and 7.2.
