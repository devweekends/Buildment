## In one paragraph

A practical course on using AI as an enabler, not a threat and not a replacement. Fellows first replace fear with evidence: why AI looks bigger than it is, what labour data and studies really show (including real harm to entry-level hiring in some fields), and why the measured pattern is fewer people doing more work, so the goal is to be one of the people a smaller team keeps. They see what an AI tool does not know and which calls stay theirs; pick tools by the job and keep their work in files they own; learn deeply from five angles with recall; run daily labs on GitHub. Then the heart: they slow down to understand (research first, ask two assistants, compare, check the primary page, ask what was left out), and learn why an assistant gives the common first answer and how context changes the right answer, on four real engineering cases. They plan a six-month project, then learn to grow fast and go deep with a team, use an AI toolbelt to show and ship (visuals, short lessons, their own voice with consent, CLIs and connectors, critique of their own work), share honestly in their own voice, and decide where a person must lead. The capstone is the real start of their project with its first 30 days carried out, a shipped demo, one published piece of content and a short defence, or a provided case.

## Learner and entry prerequisites

- **Learner:** Gaza fellowship fellows building careers in tech, mostly early-career engineers aiming at remote work, second-language English, mostly on phones with weak data and power cuts.
- **Prior knowledge:** as v1 for modules 1 to 4. Modules 5, 6 and 9 assume a little coding, Git and GitHub; every task has a provided case for fellows who cannot run tools.
- **Time:** about 44 hours: about 40 hours of chapters (2,385 minutes) and about 4 to 5 hours of lab days that sit inside the capstone's 30 days. Open decision 10 offers a 37-hour path.
- **Entry diagnostic:** none graded; the predict in 1.1 sets the tone.

## Where it sits

After course 9 (checking one AI answer) and before course 8 (building AI systems) and the system design course. Links out, without repeating: course 16 "Your Career Brand" for resume, LinkedIn and blog mechanics; course 14 "The Founder Interview" for stories and the AI interviewer; courses 5 and 6 for collaboration and ownership; the system design course for time series data, caching and the Outbox pattern in depth.

## Capstone (11.6, Build, 150 minutes plus the 30 days)

The five v1 parts stay, for **their own project** or **a provided case** (always available, so nobody needs a laptop, a project or a public profile):

1. **A six-month plan:** the project in one sentence, the areas month by month, hours a week, the learning share and why.
2. **This week's learning plan:** resources an AI suggested that the fellow checked exist, five angles, a recall test.
3. **A GitHub lab log with at least ten lab days** inside the first 30 days, with one lab where a part was broken on purpose and explained.
4. **One page of structured notes** in their own words.
5. **One public write-up**, adapted for a second platform, with an honest AI-use line. Public posting stays the fellow's choice; a private draft is accepted.

v2 adds part 6, **the first 30 days carried out as an "AI advantage" plan**: the fellow picks one deep project or one series, and by day 30 hands in (a) one decision researched with two assistants and primary pages, with the context that changed the answer (modules 5 and 6), (b) a shipped demo: a link that opens on a phone, or a short recording, made with the toolbelt (module 9), and (c) the write-up from part 5 published as the piece of content, built from real commits (module 10). The lab log from part 3 is the 30-day log.

**Defence (11.7):** a short defence of the project and its decision with the existing AI interviewer block (the learner's own OpenRouter key; focus on best technical work, five whys and impact), or a written defence that completes the same step. The mentor then asks one "why not the other option?" question.

**Provided case:** Ibrahim's clinic queue app from v1, extended with a 30-day log, a decision about where to keep the queue (an AI's default answer and the clinic's context), a demo recording described in text, and a draft post with fake-sounding claims to fix.

**Five-minute walkthrough:** as v1, plus the 30-day log, the demo and the decision record.

## Open decisions for instructors

13. **Disputed company cases.** Name Builder.ai, Amazon Just Walk Out and the July 2025 database deletion with dated, neutral wording, or use only research?

15. **Voice cloning.** Free plans cannot clone, Professional cloning needs a paid plan, and no supported-countries page exists. Keep own-voice cloning as an optional demonstration (current plan), or drop it?
16. **Cloud cost practice.** Card-gated sign-ups make a live account hard; the chapter uses a supplied sample cost report. Who supplies a real anonymised report?
17. **AI-use line for grammar help.** Some platforms need no label for grammar-only help. Keep the course rule (always add the line) or follow each platform?
18. **Course title.** Keep the title, or add the sponsor's theme as a subtitle ("Fear less, build more")?
