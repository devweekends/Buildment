# Critique A: Product Sense for Engineers (course 18)

## Method and limits

- I read all 53 chapters and all 49 canvas `data.ts` and `spec.md` files, the rubric, the authoring standards, the playbook and the dossier summary. I did not read the writers' rationale.
- I measured the form of every assessment item with scripts over `course.md`: 82 MCQs and 25 predicts. I also counted beats, words per beat, diagrams, canvases, open questions and estimated reading load. I recomputed 19 of the contrast ratios in the course by the WCAG formula; all matched (for example 2.85, 4.48, 4.54, 7.00, 4.13, 5.17, 5.70, 6.70, 3.45).
- Not available to me: the course audit report, the canvas QA report and unit-test results, the rendered reader at 375px, and `roadmap-confirmation.md`. Dimension 20 is therefore scored from specs and data only, and dimension 19 cannot be scored.
- Clean checks: zero en or em dashes in `course.md` and `canvases.md`; no AI attribution; no `BigWordAlert` in beats chapters; every Learn chapter has 4 to 6 beats; no beat has more than 68 words of its own text; every Learn chapter has folds of 930 words or more.

## What works (preserve these)

1. **Beat discipline is real.** All 25 Learn chapters have 4 to 6 beats, each beat's own text is 68 words or fewer (median 53), every chapter has one predict and 1 to 2 quizzes, and folds carry 930 to 1,900 words with sources and labels (standard, research, heuristic, practice).
2. **Specimen canvases are the right medium** for weak data and phones: small SVG screens drawn from data, no screenshots, one claim per canvas, honest "Limits" in the data. The best are 2.5 contrast ladder, 3.2 four states, 3.4 tap targets, 5.2 spot-the-slop, 7.3 skim before/after.
3. **Good predicts that really predict:** 1.2 (last time versus next time), 1.5 (count rose, share fell), 3.2 (0.4 seconds needs nothing), 6.3 (swap the order, the winner flips). These are answerable from intuition plus the preceding beat and then show a real surprise.
4. **Every MCQ option has its own explanation**, stems are scenarios with a new fictional case, and keys follow taught rules (WCAG levels, NN/g heuristics, the course's own four-part no, x of y). I found no key I would call wrong.
5. **Every practice chapter models a failure.** Maryam's wrong first attempt (a rule copied from a blog, "Not now: too hard to build", an empty state written as an error, all elements deleted) shows the near-miss and the fix. This is the strongest teaching device in the course.
6. **Rubrics are mostly checkable.** Met, Partly, Not yet, with a stated pass rule and a named mentor follow-up question. 2.6, 4.6, 5.5 and 6.4 are close to inter-rater safe because they count things (sizes, steps, lines, rows).
7. **Gate 3 (Samir's page, six planted faults) and 4.6 (three Wasel order forms written out in full)** are doable on a phone, need no account, and the mentor key matches the case.
8. **The capstone maps to the practices** (1.6 to case study, 2.6 and 3.7 and 5.5 to screens, 4.6 and 6.4 to the log) and accepts a storyboard, a private submission and an honest "not tested yet". The written defence always completes the gate; the AI interviewer is `required: false` and the privacy warning is plain.
9. **Honesty about evidence** (F-pattern as a failure, five-user limits, "no Arabic numbers exist", vendor lists as questions not rules, dated tool facts) matches the production bar.
10. **Module 6's method** (same rubric to three tools, order swap, check or ask or drop, decision log) gives learners a transferable habit and feeds the capstone log directly.

## Judgement on the measured form of the answer keys

- **Predicts (25):** the correct label is shorter than or equal to the wrong one in 25 of 25 (strictly shorter in 24). With two options this is a perfect rule: "pick the shorter label" scores 100%. Usable cue. Defect A-01.
- **MCQs (82):** the correct option is the strict longest in only 2 of 82 (9 of 82 counting ties) when chance is about 25%, and strict shortest in 19. This is a mild inverse cue: dropping the longest option almost never removes the key. Defect A-10.
- **A stronger cue the length audit misses:** the word "because" appears in 80 of the 246 wrong options (33%) and in 3 of the 82 correct options (4%); "since" adds 24 versus 2. Distractors carry a flawed justification, keys are stated plainly. In 40 of 82 questions a distractor has "because" and the key does not; in 10 questions three of four options have it and the key is the odd one out. A learner who knows nothing but rejects options with "because" or "since", options with absolute words (never, always, any, only) and the longest option scores about 54% (45 of 82), against 25% for blind guessing; the "because" filter alone gives 44%. Defect A-02.
